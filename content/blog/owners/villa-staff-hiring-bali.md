Hiring villa staff in Bali starts with deciding the role clearly — housekeeper, pool and garden crew, maintenance, guest-facing host — before looking for candidates, then verifying references directly with past employers rather than trusting a written recommendation letter alone. Word-of-mouth referrals from other villa owners tend to produce better outcomes than open advertising.

This guide covers how the hiring process actually works for a Bali villa, what to check before offering a role, and the trade-off between hiring staff directly yourself versus having a management company do it as part of a wider service.

## Define the role before you look for people

The most common hiring mistake is advertising for "villa staff" without specifying what the role actually covers. A housekeeper hired without a clear brief may assume pool maintenance isn't their job; a gardener may not expect to also handle minor repairs. Write the role down — hours, specific duties, who they report to, and what's explicitly out of scope — before you start looking.

This matters more for smaller villas where one person often covers multiple functions. Being vague at the hiring stage creates disputes later about what someone is or isn't required to do.

## Where candidates come from

- Referrals from other villa owners or managers in the same area, generally the most reliable source
- Existing staff recommending someone they've worked with before
- Local employment networks and word of mouth within the villa staff community
- A management company's existing pool of vetted staff, if you're hiring through one

Open online advertising works less well for villa roles than it might for other jobs, since trust and local reputation matter more than a CV in this line of work.

## Checking references properly

A reference check that consists of reading a letter the candidate hands you isn't a reference check — those letters are sometimes written by the candidate themselves. Call the previous employer directly, ask specific questions about reliability, honesty and how they handled problems, not just "were they a good worker." Ask how long they worked there and why they left. Any inconsistency between what the candidate says and what the former employer says is worth pausing on.

For roles involving keys, security or unsupervised access to the villa when guests are present, this checking step matters more, not less.

## What to agree in writing before day one

| Item | Why it needs to be explicit |
|---|---|
| Job title and specific duties | Prevents disputes about what's in or out of scope |
| Pay and payment schedule | Avoids ambiguity, especially for staff paid partly in cash |
| Working hours and days off | Villa work often has irregular hours around bookings |
| Who supervises them | Especially important for owners not resident in Bali |
| Probation period, if any | Gives both sides an exit without drama early on |
| Notice period | Protects continuity of villa operations |

Skipping this step because "everyone in the area does it this way" is a common shortcut that causes problems the first time something goes wrong.

## Hiring direct vs through a management company

Hiring staff directly gives an owner full control over who's chosen and how they're paid, but it puts the owner on the hook for payroll administration, disputes, and — critically — supervision, which is hard to do well from outside Bali. Hiring through a management company shifts that load: staff are recruited, supervised and paid as part of the service, usually funded through a pre-approved pass-through cost rather than a separate fee the owner administers directly.

Neither approach is universally better. An owner who lives in Bali or visits often, and wants direct control, may prefer hiring themselves. An owner managing remotely usually finds the supervision gap in direct hiring becomes the real cost, not the wages themselves — which is the argument for [villa management](/villa-management/) covering staffing as part of a single accountable service.

## Training after hiring

Hiring well is only half the process — a new hire still needs onboarding into villa-specific procedures: key handling, alarm systems, guest communication boundaries, and pool chemical handling if that's part of the role. Skipping this step because a candidate has "experience" elsewhere assumes every villa runs the same way, which they don't. We cover what a reasonable onboarding and training process looks like in [villa staff training standards](/blog/owners/villa-staff-training-standards/), the natural next step after a hire is made.

## What to do next

Hiring villa staff in Bali well is mostly about being specific upfront — the role, the pay, the supervision line — and checking references properly rather than trusting paperwork alone.

- Write the role and duties down before advertising or asking for referrals

## FAQ

### Should I hire staff before or after buying a villa?

Most owners wait until closer to completion, since staffing needs depend on the specific property — pool size, number of bedrooms, layout. If you're planning to self-manage from day one, it's worth lining up candidates in the weeks before handover rather than starting the search after you already need coverage.

### How long does it typically take to hire villa staff?

It varies with the role and how specific the requirements are — referral-based hiring for a well-known role like housekeeping can move quickly, while a guest-facing role needing strong English and hospitality experience may take longer to fill well. Rushing the process to fill a gap tends to produce worse outcomes than waiting for the right candidate.

### Can one person cover multiple roles in a small villa?

Yes, this is common for smaller 1–2 bedroom villas, where a single caretaker might handle light housekeeping, garden upkeep and basic maintenance checks. Be explicit in the hiring brief about which combined duties are expected, since assuming it's obvious is where disputes start.

### What happens if a hire doesn't work out?

A written probation period, agreed before day one, gives both sides a clean way to end the arrangement without drama. Without one, ending an unsuccessful hire becomes more awkward and can affect the villa's reputation within the local staff network, which matters for future hiring.

### Is it better to hire staff who've worked in villas before?

Prior villa experience is a strong positive signal, particularly for guest-facing roles, but it's not the only factor — attitude, reliability and how references describe their honesty often matter more than years of experience. Check references regardless of stated experience level.
