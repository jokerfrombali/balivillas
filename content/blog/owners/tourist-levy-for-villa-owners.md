The Bali tourist levy is a fee charged to foreign visitors entering Bali, introduced to fund environmental and cultural preservation, and it is separate from any tax or licence a villa owner holds. For a villa owner, the levy itself is not a cost you pay — it is paid by the guest, usually before or on arrival — but it does affect guest communication, and it is often confused with unrelated owner obligations like accommodation tax or licensing fees.

This guide covers what the levy actually is, how it touches a villa booking, and where owners commonly mix it up with something that does apply directly to them.

## What the levy is and who pays it

The levy applies to foreign tourists entering Bali and is typically paid as part of the arrival process, either online in advance or at designated points on entry (as of 2026, verify the current amount and exact collection mechanism at the official government portal, since the process has been adjusted since introduction). It funds is directed toward environmental and cultural initiatives rather than going to individual businesses, and villa owners and managers have no role in collecting or remitting it.

## Why villa owners still need to know about it

Guests booking a villa stay sometimes assume the levy is bundled into their accommodation cost, or ask the villa to arrange it on their behalf, particularly if they are unfamiliar with how it works. A villa that proactively tells guests about the levy before arrival — what it costs, how to pay it, and that it is separate from the booking — avoids a confused or frustrated guest showing up without having sorted it.

- Mention the levy in pre-arrival guest communication, with a link to the official portal
- Make clear it is paid to government, not to the villa or the booking platform
- Do not collect or process levy payments yourselves unless you have a specific, confirmed arrangement to do so

We include this kind of guidance in pre-arrival messaging for the villas we manage, since it heads off a surprisingly common source of guest confusion at check-in.

## What the levy is not

The tourist levy is not the same as Indonesian income tax on rental revenue, which is the owner's obligation and covered in [tax on villa rental income in Bali](/blog/owners/bali-rental-income-tax/). It is also not the same as any local accommodation tax some regencies apply to villa rates, covered in [villa tax and service charge in Bali](/blog/owners/bali-villa-tax-and-service-charge/). All three sit in the same general territory of "money related to tourism in Bali" but go to different places and apply to different parties — the guest, the villa's declared income, and the accommodation rate respectively.

| Charge | Who pays | Goes toward |
|---|---|---|
| Tourist levy | Foreign visitor, on arrival | Environmental and cultural fund |
| Income tax on rental revenue | Villa owner | National tax office |
| Local accommodation tax (where applicable) | Guest, via the rate | Local government |

## What guests ask us

Guests occasionally ask whether the levy is included in their quoted villa rate. It is not, and we are clear about that in the booking conversation — the levy is a separate arrival cost paid directly to the official channel, not something that flows through the villa booking at all. Being upfront about this avoids the awkward situation of a guest discovering it at immigration and assuming it should have been mentioned earlier.

## Should a villa help guests pay the levy?

Some villas and managers offer to help guests navigate the levy payment process as a guest-service touch, particularly for repeat or long-stay guests less familiar with Bali's entry requirements. This is a courtesy, not a legal obligation, and villas should be careful not to represent themselves as an official collection point, since the levy must be paid through the correct government channel.

## What to do next

## FAQ

### Do villa owners pay the Bali tourist levy?

No. The levy is paid by foreign visitors on arrival, not by villa owners or managers. Owners have separate tax and licensing obligations tied to renting the villa, which are unrelated to the guest-paid tourist levy.

### Should the villa collect the tourist levy from guests?

No, not unless there is a specific official arrangement to do so. The levy should be paid through the correct government channel, and villas should direct guests there rather than attempting to collect it as part of the booking.

### Is the tourist levy the same as Bali's accommodation tax?

No. The tourist levy is a visitor-entry fee funding environmental and cultural initiatives. A local accommodation tax, where it applies, is a separate charge on the villa's rate and goes to local government. They are commonly confused but administered differently.

### How much is the Bali tourist levy?

The amount and payment process are set by the relevant government authority and have been subject to change since introduction (verify the current figure and process at the official portal before advising guests, since quoting a specific number here risks being out of date).

### Does the tourist levy affect how much I can charge for my villa?

Not directly. It is a separate cost paid by the guest to government, not part of the villa's rate. It is worth mentioning to guests during booking so they budget for it, but it does not change what you charge for the stay itself.
