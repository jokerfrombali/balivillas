Taking payments for direct villa bookings in Bali means setting up a way to collect deposits and balances without the built-in protection an OTA's payment system provides, so the terms need to be clear and written down before the first payment is requested. Skipping this step is the most common reason direct-booking disputes turn ugly.

Booking direct — over WhatsApp or a company's own site — cuts out OTA commission, which is the whole appeal, but it shifts the responsibility for handling money cleanly onto the owner or manager. This guide covers what that responsibility actually involves.

## Why direct payment needs more structure than a platform booking

When a guest books through Airbnb or Booking.com, the platform holds the payment terms, handles the guest's card details, and mediates disputes according to its own policy. Direct booking removes all of that. There is no platform sitting between the guest and the villa deciding what is fair if a cancellation happens two days before arrival.

That is not a reason to avoid direct booking — it is the reason terms need to be explicit, agreed in writing (even a WhatsApp message confirming the terms counts), and consistent every time, rather than negotiated fresh with each guest.

## Deposits: how much and when

A typical structure is a deposit to confirm the dates, with the balance due before or on arrival. The exact split varies by villa and season — some request a larger deposit for peak dates given the higher opportunity cost of holding those dates off the market. What matters more than the specific numbers is that the deposit terms are sent to the guest before any money changes hands, in writing, including what happens if they cancel.

We do not publish one fixed deposit percentage here because it genuinely varies by season, group size and how far ahead the booking is made — ask your manager what applies for a specific villa and date range.

## Choosing how to actually collect the money

| Method | Typical use | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Bank transfer (international) | Deposits, balances for longer stays | Currency conversion costs, transfer delays |
| Payment link / card gateway | Faster confirmation, smaller amounts | Gateway fees, chargeback risk |
| Cash on arrival | Balance for some guests | Only for balance, never full trust on deposit |

Nothing is charged automatically on thehostbali.com's own site — payment is arranged after dates are confirmed directly with the guest, and rates are quoted the same day on request. That sequencing (confirm dates and terms first, then arrange payment) is worth copying whatever gateway or method an owner uses independently, because it avoids the awkward situation of holding a guest's money before both sides have agreed the specifics.

## Currency and exchange rate risk

Most international guests think in their home currency; most Bali costs are in rupiah. Quoting a fixed rupiah or US dollar figure and being clear about which currency the deposit and balance are calculated in avoids a common dispute: a guest who transferred an amount that converted to slightly less than expected once bank fees and exchange rates were applied, then feels short-changed on arrival.

State the currency and the exact figure, not just a percentage, and confirm it again close to the payment date if exchange rates have moved significantly.

## Refund and cancellation terms

This is where direct bookings most often go wrong. A guest who paid a deposit and then needs to cancel will expect the same policy an OTA might offer, unless told otherwise in advance. Write the cancellation and refund terms into the same message that confirms the deposit request — for example, what portion is refundable and by what date — rather than leaving it to be worked out if and when a cancellation happens.

Owners who skip this step end up negotiating refunds guest by guest under pressure, which is both stressful and inconsistent, and inconsistency is what damages a villa's reputation when guests compare notes.

## Building a simple checklist

- Confirm dates and the exact rate before requesting any payment
- State the deposit amount, currency and due date in writing
- State the cancellation and refund policy in the same message
- Confirm the balance due date and accepted payment method before arrival
- Keep a written record of every payment request and confirmation

## What to do next

Direct booking is worth the effort of setting up clean payment terms, because the commission saved versus an OTA is real money. The risk is manageable as long as deposit, currency and cancellation terms are written down before any payment is requested, every time, not negotiated fresh per guest.

## FAQ

### What deposit percentage should I ask for direct villa bookings in Bali?
There is no single figure that applies to every villa and season — it depends on group size, season and how far ahead the booking is. What matters most is stating the exact deposit amount and due date in writing before requesting payment, consistently for every guest.

### Is it safe to take full payment before arrival on a direct booking?
It carries more risk than a platform-mediated payment because there is no built-in dispute process. Splitting into a deposit to confirm the dates and a balance closer to or on arrival is generally lower risk than collecting the full amount far in advance.

### What currency should I quote in for international guests?
Whichever currency you actually settle in, stated clearly and as an exact figure rather than a percentage, so the guest is not surprised by exchange-rate movement between quoting and paying. Reconfirm the figure close to the payment date if rates have shifted noticeably.

### How do I handle a guest who wants to cancel after paying a deposit?
Refer back to the cancellation terms you sent when the deposit was requested. This is exactly why those terms need to be written down at the start — negotiating them after a cancellation request arrives is far harder and less consistent.
