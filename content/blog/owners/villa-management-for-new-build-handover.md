Managing a newly built villa in Bali starts before the first guest arrives, not after — a proper handover involves a snag-list inspection, testing every system under real conditions, and a staged approach to listing and pricing rather than opening the calendar at full rate on day one. Skip this and the villa's first reviews often carry avoidable complaints about things a walkthrough would have caught.

We've taken over management of newly completed villas at this stage, and the pattern is consistent: the villas that launch well are the ones where handover was treated as a proper process, not a formality.

## Start with a snag list, not a guest booking

A snag list is a room-by-room inspection noting every defect, however small — a door that doesn't close flush, a tap with low pressure, uneven grout, a pool light that flickers. On a newly built villa, this list is often long, and that's normal; the builder should be given a defined window to fix each item before the villa opens to paying guests, with a follow-up inspection to confirm the fixes actually held.

Booking the first guest before this process is complete is the single most common mistake we see with new-build handovers — it trades a few extra nights of income for a guest experience (and often a public review) shaped by unresolved defects.

## Test systems under real conditions, not just on paper

A generator, hot water system, Wi-Fi router and pool pump can all look fine in a quick contractor walkthrough and still fail under the load of an actual stay — multiple showers running, several devices on Wi-Fi at once, a full day of air conditioning in several rooms. Testing these systems under something closer to real guest conditions, for at least a few days, catches problems a five-minute inspection misses.

| Stage | What to check |
|---|---|
| Snag list inspection | Every room, fixture, finish |
| Systems test (3–5 days) | Water pressure, hot water, Wi-Fi, AC, pool equipment under real load |
| Soft-open listing | Lower rate, close friends/family or short trial bookings |
| Full listing | Standard rate, full marketing push |

## Licensing and legal status before opening

Whether a newly built villa's occupancy permit and business licence are fully in place before the first paying guest arrives is a genuine legal question, and it is not something to assume is automatically sorted just because construction is finished (as of 2026, verify). This is worth confirming directly with the developer or builder and, ideally, an independent check with the relevant local office, before any booking calendar goes live. Operating without the correct permits carries real risk, separate from the practical handover issues covered above.

## Staffing a villa that's never been run before

An established villa has staff who know its quirks — which tap needs a firm turn, where the spare pool filter is kept. A brand-new villa has none of that institutional knowledge yet, which means the first weeks of staffing need closer supervision than normal, with a manager or senior staff member physically present more often than they would be for an established property.

- Assign a dedicated point of contact for the villa's first month, not a rotating rota
- Walk new housekeeping and maintenance staff through every system personally before the first guest
- Keep a running issue log for the first two months to catch patterns early

## Listing photography and setup

Professional photography matters more for a new villa than an established one, because there's no guest-review history yet to build trust — the photos and listing description are doing all the persuading. It's worth waiting until the snag list is genuinely closed and the garden has had time to establish before shooting, rather than photographing on the handover day when finishes still look raw.

## A staged pricing approach

Opening a brand-new villa at the same rate as a comparable, established villa nearby is usually a mistake in the first month or two — without reviews, a new listing has less to persuade a guest with, so demand at full rate tends to be weaker than the villa will eventually achieve once it has a track record. A staged approach — a modestly lower introductory rate for the first bookings, rising as reviews and repeat demand build — tends to get the villa to full rate faster than holding out for full rate from day one.

This connects to the broader question covered in [peak season pricing](/blog/owners/villa-pricing-strategy-peak-season/), which assumes an established villa with booking history to read demand from — a new villa doesn't have that yet, so early pricing has to be more conservative by necessity.

## When the builder and the manager are different parties

If the construction company and the villa manager are separate, make sure responsibility for post-handover defects is clearly assigned — a manager shouldn't be left absorbing costs or guest complaints for a builder's unresolved snag-list item. This is worth agreeing explicitly at the point the manager takes over, not discovered later when something breaks.

## What to do next

A newly built villa deserves the same careful handover process regardless of how eager everyone is to start earning from it. The villas that launch smoothly are the ones where the snag list was genuinely closed, systems were tested under real load, and pricing started conservative before building up.

- Commission or personally walk through a full snag-list inspection before any listing goes live
- Confirm licensing status directly rather than assuming it's settled
- Plan a staged pricing approach for the first two to three months

## FAQ

### How long should a snag-list process take for a new villa in Bali?
It varies with the size and complexity of the build, but rushing it to meet a booking date is the most common mistake. Give the builder a defined window to fix issues, then re-inspect before listing, rather than opening with a partial list of unresolved items.

### Should I price a new villa the same as similar established villas nearby?
Generally no, at least initially. A new listing has no guest reviews to build trust yet, so a modestly lower introductory rate for the first bookings, rising as reviews accumulate, usually reaches full rate faster than starting at full rate immediately.

### Do I need to confirm licensing before listing a newly built villa?
Yes — this is a genuine legal question and should be confirmed directly with the relevant local authority and your own adviser rather than assumed to be automatically settled once construction finishes (as of 2026, verify).

### What's the biggest risk of skipping a proper handover process?
Early guest complaints and reviews about defects a walkthrough would have caught, at a stage when the villa has no track record yet to offset a bad review. The first reviews matter disproportionately for a new listing.

### Who should test the villa's systems before the first guest?
Ideally the manager or a senior staff member, running the systems under conditions closer to real guest use for several days — multiple showers, full Wi-Fi load, extended air conditioning — rather than relying on a brief contractor walkthrough.
