Writing a villa listing description that converts starts with accepting that most guests skim rather than read. They're comparing several listings at once, scanning for who it suits, what's included, where it is, and what the standout feature is — and a description that opens with scenic language before getting to any of that loses guests who are moving on to the next listing within seconds.

This guide covers the structure and tone that consistently perform better, based on what we've found works across the listings we manage.

## Lead with who it suits and the headline feature

The first two lines carry the most weight, since they're often visible before a guest even expands the full description. State plainly who the villa works for — a family, a couple, a remote-working group — and its single strongest feature, rather than opening with generic scene-setting. "A quiet 3-bedroom pool villa in Sanur, built for families wanting space and a five-minute walk to the beach path" tells a guest more in one sentence than a paragraph of atmospheric description.

## Structure that matches how guests actually scan

| Section | What to cover |
|---|---|
| Opening (1–2 lines) | Who it suits, bedroom count, area, one standout feature |
| The space | Room layout, pool size, indoor-outdoor flow, notable amenities |
| Location | Walking distance to beach or key landmarks, what's nearby |
| Guest access | What's included, house rules in brief, check-in process |
| Getting around | Transport options, distance to the airport |

Keep paragraphs short — two to four sentences — since long blocks of text get skimmed past entirely on a phone screen, which is where most guests are reading.

## Be specific instead of using adjectives that mean nothing

"Stunning," "luxurious" and "amazing" appear in thousands of villa listings and carry no information a guest can act on. Specifics do the work instead: "300 Mbps Wi-Fi and a dedicated desk in every bedroom" tells a remote worker exactly what they need to know; "fast Wi-Fi" makes them guess. The same applies to the pool, kitchen and bathrooms — state the size, the equipment, the layout, rather than describing them emotionally.

- Replace "beautiful pool" with the pool's approximate size or a specific feature (shallow end, sun loungers, shade)
- Replace "fully equipped kitchen" with what's actually included (oven, blender, coffee machine)
- Replace "close to everything" with actual walking or driving times to specific landmarks
- Replace "great for families" with what makes it work for families specifically (a shallow pool area, extra bedding, proximity to a quiet beach)

## Match the description to the villa's actual guest type

A villa suited to remote workers should lead with workspace, Wi-Fi speed and quiet — not with a generic "explore Bali" framing aimed at holidaymakers. A family villa should lead with space, safety around the pool, and proximity to calm activities. Writing one generic description and hoping it appeals to everyone usually converts worse than a description clearly aimed at the guest type the villa genuinely suits best.

## Location details guests actually want

Vague distance claims ("minutes from the beach") are common enough that guests have learned to distrust them. Specific, honest distances — "an 8-minute walk to Sanur's beach path, 15 minutes by car to the airport" — build more trust precisely because they're checkable and specific. If the walk includes anything relevant (a busy road crossing, no direct beach access from that stretch), mention it rather than letting a guest discover it on arrival.

## Keep it honest, especially about limitations

A description that oversells — claiming an ocean view that's actually a partial glimpse, or "quiet" for a villa near a busy road — generates bookings that convert into disappointed reviews, which cost more in the long run than the honest version would have in lost enquiries. If a limitation matters to some guests and not others (a shared pool, stairs with no lift), state it plainly rather than hiding it; the guests it doesn't suit will self-select out, and the ones it suits will trust the listing more.

## Length and formatting

Airbnb and Booking.com both support formatted descriptions with headers or bold text in some form — use that structure rather than one dense wall of text. Aim for enough detail to answer the practical questions above without padding; a description that's twice as long without adding new information doesn't convert better, it just takes longer to skim.

## FAQ

### How long should a villa listing description be?

Long enough to cover who it suits, the space, location and practical details, without padding. There's no fixed word count that performs best — focus on covering the scannable structure above rather than hitting a target length.

### Should I write different descriptions for Airbnb and Booking.com?

Ideally yes, adapted to each platform's format and typical guest expectations, though the core facts (space, location, amenities) can stay consistent. A description copied verbatim between platforms often doesn't use each one's formatting options well.

### What's the most common mistake in villa listing descriptions?

Opening with generic, scenic language instead of stating who the villa suits and its headline feature immediately. Guests skimming multiple listings move on quickly if the opening doesn't tell them anything specific and comparable.

### Should I mention limitations like a shared pool or no lift?

Yes — honest limitations help the right guests self-select in and the wrong ones self-select out before booking, which reduces disappointed reviews later. Hiding a known limitation tends to cost more in reputation than it saves in bookings.

### How often should I update my listing description?

Whenever something material changes — new furniture, a renovated bathroom, updated Wi-Fi — and periodically even without a change, since a description that hasn't been revisited in years can drift out of step with how the villa is actually presented elsewhere.
