A cancellation policy sets out what happens to a guest's payment if they cancel a booking, and how far in advance they need to cancel to get a partial or full refund. For a Bali villa, getting this right matters more than owners sometimes expect, because a single last-minute cancellation on a longer stay or during high season can be a meaningful revenue loss with little time to rebook the dates.

There's no single correct policy; the right structure balances protecting your revenue against not discouraging bookings with terms guests find unreasonably rigid. What matters is that the policy is clear, applied consistently, and communicated before a guest books, not negotiated case by case after the fact.

## Common cancellation policy structures

| Structure | How it works | Best suited to |
|---|---|---|
| Flexible | Full refund up to a short window (e.g. 24-48 hours) before check-in | High-demand periods, easy-to-rebook dates |
| Moderate | Full refund up to a week or two before check-in, partial refund closer in | General use, most villas |
| Strict | Partial refund only up to a set cutoff (e.g. 30 days), little or no refund after | High season, longer or higher-value stays |
| Non-refundable rate | Discounted rate in exchange for no refund | Guests confident in their dates, price-sensitive segment |

Many owners use different policies for different seasons: more flexible in low season, when rebooking a cancelled date is harder, and stricter in high season, when cancelled dates typically rebook quickly enough that the risk is lower, or where the lost revenue from a late cancellation is higher.

## Why cancellation policy should track minimum stay

Longer minimum stays put more revenue at stake in a single booking, since a week or month-long cancellation is a bigger loss than a two-night one. As minimum stay length increases, particularly for weekly and monthly direct bookings, a clearer, somewhat stricter cancellation policy becomes more important to protect against that larger financial exposure. See our guide on [minimum stay policy](/blog/owners/minimum-stay-length-bali-villa/) for how the two work together.

## Communicating the policy clearly

Cancellation terms should be visible to a guest before they book, not discovered afterward. Ambiguous or inconsistently enforced policies are one of the more common sources of guest complaints and disputes, and they can damage a villa's reviews even when the policy itself is reasonable, simply because the guest felt blindsided. See our guide on [guest communication](/blog/owners/guest-communication-villa-bali/) for how to set expectations proactively.

## How cancellation policy relates to damage deposits

Cancellation policy and damage deposit policy are often confused but serve different purposes: cancellation policy protects against lost booking revenue, while a damage deposit protects against property damage during a stay. Both should be communicated together, clearly, before booking. See our guide on [damage deposits](/blog/owners/guest-damage-deposit-bali-villa/) for how that side works.

## Handling disputed or unusual cancellation situations

Genuine emergencies, illness, or circumstances outside a guest's control sometimes come up, and how you handle these — strictly by the letter of the policy or with some flexibility — is a judgement call that affects your reputation as much as any single booking's revenue. Many owners and managers apply the written policy as a default but retain some discretion for clearly genuine, well-documented exceptional circumstances.

## Cancellation policy across different booking channels

OTA platforms like Airbnb and Booking.com typically offer a set menu of standard cancellation policy options a host chooses from, rather than fully custom terms, and platform-level policies (including any platform-wide flexibility during extraordinary circumstances) can sometimes override or interact with your chosen setting. For direct bookings, you have full control to set custom terms, which is worth taking advantage of rather than defaulting to whatever the platform suggests without considering whether it suits your specific villa and season.

## What guests actually look for in a cancellation policy

Guests, particularly those booking further in advance or for higher-value stays, increasingly factor cancellation flexibility into their booking decision itself, sometimes choosing a slightly more expensive villa with a more flexible policy over a cheaper one with strict terms. This means an overly rigid cancellation policy can cost bookings even before a cancellation ever happens, simply by looking less attractive at the comparison stage. Weighing this against your genuine need to protect revenue from late cancellations is part of setting the policy thoughtfully rather than defaulting to the strictest option available.

## Documenting the policy for consistent enforcement

Whatever policy you choose, write it down clearly and apply it consistently rather than making ad-hoc decisions under pressure when a guest requests an exception. Inconsistent enforcement, refunding one guest fully while holding another strictly to the same policy, is a common source of unfairness and, if it becomes visible, can damage trust even among guests who never experienced it directly.

## A practical checklist for setting cancellation policy

1. Choose a structure (flexible, moderate, strict, or non-refundable) that matches your season and typical booking length.
2. Consider a stricter policy for longer stays given the higher revenue at stake.
3. Display cancellation terms clearly before a guest books, on every channel you list on.
4. Decide in advance how you'll handle genuine exceptional circumstances.
5. Review whether your policy differs sensibly by season, or needlessly applies one rule year-round.

## FAQ

### What cancellation policy should I use for my Bali villa?

There's no single right answer; a moderate policy (full refund up to one to two weeks before check-in, partial refund closer in) suits most villas, while high season or longer stays often justify a stricter policy given the higher revenue at stake. The right choice depends on your area, season and typical booking pattern.

### Should my cancellation policy change by season?

Often yes. A more flexible policy in low season, when a cancelled date is harder to rebook, and a stricter policy in high season, when demand typically rebooks cancelled dates more quickly, is a common and sensible approach rather than applying one fixed rule year-round.

### How does cancellation policy relate to minimum stay?

Longer minimum stays put more revenue at stake per booking, so as minimum stay length increases, particularly for weekly or monthly direct bookings, a clearer and somewhat stricter cancellation policy becomes more important to protect against that larger financial exposure.

### Is a cancellation policy the same as a damage deposit?

No, they serve different purposes. Cancellation policy protects against lost booking revenue if a guest cancels before arrival, while a damage deposit protects against property damage during the stay itself. Both should be clearly communicated to guests before booking, but they're separate terms.

### Should I make exceptions to my cancellation policy for emergencies?

Many owners apply the written policy as a default but retain some discretion for clearly genuine, well-documented exceptional circumstances, since handling these well affects your reputation as much as any single booking's revenue. Deciding your approach in advance avoids inconsistent, case-by-case decisions under pressure.
