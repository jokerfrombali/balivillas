A villa handover checklist for Bali needs three separate tracks running at once: closing out the old management contract cleanly, a physical inventory and condition walk-through of the property, and moving every digital account — booking channels, utilities, smart locks — into the new manager's name before the old one loses access. Owners who skip any one of these tend to lose bookings, deposits or, occasionally, a working front door lock during the gap.

The trigger is usually dissatisfaction with occupancy, communication or reporting from the outgoing manager, and once the decision is made, timing matters. A handover done mid-season with live bookings on the calendar is riskier than one done in a quiet month, so where possible, plan the switch date around your booking calendar rather than around when you happen to have made the decision.

## Ending the old contract without losing deposits or bookings

Read your existing management agreement before saying anything to the outgoing company. Most contracts have a notice period (commonly 30-60 days, but this varies by contract) and a clause on what happens to bookings already confirmed for dates after the handover. Get clarity in writing on three points: the exact end date, who honours bookings that fall after that date, and how any security deposit or float you paid gets returned or transferred.

If the outgoing manager holds a security deposit, guest deposits, or an operating float for staff wages and utilities, get a written reconciliation of that money before the last day, not after. This single step is where most handover disputes start, because once the manager has moved on to other clients, chasing a refund gets slower and less of a priority for them.

## The physical inventory and condition walk-through

Walk the villa with both the outgoing manager (if they'll cooperate) or alone with the new manager, and go room by room against a written inventory, not memory. Photograph everything: furniture, appliances, linens, kitchenware, pool equipment, garden tools, and note any existing damage or wear so it's not later blamed on the new team. Our guide on building a [villa inventory and asset register](/blog/owners/villa-inventory-and-asset-register/) covers how to structure this list so it stays useful for maintenance planning after the handover too, not just as a one-off document.

Check consumables and equipment condition specifically: pool pump and filter, AC units, water heater, generator or backup power if fitted, and any smart home or security system. A villa that looks fine on a walk-through can still have a pool pump close to failure or an AC unit overdue for servicing, so ask for service records if the outgoing manager has them.

## Moving booking channels and calendars without a gap

This is the part that costs owners money if rushed. Every channel the villa is listed on — Airbnb, Booking.com, your direct website — needs its calendar and any confirmed future bookings transferred to the new manager's control, ideally with zero gap where nobody is watching messages or confirming availability. A missed guest message during a handover gap is a lost booking and, on platforms with response-time metrics, a dent to your listing's ranking.

Confirm specifically: who owns the Airbnb/Booking.com listing account (transferring account ownership is different from just changing who manages it day to day), whether any existing guest reviews and superhost-type status carry over, and how confirmed future bookings get honoured under the new manager's operational setup.

## Handover checklist: what to confirm before the switch date

| Item | Confirm in writing | Typical risk if skipped |
|---|---|---|
| Contract end date and notice period | Exact date, any early-termination cost | Overlap billing or a coverage gap |
| Security deposit / operating float | Amount, reconciliation, return timeline | Disputed or delayed refund |
| Confirmed future bookings | Who honours them, at what rate | Guest arrives to confusion or cancellation |
| Booking channel accounts | Who holds login/ownership | Lost listing, lost review history |
| Physical inventory | Photographed, dated, both parties sign off | Damage blamed on new manager |
| Utility accounts (power, water, internet) | Names on account, outstanding balances | Service cut off mid-handover |
| Staff (if villa has dedicated staff) | Who employs them going forward, notice given | Staff leave, gap in housekeeping |
| Keys, locks, access codes | All copies accounted for, codes changed | Security exposure from old copies |

## Utilities, staff and access: the practical loose ends

If the villa has dedicated staff (a housekeeper, a gardener, a pool technician), find out early whether they're employed directly by the owner or contracted through the outgoing management company, because that changes what happens to them at handover. Utility accounts — power, water, internet — are sometimes in the manager's name rather than the owner's; get these transferred or at minimum confirm who is paying the next bill so service isn't interrupted.

Change every access code and rekey or reprogram smart locks as one of the last steps, after the outgoing manager has finished their final walk-through but before the new manager takes over guest operations. Old key copies and unchanged codes are a security gap that's easy to overlook in the rush of a handover.

## What a good handover looks like on paper

By the end, you should have a signed handover document (even a simple one) covering: contract closure and any final payment, the inventory with photographs and both parties' sign-off, a list of transferred accounts and who now controls each, and a clear date from which the new manager is fully responsible. This protects you if a dispute comes up later about who was responsible for what and when.

## FAQ

### How long does a villa management handover usually take in Bali?

It depends on the contract's notice period and how organised the outgoing manager is, but a clean handover with cooperation from both sides typically takes two to four weeks from decision to full transfer. A contested or uncooperative handover can take considerably longer, particularly if deposit reconciliation or booking channel ownership is disputed.

### What happens to bookings already confirmed when I switch managers?

This should be agreed in writing before the switch: either the outgoing manager honours bookings falling after the handover date at the agreed commission, or the new manager takes them over with full visibility of the original booking terms. Never assume; get this specific point confirmed before signing off on the handover.

### Who should hold the villa inventory list?

The owner, ultimately, with a copy held by whichever company is currently managing. Keeping your own master copy, updated at each handover, means you always have an independent record that doesn't disappear when a management relationship ends.

### Can I do a villa handover myself without a lawyer?

For most single-villa handovers, yes, provided both parties cooperate and the original management contract is clear on notice and deposit terms. If there's a dispute over money, bookings, or contract terms, it's worth a short consultation with a local lawyer before things escalate further.

### Should I change all the locks during a handover?

Yes, for any villa relying on physical keys or programmable codes, rekeying or reprogramming access at handover is standard practice and closes the loop on old key copies that may still be circulating with previous staff or contractors.
