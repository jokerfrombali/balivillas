OTA commission for Bali villas is the cut a platform like Airbnb or Booking.com takes on each booking made through its site, and it's separate from any management commission a villa manager charges. The two deductions stack: a booking made through Airbnb typically has the platform's commission taken first, with the management commission then calculated on the same booking, either before or after the platform fee depending on how the manager structures it.

Understanding that stacking, and asking exactly how it's calculated, is the difference between a rate that looks competitive on paper and one that actually reaches the owner's account.

## Why platform commission and management commission are separate

Some owners assume a management company's fee already includes whatever the platforms charge, since both numbers are "commission." They don't overlap. The platform charges for bringing the guest and processing the booking; the manager charges for running the property, replying to guests, coordinating cleaning and maintenance, and keeping the calendar current. Ask any manager specifically whether their quoted commission is calculated on gross booking value (before OTA fees) or net (after), because that changes the effective cost meaningfully.

| Question to ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is management commission calculated before or after OTA fees? | Changes the real percentage you pay overall |
| Does the manager pass OTA fees through at cost? | Some structure it differently; ask directly |
| Are direct bookings (no OTA) charged the same management rate? | Direct bookings avoid the platform fee entirely |
| Who absorbs a guest cancellation refunded by the platform? | Policies vary by platform and by manager agreement |

## Direct bookings avoid the platform fee entirely

A booking made directly with the owner or manager, outside any OTA, has no platform commission at all — only the management fee, if one applies. That's a meaningful gap over a full year, which is why most established operators, including us, push guests toward direct booking wherever possible: no OTA fee, no booking fee to the guest, payment arranged after dates are confirmed. ## Comparing platforms isn't just about the headline percentage

Airbnb, Booking.com, Agoda and Expedia each structure their commission and payment terms slightly differently, and the "cheapest" platform on paper isn't necessarily the one that delivers the most net income, because booking volume and guest quality vary a lot between them. ## A worked way to compare two proposals

Rather than comparing OTA commission percentages in the abstract, run the same hypothetical booking through each option:

- Take a sample booking value, say a one-week stay at your typical rate
- Subtract the OTA commission the platform would charge on that channel
- Apply the management commission using the same base (gross or net) the manager quoted
- Compare what's left across each channel and each manager

This turns an abstract set of percentages into a number you can actually compare, and it usually reveals that the gap between "cheap" and "expensive" proposals is smaller, or larger, than the headline rates suggested.

## Cancellations and refunds complicate the maths

OTA cancellation policies are set by the platform, not the villa, and a guest refund under a flexible policy can mean the manager's commission on that booking effectively disappears too, since there's no completed booking to earn from. Ask how your manager handles commission on bookings that are later cancelled and refunded under platform policy, since this varies and isn't always obvious from the headline fee structure.

## A worked example: comparing two management proposals

Say two management companies quote you commission rates that look close on paper — one slightly lower than the other. Run the same sample booking through both, as described above: for Company A, the commission is calculated on gross booking value before any OTA fee is subtracted; for Company B, it's calculated on the net amount after the OTA has already taken its cut. Even with Company A's headline rate looking higher, the actual amount you keep from an OTA booking can end up similar or even better than Company B's, because the base each commission is applied to is different.

This is exactly the kind of detail that's easy to miss when comparing proposals only on their headline percentage, and it's worth asking both companies to walk through a real sample booking with you rather than trusting the quoted rate alone.

## FAQ

### Does my management fee include OTA commission?

No. OTA commission is charged separately by the platform on any booking made through it, and management commission is calculated on top, either before or after the platform fee depending on the manager's structure. Ask specifically which base is used.

### Is it cheaper to book direct instead of through Airbnb or Booking.com?

Usually, because a direct booking has no platform commission at all, only any applicable management fee. That's why most managers, including us, encourage direct enquiries over WhatsApp with no booking fee to the guest.

### Which OTA charges the lowest commission for Bali villas?

Commission structures change and vary by platform agreement, so there's no fixed figure worth quoting here without verifying current terms directly with each platform. Compare net proceeds on a worked sample booking rather than relying on a headline percentage.

### What happens to commission if a guest cancels?

It depends on the platform's cancellation policy and the manager's own agreement — a refunded booking under a flexible policy often means no commission is earned on that stay at all. Ask your manager how they handle this specifically.

### Should I list on every OTA to maximise bookings?

Not automatically. More platforms mean more calendar-sync risk without a channel manager, and diminishing returns once your main platforms are already performing well. See [channel manager for Bali villas](/blog/owners/channel-manager-bali-villas/) before adding a third or fourth listing.
