Annual running costs of a Bali villa fall into six broad categories: staff, utilities, pool and garden upkeep, maintenance and repairs, insurance, and licensing/compliance. There's no single published total because it scales with villa size, pool size and how much is delegated versus handled by the owner — but understanding each category lets you build a realistic budget for a specific property.

This guide walks through each cost category, what drives it up or down, and how they interact, so a prospective or current owner can budget properly rather than relying on a single rule-of-thumb figure.

## The six cost categories

| Category | What it covers | Main cost driver |
|---|---|---|
| Staff | Housekeeping, pool/garden crew, maintenance, management | Villa size, whether staff are shared or dedicated |
| Utilities | Electricity, water, internet | Villa size, aircon use, occupancy |
| Pool and garden | Chemicals, equipment, landscaping upkeep | Pool size, garden extent |
| Maintenance and repairs | Aircon servicing, appliance repairs, general upkeep | Villa age, build quality, usage intensity |
| Insurance | Property and liability cover | Villa value, cover level chosen |
| Licensing and compliance | Pondok wisata licence, NIB, NPWP, related paperwork | Mostly fixed, some annual renewal costs |

Each of these is indicative and varies a great deal between a 1-bedroom teak villa and a large multi-bedroom compound — treat this as a framework for building your own number, not a benchmark to compare against.

## Staff: usually the largest recurring line

For most villas, staff costs — even when shared across a small portfolio — make up the largest single category of annual running cost, simply because it's an ongoing wage obligation rather than an occasional expense. We cover what drives staff cost specifically in [villa staff costs in Bali](/blog/owners/bali-villa-staff-costs/), worth reading in detail if this is the category you're trying to budget most precisely.

## Utilities: the most occupancy-sensitive line

Electricity in particular swings a lot with occupancy and aircon use — a villa run at high occupancy through the dry season costs meaningfully more to power than the same villa sitting vacant. Water and internet are comparatively stable. We break utility costs down specifically in [Bali villa utility costs](/blog/owners/bali-villa-utility-costs/), the natural companion to this guide for the most variable line in the budget.

## Pool and garden: a fixed, recurring cost regardless of occupancy

Unlike utilities, pool and garden upkeep runs on a schedule independent of whether the villa is occupied — chemicals, filtration and planting maintenance are needed whether or not a guest is staying. This is one of the categories owners sometimes underestimate when a villa sits vacant for an extended period, assuming costs pause along with income. They don't.

## Maintenance: budget for it as a percentage, not a fixed number

Maintenance and repair costs are the hardest to predict precisely because they're irregular by nature — an aircon unit fails, a water heater needs replacing, tropical humidity accelerates wear on certain materials faster than a temperate climate would. A sensible approach is budgeting a percentage of the villa's value annually for maintenance reserve, rather than assuming costs will match whatever happened last year, since maintenance needs cluster unpredictably.

## Insurance and licensing: smaller but non-optional

Insurance and licensing costs are typically smaller line items than staff or utilities, but they're not optional in the way a discretionary upgrade might be. Skipping insurance to save the annual premium, or letting a licence lapse to avoid a renewal fee, exposes the owner to risks — an uninsured claim, or a compliance issue that halts short-term rental — that dwarf the cost saved. We cover villa insurance and the pondok wisata licensing process in separate guides for owners who want the detail on each.

## Building your own annual budget

- List each of the six categories against your specific villa, not a generic template
- Get actual quotes for staff, insurance and licensing rather than estimating from memory or a forum post
- Set aside a maintenance reserve as a percentage of villa value, not a fixed guess
- Revisit the full budget annually, since wage levels, utility rates and maintenance needs all shift year to year
- Compare your total running cost against expected rental income before committing to a purchase or a management structure

## How running costs interact with management fees

A management commission and annual running costs are related but distinct — the commission pays for the service of running the villa (marketing, guest care, staff supervision), while running costs are the actual expenses of operating the property, most of which are billed as pass-through costs regardless of who manages the villa. Understanding both separately, rather than conflating them into one number, is what lets an owner properly judge whether a rental property makes financial sense. ## FAQ

### What's the biggest annual running cost for a Bali villa?

Staff costs are typically the largest single category for most villas, since they're an ongoing wage obligation rather than an occasional expense, followed by utilities and pool/garden upkeep. The exact ranking shifts with villa size and pool footprint, so it's worth building your own category breakdown rather than assuming.

### Do running costs stop if the villa is vacant?

No — staff, pool and garden upkeep, insurance and most utilities continue regardless of occupancy, which is a common misconception among first-time owners. Only strictly usage-based costs like electricity for aircon drop meaningfully during vacant periods.

### How much should I set aside for maintenance each year?

There's no single figure we'd quote with confidence, since it depends on villa age, build quality and usage. A reasonable approach is budgeting a percentage of the villa's value annually as a reserve, since maintenance needs are irregular rather than evenly spread.

### Are running costs different for a self-managed vs professionally managed villa?

The underlying running costs — staff, utilities, pool, maintenance — are broadly similar either way, since they're the actual operating expenses of the property. What differs is who administers them and whether a management commission sits on top for the service of running the villa.

### Should I budget running costs before or after estimating rental income?

Build the running cost budget first, based on the property itself, rather than working backward from a hoped-for income figure. We don't quote occupancy or income figures, since they vary too much to generalise — running costs are the more stable, plannable side of the equation.
