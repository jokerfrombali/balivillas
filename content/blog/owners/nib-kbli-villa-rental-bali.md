NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha, or Business Identification Number) is issued through Indonesia's OSS (Online Single Submission) system and serves as the core business registration for a company or business activity, including villa rental where it's operated commercially. KBLI (Klasifikasi Baku Lapangan Usaha Indonesia) codes classify exactly what kind of business activity is being registered, and choosing the right code for short-term villa rental matters, since it affects what licences and permits follow from the registration.

We're not a legal or licensing service, and the specific process, required documents and applicable codes can change; this guide explains the general landscape so you know what to check, not a substitute for advice from a notary or licensed consultant handling your specific registration. ## What NIB and KBLI actually cover

| Element | What it is | Why it matters for villa rental |
|---|---|---|
| NIB | Business Identification Number via OSS | Core registration required for formal commercial operation |
| KBLI code | Classification of the specific business activity | Determines which follow-on licences and requirements apply |
| OSS system | Indonesia's online single-submission registration platform | Where NIB and KBLI registration is processed |

Getting the KBLI code right at registration matters because it shapes everything downstream — the wrong classification can mean missing a required permit or, conversely, registering for requirements that don't actually apply to a small-scale villa rental operation.

## How this connects to accommodation licensing

NIB and KBLI registration is business-level registration; it typically sits alongside, not instead of, accommodation-specific licensing such as a pondok wisata licence where that category applies. See our guide on [pondok wisata licensing](/blog/owners/pondok-wisata-licence-bali/) for how the two connect, since a villa generally needs both the correct business registration and the correct accommodation licence to operate fully compliantly.

## Individual owners versus company structures

Whether an individual owner needs a full NIB/KBLI registration, or whether this applies primarily to company (PT) structures managing multiple properties commercially, depends on how the villa is owned and operated, and this is a genuinely technical distinction worth confirming directly with a notary rather than assuming one way or the other. ## Registration and tax obligations

NIB/KBLI business registration is connected to, but distinct from, personal or company tax registration (NPWP). See our guide on [NPWP for villa owners](/blog/owners/npwp-villa-owner-bali/) for how tax registration fits alongside business licensing. Business registration through OSS establishes what activity you're legally conducting; NPWP establishes how that activity is taxed. Owners sometimes assume completing one automatically resolves the other, and that assumption is worth checking directly with a notary rather than carrying forward unconfirmed.

## The OSS registration process in outline

The OSS (Online Single Submission) system is designed to let a business register centrally rather than visiting multiple separate offices, and for a straightforward villa rental structure the process typically involves confirming the legal entity or individual applying, selecting the correct KBLI code or codes for the activity, and submitting supporting documents through the online platform. In practice, most foreign owners work with a notary or licensed consultant to handle this rather than navigating OSS independently, partly because selecting the wrong KBLI code or missing a required supporting document can mean delays or having to resubmit. The process and required documents can change, so treat any specific timeline or document list you're given as current only at the time it's confirmed, not as a fixed reference. (as of 2026, verify)

## Common mistakes owners make with NIB and KBLI

A few patterns come up repeatedly. Registering under a KBLI code that doesn't actually match short-term villa rental, sometimes because a generic "real estate" or "property management" code was used instead of a code specific to short-term tourist accommodation, is one of the more common gaps, and it can mean the business is technically registered for a different activity than what it's actually doing. Another common issue is registering once at the start of operation and never revisiting the registration as the business changes, for instance if a villa that started as a single private rental expands into managing multiple properties commercially, which may call for a different structure or additional registration. A third pattern is assuming that because a management company or notary handled the paperwork years ago, the registration remains current and correctly matched to how the villa operates today, without ever checking back.

## Why getting the classification right protects you, not just the paperwork

The registered KBLI code isn't just an administrative formality; in practice it can shape which follow-on requirements, inspections or local government interactions apply to a specific business. A villa correctly classified and registered has a cleaner basis to resolve any dispute, insurance question or local government enquiry than one operating under an unclear or mismatched registration. This is one of the reasons it's worth treating NIB and KBLI registration as a foundational compliance step rather than a box to tick once and forget.

## FAQ

### What is an NIB and do I need one for my Bali villa?

NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha) is a Business Identification Number issued through Indonesia's OSS system, required for formal commercial business operation. Whether your specific villa needs one depends on its ownership structure and how it's operated; confirm with a licensed notary rather than assuming.

### What is a KBLI code and why does it matter?

KBLI codes classify the specific type of business activity being registered, and choosing the correct code for short-term villa rental determines which follow-on licences and requirements apply. Registering under the wrong classification can create gaps or unnecessary requirements, so getting this right at registration matters.

### Is NIB/KBLI the same as a pondok wisata licence?

No, they're different but connected. NIB/KBLI is business-level registration through the OSS system, while pondok wisata is an accommodation-specific licensing category. Most compliant villa operations need both the correct business registration and the correct accommodation licence, not one instead of the other.

### Do individual villa owners need to register for an NIB?

This depends on ownership structure and how the villa is operated, and it's a genuinely technical distinction between individual and company (PT) structures. Confirm your specific situation with a licensed notary rather than assuming one way or the other applies to you.
