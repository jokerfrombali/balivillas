Screening guests for a Bali villa is mostly about the questions you ask before confirming a booking, not a formal background check — there is no practical way to run a criminal record check on an international traveller before a stay, so the real tools are booking conversation, deposit structure and house rules, applied consistently.

We manage 20 villas across Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan, and problem bookings are rare but not unheard of — usually large group bookings with unclear purpose, or short-notice requests with unusual urgency. Here is what we actually check, and what we don't bother with.

## What a booking conversation actually reveals

Most useful signal comes from a normal conversation before confirming: how many guests, purpose of the trip, whether it's a family, a couple, or a larger group of friends, and whether the dates and group size make sense together. A booking for a two-bedroom villa with eight names attached, or a request that avoids answering how many people are actually staying, is worth a direct follow-up question before confirming.

This isn't about being suspicious of every guest — the overwhelming majority of enquiries are straightforward holidaymakers, remote workers or families — but a short conversation before confirming catches the small number of bookings that would otherwise cause a problem.

## Group size and villa capacity

Villas have a stated maximum guest count for a reason — beyond fire safety and neighbour noise, it is also about wear on a property built for a specific number of people. [Leonardo](/villa/canggu/leonardo-9br-resort-pererenan/), our 9-bedroom resort villa in Pererenan, sleeps up to 18 guests comfortably; trying to fit substantially more than that into a smaller villa is the kind of request that should prompt a direct conversation about numbers before confirming, not an assumption that "a few extra people" is fine.

| Signal | What it might mean | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Guest count doesn't match booking size | Under-declared group, possible unlisted event | Ask directly before confirming |
| Very short notice for a high-value villa | Sometimes fine, sometimes a red flag | Standard deposit terms still apply |
| Reluctance to share travel purpose | Often nothing, occasionally an undisclosed party/event | Ask plainly, note the answer |
| Requests to bypass deposit terms | Inconsistent with normal booking flow | Hold the line on standard terms |

## Deposits as a screening tool, not just a payment tool

A deposit does more than secure cash flow — see our guide on [taking payments for direct bookings](/blog/owners/villa-payment-gateway-and-deposits/) for the payment mechanics specifically. On the screening side, a guest who pushes hard to skip a modest, standard deposit, or wants to pay cash on arrival only with no upfront commitment at all, is a guest worth a slower, more careful confirmation process rather than an instant yes.

This is a soft signal, not a rule — plenty of legitimate guests ask questions about deposit terms. What matters is holding a consistent policy rather than making exceptions under pressure, because exceptions are exactly where problems slip through.

## House rules, stated upfront

Clear house rules sent before arrival — no parties without prior agreement, guest count limits, quiet hours, no smoking indoors where applicable — do two useful things: they set expectations honestly, and they give the manager a clear basis to act if something is breached mid-stay, rather than an awkward on-the-spot negotiation.

- State the maximum guest count clearly in the booking confirmation
- Send house rules before arrival, not at check-in
- Keep a record of who is actually named on the booking
- Have a clear, calm process for addressing a rule breach mid-stay

## What we watch for during a stay, not just before

Screening doesn't end at booking confirmation. Housekeeping staff and the on-the-ground team are often the first to notice if a booking has grown well beyond the agreed guest count, or if the property is being used differently than described — an unplanned event, for instance. Because we manage villas ourselves rather than through remote booking only, our staff are on the property regularly enough to catch this early, which matters more than any pre-booking check.

## When screening becomes a legal question

If a stay genuinely breaches the agreement — serious property damage, an unauthorised event, guests who refuse to leave at checkout — this moves from a screening question into a dispute-handling one. Our guide on [disputes with a villa management company](/blog/owners/villa-legal-disputes-with-manager/) covers a different angle (owner vs manager disputes), but the same principle of clear written terms from the outset applies to guest disputes too: it's much easier to act on a breach when the rules were stated plainly upfront.

## Balancing caution against booking volume

Overly strict screening — demanding ID copies, excessive questioning, large non-refundable deposits for every booking — can put off the legitimate majority of guests and cost more in lost bookings than it saves in avoided problems. The right level of screening is proportionate: normal conversation and standard deposit terms for most bookings, with more careful attention only when a specific signal appears.

## What to do next

Good guest screening for a Bali villa is mostly about consistency: the same booking questions, the same deposit terms, the same house rules, applied to every guest rather than tightened only after something goes wrong.

- Write a short standard set of booking questions and use them every time
- Keep deposit terms consistent rather than waiving them under pressure
- Send house rules before arrival, every booking, no exceptions

## FAQ

### Can I run a background check on a guest before a Bali villa booking?
Not in any practical or legally reliable way for international travellers. The realistic tools are a normal booking conversation, consistent deposit terms and clear house rules, rather than a formal check.

### What group size should raise a question before confirming a booking?
Any mismatch between the declared guest count and the villa's normal capacity, or a booking where the group size seems to grow after confirmation, is worth a direct follow-up conversation rather than an assumption that it's fine.

### Should I ask for ID before confirming a villa booking?
Most Bali villa bookings don't require ID upfront, and asking for it can put off legitimate guests without adding much real protection. A clear booking conversation and standard deposit terms generally do more useful screening than an ID request.

### What if a guest exceeds the stated guest count during their stay?
This is a house-rules breach, and having stated the maximum clearly in the booking confirmation gives you a basis to address it directly and calmly, either by requesting an additional guest fee where appropriate or asking excess guests to leave.

### How does The Host Bali handle problem bookings across its villas?
Because we manage villas ourselves with staff regularly on the properties, unusual situations tend to be caught early rather than only discovered at checkout. We apply consistent booking questions and deposit terms across all our villas rather than case-by-case exceptions.
