The questions to ask a villa management company that matter most are not about the headline commission rate. They're about what that rate actually includes, who holds the licences your villa needs, who's physically on site when something breaks, how you'll see your own numbers, and what it costs to leave if the relationship doesn't work out.

Ask those five categories of question before you sign anything, and press for specifics rather than reassurance. A company that answers precisely, with numbers and named processes, is telling you more than one that answers warmly with generalities.

## Start with scope, not price

Before any fee conversation, get a plain list of what's actually covered: guest messaging and booking management, pricing and calendar updates, housekeeping and laundry, maintenance response, staff supervision if the villa has dedicated staff, and monthly reporting. Ask directly which of these are included in the commission and which are billed separately.

Some companies market themselves as full villa managers but are closer to booking agents who forward enquiries and leave operations to the owner. Others run the entire day-to-day. Neither is wrong, but you need to know which one you're hiring before comparing their fee to anyone else's — a lower percentage covering less isn't cheaper, it's a different service.

## Ask exactly how the commission is calculated

"What's your commission?" is the wrong first question. The better one is: commission on what base, calculated when, and what's excluded from it.

- Is it a percentage of gross booking value, or net after OTA commission and payment processing fees?
- Does the rate change with occupancy, season, or length of stay?
- Is a fixed monthly fee charged in addition to commission, or is commission the only charge?
- What counts as a billable extra — maintenance call-outs, marketing spend, licensing renewal, staff payroll administration?
- Who approves repairs above a set amount before the cost is incurred?

We don't publish a single commission figure because it depends on the villa's size, location and condition — ask any company for written terms specific to your property rather than accepting a number quoted before they've seen it.

## Press on licensing and legal compliance

This is where vague answers should worry you most. Ask which licences the company holds or arranges for your villa specifically — typically a tourism accommodation permit (pondok wisata or equivalent), an NIB business registration number, and NPWP tax registration — and ask to see evidence tied to a property they currently manage, not a general statement that "we handle it."

Renting short-term without correct licensing is a real compliance risk for an owner, and it sits with the villa, not just the manager. A company that can't explain, clearly and specifically, how licensing works for your situation is not one to hand keys to.

| Question | What a good answer sounds like |
|---|---|
| Which licences does my villa need? | Names the specific permit and registration numbers, not "we sort the paperwork" |
| Who is the licence registered to? | Explains ownership vs management registration clearly |
| What tax applies to rental income? | Explains the mechanism, refers you to a tax adviser for exact current rates |
| Can you show a compliant villa you manage now? | Offers to, without hesitation |

Tax on Bali rental income does apply and rates have shifted in recent years (as of 2026, verify current figures with a licensed Indonesian tax adviser) — a competent manager explains the mechanism honestly rather than guessing a number to sound confident.

## Ask who's actually on the ground

A villa management pitch often centres on marketing and revenue systems, but the daily reality is staff: housekeeping, pool and garden maintenance, a maintenance response person. Ask who these people are, whether they're employed directly by the company or subcontracted, and what the response time looks like for a guest reporting, say, no hot water at 9pm.

Ask what happens outside office hours. A company with 24/7 guest support and its own maintenance crew responds very differently from one that relays messages to a call centre and waits for a subcontractor the next morning.

## Ask what reporting looks like before you need it

Request a sample monthly owner report — with another client's identifying details removed — rather than a description of what reporting "covers." A real sample tells you whether bookings, revenue, expenses and any pending decisions are laid out clearly, or buried in a format designed to be hard to audit.

Ask how often you're paid out, what the payout schedule is relative to guest checkout, and whether you can block your own dates for personal use without a fee or lengthy notice period. These operational details matter more day to day than the headline commission number.

## Ask what happens if you want to leave

Exit terms are the question owners forget until they need it. Ask directly: what's the notice period, what happens to bookings already confirmed for dates after that notice ends, and who owns the guest relationships and listing accounts once you leave — you, or the outgoing manager.

A company confident in its service usually answers this clearly and doesn't try to lock you in with vague or lengthy notice terms. ## A short list to bring to every conversation

1. What exactly is covered by the commission, and what's billed separately?
2. Gross or net commission base — ask for a worked example on a real booking
3. Which licences do you hold or arrange, and can you show evidence?
4. Who handles maintenance and guest support outside office hours?
5. Can I see a real (anonymised) monthly owner report?
6. What's the notice period and handover process if I leave?

Run the same six questions past every company on your shortlist and compare the written answers side by side — not the impression each conversation left you with. ## FAQ

### What is the single most important question to ask a villa management company?

How the commission is calculated — gross or net, and what's included versus billed separately. The headline percentage means little until you know the base it's applied to and what extra costs sit outside it, so ask for a worked example using a real recent booking rather than a general description.

### Should I ask for references from current owners?

Yes, and a company confident in its service usually arranges it. Ask to speak with an existing owner client, or at minimum see an anonymised sample monthly report, before signing. A company that deflects both requests repeatedly is worth treating with more caution than one that hesitates once and then follows through.

### How do I know if a villa management company's licensing claims are real?

Ask for the specific permit type and registration details tied to a villa they currently manage, not a general statement. A company genuinely compliant will produce this without friction; one that talks around the question or promises to "sort it later" is a real warning sign worth taking seriously before you sign anything.

### Is a lower commission rate always the better deal?

No. A lower rate covering less scope, with maintenance, marketing or licensing billed separately, can cost more overall than a higher all-inclusive rate. Compare total covered scope against total likely cost, not the headline percentage alone, and ask each company for the same worked example so the comparison is fair.

### What should I ask about staff if my villa has live-in or daily staff?

Ask whether staff are employed directly by the management company or by you as the owner, how payroll and BPJS contributions are handled, who supervises day-to-day performance, and what happens to staff continuity if you change management companies later. This affects both cost and legal responsibility, so get it in writing.
