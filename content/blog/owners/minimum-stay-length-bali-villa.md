Minimum stay policy sets the shortest booking a villa will accept for a given date range, and it's one of the more under-used levers owners have. A fixed one-night or two-night minimum year-round is the simplest approach, but it usually leaves revenue on the table in both high and low season compared with a policy that flexes with demand.

The core trade-off is straightforward: a shorter minimum stay opens the calendar to more potential bookings but increases housekeeping, turnover and guest-communication workload per night sold. A longer minimum stay reduces that overhead but narrows the pool of guests willing to book. Getting the balance right by season is what actually improves both occupancy and net revenue.

## How minimum stay should change by season

| Period | Suggested approach | Why |
|---|---|---|
| High season (roughly Jul-Aug, Dec-Jan) | Shorter minimum (1-2 nights) | Strong demand fills short gaps efficiently |
| Shoulder periods | Moderate minimum (2-3 nights) | Balances flexibility with turnover cost |
| Low season (roughly Feb-Mar, Sep-Nov) | Longer minimum (3-7 nights, or a weekly/monthly push) | Targets long-stay guests, reduces turnover cost relative to revenue |

This is a general pattern; the right numbers for a specific villa depend on its area, size and typical guest profile as much as the calendar. See our [seasonality calendar](/blog/owners/bali-villa-seasonality-calendar/) for the detail on when these periods typically fall.

## Why turnover cost matters more than it looks

Every guest changeover involves cleaning, laundry, inspection and often small maintenance checks, all of which cost time and money regardless of how many nights the previous guest stayed. A villa filled with a string of single-night stays can end up with lower net revenue than one filled with fewer, longer stays at a similar overall occupancy rate, once turnover cost is accounted for. This is a big part of why we promote weekly and monthly direct bookings with 10% and 15% discounts respectively, rather than treating every length of stay the same.

## Minimum stay and channel mix

Minimum stay policy can, and often should, differ slightly by channel. Direct bookings, where you control the terms directly, are a natural place to push longer stays with a discount incentive; OTA platforms sometimes have their own conventions and guest expectations around minimum stay that are worth checking before setting policy that conflicts with typical platform behaviour on that channel.

## Communicating minimum stay clearly

Minimum stay policy should be visible before a guest starts an enquiry, not discovered partway through a booking conversation. Unclear or inconsistently applied minimum stay rules are a common source of guest frustration and can cost a booking that would otherwise have gone ahead at a slightly different date range. See our guide on [guest communication](/blog/owners/guest-communication-villa-bali/) for how this fits into the broader pattern of setting expectations early.

## How minimum stay interacts with cancellation policy

A longer minimum stay increases the financial impact of a cancellation, since more nights are at stake per booking. This makes a clear, fair cancellation policy more important as minimum stays lengthen, not less. See our guide on [cancellation policy for villa owners](/blog/owners/villa-cancellation-policy-owners/) for how the two should be set together.

## Setting minimum stay by villa size, not just season

Larger villas with more bedrooms often suit a slightly different minimum stay approach than smaller ones, since group and family bookings for bigger properties tend to plan further ahead and commit to longer stays more readily than a couple booking a smaller villa on short notice. A 1-bedroom villa might reasonably keep a shorter minimum stay year-round to stay flexible for spontaneous bookings, while a 4-bedroom family villa may perform better with a moderate minimum stay even in shoulder periods, since the guests searching for that size property are usually already planning a longer trip.

## Reviewing minimum stay policy against actual booking data

If you have booking history for your villa, look at the actual length of stay guests have chosen when given flexibility, not just the minimum you set. If most bookings already run well beyond your current minimum, tightening the minimum stay slightly may not cost you many bookings while reducing turnover cost on the occasional short stay. If bookings cluster right at your minimum, that's a sign guests are being constrained by it, and loosening it slightly in certain periods might open up more volume.

## Handling requests to break the minimum stay

Guests occasionally ask for an exception to a posted minimum stay, particularly for a short gap in the calendar between two other bookings. There's no universal right answer here; some owners hold firm on the minimum to protect turnover economics, while others make a case-by-case exception when it fills an otherwise empty gap that would earn nothing at all. Having a general rule of thumb decided in advance, rather than deciding fresh under time pressure each time, keeps this consistent and fair.

## A practical checklist for setting minimum stay policy

1. Set a shorter minimum in high season, longer in low season, rather than one fixed number.
2. Factor in turnover cost, not just occupancy, when deciding how short to go.
3. Make minimum stay policy visible before a guest starts an enquiry.
4. Check whether OTA platform conventions conflict with your intended policy.
5. Review minimum stay alongside cancellation policy, since longer stays raise the financial stakes of a cancellation.

## FAQ

### Should minimum stay be the same all year?

Generally not. A shorter minimum in high season captures more of the strong available demand, while a longer minimum in low season reduces how often turnover costs eat into revenue on very short, less profitable bookings. Adjusting it by season typically improves both occupancy and net revenue.

### Does a shorter minimum stay always mean more bookings?

Not necessarily more profitable ones. A very short minimum stay can fill the calendar with frequent single-night changeovers that increase housekeeping and turnover cost per night sold, sometimes reducing net revenue compared with fewer, longer stays at a similar overall occupancy.

### How does minimum stay affect cancellation risk?

Longer minimum stays put more nights at stake in a single booking, which raises the financial impact of any cancellation. This makes having a clear, fair cancellation policy more important as minimum stays lengthen, not less, so the two should be reviewed together.

### Should minimum stay differ between direct bookings and Airbnb?

It can. Direct bookings give you full control to set and communicate minimum stay terms directly, which is a natural place to encourage longer stays with a discount. OTA platforms sometimes have their own conventions worth checking so your policy doesn't create confusion for guests browsing there.

### How do I decide the right minimum stay for my specific villa?

Consider your area's typical guest profile (families need different flexibility than remote workers), the season, and your villa's turnover cost. There's no single right number for every villa; an experienced local manager can usually suggest a starting policy based on comparable properties in your area.
