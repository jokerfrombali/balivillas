The honest answer is that the best time to visit Bali depends on what trade-off matters most to you: reliable weather, lower prices, fewer crowds, or specific events like Nyepi or a family school holiday window. There isn't a single month that wins on every measure at once.

If you want the safest bet on weather with minimal planning around rain, the dry season from roughly April through October is the standard recommendation. If you're more price-sensitive or crowd-averse and can tolerate more rain showers, the wetter months from November through March, and particularly the shoulder months either side, often deliver a better overall experience for less money.

## The two seasons, in practical terms

Bali has a wet season and a dry season rather than four distinct seasons, and the difference is less about temperature — which stays warm year-round — and more about rainfall pattern and humidity. Dry season months see less rain, generally clearer skies, and are the peak period for both domestic and international tourism, which pushes villa and flight prices up accordingly. Wet season months see more frequent, often short and intense rain showers, higher humidity, and noticeably fewer crowds and lower prices.

| Priority | Best window | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Most reliable weather | Dry season, roughly April-October | Higher prices, more crowds, especially July-August |
| Lower prices, fewer crowds | Wet season, roughly November-March | More rain, higher humidity |
| Balance of both | Shoulder months either side of dry season | Good value with mostly manageable weather |
| Avoiding a total island shutdown | Anytime except Nyepi | Nyepi (Day of Silence) closes the island for a full day |

## Why the dry season isn't automatically "best" for everyone

Peak dry season, especially July and August, brings the highest prices and busiest roads, beaches and restaurants of the year, alongside school holiday crowds from several countries at once. If your priority is a relaxed, less crowded experience rather than guaranteed sunshine every day, the shoulder months or even wetter months can genuinely suit you better, particularly since Bali's rain showers are often brief and don't necessarily ruin a whole day the way sustained rain elsewhere might.

## What the wetter months actually feel like

Rain in Bali's wetter season tends to arrive in shorter, heavier bursts rather than all-day drizzle, often in the afternoon, which still leaves plenty of the day usable. Humidity is noticeably higher, and some travellers find this more uncomfortable than the rain itself. Villa stays with a private pool, like most of ours, reduce how much a shower disrupts your day compared to relying entirely on public beaches or outdoor activities.

## Matching the season to your trip type

A honeymoon or a trip built heavily around beach days and outdoor activities leans toward favouring the dry season, when the odds of consistent good weather are simply higher. A longer stay, a work-from-Bali trip, or a visit built more around villa time, food and culture than beach weather is far less sensitive to season, and the savings and quieter atmosphere of the wetter months can be a genuine upside rather than a compromise.

Families travelling around specific school holiday windows often have less flexibility on timing anyway, in which case the more useful planning question becomes how to manage whichever season you land in rather than which season to pick.

## Specific dates and events worth checking before you book

Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, closes the entire island for around 24 hours once a year, including the airport, and falls on a different date annually since it follows the Balinese lunar calendar; our dedicated Nyepi guide covers what this means for arriving or departing guests. Galungan and Kuningan, a significant Balinese holiday period, also affects local rhythms, staffing and traffic without closing the island the way Nyepi does.

- Check the current-year date for Nyepi if your trip falls near it
- Check school holiday calendars for your home country if travelling with kids, since this affects both crowds and prices

## Booking timing and price relative to season

Villa and flight prices in Bali generally track the season closely: peak dry season and around major holidays commands the highest prices, while wetter and shoulder months see noticeably better value, including on villa stays. Booking further ahead matters more in peak season, when good villas in popular areas like Canggu and Sanur are more likely to be taken early; shoulder and wet season stays typically have more flexibility for later booking.

## FAQ

### Is there a genuinely bad time to visit Bali?
No single month makes a trip unworkable; the main exception is Nyepi, when the island closes for about a day, which is worth planning around rather than avoiding the whole season for. Otherwise it's a trade-off between weather reliability, price and crowds.

### Is the dry season always more expensive than the wet season?
Generally yes, particularly July and August and around major holidays, when villa and flight prices peak alongside demand. Shoulder months and wetter months typically offer better value.

### Does rain in the wet season ruin a Bali holiday?
Usually not; rain tends to arrive in shorter, heavier bursts rather than sustained all-day drizzle, leaving much of the day still usable. A villa with a private pool also reduces how much a shower disrupts your plans compared to relying on public beaches.

### What is the best time to visit Bali with young children?
This depends more on school holiday windows than season specifically, since families often have limited flexibility anyway. Within whatever window you have, the dry season offers more predictable outdoor days, but a wetter month is manageable with a villa that has good indoor and pool space.

### Should I avoid booking around Nyepi?
Not necessarily avoid, but plan around it: the island closes for about 24 hours including the airport, so check the current year's date if your travel dates are close to it. Our dedicated Nyepi guide covers what to expect for arrival and departure timing.
