Card payment is widely accepted in Bali's main tourist areas — Sanur, Canggu, Umalas, Ungasan, Seminyak, Ubud — at villas, hotels, restaurants, supermarkets and larger retail. The exceptions are small local warungs, traditional markets, street vendors, many scooter rentals, and smaller transport options, which generally expect cash. For a typical villa stay, you can rely on card for most spending and keep a modest amount of cash for the rest.

International cards (Visa and Mastercard especially) work reliably at most larger businesses. Some smaller establishments only accept local Indonesian cards or cash, so it's worth asking upfront if you're unsure, particularly outside the main tourist strips.

## Where card payment works reliably

Villas, hotels and larger restaurants across the main tourist areas almost universally accept card, often with a card machine or QR-based payment system. Supermarkets and larger retail chains are reliable too. Booking a villa direct, as with our own properties, typically involves payment arranged once dates are confirmed rather than anything charged upfront on a website — worth checking how any specific booking you're making actually handles payment.

## Where you'll still need cash

| Situation | Card usually accepted? | Notes |

## Fees and surcharges to check before you pay

Some businesses apply a small surcharge for card payment, typically to cover the processing fee, so it's worth asking before paying if the amount matters to you — this is more common at smaller businesses than at larger hotels and villas. Separately, check your own card's foreign transaction fee with your bank before travelling, since a fee on every purchase adds up over a longer stay; a card with no foreign transaction fee is worth getting before a trip if you don't already have one.

## Contactless and QR payment

Contactless card payment and QR-code-based mobile payment are both increasingly common in Bali's more developed areas, often faster than inserting a card and entering a PIN. If your card supports contactless, it's generally accepted anywhere a standard card machine is present; QR payment tends to be more relevant for locals using Indonesian mobile payment apps and less directly useful for most short-term visitors unless you set up a local e-wallet.

## Debit versus credit cards, and what to tell your bank

Credit cards often carry stronger fraud protection than debit cards, which is worth weighing if you're planning larger transactions like booking activities or a longer villa stay. Whichever type you use, tell your bank your travel dates before you leave if your provider suggests it, since an unexpected charge from Indonesia on a card that's never been used abroad can trigger a fraud block at exactly the wrong moment, like mid-checkout at a restaurant or villa. Carrying two cards from different providers, kept in separate places, is a simple way to avoid being stuck if one is blocked, lost or declined partway through a trip.

## Currency conversion at the point of sale

Some card machines in Bali offer to charge you in your home currency instead of Indonesian rupiah, a feature usually called dynamic currency conversion. This almost always works out worse for you than letting the transaction go through in rupiah and letting your own bank handle the conversion, because the exchange rate applied at the terminal is typically less favourable than your card provider's rate. When a card machine asks which currency to charge in, choose rupiah (the local currency) rather than your home currency, even if the machine defaults to the other option.

## A practical approach for a villa stay

- Set up at least one card with low or no foreign transaction fees before you travel.
- Keep a modest amount of rupiah cash for local warungs, markets and small transport — see our guide on [how much cash to bring to Bali](/blog/travel/how-much-cash-to-bring-to-bali/).
- Ask about card surcharges at smaller businesses before paying if it matters to you.
- Notify your bank of your travel dates if your card provider requires it to avoid a blocked transaction.
- Keep a backup card in case one is declined or lost.

## FAQ

### Can I pay for a villa in Bali by card?

Many villas, including ours, arrange payment once your dates are confirmed rather than charging anything on the website upfront, and card is generally an accepted payment method — confirm the specific process with whichever villa or manager you're booking through.

### Do restaurants in Bali accept card payment?

Larger and mid-range restaurants in the main tourist areas generally do, often with a card machine at the table or counter, but smaller local warungs typically expect cash, and prices there are usually low enough that this isn't a major inconvenience.

### Is there a surcharge for paying by card in Bali?

Some smaller businesses add a small surcharge to cover card processing fees, though this is less common at larger hotels, villas and restaurants. It's worth asking before paying if the amount matters to you.

### Should I bring a specific type of card to Bali?

A card with no or low foreign transaction fees is the most cost-effective option for a longer stay, since fees on every purchase add up. Visa and Mastercard are the most widely accepted networks across Bali.

### Can I use my phone's contactless payment in Bali?

Contactless card payment is increasingly accepted at larger businesses with modern card machines, generally as reliable as inserting a physical card, though it's worth carrying the physical card too in case a particular machine doesn't support contactless.
