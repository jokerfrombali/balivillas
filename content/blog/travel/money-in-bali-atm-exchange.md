For most visitors, the practical answer to getting rupiah in Bali is: use ATMs attached to recognised Indonesian banks (BCA, Mandiri, BNI, BRI) rather than unbranded standalone machines, and for larger cash exchanges use a licensed money changer with a visible rate board and a good, checkable reputation, not the too-good-to-be-true rates advertised by unmarked kiosks. Bali has had a long-running problem with rigged rate-calculation exchange counters and skimming devices on poorly maintained ATMs, and both are avoidable with a bit of care.

Card payment is widely accepted at hotels, villas, restaurants and larger shops across Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan, so you don't need to carry large amounts of cash for a typical stay — see our guide on [paying by card in Bali](/blog/travel/paying-by-card-in-bali/) for where cash is still genuinely needed.

## ATMs: what to look for and what to avoid

Bank-branded ATMs physically attached to or inside a bank branch are the safest option, since they're better maintained and monitored than standalone machines in convenience stores or tourist strips. Standalone ATMs from lesser-known operators sometimes carry higher fees and have historically been targeted for card-skimming devices; if a machine looks tampered with, has a loose card slot, or you're at all unsure, use a different one.

Check your own bank's foreign transaction and ATM withdrawal fees before you travel — Indonesian ATMs typically charge their own withdrawal fee on top of whatever your home bank charges, so a handful of larger withdrawals usually costs less in fees than many small ones.

## Comparing ways to get cash in Bali

| Method | Where | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Bank-branded ATM | Attached to bank branches, malls, airport | Card skimming on poorly maintained standalone machines elsewhere |
| Licensed money changer | Marked "authorised" / licensed, visible rate board | Rigged calculators or hidden fees at unlicensed kiosks |
| Airport exchange counters | Bali airport arrivals | Convenient but typically the worst rates — exchange only what you need immediately |
| Card payment | Hotels, villas, restaurants, shops | Confirm any card surcharge before paying |

## Spotting a dodgy money changer

Licensed, reputable money changers display "PT" licensing details and a clear, current rate board, and the amount they count out should match the calculation exactly. A common trick at less scrupulous counters is a rate that looks excellent on the board but a final calculation that comes out lower once fees or a "correction" are applied, or physically shortchanging the count. Count the cash yourself before leaving the counter, and if a deal looks unusually generous compared with other changers nearby, treat that as a warning sign rather than good luck.

## How much cash to actually carry

Most day-to-day spending at villas, restaurants and larger shops can go on a card, so cash is mainly for smaller purchases: local warungs, markets, temple donations, tips, and some scooter rentals or smaller transport. See our guide on [how much cash to bring to Bali](/blog/travel/how-much-cash-to-bring-to-bali/) for a fuller breakdown by trip type; as a starting principle, withdraw a moderate amount for the first few days rather than a large sum on arrival, and top up from a bank ATM as needed.

## A short safety checklist for ATMs and exchange

1. Use bank-branded ATMs where possible, ideally attached to a branch.
2. Cover the keypad when entering your PIN, as you would anywhere.
3. Check the machine and card slot look untampered before inserting your card.
4. At money changers, confirm the rate, watch the calculation, and count the cash before leaving.
5. Avoid exchanging large sums at airport counters; exchange only what covers your first day or two.

## FAQ

### Are ATMs safe to use in Bali?

Bank-branded ATMs, particularly those attached to a branch, are generally safe and widely used by visitors and residents alike. The main precaution is avoiding standalone, poorly maintained machines that have historically been more vulnerable to card skimming.

### Is it better to exchange money before travelling or in Bali?

Rates in Bali at a licensed, reputable money changer are often better than what you'd get at a home-country exchange counter, but airport counters in Bali specifically tend to offer the weakest rates on the island, so it's worth exchanging only a small amount there and doing the bulk elsewhere.

### How do I know if a money changer in Bali is legitimate?

Look for visible licensing details, a clear current rate board, and count the cash yourself before leaving. If the rate seems unusually better than every other changer nearby, that is a warning sign rather than a genuine bargain.

### Do Indonesian ATMs charge a withdrawal fee?

Yes, most charge a local withdrawal fee on top of whatever fee your own bank applies, so it's generally more cost-effective to withdraw larger amounts less frequently rather than making many small withdrawals.

### Can I avoid carrying cash entirely in Bali?

Not entirely — card is widely accepted at villas, hotels, restaurants and larger shops, but smaller local businesses, markets and some transport still expect cash, so carrying a modest amount is worth doing even on a card-focused trip.
