Bali observes two overlapping calendars: Indonesia's national public holidays (which apply across the whole country) and the Balinese Hindu religious calendar, which is specific to the island and includes days like Nyepi, Galungan and Kuningan that have a much bigger practical effect on a visitor's trip than most national holidays do. National holidays mostly affect government offices, banks and some local businesses; the Balinese religious calendar can affect flights, shops, and even movement around the island on certain days.

Because both calendars shift from year to year (national holidays partly follow the Islamic lunar calendar, and the Balinese calendar follows its own 210-day cycle), it's worth checking the specific dates for the year you're travelling rather than assuming they fall on the same date annually. ## The holiday that affects every visitor: Nyepi

Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, is the single most disruptive date on the calendar for travellers and is worth planning around specifically. For roughly 24 hours, the entire island shuts down: no flights in or out of the airport, no vehicles on the roads, minimal lighting, and visitors are expected to stay inside their accommodation. See our dedicated guide on [Nyepi Day in Bali](/blog/travel/nyepi-day-bali/) for exactly what to expect and how to plan a stay that includes it. (as of 2026, verify)

## Galungan and Kuningan: less disruptive, still worth knowing about

Galungan and Kuningan are major Balinese Hindu celebrations marking the victory of good over evil, occurring roughly every 210 days according to the Balinese calendar. They don't shut the island down the way Nyepi does, but they bring noticeably more ceremonial activity, decorated penjor bamboo poles along the streets, and some businesses closing briefly or adjusting hours. It's actually one of the more interesting times to be in Bali as a visitor, offering a genuine look at local culture, provided you're prepared for slightly different rhythms around town. See our [Galungan and Kuningan guide](/blog/travel/galungan-and-kuningan/) for specific timing and what to expect.

## National holidays: lower impact, but worth knowing

| Holiday type | Typical effect on travellers |
|---|---|
| Nyepi (Day of Silence) | Major — airport closed, no movement outside accommodation |
| Galungan and Kuningan | Moderate — more ceremony and decoration, some businesses adjust hours |
| National public holidays (New Year, Independence Day, religious national holidays) | Minor for tourists — banks/offices closed, most tourist businesses operate normally |
| Christmas and New Year period | High demand, not a closure — book well ahead |

Indonesia's standard national public holidays (New Year's Day, Independence Day on 17 August, and various religious holidays observed nationally) mostly affect government offices, banks and some local shops. Most villas, restaurants and tourist-facing businesses in Bali operate normally through these, though it's worth checking specific business hours if you have a plan that depends on a bank or government office being open.

## Booking a villa around holiday periods

The Christmas and New Year period is Bali's highest-demand travel window and books out well in advance across most areas, so plan and book earlier than you would for other times of year if you're travelling then. Nyepi requires a different kind of planning — not about availability, but about accepting a full day where you cannot leave your accommodation, so choose a villa you'll be comfortable spending that day in, ideally with a private pool and enough space if travelling with family.

## A short planning checklist around Bali's holiday calendar

1. Check the specific dates for Nyepi and Galungan/Kuningan in the year you're travelling.
2. If your trip includes Nyepi, confirm with your villa or accommodation what the day involves and plan accordingly.
3. Book well ahead for the Christmas-New Year period given high demand across all areas.
4. Check bank and government office hours separately if your trip depends on either during a national holiday.
5. Treat Galungan and Kuningan as a chance to see genuine local ceremony, not a disruption to avoid.

## What to do next

## FAQ

### Does the whole of Bali shut down for Nyepi?

Yes, for roughly 24 hours the airport closes, no vehicles are allowed on the roads, and visitors are expected to stay inside their accommodation. It is the one Balinese holiday that genuinely affects every visitor on the island regardless of where they're staying. (as of 2026, verify)

### Are shops and restaurants open during Galungan and Kuningan?

Most tourist-facing businesses stay open, though some may adjust hours briefly for family ceremonies, and you'll see noticeably more decoration and ceremonial activity around town. It's generally not disruptive to a normal travel schedule.

### Do Indonesian national holidays affect villa bookings or availability?

Not directly — standard national holidays mainly affect banks and government offices, not tourist accommodation or restaurants, which typically operate as usual. The main demand spike to plan around is the Christmas-New Year period specifically.

### When does Nyepi fall each year?

Nyepi's date shifts annually based on the Balinese Saka lunar calendar, so it doesn't fall on a fixed date. Check the specific date for your travel year well ahead of booking flights, since airport closure affects arrival and departure planning directly. (as of 2026, verify)

### Should I avoid visiting Bali during its holiday periods?

Not necessarily — Galungan and Kuningan in particular offer a genuine, interesting cultural experience for visitors. Nyepi requires specific planning rather than avoidance, and the Christmas-New Year period is simply about booking earlier due to high demand.
