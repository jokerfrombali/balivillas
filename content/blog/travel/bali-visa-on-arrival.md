Bali visa on arrival is a short-stay entry option that lets eligible nationalities pay a fee and receive a visa at the point of entry (or, increasingly, in advance through Indonesia's official e-visa system) rather than applying at an embassy beforehand. It covers a fixed maximum stay and is meant for tourism, not work or long-term residence.

We host guests from dozens of countries every year and get asked about this constantly, so here's the practical version: what it actually covers, who it's for, and what to double-check before you book flights.

## Who visa on arrival is for

Visa on arrival is designed for short tourist visits — sightseeing, a holiday, visiting family, or the kind of stay most of our villa guests are taking. It is not the right route if you plan to work remotely for an extended period, invest, or stay longer than the visa allows, and it doesn't cover business activities beyond attending meetings.

Not every nationality is eligible for visa on arrival; the eligible list is set and updated by Indonesian immigration and does change, so it needs checking against the current official list rather than assumed from a previous trip or a general travel article (as of 2026, verify). The authoritative source is imigrasi.go.id.

## How to actually get it

In practice, most travellers today apply and pay online in advance through Indonesia's official e-visa system before flying, which generates a QR code or confirmation used at immigration on arrival — this tends to be faster than paying in person at the airport counter, though a pay-on-arrival option typically still exists at major entry points. Given how often procedures and fee amounts are updated, check the current process on imigrasi.go.id close to your travel date rather than relying on how it worked on a previous trip.

## Which airport, and does it matter

Bali's main international gateway is Ngurah Rai International Airport (Denpasar), and this is where the vast majority of villa guests arrive. If you're arriving via another Indonesian international airport before an onward domestic flight to Bali, the visa on arrival process happens at that first port of entry into Indonesia, not necessarily in Bali itself — worth knowing if your itinerary routes through Jakarta or another city first.

## Visa on arrival vs the arrival card

These are two separate things that guests often confuse. Visa on arrival is about your legal permission to enter and stay; the arrival card (sometimes now handled digitally) is a separate customs and immigration declaration required regardless of visa type. Our guide on the [Indonesia arrival card](/blog/travel/indonesia-arrival-card-customs/) covers that process specifically — you'll likely need to deal with both before clearing immigration.

## How long you can actually stay

Visa on arrival covers a fixed maximum period, and whether it can be extended once in Indonesia, and for how long, is worth confirming directly on the official immigration site before you travel, since this is exactly the kind of detail that changes (as of 2026, verify). If your trip might run longer than the standard visa-on-arrival period, it's worth checking alternative visa types before you fly rather than assuming an extension will be straightforward once you're here.

## What happens if you overstay

Overstaying a visa in Indonesia carries penalties that scale with how long the overstay runs, and can affect future entry. If your travel plans are uncertain or might extend, it is worth building in a buffer or looking at the extension process in advance rather than assuming a short overstay is a minor issue — check current penalty details on imigrasi.go.id if this is a live concern for your trip.
