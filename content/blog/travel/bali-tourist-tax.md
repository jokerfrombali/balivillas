The Bali tourist levy is a one-off fee that foreign visitors pay on or before arrival in Bali, introduced by the Bali provincial government to help fund cultural preservation, environmental protection and local infrastructure. It is separate from your Indonesian visa or any visa-on-arrival fee, and it is specific to Bali rather than a nationwide Indonesian charge. (as of 2026, verify)

The amount and payment method have been set by provincial regulation, and it's worth checking the current, official figure and process directly on the official Love Bali platform or through your airline or accommodation before travelling, rather than relying on older or third-party sources, since amounts and processes for levies like this can change. (as of 2026, verify)

## How and when to pay

The levy is typically paid online in advance of arrival through the official government-run platform, though on-arrival payment options have also existed depending on current arrangements. Paying online ahead of time avoids any queue or additional step on arrival, and you'll usually receive a QR code or confirmation to show if asked. Always use the official government platform rather than a third-party site claiming to process the payment, since unofficial sites have circulated and can charge extra or mishandle payment details. (as of 2026, verify)

## Who needs to pay and who is exempt

| Traveller type | Typically required to pay? |
|---|---|
| Foreign tourists arriving in Bali | Yes |
| Indonesian citizens | No |
| Diplomatic passport holders | Generally exempt, check current rules |
| Visitors on certain official/work visas | May be exempt depending on visa category, check current rules |
| Children | Check current rules — exemptions have varied |

Always confirm current exemption categories on the official platform before travelling, since rules and thresholds are set by regulation and can be updated. (as of 2026, verify)

## How this differs from your visa fee

The tourist levy is entirely separate from whatever visa arrangement you're using to enter Indonesia — a visa-on-arrival fee, an e-visa, or a visa exemption where applicable. Paying the tourist levy does not replace or affect your visa requirement, and having a valid visa does not exempt you from the levy. Treat them as two separate items to sort before or on arrival. See our guide on the [Bali visa on arrival](/blog/travel/bali-visa-on-arrival/) for the visa side of arrival planning. (as of 2026, verify)

## What the levy is intended to fund

The stated purpose of the levy, as set out by the Bali provincial government, is supporting cultural preservation (temple maintenance, traditional ceremonies and arts) and environmental protection initiatives (waste management, natural site conservation) across the island. As with any public levy, specifics on how funds are allocated are set and reported by the provincial government rather than something we can speak to directly; check official provincial sources for detail if this matters to you. (as of 2026, verify)

## What to do if you can't find your confirmation on arrival

If you paid online but can't locate the QR code or confirmation email at immigration or elsewhere, keep the payment reference or transaction ID handy as a backup — most confirmations are also retrievable by re-visiting the official platform and entering the same details you used to pay. Avoid relying on a screenshot alone without also knowing your reference number, since a lost or corrupted screenshot with no backup can mean re-explaining the payment on the spot. Airlines flying into Bali have increasingly built the levy into pre-arrival information for passengers, so check your airline's own arrival guidance too, alongside the official government source, in case a specific process applies to your flight.

## How the levy fits into overall trip budgeting

Compared with the overall cost of a Bali trip — flights, accommodation, activities — the tourist levy is a minor, one-off line item, but it's worth budgeting for explicitly alongside your visa fee rather than assuming arrival costs stop at the visa. If you're travelling as a family or a larger group, note whether the levy is charged per person (as most such levies are) so you can budget the total accurately rather than assuming a single household charge. (as of 2026, verify)

## A short checklist before you travel

1. Check the current levy amount and payment process on the official government platform before booking.
2. Pay online in advance if that option is available, to avoid any arrival-day step.
3. Keep your payment confirmation or QR code accessible on your phone for arrival.
4. Confirm whether you fall into any exemption category if relevant to your visa type.
5. Avoid third-party sites claiming to process the levy; use only official government channels.

## FAQ

### How much is the Bali tourist tax?

The amount is set by Bali provincial regulation and is best confirmed on the official government payment platform before you travel, since published figures can be updated. Do not rely on unofficial or outdated sources for the current amount. (as of 2026, verify)

### Is the Bali tourist tax the same as a visa fee?

No, they are separate. The tourist levy funds cultural and environmental initiatives specific to Bali province, while your visa (visa-on-arrival, e-visa, or exemption) is a separate national immigration requirement. Both need to be handled independently. (as of 2026, verify)

### Do I need to pay the Bali tourist tax every time I visit?

Current rules have generally treated it as a per-visit charge rather than a one-time lifetime payment, but confirm this on the official platform for your specific travel plans, particularly if you're making multiple trips within a short period. (as of 2026, verify)

### Where do I pay the official Bali tourist levy?

Payment should be made only through the official government-run platform (commonly referred to as Love Bali), either in advance online or through official on-arrival arrangements. Avoid third-party sites that aren't the official government channel. (as of 2026, verify)

### Are children exempt from the Bali tourist tax?

Exemption rules for children and other categories are set by current provincial regulation and have varied, so check the official platform for the current policy before your trip rather than assuming a blanket exemption. (as of 2026, verify)
