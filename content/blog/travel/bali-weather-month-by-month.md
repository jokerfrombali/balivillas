Bali's weather month by month is best understood as a gradual shift between two broad seasons rather than a sharp switch or four distinct seasons. Temperature barely changes across the year, staying warm and humid throughout; what shifts is how much and how often it rains.

Roughly, November through March is the wetter season, April through October is the drier season, and the months right at each boundary (October-November and March-April) behave as a genuine transition, sometimes leaning one way, sometimes the other, from year to year.

## The pattern across the year, at a glance

| Period | General pattern | What it means for a trip |
|---|---|---|
| December-February | Wettest months, higher humidity | Short, heavy showers likely most days; plan indoor/pool time |
| March-April | Transition, gradually drying | Increasingly reliable but still some rain |
| May-September | Driest, most consistent months | Lowest rain chance, busiest and priciest, especially July-August |
| October | Transition, gradually wettening | Good value, weather often still decent |
| June-August specifically | Peak dry season | Cooler evenings relatively, higher winds in some areas, busiest crowds |

## Why year-to-year variation matters more than a fixed calendar

Bali's weather, like much of the tropics, is influenced by broader regional climate patterns that shift the exact timing and intensity of the wet season somewhat from year to year. Treat month-by-month averages as a strong guide rather than a guarantee: a specific November might run drier than usual, and a specific June might see an unusual late shower pattern. If your trip is weather-sensitive, checking a closer-to-date forecast a week or two before travel is more useful than relying solely on historical monthly averages.

## What actually changes: rain pattern, not temperature

Daytime temperatures across Bali stay broadly similar throughout the year, generally warm, with humidity as the bigger variable than heat itself. What changes month to month is the frequency and intensity of rain: wet season months see more days with rain, often in shorter, heavier afternoon bursts, while dry season months see long stretches of clear days with rain far less frequent. Neither season brings genuinely cold weather, so packing for temperature swings isn't necessary the way it might be for a temperate destination.

## Humidity versus rainfall: two different discomforts

Humidity is consistently high across Bali year-round but peaks alongside the wetter months, which is often what visitors actually find more uncomfortable than the rain itself. Dry season months, while less humid on average, can still feel intensely warm in the middle of the day, particularly inland or away from sea breeze. Packing breathable fabrics (covered in our wider packing guide) matters regardless of which month you're visiting.

## Planning activities around the pattern

If you're visiting during wetter months, building some flexibility into outdoor plans — an alternate indoor activity or a flexible tour booking — avoids a fully rained-out day. Since showers are often short and afternoon-concentrated, planning outdoor activities for the morning and treating afternoons as more flexible is a pattern that works well across much of the wetter season.

During peak dry months, particularly July and August, the practical planning challenge shifts from weather to crowds and pricing: booking popular restaurants, tours and villas further ahead matters more than checking the forecast.

## Areas we manage villas in: any local weather differences

Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan don't differ dramatically in weather pattern, since they're all on the same island within a relatively compact geographic area, but Ungasan's clifftop position on the Bukit can see slightly different wind patterns than the flatter areas, occasionally relevant for beach conditions at Melasti or Green Bowl below. Otherwise, the month-by-month pattern described here applies fairly evenly across all four areas.

## Reading a forecast versus reading the season

A seasonal overview like this is useful for booking decisions months in advance; a proper short-range forecast, checked closer to your travel date, is more useful for daily planning once you're in Bali or in the final couple of weeks before you travel. Treat this guide as the "which month should I even consider" layer, and a live forecast app as the "what should I actually plan tomorrow" layer.

## FAQ

### What is the rainiest month in Bali?
The wet season, roughly December through February, sees the most frequent and heaviest rain, though the exact peak shifts somewhat year to year. Our dedicated rainy season guide covers this period in more detail.

### What is the driest month in Bali?
The driest stretch generally falls within the May-September window, with July and August typically the busiest and highest-priced months of the year alongside being reliably dry.

### Does Bali get cold at any point in the year?
No, temperatures stay warm and fairly consistent year-round; there's no genuine cold season. What varies is humidity and rainfall rather than temperature.

### Is it worth visiting Bali during the wet season?
Yes for many travellers, particularly those prioritising value and fewer crowds over guaranteed dry days. Rain tends to arrive in shorter, heavier bursts rather than all-day drizzle, and a villa with a private pool reduces how much a shower disrupts your plans.

### How accurate are month-by-month weather averages for planning a specific trip?
They're a solid guide for choosing a travel window months ahead, but actual conditions vary year to year, so checking a closer-range forecast a week or two before your trip is more useful for day-to-day planning once dates are set.
