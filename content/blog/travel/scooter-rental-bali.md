Renting a scooter in Bali is straightforward logistically — most rental shops and many villas can arrange one within an hour — but legally you need a valid licence that covers motorbikes to ride one, which for most foreign visitors means an International Driving Permit (IDP) with a motorcycle category, not just a home-country car licence. Riding without one is common among tourists but carries real legal and insurance risk if something goes wrong. (as of 2026, verify)

Beyond the paperwork, the practical risk on Bali's roads is genuinely worth taking seriously: heavy, sometimes chaotic traffic, inconsistent surface conditions, and a high number of scooters on the road at once, especially around Canggu, Seminyak and central tourist areas. Experience riding scooters elsewhere doesn't automatically translate to comfort on Bali's roads, so it's worth being honest with yourself about your own riding ability before committing to daily scooter use.

## The licence requirement, explained simply

To legally ride a scooter in Indonesia as a foreign visitor, you generally need an International Driving Permit that includes a motorcycle endorsement, obtained in your home country before travel (Indonesia does not issue IDPs to foreign visitors on arrival). A standard car licence or an IDP covering only cars does not cover riding a motorbike or scooter. See our dedicated guide on the [international driving permit for Bali](/blog/travel/bali-driving-licence-idp/) for exactly how to get one and what it covers. (as of 2026, verify)

In practice, many tourists rent and ride scooters without the correct permit, and rental shops rarely check thoroughly. This does not make it legal, and it matters most if you're stopped at a police checkpoint (common in tourist areas) or if you're involved in an accident, where lacking the correct licence can affect both any fine and whether travel insurance covers you.

## What a typical scooter rental includes

| Item | Usually included | Worth checking separately |
|---|---|---|
| The scooter itself | Yes, various models (automatic matic scooters most common) | Condition, age, tyre wear |
| Helmet | Usually one, sometimes two | Ask for a second if riding with a passenger |
| Fuel | No — you fill it yourself | Return with a similar fuel level to avoid a top-up charge |

| Deposit | Often required (cash or passport, though passport deposits are best avoided) | Never leave your passport as security if avoidable |

## Insurance: the gap that catches people out

Many casual scooter rentals in Bali come with minimal or no real insurance, and the rental shop's "insurance" sometimes just means a shared risk pool for damage to the bike itself, not medical costs or third-party liability if you're involved in an accident. Ask directly what the rental's insurance actually covers, and separately confirm whether your own travel insurance covers scooter accidents — many standard travel policies exclude motorbike riding entirely, or require the correct licence to be valid. Check this before you ride, not after an incident.

## Choosing a rental shop you can trust

A reliable rental typically has well-maintained bikes (check tyres, brakes and lights before riding off), is upfront about deposit terms, and does not pressure you to leave your passport as security — a cash deposit or a photocopy of your passport is a fairer and safer arrangement. Ask your villa for a recommendation if you're unsure; we can point guests toward rental shops we've had reliable experience with.

## Practical safety habits that matter more than the paperwork

- Always wear the helmet provided and check it fits properly before riding off.
- Avoid riding at night or in heavy rain until you're confident with local traffic patterns.
- Keep to slower, quieter roads while you get a feel for how traffic moves in your area.
- Never ride with more passengers than the bike is rated for.
- Check mirrors and lights work before you leave the rental shop.

## Alternatives if you'd rather not ride yourself

Scooters aren't the only way to get around. App-based rides through [Grab or Gojek](/blog/travel/grab-vs-gojek-bali/) cover most short trips without the licence question or traffic risk of riding yourself, and a [private driver](/blog/travel/bali-private-driver-cost/) makes sense for a full day of sightseeing, especially with a family or a group not confident on a scooter.

## FAQ

### Do I legally need an international licence to rent a scooter in Bali?

Yes, a valid licence covering motorbikes is required, and for most foreign visitors this means an International Driving Permit with a motorcycle category obtained before you travel. A standard car licence alone does not cover riding a scooter. (as of 2026, verify)

### Is scooter rental in Bali safe for someone with limited riding experience?

It carries real risk given traffic density and road conditions in the main tourist areas, so limited-experience riders should consider starting on quieter roads, avoiding peak traffic times, and being honest about whether a private driver or app-based rides might suit them better for this trip.

### What happens if I have an accident on a rented scooter without the right licence?

It can affect both any police handling of the incident and whether your travel insurance pays out for medical costs or liability, since many policies require a valid licence for the vehicle involved. This is the main practical reason to sort the correct permit before riding.

### Should I leave my passport as a deposit for a scooter rental?

It's best avoided where possible; a cash deposit or a photocopy of your passport is a safer arrangement, since losing access to your original passport creates far bigger problems than losing a rental deposit.

Many villas, including ours, can point guests toward rental shops with a track record of well-maintained bikes and fair terms, which is often a safer starting point than picking an unfamiliar shop at random.
