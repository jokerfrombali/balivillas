Travel insurance for Bali needs to cover, at minimum, medical treatment and emergency evacuation, trip cancellation or interruption, and — importantly for many visitors' actual plans — any activity you intend to do, since scooter or motorbike riding, diving, surfing and some other activities are commonly excluded from standard policies unless specifically included. Reading the exclusions is more important than comparing headline price, because a cheap policy that doesn't cover what you'll actually be doing isn't real cover at all.

Indonesia does not have reciprocal healthcare agreements that cover most foreign visitors' medical costs the way some countries do for each other, so private medical treatment in Bali is generally paid for directly, with insurance reimbursing afterward or, for larger claims, sometimes paying providers directly. Without insurance, a serious medical issue or an evacuation can be extremely costly, which is the main reason travel insurance for Bali isn't optional in any real sense.

## What a solid policy should include

Medical cover should be high enough to handle a serious hospital stay or emergency evacuation, since evacuation costs for a serious injury or illness can run into tens of thousands of dollars depending on circumstances. Trip cancellation and interruption cover protects the cost of flights and accommodation if illness, a family emergency, or another covered event forces you to cancel or cut a trip short. Baggage and personal effects cover matters less for most travellers but is worth having for higher-value items like laptops or camera equipment if you're travelling with them.

## Common exclusions that catch travellers out

| Activity or situation | Often excluded unless specified | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Scooter or motorbike riding | Yes, especially without correct licence | Confirm cover and licence requirements explicitly |
| Diving or other water sports | Sometimes, depending on depth/certification | Check if your planned activities are covered |
| Pre-existing medical conditions | Often excluded or need declaration | Declare honestly; undeclared conditions can void claims |
| Alcohol-related incidents | Frequently excluded | Standard across most policies, not Bali-specific |
| Adventure activities (rafting, hiking certain routes) | Varies by policy | Check specific activity list, not just general wording |

## The scooter and licence question specifically

This is one of the most common gaps travellers discover only after an incident: many policies require a valid, correctly categorised licence (an International Driving Permit with the right vehicle category, in most cases) for any driving or riding-related claim to be valid. If you plan to rent a [scooter in Bali](/blog/travel/scooter-rental-bali/), check this specific clause in your policy and make sure you have the [correct international driving permit](/blog/travel/bali-driving-licence-idp/) before you travel, rather than discovering the gap after an accident.

## Choosing a policy: what actually matters

Compare a small number of reputable insurers on the specifics that matter for your trip — medical and evacuation limits, activity-specific inclusions, and cancellation cover terms — rather than shopping purely on headline price. A policy that's slightly more expensive but genuinely covers scooter riding, or has a higher medical limit, is usually worth it given how large the cost gap can be between minor and serious medical claims in a foreign country.

## A short checklist before you buy

1. Confirm the medical and evacuation cover limit is genuinely high enough for a serious incident.
2. Check whether scooter/motorbike riding is covered, and what licence requirement applies.
3. List any specific activities you plan (diving, surfing, hiking) and confirm each is covered.
4. Declare any pre-existing medical conditions honestly rather than risking a voided claim.
5. Keep a digital and physical copy of your policy and emergency contact number accessible throughout the trip.

## What to do if you need to make a claim

Keep receipts and documentation for anything you'll claim — medical treatment, cancelled bookings, damaged or stolen items — since most insurers require this to process a claim. For medical treatment, contact your insurer's emergency line as early as possible if the situation is serious, since some policies require pre-approval for certain treatments or evacuation to be fully covered.

## What to do next

Buy travel insurance before you travel, not as an afterthought, and spend the extra few minutes checking the specific exclusions that matter for your actual plans — particularly scooter riding, since this is the single most common gap we see travellers discover too late.

## FAQ

### Does standard travel insurance cover scooter accidents in Bali?

Often not automatically — many policies exclude motorbike or scooter riding entirely, or require a valid, correctly categorised licence for any claim to be valid. Check this specific clause before you travel if you plan to ride.

### Is travel insurance really necessary for a trip to Bali?

Yes — Indonesia doesn't have healthcare agreements that cover most foreign visitors' medical costs, so private treatment is paid directly, and a serious medical issue or evacuation without insurance can be extremely expensive. It's one of the few genuinely essential items to arrange before travelling.

### What medical cover limit should I look for in a Bali travel insurance policy?

Look for a limit high enough to cover a serious hospital stay and, importantly, emergency evacuation, which can be one of the largest costs in a serious incident. Compare this figure specifically rather than relying on headline policy price alone.

### Does travel insurance cover Bali belly or other stomach illness?

Most policies cover medical treatment for illness contracted during the trip, including stomach illness requiring a clinic visit, though minor self-treated cases wouldn't typically involve a claim. Check your specific policy's medical treatment terms.

### Can I buy travel insurance after I've already arrived in Bali?

Most insurers require the policy to be purchased before departure or very early in the trip; buying after an incident has occurred, or sometimes after arrival at all, may not be possible or may exclude pre-existing issues. Arrange cover before you travel.
