Price per square metre in Bali varies too widely across areas, land status and build type for a single average figure to be useful for a specific purchase decision. A number quoted for beachfront Canggu land tells you almost nothing about what a quiet inland plot in Ungasan or a townhouse-style unit in Sanur is likely to cost. The metric is useful only once you are comparing genuinely similar properties in a genuinely similar location.

We are not going to publish a market-average price per sqm figure here, because doing so responsibly would require survey data we do not have and cannot verify. What we can offer is what actually moves the number, so you can judge a specific listing sensibly instead of anchoring to a figure pulled from an unrelated part of the island.

## What actually drives price per sqm

| Factor | Effect on price |
|---|---|
| Distance to the beach or a landmark area | Closer generally costs more per sqm |
| Land certificate type and leasehold term remaining | Longer remaining lease and cleaner certificate generally command a premium |
| Building versus land-only price | Built structures price differently per sqm than raw land |
| Access (main road versus narrow lane) | Easier access typically adds value |
| Local zoning and permitted use | Tourism-zoned land generally prices higher than agricultural-zoned land |
| Build quality and age | Recent, well-finished construction prices higher per sqm of building |

## Land price per sqm versus building price per sqm

These are two different numbers that get conflated easily. Land price per sqm reflects the value of the plot itself, driven by location, zoning and certificate status. Building price per sqm reflects construction cost and quality, and is a separate calculation entirely, more closely tied to materials, finish level and the builder's rates than to location. A villa listing's total price divided by total square metres blends both, which is why comparing two villas' blended per-sqm figures can mislead if one has a much larger plot relative to its building, or vice versa.

## Why area comparisons need care

Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan, the four areas we operate in, each have distinct pricing character. Canggu's beachfront and inner-lane property has historically commanded some of the highest land values on the island given its concentration of cafés, coworking spaces and beach clubs. Sanur offers a calmer, more spread-out market with a mix of family-oriented and long-stay stock. Umalas sits between Seminyak and Canggu with a quieter lane character. Ungasan, on the clifftop Bukit peninsula, has its own dynamic tied to sea views and beach proximity at Melasti and Green Bowl. Comparing a per-sqm figure across these areas without accounting for what each buys you is not a useful exercise.

## How to use price per sqm sensibly when comparing listings

1. Only compare villas within the same area, or explicitly note the area difference and what it typically explains
2. Separate land price per sqm from building price per sqm where the listing allows it
3. Check the leasehold term remaining, since a shorter remaining lease should generally reduce the fair per-sqm price even if the headline number looks similar
4. Confirm zoning and permit status, since land priced attractively per sqm but zoned incorrectly for your intended use is not actually comparable
5. Weight build quality and age into the comparison rather than treating all "built" villas as equivalent

## A worked example of what not to do

Imagine comparing a 300 sqm plot with a finished 200 sqm villa in Sanur against a 150 sqm plot with a finished 120 sqm villa in Canggu, purely on blended price per total sqm. The comparison tells you almost nothing useful, because the land-to-building ratio, the area's typical buyer profile, the remaining lease terms and the build quality could all differ substantially. A meaningful comparison would separate land and building value, note the lease term for each, and compare within the context of what each area's rental or resale market actually looks like.

## FAQ

### Is there a single reliable average price per sqm for Bali?
No credible single figure covers the island meaningfully, because prices vary too widely by area, land status and build quality. Treat any quoted island-wide average with caution and ask what specific data it is based on.

### Why does the same size villa cost so differently in Canggu versus Ungasan?
Location character, proximity to the coast and to amenity clusters, zoning, and typical buyer demand all differ by area, and these factors move price per sqm more than raw size does. Comparing across areas needs to account for what each location actually offers.

### Should I compare land price per sqm or total villa price per sqm?
Both, but separately. Land price per sqm isolates the plot's value; total villa price per sqm blends in the building, which is driven by different factors like construction quality and age. Confusing the two leads to misleading comparisons.

### Does a shorter remaining leasehold term lower the fair price per sqm?
Generally yes, since you are buying fewer years of usable rights. A villa priced similarly per sqm to a neighbour but with a much shorter remaining lease is arguably more expensive on a like-for-like basis, not equivalently priced.

### How do I find a genuinely comparable listing to benchmark against?
Focus on the same specific micro-area, similar land-to-building ratio, similar remaining lease term and similar build quality and age. A handful of genuinely comparable listings tells you more than any published area average.
