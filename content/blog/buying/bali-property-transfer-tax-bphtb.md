BPHTB is a one-off tax paid when rights to land or a building change hands in Indonesia, calculated against the transaction value (or an official assessed value, whichever governs in your specific case). It sits alongside notary fees and registration costs as one of the standard components of closing a property purchase, and it is separate from the ongoing annual land and building tax (PBB) that continues after you own the property.

Yes, Bali has property tax, in two distinct forms: BPHTB at the point of transfer, and PBB annually thereafter. They are easy to confuse because both relate to land and buildings, but they apply at different times and are calculated differently.

## BPHTB versus PBB: two different taxes

| | BPHTB | PBB |
|---|---|---|
| When it applies | Once, at the point of transfer | Annually, for as long as you own |
| What it is based on | Transaction or assessed value | Assessed property value |
| Who typically pays | Buyer, in most standard structures | Current owner |
| Purpose | Tax on acquiring the right to the property | Ongoing land and building tax |

A bali property tax calculator you find online will usually only estimate one of these, often PBB, so check which tax any tool or figure is actually referring to before using it to budget your purchase.

## How BPHTB is generally calculated

BPHTB is typically calculated as a percentage of the transaction value or the officially assessed value of the property, whichever the relevant regulation designates as the base for that transaction, minus any non-taxable threshold that may apply. The exact rate and threshold should be confirmed with your notary for the current year and the specific transaction structure, since this is a figure that can be adjusted by regulation and varies by transaction type (as of 2026, verify the current rate directly with your notary or the relevant tax office before budgeting).

## Does BPHTB apply the same way to leasehold?

This is one of the more commonly misunderstood points. Most foreign buyers in Bali acquire villas through leasehold or other structures available to non-citizens, rather than direct freehold ownership. Whether and how BPHTB applies can differ depending on the exact legal structure of the transaction, since a lease assignment is not always treated identically to a full transfer of ownership rights under Indonesian tax law. Do not assume the same calculation applies to your specific deal; ask your notary to confirm how BPHTB treats the structure you are actually using.

## Who pays it, and when

Convention in most transactions has the buyer pay BPHTB as part of closing costs, though this is a negotiable point between buyer and seller and should be confirmed in writing in the sale agreement rather than assumed. Payment is typically required before or as part of the deed registration process, meaning it is usually settled at closing rather than afterwards.

## Common mistakes buyers make with BPHTB

- Assuming the advertised purchase price is the full cost, without budgeting BPHTB separately
- Using an outdated or generic online rate instead of confirming the current figure with a notary
- Not checking whether their specific transaction structure (leasehold vs other arrangements) changes how the tax applies
- Leaving BPHTB calculation until the last minute instead of budgeting it from the outset
- Confusing BPHTB (one-off) with PBB (annual), and under-budgeting for one or the other

## A simple checklist before you commit

1. Ask your notary for a written estimate of BPHTB specific to your transaction structure and value
2. Confirm who is responsible for paying it in the sale agreement
3. Budget it as part of total purchase costs, alongside notary fees and registration
4. Separately confirm the ongoing PBB rate you will owe annually once you own the property
5. Keep the calculation and payment confirmation as part of your transaction records

## What goes wrong when BPHTB is not budgeted properly

- **A verbal figure from an agent quoted as if it were confirmed.** Agents are not always tax professionals, and a rounded percentage passed on informally can be wrong for your specific structure or out of date.
- **Assuming leasehold and freehold-equivalent purchases are taxed identically**, then discovering at closing that the actual calculation basis for your transaction differs from what you budgeted.
- **Leaving the BPHTB conversation until the sale agreement is already being drafted**, when there is little room left to adjust the offer price if the tax turns out higher than expected.
- **Not confirming who pays it in writing.** Buyer-pays is conventional but not universal, and an unclear agreement here can turn into a dispute right at closing, the worst possible time.

## A simple framework for budgeting it correctly

1. Ask your notary, early in the process, for a written BPHTB estimate specific to your exact transaction structure, not a general figure for "buying in Bali."
2. Confirm whether that estimate is based on the transaction price or an officially assessed value, since these can differ, and ask which one governs your case.
3. Get the payment timeline in writing, so you know exactly when the funds need to be available relative to closing.
4. Keep the written estimate and the eventual payment confirmation together with your other transaction records, both for your own accounting and for a future resale.

## How this compares across a leasehold assignment and a resale

A first purchase of a leasehold villa and a later resale of that same leasehold to another buyer are not automatically treated the same way for tax purposes, since a lease assignment can sit differently under the regulations than an original transaction. If you are buying a villa that has already changed leasehold hands once or twice, ask your notary specifically how this transaction's history affects the BPHTB calculation, rather than assuming the figure will match a first-sale scenario.

## FAQ

### Is BPHTB the same as annual property tax in Bali?
No. BPHTB is a one-off tax paid when property rights transfer. PBB (Pajak Bumi dan Bangunan) is a separate, ongoing annual tax owners pay for as long as they hold the property. Both exist, and budgeting for only one is a common early mistake.

### Can I use an online bali property tax calculator to estimate BPHTB?
Treat online calculators as a rough starting point only. Confirm the current rate, threshold and calculation basis with your notary for your specific transaction, since figures change and vary by structure.

### Does BPHTB apply if I am buying on leasehold rather than freehold?
It can, but how it applies may differ from a full ownership transfer. Ask your notary specifically how BPHTB treats the leasehold or other structure you are using, since this is not always identical to a standard freehold-equivalent transaction.

### Who is responsible for paying BPHTB, buyer or seller?
By common convention the buyer pays it, but this is negotiable and should be written into the sale agreement rather than assumed. Confirm it explicitly before signing.

### When exactly is BPHTB paid during the transaction?
It is typically settled around closing, as part of or before the deed registration process. Your notary should give you a clear payment timeline as part of the transaction schedule.
