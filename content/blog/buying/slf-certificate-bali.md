SLF stands for Sertifikat Laik Fungsi, or "certificate of function worthiness." It is issued after a building inspection confirms the finished structure was built according to its approved building permit (PBG) and is structurally sound and safe to use. For a villa, that means the local government has checked the building against its plans, not just approved the plans on paper.

Buyers searching for an slf certificate bali guide are usually trying to work out whether a specific villa has one, and if it does not, what that actually means for them. In short: no SLF does not automatically mean the building is unsafe, but it does mean nobody has independently verified that it matches what was approved, and that gap becomes your problem the moment you own it.

## Why the SLF exists as a separate step from the building permit

Indonesia's permit system splits the process into two stages. First comes the PBG (Persetujuan Bangunan Gedung, the building approval), granted before construction starts, based on architectural and structural drawings. Second comes the SLF, granted after construction finishes, based on an actual inspection of what got built.

This two-stage structure exists because plans and reality diverge. A villa can be approved on paper and then built with changes: an extra room, a different roofline, a pool moved closer to the boundary than drawn. The SLF is the check that catches this. Skipping it means nobody has confirmed the as-built villa matches what was legally approved.

## What the inspection actually looks at

| Area checked | What it confirms |
|---|---|
| Structural elements | Foundation, columns, beams built to code |
| Fire and electrical safety | Wiring, extinguishers, exits where required |
| Match to approved plans (PBG) | Built footprint and layout match what was permitted |
| Site and setback compliance | Building sits within approved boundaries and heights |
| General occupancy readiness | Water, sanitation and access are functional |

If any of these fail, the SLF is not issued until the owner fixes the problem or applies for a revised permit that matches what was actually built.

## What happens if a villa has no SLF

A villa without an SLF is not automatically illegal to own, but it sits in a grey zone: the local authority has not confirmed it is safe and compliant, which matters in three practical situations. First, if you ever want to formalise a rental business through the building, the absence of an SLF can block licensing. Second, if you resell, a buyer's lawyer will flag it and likely negotiate the price down or ask you to obtain it before closing. Third, if there is ever a safety incident, the absence of a valid SLF weakens your position.

## How to check whether a villa already has one

1. Ask the seller or agent directly for a copy of the SLF certificate, not just a verbal confirmation
2. Cross-check the SLF's building description (floor area, function, address) against the actual villa
3. Ask your notary or PPAT to verify the certificate against local government records rather than taking a photocopy at face value
4. If there is no SLF, ask specifically why: newly built and pending, older building that predates current rules, or genuinely never applied for
5. Get a written answer on who will obtain or renew the SLF and by when, before you sign anything binding

## Getting an SLF for a villa that does not have one

If you are buying a villa without an SLF and still want to proceed, the practical path is to make obtaining it a condition of the sale, or to negotiate the price to reflect the cost and time of getting it yourself after purchase. Getting one after the fact means engaging a licensed inspector or consultant to assess the building against the approved PBG, correcting any mismatches, and submitting the application to the local authority. This is not usually fast, and it is not free, so budget both time and cost into your decision rather than treating it as a formality.

For villas being newly built, the SLF should follow naturally once construction finishes and passes inspection. Ask your builder or developer to confirm SLF is included as a deliverable of the build contract, not an afterthought left to you.

## How this interacts with other permits

The SLF sits downstream of two other approvals worth understanding together: the PKKPR, which confirms the land use is legally permitted for the intended function before anything is built, and the PBG, which approves the building design itself. If either of those is missing or was obtained incorrectly, the SLF process will likely surface the problem, because inspectors check against the approved PBG. ## What goes wrong in practice

- **A photocopy accepted without verification.** A scanned SLF looks convincing on a phone screen; it tells you nothing about whether it is genuine, current, or matches the actual building until your notary checks it against local records.
- **An SLF for a different footprint.** Villas get extended after the SLF inspection, an extra bedroom, an enclosed terrace, a second-floor addition. The certificate on file may cover the original structure only, leaving the extension unaccounted for.
- **"Pending" treated as "in hand."** A newly built villa mid-application is a different risk profile from one with a certificate already issued. If a seller says the SLF is "in process," ask for the application reference and a realistic timeline, not just a verbal assurance it is coming.
- **No SLF discovered only at resale.** Some owners never chase the certificate because nothing forced the issue during years of quiet ownership, then discover the gap when a buyer's due diligence surfaces it and the deal stalls while it gets resolved.

## A simple framework for deciding what to accept

| What you find | How to treat it |
|---|---|
| Valid SLF, verified against local records, matches current structure | Standard due diligence item, no special adjustment needed |
| SLF exists but does not cover a later extension | Get the extension regularised or priced into the offer before completing |
| No SLF, newly built, application genuinely in progress | Reasonable to proceed if the seller commits in writing to delivering it, with a deadline and a consequence if they do not |
| No SLF, older building, no application started, vague explanation | Treat as a real cost and delay to budget for yourself, and negotiate the price accordingly, or walk away |

## FAQ

### Is an SLF the same as a building permit?
No. The PBG (building permit) is approval granted before construction starts, based on drawings. The SLF is granted after construction, based on an actual inspection confirming the finished building matches those approved drawings and is safe to occupy. They are sequential, not interchangeable.

### Can I buy a villa in Bali without an SLF?
You can, but treat it as a negotiating point rather than ignore it. Ask why it is missing, get a written plan and cost estimate for obtaining it, and consider whether the price reflects that extra work. A villa with a clean, verified SLF removes this uncertainty entirely.

### Does the SLF expire?
Some jurisdictions require periodic renewal or re-inspection of an SLF, particularly for commercial-use buildings. The exact renewal cycle for villas in Bali should be confirmed with a local notary or licensed consultant, since rules can vary by area and building function (as of 2026, verify).

### Who is responsible for getting the SLF, buyer or seller?
This is negotiable and should be written into the sale agreement. Many sellers obtain it before listing to make the property easier to sell; where it is missing, buyers often negotiate either a price reduction or a completion condition requiring the seller to obtain it before closing.

### Does a villa without an SLF affect my ability to rent it out?
It can. Formal short-term rental licensing in many areas expects a valid SLF as part of the compliance package. Even where enforcement varies, operating without one adds risk that a management company or notary should walk you through before you commit to renting the villa out.
