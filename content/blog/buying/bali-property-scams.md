Bali property scams tend to follow a small number of recurring patterns: sellers offering a leasehold or ownership interest they do not actually hold, agents pressuring buyers to pay a deposit before any documentation is verified, and paperwork that looks official but has not been checked against the actual land certificate. Recognising the pattern matters more than memorising a list of specific incidents, because new variations appear regularly while the underlying trick, skip verification, move fast, stays the same.

We manage 20 villas for owners in Bali and go through documentation checks as a matter of course on every transaction we touch. This guide sets out the patterns worth watching for and, more importantly, the specific checks that neutralise almost all of them regardless of how the scam is dressed up.

## Pattern 1: pressure to pay before verification

The clearest warning sign across almost every property scam is pressure to move fast, pay a deposit today, secure the price before it changes, before you have independently verified the land certificate, leasehold agreement and the seller's actual right to sell. A legitimate seller has no reason to object to a short delay for proper checks.

## Pattern 2: a seller who does not actually hold clear rights

Some scams involve a "seller" offering rights they do not fully hold, an expired or disputed lease, a family member selling without full agreement from co-owners, or a property still tied up in a dispute. This is why checking the land certificate and leasehold agreement directly, not just trusting a printed copy shown by the seller, is essential.

| Warning sign | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Urgency and pressure to pay quickly | Designed to prevent you from verifying documentation properly |
| Reluctance to let you use an independent notary | Seller may not want independent scrutiny of the paperwork |
| Price significantly below comparable villas | Can be genuine distress sale, but also a common scam lure |
| Documents that cannot be independently verified | Certificate or permit details that do not match official records |
| Requests to pay outside a proper escrow or documented process | Removes your recourse if something goes wrong |

## Pattern 3: fake or manipulated permits

A villa presented as fully permitted, with PKKPR, PBG and SLF in place, should have those documents checkable against official records, not just a printed copy handed over during a viewing. Verifying permit status independently, rather than taking a seller's word, is one of the highest-value checks you can make.

## How escrow and proper payment structure protect you

A structured, documented deposit and payment process, ideally with funds held in escrow rather than paid directly to a seller before transfer, is one of the strongest practical defences against scams, because it removes the seller's ability to take payment without completing their side of the deal. Our guide on escrow and deposits when buying property in Bali covers how to set this up properly.

## A verification checklist before you pay anything

1. Verify the land certificate and leasehold agreement independently, not from copies supplied by the seller alone
2. Confirm the seller's legal right to sell, including any co-owner or heir consents needed
3. Check PKKPR, PBG and SLF permit status against official channels, not just documents shown to you
4. Use a proper notary and, where appropriate, independent legal advice not selected by the seller
5. Insist on a documented, escrow-protected payment process rather than direct payment before transfer

## Does using a notary guarantee safety?

A notary's involvement is an important safeguard, but it is not an automatic guarantee; the strength of the protection depends on the notary genuinely verifying documentation independently rather than simply processing paperwork the seller supplies. Choosing a notary independent of the seller, and asking direct questions about what has actually been checked, matters. ## What if something feels rushed or opaque?

Slow down. A legitimate deal can withstand a short delay for proper verification; a scam usually cannot, because the pressure to move fast is often the mechanism the scam depends on. Walking away from a deal that resists normal verification is rarely the wrong call.

## What to do next

Treat pressure to move quickly and reluctance to allow independent verification as the two clearest warning signs, and insist on checking the land certificate, leasehold agreement and permits independently before paying anything.

## FAQ

### What is the most common Bali property scam?
Pressure to pay a deposit before documentation, the land certificate, leasehold agreement and permits, has been independently verified is the most common pattern, because it removes the buyer's chance to catch a problem before money changes hands.

### How do I verify a leasehold or land certificate is genuine?
Verification should happen through an independent notary checking against official records, not by relying on copies the seller provides. If a seller resists this step or tries to rush past it, treat that as a serious warning sign.

### Is a low asking price always a scam sign?
Not always; genuine distress sales exist. But a price well below comparable villas combined with pressure to move fast and reluctance to allow independent verification is a common scam pattern worth treating with real caution.

### Should I ever pay a deposit before permits are confirmed?
Generally no. A properly structured deal allows verification of key documentation, or at minimum a clear, binding conditional agreement, before any significant payment changes hands. Escrow arrangements exist specifically to protect this sequence.

### Can a notary be involved in a scam?
It is uncommon but not impossible, particularly if the notary is chosen and controlled by the seller rather than independent. Using a notary you have selected yourself, and asking specifically what has been verified, reduces this risk significantly.
