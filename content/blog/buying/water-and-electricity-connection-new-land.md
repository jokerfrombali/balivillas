Water and electricity connection for a Bali plot depends on proximity to existing infrastructure, and the two utilities work quite differently: electricity generally comes from the state grid (PLN) and connection cost and timeline scale mainly with distance from the nearest existing line, while water access varies much more by area — some plots connect to municipal (PDAM) supply, others rely primarily on a drilled well, and many villas use a mix of both. Neither should be assumed available or cheap to add without checking the specific plot.

This is a step buyers sometimes leave until after the land purchase, treating it as a construction-phase detail. It's better handled during due diligence, because a plot far from existing infrastructure can add meaningful, sometimes substantial, cost and delay to a build that wasn't budgeted for.

## Electricity connection

PLN (the state electricity utility) connection cost and speed depend heavily on how far the plot is from an existing distribution line or transformer. A plot on or near an established road with existing villas nearby usually connects relatively straightforwardly; a plot in a less developed area may require PLN to extend infrastructure, which adds both cost (sometimes borne partly by the buyer) and time to the process. Ask specifically, before buying, how far the nearest PLN connection point is and whether any neighbouring properties are already connected, since that's a practical indicator of how straightforward your own connection will be.

## Water: PDAM, wells, or both

Municipal water supply (PDAM) coverage varies significantly across Bali and isn't guaranteed even in developed-looking areas. Many villas, particularly in less central locations, rely primarily on a drilled well with a pump and storage tank system, sometimes supplemented by PDAM or bottled water for drinking. A well requires its own permit process and, depending on the area and water table, may face restrictions related to groundwater extraction rules aimed at preventing over-extraction in high-demand coastal areas. Ask specifically whether the plot has, or can obtain, a groundwater extraction permit, and what the local water table and quality are like in that specific area. (as of 2026, verify)

## A comparison of utility connection scenarios

| Scenario | Typical cost/time impact | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Plot near existing infrastructure, neighbours already connected | Lower cost, faster connection | Confirm distance to nearest PLN line and PDAM main |
| Plot in a developing area, infrastructure nearby but not on-site | Moderate added cost/time | Ask PLN/PDAM directly what extension would involve |
| Remote plot, no nearby infrastructure | Higher cost, longer timeline, possible reliance on well/generator backup | Get a written estimate before finalising land purchase |
| Groundwater-restricted coastal zone | May require permit or alternative water source | Confirm current extraction rules for the specific area |

## Backup systems are common, not exceptional

Even well-connected villas in Bali commonly install backup systems: a generator for power outages, and a water storage tank sized for at least a day or two of usage in case of supply interruption. This isn't a sign of poor infrastructure specifically, it's standard practice across the island given that both grid power and municipal water can have periodic interruptions. Budget for this as a normal part of a build, not as a contingency for a problem plot.

## Questions worth asking before buying land specifically for utility feasibility

- How far is the plot from the nearest PLN distribution line, and are neighbouring plots already connected?
- Is PDAM water available in this area, or is a well the primary source?
- If a well is needed, what does the local water table and quality look like, and is a groundwater extraction permit required?
- What's a realistic estimated cost and timeline for full utility connection, in writing from the relevant providers or an experienced local contractor?
- Does the plot's access route support running utility lines, or would a separate easement be needed?

## Budgeting for utility connection

Treat utility connection as a distinct line item in your land-and-build budget, separate from construction itself, and get a written estimate specific to the plot rather than relying on general area assumptions. For plots with straightforward, nearby infrastructure this can be a modest, predictable cost; for remote plots it can be a genuinely significant factor in whether the land is a sound purchase at the asking price.

## FAQ

### Is every plot of land in Bali connected to water and electricity?

No, undeveloped land is not automatically connected, and availability varies significantly by area and proximity to existing infrastructure. Always check the specific plot's distance to the nearest PLN line and whether PDAM water is available, rather than assuming based on the general area's reputation.

### Do most villas in Bali use well water or municipal water?

It varies by area; many villas, particularly outside the most central locations, rely primarily on a drilled well, sometimes supplemented by municipal (PDAM) supply where available. Well water requires its own permit process, and some coastal areas have groundwater extraction restrictions worth checking before assuming a well is a straightforward solution. (as of 2026, verify)

### How much does it cost to connect electricity to a remote plot in Bali?

There's no fixed figure; cost depends heavily on the plot's distance from the nearest existing PLN distribution line or transformer, and can range from a modest standard connection fee to a substantial cost if PLN infrastructure needs extending. Get a written estimate specific to the plot before finalising a land purchase.

### Should I plan for a generator and water storage even on a well-connected plot?

Yes, this is standard practice across Bali given that both grid power and municipal water can experience periodic interruptions. A generator for backup power and a water storage tank sized for at least a day or two of usage are common, sensible additions regardless of how well-connected the plot is.

### When should I check utility feasibility relative to buying the land?

Before finalising the purchase, ideally during the same due diligence phase as title and access checks. Utility connection cost and feasibility should factor into your assessment of whether the asking price for a specific plot is reasonable, not be left as a surprise for the construction phase.
