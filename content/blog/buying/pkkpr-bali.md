A PKKPR, Persetujuan Kesesuaian Kegiatan Pemanfaatan Ruang, is the official Indonesian government confirmation that a proposed land use is compatible with a specific plot's zoning. It is issued through the national OSS system and is generally the first formal document a buyer or developer needs before applying for a building permit (PBG). Skipping it, or assuming it is a formality, is a common and costly mistake.

This guide explains what a PKKPR actually is, why it comes before other permits, and how the process generally works.

## What a PKKPR actually confirms

The PKKPR answers one specific question: does the zoning on this plot allow the use you intend, whether that is residential, tourism, mixed-use or something else. It does not confirm ownership, and it does not grant permission to build; it is a compatibility check that other permits, including the building permit itself, rely on as a prerequisite. Our guide on [Bali zoning explained](/blog/buying/bali-zoning-explained/) covers the underlying zoning categories the PKKPR is checked against.

## Why the order of permits matters

Applying for a building permit (PBG) without a confirmed PKKPR is generally not possible, or at minimum creates unnecessary risk and delay, since the building permit process assumes zoning compatibility has already been established. Our guide on the [PBG permit process](/blog/buying/pbg-permit-bali/) explains that next step and how it depends on the PKKPR being in place first.

## The general process

1. Confirm the plot's exact certificate and coordinate details
2. Define your intended use precisely, residential, tourism/villa rental, or another category
3. Submit through the OSS system, typically via a notary or licensed land consultant
4. Receive the PKKPR result confirming compatibility, or identifying a mismatch
5. If compatible, proceed to building permit (PBG) and later occupancy certificate (SLF) applications

## What a PKKPR result tells you as a buyer

| Result | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Compatible with intended use | You can proceed toward a building permit application with confidence |
| Compatible with a different use than planned | Reassess your project or look for a different plot |
| Not compatible | Do not proceed on the current plan; a zoning change is uncertain and not something to rely on |

## Common mistakes buyers make around PKKPR

- Relying on a seller's claim that "the zoning is fine" without a formal PKKPR check
- Assuming a PKKPR obtained for one use, say residential, automatically covers a different one, such as tourism rental
- Starting construction before a PKKPR and building permit are both confirmed
- Treating an in-progress PKKPR application as equivalent to an approved one when negotiating a purchase price

## What happens if you buy land without checking first

Buyers who skip the PKKPR step and proceed straight to construction, or who buy based on an assumption that "everything nearby is zoned the same," sometimes discover the mismatch only once they try to formalise a building permit or register a rental business. At that point, options are limited and generally worse than if the check had been done before purchase: pursuing a formal zoning change with no guarantee of success, redesigning the project to fit what is actually allowed, or accepting a loss on land that cannot be used as planned. All of this is avoidable with a check that typically costs very little relative to the purchase itself.

## Who typically handles the PKKPR application for you

Most foreign buyers are not set up to navigate the OSS system directly, particularly given language and procedural familiarity requirements, so this is usually handled by a notary, a licensed land consultant, or in some cases a developer's own legal team on a buyer's behalf. If a developer is handling it for you, it is still worth having your own independent notary review the result rather than relying solely on the developer's confirmation that "it is fine."

A PKKPR check is one part of a broader due diligence process that should also confirm the land certificate type and, ideally, review any existing structures for permit compliance. ## What to do next

Treat a confirmed PKKPR as a prerequisite, not a formality, before committing to a purchase built around a specific intended use, and never rely on a verbal assurance from a seller in place of the formal result.

## FAQ

### What does PKKPR stand for and what does it do?
PKKPR stands for Persetujuan Kesesuaian Kegiatan Pemanfaatan Ruang, and it confirms whether a proposed land use is compatible with a specific plot's zoning. It is generally the first formal permit step before a building permit application.

### Do I need a PKKPR before applying for a building permit in Bali?
Yes, generally the PKKPR needs to be confirmed first, since the building permit (PBG) process assumes zoning compatibility has already been established through the PKKPR.

### How long does a PKKPR application take?
The current timeline should be confirmed with a notary or licensed consultant, since OSS processing times can vary and are periodically updated (as of 2026, verify).

### Can a PKKPR be denied?
Yes, if the plot's zoning does not support your intended use. In that case you would need to either change your plan to match what is allowed, seek a formal zoning change, which is uncertain, or consider a different plot.

### Is a PKKPR the same thing as a building permit?
No. The PKKPR confirms zoning compatibility only; the building permit (PBG) is a separate, later application that depends on the PKKPR being confirmed first.
