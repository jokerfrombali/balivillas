Checking a Bali property developer before buying off-plan means going beyond their marketing materials and sales gallery: visiting a completed, occupied project they built previously, speaking directly with buyers who purchased from them before, and independently verifying the legal and financial basics rather than taking the developer's word for them. This is the single most effective thing an off-plan buyer can do to reduce risk, more effective than reviewing contract terms alone.

This guide walks through exactly how to do it.

## Start with their completed track record

A developer's sales gallery and renders tell you almost nothing about construction quality or reliability; their previous completed projects tell you a great deal. Visit at least one finished, ideally occupied, project they built, and assess build quality directly, finishes, structural details, how the property has aged if it has been standing a few years. If a developer has no completed projects to show you, that alone is a significant risk signal worth weighing heavily.

## Talk to previous buyers directly

Ask the developer to connect you with previous buyers, and independently seek out others if possible through owner communities or online searches. Ask specifically about whether the completed product matched what was marketed, whether the timeline held, and how disputes or issues were handled during and after construction.

## Verify the legal and financial basics independently

Do not rely on the developer's own legal team for your due diligence; use your own notary or property lawyer to confirm the land's certificate, zoning and permit status independently, and to review the sale and purchase agreement before signing. Our guide on [Bali property due diligence checklist](/blog/buying/bali-property-due-diligence-checklist/) covers this side in more depth, and it applies whether you are buying a completed villa or an off-plan project from this developer.

## What to check, organised

| Area to check | How to check it |
|---|---|
| Build quality | Visit a completed, occupied prior project in person |
| Reliability | Speak directly with previous buyers |
| Legal standing of the land | Independent verification by your own notary, not the developer's team |
| Financial stability | Ask about project financing structure; be cautious of vague or evasive answers |
| Contract terms | Independent legal review, particularly of payment milestones and delay clauses |

## Warning signs worth taking seriously

- No completed, inspectable prior projects, only renders and plans
- Reluctance to connect you with previous buyers, or no previous buyers who can be found independently
- Payment terms requesting a large upfront sum not tied to construction milestones
- Vague or inconsistent answers about the land's certificate or zoning status
- Pressure to sign quickly before you have completed independent checks

## Questions worth asking in person, not over email

Some of the most useful signals come from how a developer responds in person rather than what their brochure says. Ask how they handled a previous project that ran into a real problem, a delay, a cost overrun, a design change, and watch whether the answer is specific and honest or vague and deflective. Ask who actually owns the land the project sits on, since some developments are built by a marketing or sales entity that does not itself hold the underlying land rights, which adds another layer worth understanding clearly before you commit any money. A developer confident in their own project should have no difficulty answering both questions directly.

## Why this due diligence effort is worth it even under time pressure

Off-plan sales often come with pressure to decide quickly, early pricing tiers, limited unit availability, a sense that waiting means losing the best terms. That pressure is real, but it should never be allowed to compress the due diligence steps above. A few extra days spent visiting a completed project and speaking to previous buyers is a small cost against the risk of committing significant capital to a developer who cannot deliver, and a genuinely good developer will not be put off by a buyer taking the time to check properly.

## FAQ

### What is the single most useful check before buying from a Bali developer?
Visiting a completed, ideally occupied, project the developer has already built. It tells you more about real build quality and reliability than any amount of marketing material or contract review alone.

### Should I use the developer's recommended notary or lawyer?
No, use your own independent notary or property lawyer for due diligence and contract review. A notary recommended exclusively by the developer has an inherent conflict of interest in your favour, not the buyer's.

### What payment structure is safest when buying from a developer?
Payments tied to verified construction milestones are safer than a large upfront sum or a fixed calendar disconnected from actual progress. This is one of the clearest protections available to an off-plan buyer.

### How do I find previous buyers from a specific Bali developer to talk to?
Ask the developer directly for references, and independently search owner communities, expat forums and social media groups for the specific development's name to find buyers outside the developer's own referrals.

### What if a developer has no completed projects yet?
Treat this as a significant risk factor. Without a track record to inspect, you are relying almost entirely on the developer's own claims, which meaningfully increases the risk profile of the purchase.
