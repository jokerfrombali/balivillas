Hak Milik is Indonesia's freehold land title: full, unlimited-term ownership, held only by Indonesian citizens and certain Indonesian entities. Foreign buyers cannot register Hak Milik in their own name, which is why almost every villa purchase involving a foreign buyer runs through leasehold, Hak Pakai or a PT PMA company structure instead of this title directly.

Understanding Hak Milik still matters even though you cannot hold it, because most land in Bali that gets leased or converted to Hak Pakai for a foreign buyer starts life as Hak Milik owned by an Indonesian individual or family. Knowing how that underlying title works helps you assess what you are actually leasing or converting from.

## What Hak Milik gives an Indonesian owner

Hak Milik has no fixed term and passes through inheritance the way freehold ownership does in most countries. It is the title category land offices and notaries treat as the cleanest and most valuable, since it carries none of the term limits or conditions attached to Hak Guna Bangunan or Hak Pakai. When you see a villa listing describe the underlying land as "freehold," it almost always means the Indonesian landowner holds Hak Milik, and the foreign buyer's actual purchased right sits on top of it as a lease or a converted Hak Pakai.

## How Hak Milik interacts with a foreign buyer's purchase

Two common structures put a foreign buyer in a relationship with Hak Milik land without holding it themselves. First, a lease agreement: the Indonesian owner keeps Hak Milik and grants the foreign buyer a long-term right to use the land and building, which is a contractual arrangement, not a title transfer. Second, in specific circumstances, Hak Milik land can be converted to Hak Pakai when transferred to an eligible foreign individual, meaning the title category itself changes at the point of sale rather than staying Hak Milik with a lease layered on top.

| Structure | What happens to the Hak Milik title | What the foreign buyer holds |
|---|---|---|
| Leasehold purchase | Stays Hak Milik with the Indonesian owner | Contractual lease right for the term |
| Hak Pakai conversion | Converts from Hak Milik to Hak Pakai at transfer | Registered Hak Pakai title |
| PT PMA purchase | Typically converts to Hak Guna Bangunan for the company | Company-held HGB right to build |

## Why the underlying title matters for due diligence

When you check a villa's certificate as part of due diligence, confirming whether the underlying land is genuinely Hak Milik, and that the person selling or leasing to you is the registered owner, is one of the most important checks in the whole transaction. Land disputes involving unclear or contested Hak Milik ownership are a real risk in Bali, particularly on land that has passed through several family members without formal registration being updated each time. Our due diligence checklist walks through how to verify this properly.

- Request a certified copy of the certificate directly from the land office, not just from the seller
- Confirm the name on the certificate matches the person you are transacting with
- Check for any existing liens, disputes or unregistered family claims
- Verify the certificate boundaries match the physical plot, ideally with a survey
- Ask how long the current owner has held Hak Milik and how they acquired it

## What happens if a foreign buyer is offered a Hak Milik "workaround"

Occasionally a seller or agent proposes holding Hak Milik through an Indonesian nominee on the foreign buyer's behalf, backed by a private agreement giving the foreign party effective control. This is not a legally recognised ownership structure for foreigners, and Indonesian courts have not reliably enforced the side agreements that are meant to protect the foreign party if the nominee arrangement breaks down. Our guide on nominee agreement risk in Bali sets out exactly what can go wrong and why it is worth avoiding regardless of how the arrangement is framed.

## What Hak Milik means for villa value and resale

Because Hak Milik land carries no term limit, villas built on land the seller holds as Hak Milik, and which you access through leasehold or Hak Pakai conversion, tend to be viewed as more stable long-term investments than land already sitting on a shorter Hak Guna Bangunan title with a countdown running. This is one factor, among several including location and remaining lease years, that shapes how a villa is priced.

## What to do next

Hak Milik itself is not something a foreign buyer will hold, but understanding it clarifies what you are actually leasing or converting into when you buy a Bali villa. The practical task is verifying the underlying title is genuine and unencumbered before you commit to a lease or a Hak Pakai purchase built on top of it.

## FAQ

### Can a foreigner ever hold Hak Milik in Bali?

No, not under current Indonesian law. Hak Milik is reserved for Indonesian citizens and certain Indonesian legal entities. Foreign buyers access Hak Milik land indirectly, either through a lease agreement with the Indonesian owner who retains Hak Milik, or through conversion to Hak Pakai at the point of transfer, in eligible cases.

### What happens to Hak Milik land when it is leased to a foreigner?

The Indonesian owner retains Hak Milik title throughout the lease term. The foreign buyer holds a separate, notarised contractual right to use the land and building, which sits alongside the unchanged Hak Milik certificate rather than replacing it. This is why the lease contract's own wording, not the underlying title, is what protects the foreign buyer.

### Is Hak Milik land more valuable than Hak Guna Bangunan land?

Generally, yes, because Hak Milik has no term limit while Hak Guna Bangunan carries a fixed term requiring renewal. Villas on land ultimately held as Hak Milik, whether leased or converted to Hak Pakai for the buyer, are often seen as more stable long-term than those on land already sitting on a shorter, ageing HGB term.

### How do I verify a seller actually holds Hak Milik?

Request a certified copy of the certificate directly from the land office rather than relying on a photocopy from the seller, confirm the registered name matches who you are dealing with, and check for existing liens or disputes. An independent notary or lawyer should carry out this verification as a standard part of due diligence before you sign anything.

### Does Hak Milik pass by inheritance?

Yes, Hak Milik functions like freehold ownership in this respect and passes through Indonesian inheritance law to the owner's heirs. This is relevant to foreign buyers mainly when assessing a seller's title history, since land that has changed hands through inheritance without formal registration updates at each step can carry unresolved ownership questions worth checking carefully.
