Choosing a builder in Bali for your own villa project means checking their completed work in person, understanding how they structure contracts and payments, and confirming they have handled a project of similar scale before. This is a distinct decision from choosing a developer for an off-plan purchase; here you are hiring a contractor directly to build to your own architect's design, which puts more of the oversight responsibility on you.

This guide covers what to check before signing with a builder.

## Start with completed, similar-scale projects

Ask any builder you are considering to show you at least two or three completed projects of a similar scale and style to yours, and visit them in person where possible. A builder experienced with small renovations may not be the right fit for a full villa build, and vice versa. Assess not just the finished look but structural details, how the property has aged, and whether the owner is willing to speak with you about their experience.

## Understand how they structure contracts and payments

A clear, written contract should specify the scope of work, materials and finish specifications, a payment schedule tied to construction milestones rather than a fixed calendar, and what happens in the event of delays or disputes. Avoid builders who resist putting specifications in writing or who ask for a large proportion of payment upfront before meaningful work has started.

## Local builder versus a contractor tied to your architect

Some architects work with a preferred contractor; others expect you to source your own builder independently. Either can work, but if using your architect's recommended builder, still do your own independent checks rather than relying solely on the relationship. ## Common mistakes to avoid

- Hiring based on price alone without checking completed work
- Paying a large sum upfront before meaningful construction progress
- Skipping a written contract in favour of a verbal or informal agreement
- Not budgeting a contingency for the delays and adjustments that are common in Bali construction
- Assuming your architect's recommended builder needs no independent verification

## How to run the first meeting with a shortlisted builder

Treat the first meeting as an interview, not a formality. Ask direct questions about how they handle unexpected issues mid-build, what their typical timeline slippage looks like on past projects, and how they communicate progress, weekly photos, a site visit schedule, a project manager you can reach directly. A builder who answers these clearly and specifically, rather than with vague reassurance, is generally a better sign than one who focuses only on price. It is also worth asking who will actually be on site day to day, since larger builders sometimes hand smaller projects to a junior team once the contract is signed.

## Working with an overseas timeline

If you are not based in Bali for the build, agree upfront on how updates will be shared and how decisions that need your input, a material substitution, a design tweak, will be communicated and approved. Builders used to working with overseas owners usually have an established process for this; builders who have only worked with local, on-site clients may need more explicit expectations set from the start. Consider whether you want an independent third party, such as an architect or project supervisor, to inspect progress on your behalf if you cannot visit regularly yourself.

## FAQ

### How do I verify a builder's quality before hiring them in Bali?
Visit completed projects of a similar scale in person, assess the finish and structural details directly, and speak with the owners about their experience with that builder if possible.

### What payment structure is safest when hiring a builder?
Payments tied to verified construction milestones, not a large upfront sum or a fixed calendar disconnected from actual progress. This is the clearest protection against paying ahead of real work done.

### Should I use my architect's recommended builder?
It can work well, but still do your own independent checks on their completed work and contract terms rather than relying solely on the architect's recommendation.

### What is the biggest mistake people make when choosing a builder in Bali?
Choosing based on the lowest quote without checking completed work first. A cheap quote from an inexperienced or unreliable builder often ends up costing more in delays, disputes or poor-quality construction.

### Do I need a written contract with a builder in Bali?
Yes, always. A written contract specifying scope, materials, payment milestones and how disputes are handled protects both sides and gives you real recourse if something goes wrong.
