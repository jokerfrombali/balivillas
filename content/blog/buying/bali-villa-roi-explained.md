Return on investment for a Bali villa is, in its simplest form, net annual income divided by total investment, expressed as a percentage. The honest version of that calculation uses net income after every real running cost — management commission, staff, maintenance, utilities, taxes and a realistic vacancy allowance — and total investment that includes furnishing and setup, not just the purchase price. The version circulating in much marketing material online skips several of those deductions, which is why headline ROI figures often look considerably better than what an owner actually experiences.

We manage villas ourselves and see real cost structures, which is exactly why we won't hand you a single "expect X% ROI" figure without knowing your specific property, area and cost base. What we can do is walk through the calculation properly so you can build your own honest number for any villa you're evaluating.

## The ROI formula and what belongs in each part

**Total investment:** purchase price, plus furnishing and setup, plus transaction costs (notary, legal, any applicable taxes on purchase). For a build rather than a purchase, this also includes land, design, permits and construction costs.

**Net annual income:** gross rental income at realistic (not peak-season) occupancy, minus management commission, staff costs, maintenance, utilities, insurance, and property/rental taxes.

**ROI = Net annual income ÷ Total investment × 100.**

This is a straightforward calculation once the inputs are honest; the difficulty is almost always in getting realistic numbers for gross income and running costs, not in the maths itself.

## Where ROI figures typically get inflated

| Inflation source | Effect on the headline number |
|---|---|
| Using peak-season nightly rate as the year-round average | Overstates gross income significantly |
| Assuming near-full occupancy | Overstates gross income, especially outside high season |
| Excluding management commission from the deduction | Overstates net income |
| Excluding maintenance, especially on an ageing property | Overstates net income, understates future costs |
| Excluding vacancy/downtime for cleaning and turnover | Overstates achievable bookings |
| Excluding taxes | Overstates net income |

Any one of these on its own can shift the ROI figure meaningfully; several together can turn a genuinely modest, realistic ROI into a headline number that looks considerably more attractive than what an owner actually receives.

## Costs that are easy to forget

Beyond the obvious running costs, a few categories are commonly underestimated: periodic larger maintenance (roof, pool resurfacing, repainting exteriors — see our guide on [best building materials for Bali's climate](/blog/buying/building-materials-bali-climate/) for how material choices affect this over time), furniture and linen replacement cycles, and the setup cost of getting a villa genuinely rental-ready (professional photography, initial marketing, listing setup) rather than just liveable.

## ROI versus payback period

ROI is an annualised percentage; payback period is the time it takes to recoup the initial investment. They're related but answer slightly different questions — ROI is more useful for comparing between different properties or investment options, while payback period is more intuitive for understanding when you'd start seeing net positive returns. ## Building a realistic ROI estimate for a specific villa

1. Ask for actual historical gross income data, not a projection, if the villa already has a rental track record.
2. Confirm the management commission structure and apply it to that gross figure realistically.
3. Get a written estimate of annual maintenance, staff and utility costs from the current owner or manager.
4. Confirm current tax obligations with a tax adviser rather than assuming a figure.
5. Apply a realistic vacancy allowance based on the specific area and season, not an assumption of near-full occupancy.
6. Divide the resulting net income by total investment, including furnishing and setup.

## Why an established villa is easier to evaluate honestly

A villa that's already operating and already has verifiable income history removes most of the guesswork from an ROI calculation. A new build or a villa with no rental history requires projecting both income and costs, which introduces more uncertainty into every number. ## FAQ

### What ROI should I expect from a villa in Bali?

There's no honest single figure without knowing the specific property's purchase price, running costs and realistic occupancy — any number quoted without those inputs should be treated with caution. Build your own calculation using net income (after all running costs) divided by total investment including furnishing and setup.

### Why do online ROI figures for Bali villas often look higher than reality?

They frequently use peak-season rates as a year-round average, assume near-full occupancy, and exclude costs like management commission, maintenance and taxes from the calculation. Each of these inflates the headline figure; together they can make a modest realistic ROI look considerably more attractive than it actually is.

### Does ROI improve if I self-manage instead of using a management company?

It can reduce the commission cost, but self-managing from abroad typically means either hiring your own staff directly (with similar cost) or accepting lower occupancy and guest service quality, both of which can offset the saved commission. Weigh the full picture, not just the commission line, before assuming self-management improves net ROI.

### Should I trust a seller's ROI projection for a villa I'm buying?

Ask for actual historical income and cost data rather than a projection, and verify it independently where possible. A seller's own projection has an incentive to look favourable; historical, verifiable numbers are a much sounder basis for your own calculation.

### How does villa size affect ROI?

Larger villas often earn more gross income but also carry proportionally higher staff, maintenance and utility costs, so ROI doesn't scale simply with size. Comparing net, not gross, figures across different villa sizes gives a much more accurate picture of relative investment performance.
