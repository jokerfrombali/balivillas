How much does it cost to buy a villa in Bali depends primarily on four things: the area (Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan each carry different price levels), the number of bedrooms and total build size, the leasehold term remaining if it is a leasehold sale, and the finish quality and age of the construction. Rather than quote a single misleading average, this guide walks through what actually drives the price of a specific villa, so you can evaluate any listing on its real merits.

## The four biggest drivers of villa price

| Driver | Effect |
|---|---|
| Area | Canggu and beachfront locations generally command a premium; quieter inland areas can offer more for the same budget |
| Bedroom count / size | More bedrooms and larger build size generally mean a higher price, though not always linearly |
| Leasehold term remaining | A longer remaining lease generally supports a higher price than a shorter one on an otherwise similar villa |
| Finish quality and age | Recently built or renovated villas with higher-spec finishes command more than older or basic-finish properties |

## What a realistic range looks like across villa types

Rather than a single number, it helps to think in terms of the type of villa. A smaller one-bedroom villa sits at the lower end of the market. A family-sized three or four-bedroom villa with a private pool sits in the middle range. Larger multi-bedroom resort-style villas with extensive amenities sit at the top. Within each category, area and leasehold term shift the price meaningfully. ## How villa buying cost compares to villa building cost

Buying a finished villa and building one from land are different financial paths with different risk profiles. Buying gives you a known price, an inspectable finished product, and usually a much shorter timeline to ownership. Building can offer more customisation and potentially a lower total cost if managed well, but carries construction risk, permitting time and the need for reliable oversight, particularly if you are not based in Bali. ## Costs beyond the purchase price

The headline villa price is not the whole cost of buying. Add notary and PPAT fees, BPHTB transfer tax, due diligence costs, and potentially the cost of closing any permit gaps found during due diligence. Our guide on the full cost of buying property in Bali breaks these down. Then, once you own the villa, ongoing holding costs, tax, staff, maintenance, utilities, continue every year; our guide on Bali villa holding costs covers that separately.

## How rental cost relates to purchase cost

A related but different question people often ask alongside "how much does it cost to buy" is how much it costs to rent a villa in Bali, either short-term as a guest or longer-term as a resident. Rental rates are a function of the same drivers, area, size, season and finish, quoted per night, week, month or year rather than as a purchase price. If you are weighing whether to rent long-term versus buy, comparing several months or years of typical rental cost against the full purchase and holding cost picture is a more useful exercise than comparing either figure in isolation.

## A practical checklist for evaluating a specific villa's price

1. Confirm the exact leasehold term remaining and how it affects the fair asking price
2. Ask for a land-versus-building price split if you want to compare against similar plots elsewhere
3. Get the villa's permit status (PKKPR, PBG, SLF) confirmed before treating the price as final
4. Budget the full purchase cost stack, not just the headline villa price
5. Compare only against genuinely similar villas in the same area, bedroom count and finish tier

## What to do next

Rather than anchor on a single average figure, evaluate any specific villa against the drivers above: area, size, leasehold term and condition. A villa priced attractively on the surface can be a poor deal once the leasehold term or permit status is factored in, and a slightly higher-priced villa with a longer lease and clean documentation can be the better buy.

## FAQ

### Is buying a villa in Bali cheaper than building one?
It depends on the specific project. Buying gives you a known, fixed price and immediate use; building can offer more customisation and potentially lower cost per square metre if managed well, but adds construction time, permitting and oversight risk. Compare both paths against your specific goals rather than assuming one is universally cheaper.

### Does leasehold term remaining significantly affect the price?
Yes, generally. A villa with a shorter remaining lease should command a lower price than an otherwise similar villa with a longer remaining term, since you are buying fewer years of usable rights. Always confirm the exact remaining term before comparing prices.

### How does villa purchase price compare to renting long-term?
This depends on how long you plan to stay and use the property, your view on ownership versus flexibility, and the full cost of ownership versus rental. A multi-year comparison of total rental cost against purchase plus holding costs is more useful than comparing a single month's rent against the purchase price.

### Do prices vary a lot between Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan?
Yes, each area has its own price character driven by proximity to the coast, amenity density and typical buyer demand. Comparing a villa's price only makes sense within the context of its specific area and what that area typically commands.

### What is the biggest hidden cost people forget to budget when buying a villa?
Holding costs after the purchase, staff, maintenance, tax and utilities, are the cost most first-time buyers underestimate, since they focus on the purchase price and closing costs and forget the ongoing annual commitment that follows.
