A villa for sale in Ungasan is on the Bukit, the limestone plateau south of the airport, close to clifftop viewpoints and beaches like Melasti and Green Bowl. It's a quieter, less built-up market than Canggu or Seminyak, and the villas for sale there tend to be newer builds on larger plots, often finished in a tropical-modern or eco style using local materials like teak.

The two things that matter most before you buy on the Bukit are road access and water supply, because both vary far more here than in the flatter, more established parts of south Bali. Get both checked specifically for the exact plot, not just the general area, before comparing prices with anywhere else.

## Why access and water need checking separately on the Bukit

The Bukit's limestone terrain means some roads to clifftop plots are narrow, steep or seasonally difficult, which affects everything from moving-in logistics to service vehicles and eventually resale. Walk the actual route to a specific villa, ideally at a different time than your scheduled viewing, rather than relying on a map.

Water supply on the Bukit has historically relied more on trucked water or private wells supplementing the mains network than in areas like Sanur, and this varies street by street. Ask the seller directly what the water source is, whether it's reliable through the dry season, and what the running cost looks like, since this is a genuine operating cost that doesn't show up in the sale price.

## Leasehold structure and legal checks

As with any foreign purchase in Bali, a villa for sale in Ungasan is almost always sold on long leasehold rather than freehold, since foreigners cannot hold freehold land directly. Confirm the remaining lease term, any extension clause and its actual conditions, and have an independent Indonesian lawyer, not one recommended solely by the seller, review the lease agreement before you commit to anything. ## Financing and payment structure

Foreign buyers rarely have access to conventional mortgage financing in Indonesia, so most Ungasan purchases are settled with a significant upfront payment or staged instalments tied to construction or transfer milestones. Some sellers or developers offer their own instalment arrangements; other buyers finance the purchase against assets in their home country. Whatever route you take, get the payment schedule and the conditions triggering each instalment written clearly into the sale agreement, including what happens if a milestone slips.

Prices are sometimes quoted in US dollars, sometimes in rupiah, but the transaction itself is normally settled in rupiah. If you're transferring funds internationally, factor in exchange rate movement between agreeing the price and completing the transfer, and talk to your bank or a currency specialist about the practical process for a transfer of this size.

## Running costs on the Bukit specifically

Beyond the purchase price, owning in Ungasan brings ongoing costs: land and building tax (PBB), utilities, water (which as covered above can run higher than in flatter areas if trucked water is part of the supply), pool and garden maintenance, and periodic repairs. If the villa isn't your full-time residence, you need a plan for who checks on it regularly and handles maintenance and any guest management if you intend to rent it.

Budgeting for professional oversight from the start, rather than waiting until something goes wrong to arrange help, tends to work out better for owners based overseas or without the time to manage a property remotely. ## Timing your purchase around Bukit development

The Bukit, including Ungasan, has seen steady new villa development over recent years, which means the specific street or pocket you're considering may look quite different in five years as neighbouring plots get built out. Ask about any known planned developments nearby, since construction noise, changed views or increased traffic can affect both your enjoyment of the property and its longer-term rental appeal. A quiet lane today isn't guaranteed to stay that way, and it's a reasonable question to raise with the seller and with neighbours if you get the chance.

## FAQ

### Is water supply really a concern for villas in Ungasan?

It can be, more so than in flatter areas like Sanur. Some Bukit properties rely partly on trucked water or private wells to supplement the mains supply, particularly in the dry season. Ask the seller directly about the water source for a specific villa and its running cost before you buy, rather than assuming it matches infrastructure elsewhere in Bali.

### Can foreigners own a villa in Ungasan freehold?

No, foreigners cannot hold freehold (Hak Milik) land in Indonesia directly. Villas for sale in Ungasan are typically offered on long-term leasehold, which grants use and rental rights for a fixed term, sometimes with an extension option. Confirm the exact structure of a specific listing before comparing it with others.

### How does Ungasan compare to Seminyak for buying a villa?

Ungasan is quieter and less built-up, with a clifftop setting near Melasti and Green Bowl beaches, while Seminyak is busier and more commercial with denser infrastructure. Which suits you better depends on whether you want a calmer, view-driven property or one closer to nightlife and shopping; see our [Seminyak buying guide](/blog/buying/buying-villa-in-seminyak/) for that comparison.

### What access issues should I check before buying on the Bukit?

Walk the actual road to the specific plot, ideally outside your scheduled viewing time, and ask whether the route is passable year-round including during heavy rain. Narrow or steep access can complicate moving furniture in, servicing the property and eventually reselling it, so treat this as a specific, non-negotiable check rather than a minor detail.

### Should I buy with rental income in mind or just for personal use?

Both are common reasons to buy in Ungasan, and the right due diligence differs slightly: a rental-focused buyer should ask for real booking history and understand local management options, while a personal-use buyer should weight water reliability and access more heavily.
