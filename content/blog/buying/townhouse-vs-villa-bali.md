In Bali property listings, "townhouse" almost always means an attached or semi-attached unit that shares a wall, a garden boundary or a pool with one or more neighbouring units on the same plot. "Villa" usually means a standalone building on its own land, even when it is small. The two terms get used loosely by agents, so the label on a listing matters less than what the floor plan and title documents actually show.

If you are choosing between a townhouse and a villa in Bali, the practical differences come down to four things: purchase price, privacy, what you share with neighbours, and how much control you have over the property long term. None of these make one option universally better — they just suit different buyers.

## How a Bali townhouse is actually built

Most townhouses on the market are two or three units built in a row on one plot, each with its own entrance, kitchen and bedrooms, but sharing a garden wall or a pool. Premier Umalas 16, a 3-bedroom pool townhouse, is a working example of this format: private ensuite bedrooms and a private pool within a compact footprint, built to be one of several similar units rather than a single sprawling villa.

Shared elements vary by development. Some townhouse rows share only the perimeter wall and driveway. Others, like Serenity 6.1 and 6.2 in Sanur, share the pool itself and a small garden between two neighbouring units while keeping separate entrances and fully equipped kitchens. Always ask exactly what is shared and get it confirmed in writing, because "shared pool" can mean anything from a symbolic garden wall to a pool you physically split with a stranger's booking calendar.

## How this changes ownership and cost

Townhouses cost less per square metre and less overall than a comparable standalone villa in the same area, mainly because the land cost is split across units. They also usually mean lower maintenance costs individually, since some upkeep — the shared wall, the driveway, sometimes the pool — is a joint responsibility rather than solely yours.

| | Townhouse | Standalone villa |
|---|---|---|
| Typical entry price | Lower for similar bedroom count | Higher |
| Land you control | Smaller, sometimes shared boundary | Full plot, yours alone |
| Privacy | Reduced, shares wall/pool/garden with 1–2 units | Full privacy |
| Renovation and exterior changes | Often needs neighbour or complex agreement | Your decision alone |
| Resale pool | Buyers wanting lower entry price | Buyers wanting privacy and full control |
| Maintenance of shared elements | Split cost, needs coordination | No coordination needed |

## Rental performance: does the difference show up in bookings?

Guests booking a private-pool villa in Bali are usually looking for privacy, and a townhouse with a genuinely private pool and separate entrance — like Premier Umalas 16 or 17 — rents comparably to a standalone villa of the same bedroom count, provided the listing photos and description are honest about the shared boundary. A townhouse with a pool split between two units, such as the Serenity 6.1/6.2 pairing, tends to attract a slightly more price-sensitive guest who is trading some privacy for a lower nightly rate, which can still fill well but rarely commands the top end of the market.

The practical lesson: if rental income matters to you, look closely at whether the pool and entrance are truly private before assuming a townhouse will rent the same as a standalone villa. The label alone tells you nothing reliable.

## Legal and title considerations specific to townhouses

Where several townhouse units sit on one plot, the certificate structure can be shared across the row or split per unit, similar to the multi-unit complex situation covered elsewhere on this site. A shared certificate is simpler to buy into but limits your ability to sell your unit independently later; a per-unit certificate costs more to set up but gives you a cleaner, more liquid asset.

Ask your notary directly how the townhouse row you are considering is titled, and get the answer in writing before you pay a deposit. Strata-style registration for row housing is used inconsistently across developments in Bali, so do not assume the structure matches what you have seen in other countries (as of 2026, verify the specific registration approach for any townhouse row with a local notary before relying on it).

## Who should buy which

A townhouse suits a buyer prioritising a lower entry price, simpler exterior maintenance, and who is comfortable with some shared boundary as long as the interior and pool feel private. It also suits a buyer building a small rental portfolio who wants more bedrooms for their budget by accepting a shared plot.

A standalone villa suits a buyer who wants full control over the land and building — for renovation, for expansion, for exterior design — and who is prepared to pay more for that autonomy. It also tends to hold slightly broader resale appeal, since it removes any question about shared responsibilities for a future buyer.

## Noise, privacy and the details listings don't show

A shared wall or shared garden boundary changes daily life in ways photos rarely capture. Ask specifically about noise transfer between units — pool parties, early check-outs, children in an adjoining townhouse — and whether the layout puts bedrooms against a shared wall or against the outer perimeter. In a row of two or three units, the middle unit, if there is one, usually has less exterior wall and more shared boundary than the end units, which is worth factoring into both your own comfort and how guests are likely to review the stay.

Also ask how check-in and check-out times are coordinated when a pool or driveway is shared between units. Two unrelated bookings arriving or leaving on the same day, using the same driveway or the same pool area, can create friction that a standalone villa never has to manage. Some townhouse owners solve this with a shared house manual and clear boundaries built into both parties' listings; others simply accept a slightly lower average rate in exchange for not having to coordinate at all.

## Insurance and running costs side by side

Insuring a townhouse unit that shares structural elements with a neighbour can be more complex than insuring a standalone villa, since a fire or structural issue in one unit can affect the other. Some insurers price shared-wall properties differently, and building insurance for a shared pool may need to be split or jointly held between owners. Ask for this in writing before you buy rather than discovering it after a claim, and compare it against the simpler, single-owner insurance picture a standalone villa gives you.

## Checklist before choosing

- Confirm exactly what is shared: wall, driveway, garden, pool — get it in writing, not from a verbal description
- Ask how the plot or row is titled and whether you can sell your unit independently
- Check who is responsible for repairs to any shared element and how costs are split
- Compare the true private space (floor area plus any private outdoor area) against the price, not just bedroom count

## FAQ

### Is a townhouse cheaper than a villa in Bali?
Generally yes, for a comparable bedroom count and area, because the land cost is split across units in the same row or plot. The gap narrows or disappears if the townhouse has fully private facilities and a larger footprint than a small standalone villa.

### Do Bali townhouses always share a pool?
No. Some townhouse rows give each unit its own private pool and entrance, like Premier Umalas 16 and 17, while others genuinely split one pool between two units, like Serenity 6.1 and 6.2 in Sanur. Always confirm which arrangement applies before comparing price.

### Can I renovate or extend a townhouse in Bali the way I could a standalone villa?
Usually with more restrictions, since exterior changes can affect a shared wall, boundary or the row's overall appearance and may need agreement from neighbouring owners or a body managing the complex. A standalone villa on its own plot gives you full decision-making control.

### Which rents better, a townhouse or a standalone villa?
A townhouse with genuinely private facilities rents comparably to a similar standalone villa. One with shared pool or garden space tends to attract a more price-sensitive guest and a slightly lower achievable nightly rate, though it can still fill reliably.

### How is a townhouse titled compared with a standalone villa in Bali?
It varies by development — some townhouse rows sit under one shared certificate, others have separate certificates per unit. This affects whether you can sell your unit independently later, so confirm the structure with a notary before buying, since registration practice for row housing is not applied consistently across Bali.
