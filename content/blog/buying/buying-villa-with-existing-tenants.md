Buying a villa in Bali that already has tenants, whether a long-term resident on a yearly lease or forward bookings on the calendar from short-stay guests, means you are buying into existing obligations, not a blank slate. Before agreeing a price, you need to know exactly what leases or bookings exist, what income they generate, what deposits are held and who is entitled to them, and whether those arrangements transfer to you as the new owner or need to be renegotiated.

We manage villas for owners and have handled several handovers where tenancy details were not fully clarified before a sale closed, which created avoidable friction for the new owner, the outgoing owner and the tenant alike. Getting this right before you sign avoids that.

## What to ask for before you buy

Request full details of any current tenancy or booking commitments: the lease or booking terms, the rent or rate being paid, the security deposit amount and who holds it, and the remaining term. For a villa taking short-stay bookings, ask for the forward booking calendar and what income those bookings represent.

| Item to verify | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Lease or booking terms and remaining duration | Determines what income and obligations you inherit |
| Rent or rate actually being paid | May differ from the asking price implied by the seller |
| Security deposit amount and holder | You may need to take on the obligation to return it |
| Forward booking calendar, if short-stay | Affects your immediate income and availability after purchase |
| Any disputes or arrears with current tenants | Can become your problem to resolve after transfer |

## How tenancy affects the sale-purchase agreement

The sale-purchase agreement should specify clearly whether existing leases and bookings transfer to you, whether the seller is responsible for any deposits or obligations up to the completion date, and what happens if a tenant dispute exists at the time of sale. Our guide on the sale-purchase agreement in Bali covers the broader structure this fits into; tenancy-specific clauses should be added on top of the standard conditions precedent.

## Long-term tenants versus short-stay bookings

A villa with a single long-term tenant on a yearly lease is a simpler situation to assess than one with an active short-stay booking calendar, since the long-term lease has clear, fixed terms while a forward booking calendar represents ongoing operational activity, guest communications, cleaning schedules, deposits already collected, that needs a clean handover process to avoid disrupting guests mid-stay.

## Handling the handover if you plan to keep the villa rented

If you plan to continue renting the villa rather than using it yourself, plan the operational handover carefully: who communicates with existing tenants or guests about the change of ownership, how deposits and any pre-paid bookings are handled, and whether you will manage the villa yourself or bring in professional management from day one. Our guide on buying a villa with management already in place covers the related scenario where the existing management team continues after the sale.

## A checklist before completing

1. Get full written details of any current lease or forward bookings
2. Confirm deposit amounts, who holds them, and how they transfer or are settled at completion
3. Specify in the sale-purchase agreement exactly what tenancy obligations transfer to you
4. Plan how existing tenants or guests will be communicated with at handover
5. Decide whether you will self-manage or engage professional management immediately after completion

## What if you want the villa vacant instead?

If you would prefer to take possession without existing tenants, this needs to be negotiated and written into the sale-purchase agreement, including the timeline for the seller to end any tenancy and any cost or compensation implications. This should be agreed before completion, not assumed.

## What to do next

Get full, written details of any tenancy or booking commitments before agreeing a price, and make sure the sale-purchase agreement specifies exactly what transfers to you and what the seller remains responsible for up to completion.

## FAQ

### Do existing leases automatically transfer when I buy a villa in Bali?
Not automatically; this needs to be specified in the sale-purchase agreement. Confirm explicitly whether existing leases or booking commitments transfer to you as the new owner or whether the seller is responsible for concluding them before completion.

### What happens to a tenant's security deposit when the villa is sold?
This depends on what the sale-purchase agreement specifies. Typically either the deposit is transferred to the new owner along with the obligation to return it at the end of the tenancy, or the seller settles it before completion. Confirm which applies in writing.

### Should I buy a villa with an existing short-stay booking calendar?
It can be an advantage, since it shows real demand and provides immediate income, but you need full visibility of the bookings, rates and any deposits collected, and a clear handover plan so guests are not disrupted by the change in ownership.

### Can I ask the seller to make the villa vacant before I buy it?
Yes, this can be negotiated and should be written into the sale-purchase agreement with a clear timeline, since ending an existing tenancy takes time and may involve cost or compensation depending on the lease terms.

### Do I need to keep the same management arrangement as the previous owner?
No, you can choose to continue with existing management, switch providers, or self-manage, though if there is an active booking calendar a clean handover plan matters regardless of which option you choose.
