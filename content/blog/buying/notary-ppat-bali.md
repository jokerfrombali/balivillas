A notary in Indonesia is a licensed legal professional who drafts and certifies agreements, while a PPAT (Pejabat Pembuat Akta Tanah) is licensed specifically to prepare and register land and property deeds. In practice, many professionals in Bali hold both licences and handle the whole transaction, from the initial sale agreement through to registering your leasehold or ownership rights.

For a foreign buyer, this person is the single most important safeguard in the deal. A good notary/PPAT verifies the seller's ownership, checks for existing debts or disputes on the land, confirms zoning and permits, and drafts an agreement that actually protects you if something goes wrong later. A weak one processes paperwork without asking the hard questions.

## What the notary/PPAT should check before you sign anything

- Confirm the seller is the genuine, legal owner of the land or leasehold rights
- Check the land certificate is clean, with no outstanding mortgage, dispute or third-party claim
- Verify zoning (PKKPR) supports the property's intended use
- Confirm building permits (PBG) and function certificate (SLF) status
- Draft the sale and purchase agreement (or lease agreement, for leasehold) with clear payment milestones tied to verified conditions
- Register the completed transaction with the relevant land office

## Notary versus PPAT: are they different people?

Sometimes yes, sometimes no. Many licensed professionals in Bali hold both qualifications and act as a single point of contact through the whole process, which is generally more convenient for a foreign buyer coordinating from a distance. Where they are separate, the notary typically handles agreement drafting and certification while the PPAT specifically handles the land deed registration. Ask upfront which structure you are getting and confirm both functions are covered by someone properly licensed.

## Red flags when choosing one

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Recommended only by the seller or their agent, with no independent option offered | Conflict of interest; they may prioritise closing the deal over protecting you |
| Reluctant to explain the leasehold or ownership structure in plain terms | Suggests either weak English communication or discomfort with scrutiny |
| Pushes you to sign quickly, before permits and land certificate are verified | A genuine safeguard takes the time due diligence requires |
| No clear, itemised fee structure given upfront | Fees should be transparent before you commit, not negotiated after the fact |
| No verifiable track record or licence number | You should be able to confirm their licensing status independently |

## How to find and vet one

1. Ask for recommendations from people with no financial stake in your specific transaction, not only the seller's side
2. Request their licence details and confirm they are currently registered to practise
3. Ask how many similar transactions (leasehold villa purchases by foreigners) they have handled recently
4. Request a written, itemised fee quote before engaging them
5. Ask directly what they check as standard due diligence, and compare the answer against the checklist above

## What using a notary/PPAT typically costs

Notary and PPAT fees in Bali are usually charged as a percentage of the transaction value, alongside separate government fees such as BPHTB (transfer tax) and registration costs. The exact percentage varies by professional and transaction complexity, and should always be confirmed in writing before you commit (as of 2026, verify current typical fee ranges with the professional directly, since this is not a fixed government tariff). ## Working with a notary remotely

Many foreign buyers, including those buying property in Bali for Airbnb-style rental income from the UK, US, Australia or elsewhere, complete the process without being in Bali for every step. A competent notary/PPAT can work with power of attorney arrangements and digital document exchange for much of the process, though certain steps (particularly final signing) may still require in-person presence or a properly authorised representative. Confirm exactly which steps need your physical presence before assuming the whole process can run remotely.

## What a good notary protects you from

The value of a strong notary/PPAT shows up most clearly when something is wrong with the property: a disputed boundary, an unregistered lien, a permit gap, a lease term shorter than advertised. Catching these before you pay is dramatically cheaper than discovering them after, when your only options are expensive legal action or absorbing the loss.

Is buying property in Bali as a foreigner a good investment? That depends heavily on getting this stage right. A well-vetted purchase with clean documentation and a properly registered structure behaves very differently from a rushed one, even if the villas look identical on the surface.

## What goes wrong when buyers cut corners here

- **Using the seller's notary without seeking a second opinion**, on the assumption that "a notary is a notary." Two licensed professionals can differ significantly in how thoroughly they verify a seller's documentation before drafting an agreement.
- **Signing before due diligence is complete**, because a deal feels time-pressured or another buyer is supposedly interested. A genuine competing offer is rarely worth skipping verification of the certificate, zoning and permits.
- **Assuming power of attorney covers everything**, without confirming which specific steps still legally require your presence or a properly authorised local representative. This varies by transaction type and should be confirmed early, not assumed.
- **Paying notary and government fees without an itemised breakdown**, then discovering later that BPHTB, registration costs and the notary's own fee were bundled together in a way that made it hard to check anything was reasonable.

## FAQ

### Is a notary the same as a lawyer in Bali?
Not exactly. A notary is licensed to draft and certify specific legal documents and, where also licensed as a PPAT, to register land deeds. A lawyer (advokat) has broader authority to represent you in disputes or litigation. Most straightforward purchases only need a notary/PPAT, but a lawyer is worth engaging if a dispute or complex negotiation arises.

### Can I use the seller's notary to save time?
You can, but it is not ideal. A notary engaged by the seller has an existing relationship with that side of the deal. An independent notary, chosen by you, has no incentive to smooth over problems to get the transaction closed.

### Do I need to be in Bali in person to complete a purchase?
Much of the process can be handled remotely with power of attorney and digital documents, but certain final steps may require in-person presence or a properly authorised representative acting on your behalf. Confirm this with your notary early in the process.

### What happens if the notary misses something important?
A licensed notary carries professional responsibility for their work, but pursuing a claim after the fact is slower and more stressful than avoiding the problem in the first place. This is why choosing someone thorough and independent matters more than choosing someone fast or cheap.

### How do I know if a notary is properly licensed?
Ask for their licence number and registration details, and verify these independently rather than relying only on their own website or business card. A reputable notary will not hesitate to provide this.
