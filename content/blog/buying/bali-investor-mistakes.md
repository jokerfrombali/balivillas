The mistakes we see foreign buyers make in Bali repeat across almost every deal that later causes regret: skipping proper due diligence on permits and leasehold terms, trusting a seller's or agent's projected rental income instead of asking for real performance data, and not budgeting the full cost of ownership beyond the purchase price. None of these are exotic risks specific to Bali; they are ordinary property-buying discipline that gets skipped under time pressure or excitement.

We manage 20 villas for owners across Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan, and we talk to buyers who have already been burned by one or more of these mistakes far more often than we would like. This guide sets out the ones worth guarding against specifically.

## Mistake 1: skipping due diligence on documentation

Foreigners buying property in Bali typically hold rights through leasehold rather than freehold ownership, and the leasehold agreement, land certificate, zoning (PKKPR), building permit (PBG) and function-worthiness certificate (SLF) all need checking before you commit, not after. Buyers under time pressure sometimes skip this step on the promise that "it will be sorted." It should be sorted before signing, not after.

## Mistake 2: trusting projected income over actual data

A projected rental yield from a seller or agent is a sales figure, not a fact. The number worth trusting is actual, verifiable rental history for that specific villa, or, if it is new, realistic comparable performance for similar villas in the same area. Our guide on the most common investor question, whether buying a villa in Bali is a good investment, covers how to evaluate real return potential properly.

## Mistake 3: underestimating the full cost of ownership

The purchase price is only the start. Notary and transfer costs, ongoing property tax, staff, maintenance, utilities and management fees all add up every year. Buyers who budget only the headline price are often surprised by the ongoing commitment once they own the villa.

| Common mistake | What it costs you |
|---|---|
| Skipping permit due diligence | Risk of an unbuildable or non-compliant property, costly to fix or impossible |
| Trusting projected income | Overpaying based on a return that never materialises |
| Underbudgeting holding costs | Cash flow strain in year one, unexpected shortfalls |
| Rushing the sale-purchase agreement | Missing protective clauses that matter if something goes wrong |

## Mistake 4: rushing the paperwork stage

The sale-purchase agreement, deposit structure and notary process each have specific steps designed to protect both sides. Rushing through them, particularly the deposit and escrow arrangements, is one of the more common ways buyers end up with less protection than they think they have. Our guides on escrow and deposits and on the sale-purchase agreement in Bali cover what a properly structured process looks like.

## Mistake 5: not recognising oversupply or bubble risk in a specific segment

Buying a generic new-build villa in a fast-growing, heavily built-up segment at the top of a local price cycle is a recurring pattern among buyers who later struggle to resell or rent at the price they expected. Checking local supply and recent comparable sales before committing, covered in our guide on villa oversupply in Bali, protects against this specific mistake.

## A pre-purchase checklist to avoid the recurring mistakes

1. Verify leasehold term, land certificate and all required permits before signing anything binding
2. Ask for actual rental history or genuinely comparable performance data, not projections
3. Budget the full cost stack: purchase, transfer, notary, then ongoing tax, staff and maintenance
4. Read the sale-purchase agreement carefully and confirm the deposit and escrow structure protect you
5. Check recent comparable sales and local supply before treating any asking price as fair

## Are foreign buyers more exposed to these mistakes than locals?

Not necessarily to different mistakes, but often to the same ones with less local context to catch them. A foreign buyer unfamiliar with Indonesian leasehold structures, permit requirements or local pricing dynamics is relying more heavily on the seller, agent or notary to flag problems, which is exactly why independent due diligence matters more, not less, for a foreign buyer.

## What to do next

Treat each of the mistakes above as a specific checklist item on any purchase you are considering, not a general warning to be cautious. Verify documentation, ask for real data instead of projections, and budget the full ownership cost before you commit.

## FAQ

### What is the single biggest mistake foreign buyers make in Bali?
Skipping proper due diligence on leasehold terms and permits is the mistake most likely to cause serious, costly problems later, because it can affect whether the property is legally usable at all, not just whether it performs financially as hoped.

### Can I trust an agent's projected rental income figures?
Treat projected figures as marketing, not fact. Ask instead for the specific villa's actual rental history, or genuinely comparable performance data for similar villas in the same area, before basing a purchase decision on expected income.

### Do I need a lawyer or notary independent of the seller?
Using a notary and, where appropriate, independent legal advice that is not selected or controlled by the seller is standard good practice and helps ensure due diligence is genuinely independent rather than a formality arranged by the party with an interest in the sale closing.

### Is buying property in Bali as a foreigner riskier than in my home country?
The risks are different rather than automatically greater: leasehold structures, permit requirements and local market dynamics are less familiar to most foreign buyers, which is why independent verification of every claim matters more than it might at home.

### How do I avoid overpaying because of local market hype?
Compare the asking price against genuine recent comparable sales and realistic rental income for the specific villa, not against what similar listings are currently asking or what a seller projects it could earn.
