Buying a villa in Bali runs through six stages: decide on a legal ownership structure, verify the property and its certificate, agree terms and sign a binding sale and purchase agreement, arrange staged payments, complete the transfer with a notary, and register the change with the relevant office. Skipping or rushing any one of these is where most disputes and regrets come from.

The process is not fundamentally different from buying property anywhere else in the sense that due diligence and a properly drafted contract do most of the protecting. What is different is the legal landscape foreigners operate in, and the fact that land certificates, permits and zoning status in Bali need active verification rather than assumption.

## Step 1: choose your ownership structure before you view villas

Decide early whether you are buying as an individual on leasehold or Hak Pakai, or through a PT PMA company, because this affects which properties are realistically available to you and how the contract will be drafted. Our guide on whether foreigners can buy a villa in Bali covers the three routes in detail if you have not settled on one yet.

Committing to a structure before you start viewing saves time later, since some sellers price or structure a deal expecting a particular buyer type, and switching structures mid-negotiation can reopen terms you thought were settled.

## Step 2: shortlist properties and check the basics early

Once you know your structure, shortlist villas against your actual criteria: area, budget, size, condition and whether it comes with existing rental income. Ask for the land certificate type, remaining lease or Hak Pakai term, and building permit status before you get emotionally invested, not after you have made an offer.

| Stage | What happens | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|
| Search and shortlist | View properties, request certificates and permits | Weeks to a couple of months |
| Offer and negotiation | Agree price and key terms verbally, then in writing | Days to a few weeks |
| Sale and purchase agreement (SPA) | Notary drafts binding contract with payment milestones | 1–3 weeks |
| Staged payments and completion | Funds released against milestones, deed signed | Weeks to a couple of months |
| Registration | Notary or PPAT registers the transfer | Weeks after signing |

## Step 3: verify the certificate and permits before you negotiate hard

This is the step buyers most often under-invest in. Confirm the certificate type matches what the seller claims (leasehold, Hak Pakai, or the underlying Hak Milik or HGB the lease sits on), check the remaining term against what was advertised, and verify the building has the correct permit for its use. Our guide on Bali land certificate types explains what each certificate actually says and does not say.

If the villa is being sold with an existing lease that you would take over, get that lease agreement itself reviewed independently, since its wording — not just its remaining years — determines what you are actually buying.

## Step 4: negotiate and sign the sale and purchase agreement

Once due diligence checks out, negotiate final price and terms, then have a notary draft a binding sale and purchase agreement (Akta Jual Beli or, for leasehold, a notarised lease deed). This document should specify exact payment milestones, what happens if either side fails to perform, and precisely what is included — furniture, fittings, existing bookings, staff continuity.

Do not rely on a verbal agreement or an informal deposit arrangement outside a proper contract. If a seller pressures you to pay before a binding agreement exists, treat that as a warning sign rather than urgency to act on.

## Step 5: stage your payments and use an independent notary

Structure payments against contract milestones rather than paying the full amount upfront. A typical pattern reserves a portion at signing, a further portion at an agreed checkpoint, and the balance at completion, though the exact split is negotiated per deal. Use a notary you or your lawyer chose, not one the seller supplied, for the actual transfer and registration.

- Confirm the notary is independent of the seller's agent or company
- Tie every payment to a specific, verifiable contract milestone
- Get written confirmation of bank details directly from the notary, not by email alone
- Keep copies of every signed document and payment receipt
- Confirm who registers the transfer and by when

## Step 6: registration and what happens after signing

After signing, the notary or a licensed land deed official (PPAT) registers the change with the relevant land office. For leasehold, this typically means registering the lease against the underlying certificate; for Hak Pakai or HGB transfers, the registration updates the title itself. Ask your notary for confirmation once registration is complete, and keep the registered documents somewhere secure and accessible.

If you are buying from outside Indonesia and cannot attend signing in person, power of attorney can cover most of this process; our guide on buying a villa remotely from abroad covers what that involves.

## Why a rushed timeline is the enemy of a safe purchase

Almost every serious problem in this sequence traces back to time pressure, real or perceived. A seller citing another interested buyer, a notary rushed to finish before a holiday, or a buyer's own eagerness to close before a return flight all push toward skipping or shortening a step that exists specifically to protect you. Build slack into your own timeline from the start, and treat any external pressure to compress it as a signal worth questioning rather than a reason to move faster.

## Common mistakes that derail a purchase

The most frequent mistake is verifying the certificate only after an offer is accepted, which puts pressure to proceed even when something looks wrong. A close second is using the seller's recommended notary without independent review, and a third is paying a large deposit before a binding sale and purchase agreement exists, leaving no real recourse if the deal falls through.

## A staged-payment framework to protect yourself

Rather than accepting whatever split a seller proposes, use this as a starting negotiation position and adjust with your notary:

| Milestone | Typical portion released | What should be confirmed first |
|---|---|---|
| Signing the SPA | A minority deposit, not the majority | Certificate verified, permits checked, contract reviewed independently |
| Mid-point checkpoint | A further portion | Any conditions in the SPA met, no outstanding disputes surfaced |
| Completion / deed signing | Remaining balance | Notary confirms all documents ready for registration |
| After registration | Any final retained amount, if agreed | Registration confirmed complete by the notary |

If a seller insists on receiving most or all of the price before a binding SPA exists, that is worth treating as a reason to slow down, not a reason to move faster.

## What goes wrong most often, in more detail

Beyond the three mistakes above, a pattern worth naming specifically: buyers who rely on translated summaries of Indonesian-language documents rather than a full independent translation, and later discover a clause they were told didn't matter actually did. Another: buyers who assume a villa's rental income or licence status transfers automatically with the sale, when in practice licences are often tied to the previous owner or operator and need to be reapplied for. Ask specifically, in writing, what transfers and what needs to be reapplied for before you finalise price.

## FAQ

### What is the first step to buying a villa in Bali?

The first practical step is deciding your ownership structure — leasehold, Hak Pakai or a PT PMA company — before you seriously shortlist properties, since this affects which villas suit your situation and how contracts will be drafted. Only after that should you move into verifying certificates and permits on specific properties you are considering.

### How long does buying a villa in Bali take?

Timelines vary widely by deal complexity, but a straightforward leasehold purchase with clean paperwork can complete within a few months from offer to registration, while purchases needing a new PT PMA or resolving certificate issues take longer. Build in buffer time rather than working to a fixed deadline, particularly for the notary and registration stages.

### Do I need a lawyer to buy a villa in Bali?

You need an independent notary at minimum, since notaries handle the binding legal documents for Indonesian property transactions. Many foreign buyers also engage a separate lawyer for additional review, particularly on certificate history, permits and any lease terms being taken over. Using only the seller's recommended notary, without independent review, removes a layer of protection.

### Can I buy a villa in Bali without visiting in person?

Yes, using power of attorney for signing and a combination of video viewings and an independent physical inspection by someone who is not the seller's agent. It works well for most of the process, though many buyers still prefer to visit before or around completion. See our guide on buying a villa remotely for the detail.

### What documents should I check before buying a villa in Bali?

At minimum: the land certificate and its type, the building permit, the lease agreement if applicable and its remaining term, proof of land and building tax payment, and, if buying an existing rental villa, records of any licences or bookings that would transfer.
