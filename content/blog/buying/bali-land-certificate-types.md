Every villa in Bali sits on land held under one of a small number of certificate types: Hak Milik (freehold, Indonesian citizens only), Hak Guna Bangunan (right to build, term-limited, commonly company-held), or Hak Pakai (right to use, available to eligible foreign individuals). A foreign buyer's leasehold (Hak Sewa) purchase is typically a contract layered on top of land the seller holds as one of these three, most often Hak Milik.

Knowing which certificate applies to a specific villa, and what that certificate actually confers, is the starting point of any serious due diligence, because it determines your legal position, your term, and what happens at the end of that term.

## The main certificate types side by side

| Certificate | Full meaning | Who holds it | Term |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hak Milik | Freehold ownership | Indonesian citizens and certain entities | Unlimited |
| Hak Guna Bangunan (HGB) | Right to build | Companies, including PT PMA | Fixed, renewable |
| Hak Pakai | Right to use | Eligible foreign individuals, certain entities | Capped, renewable |
| Hak Sewa | Leasehold (contract, not a certificate) | Any foreign buyer, via contract | Set by contract |

Hak Sewa is included here because it is the route most foreign buyers actually use, even though it is a private contract rather than a registered certificate in the way the other three are.

## Hak Milik: the freehold benchmark

Hak Milik is what the other certificate types are measured against. It has no term limit and passes by inheritance, and it is the title category most Indonesian-owned land defaults to. Our full explainer on Hak Milik covers how this title works and why it matters even to buyers who cannot hold it themselves.

## HGB: company-held right to build

HGB is a fixed-term, renewable right to construct and own buildings on a plot, most relevant to buyers setting up or buying into a PT PMA that will hold the villa as a company asset. Our HGB guide covers the term structure and what to check if a villa you are considering is held this way.

## Hak Pakai: registered use rights for eligible individuals

Hak Pakai gives eligible foreign individuals a registered title, rather than only a private contract, offering a stronger legal footing than leasehold for buyers who meet the current eligibility conditions. Our Hak Pakai guide covers eligibility and term in detail.

## Hak Sewa: the contract most buyers actually use

For most foreign buyers, the practical purchase is a Hak Sewa lease contract on land the seller holds as Hak Milik, HGB or occasionally Hak Pakai. This is a private, notarised agreement rather than a certificate change, and its protection comes from the contract's own drafting. Our Hak Sewa guide covers what a strong lease contract should include.

## How to identify which certificate applies to a villa you are considering

- Ask the seller or agent directly which certificate type the underlying land holds
- Request a certified copy of the certificate through your own notary
- Confirm whether your purchase is a lease on that land, a Hak Pakai conversion, or a company purchase involving HGB
- Check the certificate's stated term against what you have been told verbally
- Verify the registered owner's name matches who you are actually transacting with

## Why certificate type affects price and risk differently

Land held under Hak Milik and leased to you tends to carry different risk and pricing dynamics than land already on a shortening HGB term, because the underlying certificate's own security differs even before your specific contract term is considered. A villa on HGB land nearing the end of its own term, with your lease sitting inside that shrinking window, needs closer scrutiny than one on Hak Milik land with a long, well-drafted lease.

## Certificate checks as part of full due diligence

Certificate verification is one pillar of a complete due diligence process, alongside permits, zoning and title history checks. Our full due diligence checklist walks through how these pieces fit together, and getting the certificate type wrong or unverified undermines everything else you check afterwards, since it is the foundation the rest of the transaction sits on.

## What goes wrong when certificate type is misunderstood

The confusion we see most often is marketing language, particularly the word "freehold," used loosely to describe a leasehold purchase on land the seller holds as Hak Milik, leading a buyer to believe they're getting something closer to ownership than they actually are. This isn't always intentional misrepresentation — sometimes it's just imprecise language carried over from how a seller or agent has always described the deal — but the buyer's understanding needs to be based on the actual certificate, not the adjective used to sell it.

A second issue: buyers who verify that a certificate exists but not that it's current and unencumbered. A Hak Milik certificate can be genuine and still carry undisclosed liens, or reflect an owner who inherited the land without formally updating the registration, both of which surface as problems only once a transaction is well underway.

## A framework for confirming certificate type before you commit

1. **Ask directly, in writing, which certificate type applies** — don't infer it from marketing language like "freehold" or "secure long-term."
2. **Get a certified copy through your own notary**, independent of the seller, and confirm it matches what you were told.
3. **If the purchase is a lease, confirm what the underlying certificate is** — Hak Milik, HGB or Hak Pakai — since this shapes the risk profile of the deal you're actually in.
4. **Check the certificate is current and unencumbered**, not just that it exists, including registration history and any liens.
5. **Match the certificate type against the structure you intend to use** — leasehold, Hak Pakai purchase, or PT PMA — and confirm they're compatible before you go further.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between Hak Milik and Hak Guna Bangunan?

Hak Milik is unlimited-term freehold ownership, available only to Indonesian citizens and certain entities. Hak Guna Bangunan is a fixed-term, renewable right to build and own structures on land, commonly held by companies including PT PMA. A villa's certificate type materially affects its term security and who can hold it.

### Can a foreigner check what certificate a Bali villa holds?

Yes, and doing so independently through your own notary, rather than relying on the seller's description, is standard due diligence. The notary can request a certified copy of the certificate from the land office, confirming both its type and the registered owner's identity.

### Which certificate type is safest for a foreign buyer?

There is no single "safest" type in the abstract; each suits different situations. Hak Pakai offers a registered title for eligible individuals, HGB suits company-held purchases through a PT PMA, and leasehold on well-documented Hak Milik land, with a carefully drafted contract, works well for straightforward personal purchases.

### Does the certificate type affect a villa's resale value?

Yes, generally. Land ultimately held as Hak Milik tends to be viewed as more stable long-term than land already sitting on a shortening HGB or Hak Pakai term, and a leasehold contract's own remaining years and extension terms further affect resale value independent of the underlying certificate.

### What happens if a seller cannot produce a clear certificate?

This is a serious warning sign and reason to pause the transaction until your notary can verify the certificate independently through the land office. Proceeding without clear certificate verification is one of the most common sources of serious disputes in Bali property purchases and should not be worked around under time pressure.
