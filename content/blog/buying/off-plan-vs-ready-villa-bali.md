Buying an off-plan villa in Bali means purchasing before construction is complete, sometimes before it has started, generally at a lower entry price with more scope to customise the layout and finish. Buying a completed villa means paying more per square metre for the certainty of an inspectable, known product with a much shorter path to actually owning and using it. Neither is universally better; the right choice depends on your risk tolerance, timeline and how much you value customisation.

This guide compares the two paths directly so you can weigh them against your own situation.

## The core trade-off

| Factor | Off-plan | Completed villa |
|---|---|---|
| Typical entry price | Generally lower | Generally higher per square metre |
| Customisation | Higher, if bought early enough | None, what you see is what you get |
| Risk | Developer and construction risk | Minimal construction risk |
| Timeline to use | Months to years, depending on build stage | Immediate or near-immediate |
| Ability to inspect before buying | Limited to plans and a sales gallery | Full physical inspection possible |
| Rental track record available | No | Sometimes, if previously rented |

## Why buyers choose off-plan

The main draws are price and customisation. An off-plan purchase, especially early in a development's sales cycle, is often priced below where the completed product will eventually sell, and buyers get more say over layout, finishes and sometimes floor plan changes than a completed villa allows. It can suit buyers with a longer time horizon who are comfortable with construction timelines slipping, which they commonly do.

## Why buyers choose a completed villa

A completed villa removes construction and developer risk entirely; you are buying something you can walk through, inspect for build quality, and in some cases review a rental track record for. It suits buyers who want to use or start renting the property soon, or who are less comfortable carrying construction risk. ## The risk side of off-plan that deserves its own read

Off-plan buying carries risks that a completed purchase simply does not: developer financial stability, construction delays, and the gap between marketing renders and the finished product. ## How your own timeline should shape the decision

Beyond price and risk tolerance, be honest about your own timeline pressure. If you need a villa usable within a few months, whether to move in, start renting, or both, off-plan is generally the wrong fit regardless of how attractive the pricing looks, since construction delays are common enough that a tight personal timeline and an off-plan purchase rarely combine well. If your timeline is genuinely flexible, off-plan's price advantage becomes more worth considering, provided the developer due diligence checks out.

## A middle path some buyers overlook

A late-stage off-plan purchase, buying into a project that is substantially complete but not yet finished, can offer some of the price advantage of off-plan with meaningfully less construction risk, since much of the building is already visible and inspectable. This is worth asking about specifically when a developer only advertises early-stage pricing; sometimes later units in the same project are available closer to completion at a smaller but still real discount to the finished market price.

## A practical decision checklist

1. Confirm your realistic timeline: do you need the villa usable soon, or is a multi-year wait acceptable
2. Assess your appetite for developer and construction risk honestly
3. For off-plan, research the developer's track record on previous projects before anything else
4. For completed villas, get a proper building inspection regardless of how good it looks
5. Compare like-for-like pricing, an off-plan price without holding costs during construction is not directly comparable to a completed villa's price

## What to do next

Off-plan suits buyers prioritising price and customisation who can tolerate construction and developer risk; a completed villa suits buyers who want certainty and a shorter path to use. Be honest with yourself about which matters more before committing capital either way.

## FAQ

### Is off-plan buying cheaper than buying a completed villa in Bali?
Generally yes at initial purchase, though the full comparison should include holding costs during construction and the risk of delays or design changes, which do not apply to a completed purchase.

### What is the biggest risk of buying off-plan in Bali?
Developer and construction risk, delays, changes to the final product versus what was marketed, or in the worst case a developer failing to complete the project. Our guide on [off-plan villa risks](/blog/buying/off-plan-villa-risks-bali/) covers this in depth.

### Can I customise a completed villa the way I could an off-plan purchase?
Only through renovation after purchase, which adds its own cost and time. If customisation matters a great deal to you, off-plan or building independently are the more direct routes.

### How do I check if an off-plan developer is trustworthy?
Research their completed track record on previous projects specifically, not just their current marketing. Our guide on [checking a Bali property developer](/blog/buying/developer-due-diligence-bali/) covers what to look for.

### Is a completed villa always a safer buy than off-plan?
It removes construction and developer risk, but you still need full due diligence on the certificate, permits and build quality. A completed villa is not automatically risk-free, just risk-different from an off-plan purchase.
