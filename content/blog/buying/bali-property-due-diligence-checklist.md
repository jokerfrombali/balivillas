Due diligence on a Bali villa purchase covers four core areas: the land certificate and its type, the building's permit status, the plot's zoning classification, and the seller's actual title history and any encumbrances. Each of these should be verified independently, by your own notary or lawyer, not taken on trust from a listing description or the seller's agent, however reputable the seller appears.

Buying property in Bali as a foreigner adds a legal-structure layer on top of ordinary due diligence, since you also need to confirm the ownership route (leasehold, Hak Pakai or PT PMA) is set up correctly for your situation. But the underlying property checks are the same discipline any careful buyer should apply, foreign or Indonesian.

## The four pillars of due diligence

Certificate verification confirms what land right actually exists and who legitimately holds it. Permit verification confirms the building itself was constructed and is used legally. Zoning verification confirms the plot's designated land use actually permits what you intend, whether that is residential use, tourism accommodation, or rental operation. Title history checks for disputes, liens or unregistered claims that could surface after you buy.

| Pillar | What to verify | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| Land certificate | Type, registered owner, remaining term | Land office, via notary |
| Building permit | Correct permit type for the structure and use | Local government, via notary or lawyer |
| Zoning | Plot's designated land use classification | Zoning map and PKKPR check |
| Title history | Prior owners, disputes, liens | Land office records, via notary |

## Checking the land certificate

Request a certified copy of the certificate directly through your notary rather than relying on a photocopy supplied by the seller, and confirm the registered name matches the person or entity you are actually transacting with. Our guide to Bali land certificate types explains the different categories you might encounter and what each one actually confers.

## Checking building permits

Confirm the villa's building permit (PBG, previously known under different terminology) matches the structure as it actually exists, since additions or renovations made without updating the permit can create compliance issues for a new owner. Our guide to PBG permits in Bali covers what this permit actually confirms and what to ask for.

## Checking zoning status

Zoning determines what a plot is legally designated for, and a villa that looks perfectly normal can sit on land zoned in a way that limits or complicates the use you intend, particularly for rental operation. Our guides on how to check Bali zoning and on PKKPR in Bali cover the practical steps for verifying this before you commit.

- Request the certified land certificate through your own notary, not the seller
- Verify the building permit matches the current structure
- Check the plot's zoning classification against your intended use
- Confirm PKKPR status if the property will be used for tourism accommodation
- Review title history for disputes, liens or unregistered claims
- Get the lease agreement, if applicable, independently reviewed
- Confirm land and building tax (PBB) is current and paid

## Who should be on your due diligence team

A straightforward leasehold purchase typically needs an independent notary at minimum, with many buyers also engaging a separate lawyer for deeper contract and title review. A PT PMA or existing-company purchase needs that same team plus someone experienced in Indonesian corporate due diligence, since you're checking a company's history as well as a property's. Whichever route applies, make sure everyone on your team is genuinely independent of the seller and their agent, not merely recommended by them — a recommendation from the seller's side is not disqualifying on its own, but it's worth verifying independence directly before relying on their findings.

## Is buying property in Bali a good investment

Whether a specific purchase is a good investment depends on factors specific to that property and your own goals, including remaining lease term, location, condition, running costs and your intended use, and we do not offer investment advice or promise returns. What due diligence does is remove the legal and structural unknowns that would otherwise make any investment judgement unreliable, so you are assessing the property itself, not a paperwork risk hiding underneath it.

## How long proper due diligence realistically takes

Buyers sometimes underestimate how long thorough due diligence takes, expecting certificate and permit checks to complete within days when in practice zoning confirmation and title history review can take several weeks depending on the specific land office and the complexity of the property's history. Build this into your purchase timeline from the outset, and treat any seller pushing for a faster close than your due diligence team says is realistic as a reason for caution, not a reason to compress the checks.

## Buying for Airbnb or rental operation

If you intend to buy specifically to operate on Airbnb or similar platforms, add licensing and zoning checks specific to tourism accommodation to your due diligence, since not every residentially zoned plot is legally set up for that use. This is covered in more depth in our zoning guides above, and it is worth confirming with a lawyer familiar with tourism accommodation rules before assuming a property is Airbnb-ready simply because other villas nearby operate that way.

## Buying from the UK or elsewhere overseas

Due diligence does not change fundamentally when you are buying from abroad, but coordinating it remotely takes more planning: engaging an independent notary or lawyer who can review documents digitally, and arranging an independent physical inspection separate from the seller's agent. Our guide on buying a villa remotely from abroad covers this coordination in detail.

## What goes wrong when due diligence is rushed

The due diligence failures we hear about most share a common trigger: pressure to move fast, whether from a seller citing other interested buyers, an agent pushing for a quick deposit, or the buyer's own excitement about a specific property. Under that pressure, the steps most likely to get skipped are independent certificate verification (relying on the seller's copy instead) and zoning checks for intended commercial use (assuming a villa is Airbnb-ready because neighbouring villas operate that way). Both are quick to check properly and expensive to discover were wrong after completion.

A second pattern: buyers who complete thorough due diligence on the property itself but skip equivalent scrutiny of the seller — confirming the seller is actually who they claim to be, and that any company involved in the sale is in good standing, particularly relevant for HGB purchases through an existing PT PMA.

## A framework for pacing due diligence properly

1. **Never let a deposit precede certificate verification.** If a seller pressures you to pay before your notary has independently confirmed the certificate, treat that as a reason to slow down, not speed up.
2. **Run the four pillars in parallel, not sequentially**, where possible, so a single slow step doesn't create pressure to skip the others.
3. **Budget real time for zoning and permit checks specifically**, since these often take longer than certificate verification and are the ones most often rushed as a result.
4. **Get a written summary from your notary or lawyer** covering all four pillars before you finalise price, not a verbal "it looks fine."

## FAQ

### What documents do I need to check before buying a villa in Bali?

At minimum: the land certificate and its type, the building permit, zoning classification, evidence of current land and building tax payment, and, for leasehold, the lease agreement itself. If the property has existing rental income or licences, request documentation of those too, since they may or may not transfer with the sale.

### Is buying property in Bali as a foreigner risky?

It carries legal complexity specific to foreign ownership structures, but it is not inherently risky when handled with proper due diligence and an independent notary or lawyer. Most of the real risk in Bali property purchases comes from skipping verification steps or relying entirely on the seller's own representations, not from foreign ownership itself.

### How do I verify a Bali land certificate is genuine?

Request a certified copy directly through your own notary, who can confirm it against the land office's own records, rather than relying on a copy supplied by the seller. This independent verification is standard practice and should be a non-negotiable part of any purchase, regardless of how trustworthy the seller appears.

### Can I do due diligence on a Bali villa from abroad?

Yes, most of it can be coordinated remotely through an independent notary or lawyer reviewing documents digitally, combined with an independent physical inspection by someone who is not the seller's agent. Our guide on buying remotely from abroad covers how to structure this so nothing gets missed by relying too heavily on one party.

### Is due diligence different if I want to use the villa for Airbnb?

Yes, add specific checks on zoning classification and tourism accommodation licensing, since not every plot is legally set up for short-term rental operation even if similar villas nearby appear to run that way. Confirm this specifically with a lawyer before assuming a property is ready for Airbnb rental.
