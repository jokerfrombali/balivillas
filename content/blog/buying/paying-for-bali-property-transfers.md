Transferring money to buy property in Bali starts with choosing a proper banking channel, coordinating the transfer timing with your notary and the sale-purchase agreement's payment schedule, and keeping full documentation of every step, since Indonesian regulations around large foreign-exchange transfers require clear paperwork and your own bank will need to understand the purpose of the transfer (as of 2026, verify current reporting thresholds with your bank).

We work with owners and buyers moving funds into Indonesia regularly, and the process that goes smoothly is always the one planned in advance rather than started once a price has already been agreed and a deadline is looming.

## Step-by-step: how the transfer process typically works

1. Confirm the receiving account structure with your notary before sending anything, ideally an escrow or notary-held account tied to the transaction rather than a personal account of the seller
2. Notify your home bank of the purpose and destination of the transfer in advance, since large international property-related transfers often require additional documentation
3. Send the transfer through a proper bank-to-bank channel, keeping full records of the sending and receiving details
4. Time the transfer against the payment milestones in the sale-purchase agreement, not before documentation and conditions are confirmed
5. Keep all transfer confirmations, invoices and notary correspondence for your own records and for any future tax reporting

## Why the receiving account matters

Where the money is sent matters as much as how it is sent. A properly structured transaction routes payment through an escrow arrangement or a verified notary account tied to specific conditions, rather than directly to a seller's personal account ahead of confirmed transfer of rights. ## Avoiding informal or undocumented payment requests

Any request to pay informally, in cash, through a third party unconnected to the transaction, or outside the documented escrow or notary process, is a serious warning sign. Our guide on spotting Bali property scams covers this pattern in more depth; the transfer process is one of the clearest places a scam can be prevented simply by insisting on a proper documented channel.

## Working with your notary on the transfer schedule

A good notary will set out a clear payment schedule tied to specific milestones, deposit on signing a preliminary agreement, further payments at defined stages, final payment on completion of transfer. Confirm this schedule in writing before sending any funds, and query anything that asks you to pay ahead of the milestone it is meant to correspond to.

## What your bank will typically ask for

Most banks handling a large international transfer for a property purchase will want to see supporting documentation: a copy of the sale-purchase agreement or PPJB, identification for both sender and recipient, and a clear statement of purpose for the transfer. Gathering these documents before you initiate the transfer, rather than scrambling once your bank asks, avoids unnecessary delay. Some banks also require the receiving account details to be confirmed in a specific format, so check with your notary exactly how the receiving account should be described on the transfer instruction.

## Coordinating timing between multiple parties

A property transfer often involves coordinating your bank, your notary, the seller's notary or representative, and potentially a currency exchange provider if you are converting funds before sending them. Each of these parties may have their own processing times and cut-off points, and a delay in any one of them can hold up the whole transaction. Building in a reasonable buffer around your target completion date, rather than assuming every step will happen exactly on schedule, reduces the stress of a purchase that is otherwise moving smoothly.

## What to do next

Plan your transfer route and timing before you are under deadline pressure, confirm the receiving account is a proper escrow or notary-verified structure, and keep full documentation of every step for your own records.

## FAQ

### What is the safest way to send money to buy a villa in Bali?
A documented bank-to-bank transfer into an escrow arrangement or a verified notary-held account, tied to specific milestones in the sale-purchase agreement, is the safest structure. Avoid sending funds directly to a seller's personal account or through informal channels.

### How long does an international transfer to Indonesia usually take?
International bank transfers can take several business days depending on the sending and receiving banks and any additional documentation required for large property-related transfers. Build this into your timeline rather than assuming same-day completion.

### Do I need to tell my home bank why I am sending a large transfer?
Most banks require you to state the purpose of a large international transfer, particularly for property purchases, and may ask for supporting documentation. Notifying your bank in advance generally speeds up the process compared with an unexplained large transfer.

### Should I ever pay a seller directly instead of through escrow?
Generally no. Paying a seller directly before rights have been verified and transferred removes your recourse if something goes wrong. A documented escrow or notary-held structure protects both sides and is standard good practice.

### Are there limits on how much money I can transfer into Indonesia for a property purchase?
