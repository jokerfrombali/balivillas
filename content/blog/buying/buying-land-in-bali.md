Foreign nationals cannot own freehold (Hak Milik) land in Indonesia, so buying land in Bali as a foreigner almost always means long-term leasehold (Hak Sewa), or, less commonly, purchasing through a PT PMA (foreign-owned company structure) that can hold a Hak Guna Bangunan (right to build) title under certain conditions. The distinction matters enormously for what you actually acquire and what happens at the end of the term, so it's worth understanding before you look at a single plot.

Most buyers we work with go the leasehold route: a long-term lease, commonly running 25-30 years with extension options, negotiated directly with the land's freehold owner and registered through a notary/PPAT. This gives you exclusive use and the right to build, sell your leasehold interest, and often extend, but you never hold the underlying freehold title. (as of 2026, verify)

## Leasehold versus the PT PMA route

Leasehold is simpler, faster to set up, and the standard route for buyers wanting a single villa or holiday property. A PT PMA structure — setting up an Indonesian foreign-owned company to hold land rights — is more common for buyers planning a commercial operation (a hotel, multiple villas, a business) and involves considerably more setup cost, ongoing compliance and reporting. For a single villa purchase, leasehold through a properly drafted agreement is almost always the more practical choice.

Whichever route, this is not a decision to make without an independent Indonesian lawyer who is not also working for the seller. The lease agreement's terms — extension rights, transferability, what happens on the freehold owner's death, dispute resolution — determine how secure your investment actually is over decades, far more than the land's location or price.

## Due diligence steps before you commit

| Step | What it checks | Who typically handles it |
|---|---|---|
| Title verification | Confirms the seller actually owns/controls the land, no competing claims | Notary/PPAT, independent lawyer |
| Zoning check | Confirms the land is zoned for the building type you intend | Architect, local government office, notary |
| Boundary survey | Confirms actual boundaries match the certificate | Licensed surveyor |
| Encumbrance check | Confirms no existing mortgage, dispute or lien | Notary/PPAT |
| Access and easement check | Confirms legal, documented access to the plot | Notary, surveyor |
| Zoning for building height/setback | Confirms buildable area and any coastal/height restrictions | Architect, local government office |

Skipping any of these to move faster is one of the most common and most expensive mistakes we see. Land that looks straightforward on a first visit can have unresolved family ownership disputes (common with inherited land in Bali), unclear boundaries, or zoning that doesn't permit the building you're planning.

## What drives land prices and why comparing per-square-metre prices is misleading

Land prices in Bali vary enormously by area, proximity to the beach or main roads, zoning, access quality and, significantly, by whether the plot has clean, straightforward title versus complications that suppress the price but add risk. A cheaper plot with murky title history is not a bargain; it's a different, larger risk wrapped in a lower number. Comparing two plots purely on price per square metre without factoring in title cleanliness, access and zoning is one of the more common ways buyers end up disappointed.

## Access and easements are worth checking specifically

A plot with no direct, documented legal access to a public road — relying instead on an informal path across a neighbour's land — is a real and recurring risk in Bali, particularly on land away from main roads. This can block or complicate a building permit application and creates ongoing dependency on a neighbour's goodwill. ## A practical checklist before signing a land deal

1. Confirm you understand exactly what right you're acquiring — leasehold term, extension terms, transferability.
2. Verify title through an independent notary/PPAT and lawyer, not one recommended solely by the seller.
3. Confirm zoning permits your intended building type and check any height or setback restrictions.
4. Verify documented legal access to a public road.
5. Get a professional boundary survey rather than relying on the seller's description.
6. Have the lease or purchase agreement reviewed by your own independent lawyer before signing anything, including a deposit agreement.

## FAQ

### Can foreigners own land outright in Bali?

No, foreigners cannot hold freehold (Hak Milik) land directly. Almost all foreign buyers use long-term leasehold (Hak Sewa) agreements, or, for commercial purposes, a PT PMA company structure that can hold different land rights. Always confirm current rules with an independent Indonesian lawyer before committing. (as of 2026, verify)

### How long do leasehold agreements typically run in Bali?

Terms vary by agreement, but 25-30 years with extension options is a common structure, negotiated directly with the freehold owner. The specific term, extension rights and transferability should be checked in each individual lease agreement rather than assumed from general norms. (as of 2026, verify)

### What's the biggest risk when buying land in Bali?

Unclear or disputed title is the biggest risk, particularly on inherited family land where multiple heirs may have competing claims. Independent title verification through a notary/PPAT and a lawyer not connected to the seller is the main protection against this.

### Do I need a PT PMA company to buy land in Bali?

Only if you're planning a commercial operation (multiple properties, a hotel, an active business). For a single villa purchase, leasehold through a properly drafted agreement is simpler, faster and the more common route for individual buyers.

### Is cheaper land in Bali ever a legitimate bargain?

Sometimes, but a lower price often reflects a real complication — unclear title, poor access, unfavourable zoning — rather than genuine value. Always price in the cost and risk of resolving any issue before treating a low price as a bargain.
