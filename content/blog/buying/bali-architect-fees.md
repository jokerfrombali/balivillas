Bali architect fees are usually quoted as a percentage of the estimated construction budget, typically somewhere in the 5-12% range depending on how much of the process the architect handles, or as a fixed fee broken into stages (concept, developed design, construction drawings, site supervision). Neither number tells you much on its own; what matters is which services are actually included and what triggers extra charges.

If you're buying land and planning to build rather than buying a finished villa, the architect is one of three professionals you'll need alongside a contractor and, in most cases, a local notary (PPAT) to handle the land and permit side. Getting the fee structure clear before you sign anything saves arguments six months into a build.

## How Bali architects usually structure their fees

Three models are common on the island, and it's worth asking which one a firm is proposing before comparing quotes.

**Percentage of construction cost.** The architect's fee scales with the build budget, so a US$400,000 villa and a US$150,000 villa pay proportionally different fees even for similar drawing work. This model aligns incentives loosely with build quality but can inflate fees on expensive finishes that have nothing to do with the architect's actual workload.

**Fixed fee per project stage.** You pay set amounts for concept design, developed design, permit drawings, construction documents and site supervision, agreed upfront regardless of final build cost. This is easier to budget against and more common with smaller local studios.

**Fixed fee per square metre of building area.** Less common but used by some studios for straightforward villa layouts, priced per square metre of built area rather than land size.

Whichever model you agree, get it in writing with a payment schedule tied to deliverables, not just dates. A schedule tied to "concept approved", "permit drawings submitted", "construction 50% complete" protects you if the timeline slips.

## What's typically included and what isn't

| Service | Usually included | Often billed separately |
|---|---|---|
| Concept design and 3D visuals | Yes | — |
| Construction drawings (for the builder) | Yes | — |
| Permit application drawings | Sometimes | Often a separate line item |
| Structural engineering | Rarely | Almost always separate |
| Interior design / FF&E | Rarely | Almost always separate |
| Site supervision visits | Sometimes, limited number | Extra visits beyond the agreed count |
| Revisions beyond an agreed round count | Rarely | Yes, usually per revision |

The single biggest cause of fee disputes we see is revisions. Most contracts include two or three rounds of design changes; a fourth round, or a change after construction drawings are finalised, is billed as extra work. Ask upfront how many rounds are included and what a "round" means (a full re-draw versus a minor tweak).

Structural engineering and interior design are the two costs buyers most often assume are bundled and then find are not. If you want a single point of contact, ask the architect directly whether they subcontract structural engineering in-house or whether you need to hire a separate structural engineer, because that's a real cost and a real coordination point either way.

## Questions worth asking before you sign

- Does the fee cover permit drawings for the current IMB/PBG process, or is that billed separately? (as of 2026, verify)
- How many revision rounds are included at each stage, and what counts as a round?
- Is site supervision included, and how many visits per month?
- Who is responsible if the contractor's build diverges from the drawings — does the architect flag it, and is that covered by the fee?
- Can we see a project the architect completed in the same area (Sanur, Canggu, Umalas, Ungasan) at a similar budget?
- What happens to the fee if we pause or cancel the project partway through?

That last question matters more than buyers expect. Some contracts have cancellation clauses that require paying the full stage fee even if the stage is only partly complete; others prorate. Read this before you sign, not after a delay forces the question.

## How architect fees fit into the total build budget

Architect fees are one line among several when you're pricing a villa build from land purchase to handover. The main categories are land, permits and legal, design (architect plus any structural engineer), construction, and contingency. As a rough guide for how these pieces relate, contingency alone is often underestimated: budgeting 10-15% above the contractor's quote for unforeseen site conditions, material price changes or design changes during construction is a sensible minimum on Bali sites, where soil conditions and access can differ significantly between two plots a few hundred metres apart.

If you're weighing whether to hire an architect at all versus using a design-and-build contractor who provides in-house design, the trade-off is oversight versus cost. A separate architect gives you an independent set of eyes checking the contractor's work against the drawings, which is valuable given how much can go wrong on a self-managed build in Bali — see our guide on [common construction mistakes](/blog/buying/bali-construction-mistakes/) for the pattern of issues an independent architect typically catches. A design-and-build contractor can be cheaper and faster but removes that check.

## What to check before you commit to an architect

Before signing, ask to see the architect's professional registration (IAI membership is common for licensed Indonesian architects) and a portfolio of completed, not just rendered, villas. Renders are easy; a finished building that matches its renders after two rainy seasons is the real test. Ask for the names of two or three recent clients you can contact directly, and actually call them.

Confirm whether the architect coordinates the permit application themselves or hands it to the developer/notary. On foreign-owned leasehold land this coordination between architect, notary and the local village office (desa) can be the slowest part of the process — see our [construction timeline guide](/blog/buying/bali-villa-construction-timeline/) for how the permit stage typically sequences against design and building work.

Finally, check the currency and payment method the contract specifies. Fees quoted in US dollars but payable in rupiah at the time of invoice can shift meaningfully if the exchange rate moves during a long project; agree upfront whether the rate is locked at contract signing or floats.

## A simple checklist before you sign an architect agreement

1. Confirm the fee model (percentage, fixed-per-stage, or per-square-metre) and get the total estimated range in writing.
2. List every deliverable per stage and what counts as a completed milestone for payment.
3. Clarify what's excluded: structural engineering, interior design, permit drawings, extra site visits, extra revisions.
4. Ask for two contactable references with completed, occupied villas.
5. Confirm the payment currency and whether the rate is fixed or floating.
6. Get a cancellation/pause clause reviewed by your own lawyer, not just the architect's.

## FAQ

### How much does an architect cost for a villa in Bali?

There's no single number; fees depend on the model (percentage of build cost versus fixed fee per stage) and what's included. Percentage-based fees commonly fall in a broad single-digit-to-low-teens range of the construction budget, but always get a written, itemised quote rather than relying on a headline percentage, since inclusions vary widely between studios.

### Do I need an architect if my contractor offers design-and-build?

Not strictly, but an independent architect gives you a second set of eyes checking the contractor's work against agreed drawings, which matters on a self-managed build. Design-and-build can be cheaper and faster with less coordination, but you lose that independent check, so weigh this against your own ability to supervise the site yourself.

### Can the architect also handle my building permit?

Some do, some hand it to the developer or notary handling your land purchase. Ask explicitly before signing, because permit coordination (IMB/PBG and any local desa approvals) is often the slowest part of a build, and it matters who owns responsibility for chasing it. (as of 2026, verify)

### What causes architect fees to go over budget?

Revisions beyond the agreed rounds are the most common cause, followed by scope not written into the original contract, such as structural engineering or interior design assumed to be included. Get the revision count and exclusions in writing at signing.

### Is it normal to pay architect fees before construction starts?

Yes, design fees are typically paid in stages as design work progresses, well before construction begins, since design has to be finished before a contractor can price and build from it. Construction costs are a separate, later payment stream to the builder, not the architect.
