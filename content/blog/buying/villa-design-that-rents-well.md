The villas that book consistently in Bali share design traits that have very little to do with luxury finishes and everything to do with flexibility and how the layout matches actual guest patterns: bedroom configurations that suit both couples and families, a pool and living area that feel connected rather than separated, decent workspace in more than one room, and a kitchen guests will actually use. Building or buying with rental performance in mind means designing around these patterns, not around what looks best in a render.

We manage villas across Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan and see the booking data on layouts that work versus layouts that sit empty at the same price point. This isn't about square metres or finish quality — plenty of expensively finished villas underperform because the layout doesn't match how guests actually use the space.

## Bedroom configuration matters more than bedroom count

A villa with all bedrooms the same size and all en-suite is more flexible to book than one with a large master and small secondary rooms, because it can serve a group of friends splitting costs equally as easily as a family. Two- and three-bedroom villas with equal-sized, all en-suite bedrooms consistently have wider guest appeal than an equivalent villa with an obviously "lesser" second bedroom, because groups negotiating who gets which room is a real friction point in group bookings.

If you're designing from scratch, consider whether bedrooms can be closed off or opened up — some owners design a layout where one bedroom has a separate entrance and lockable connecting door, so it can be rented as part of the main villa or, later, run somewhat independently. This adds flexibility at relatively low extra design cost.

## The pool and living area need to feel like one space

Guests choosing a private-pool villa in Bali are choosing it largely for the experience of being near the pool while relaxing indoors — cooking, working, or just sitting. A layout where the pool is visually and physically disconnected from the main living space (behind a wall, down a level, screened by a solid fence) consistently underperforms compared to an open-plan living area that faces directly onto the pool and garden.

This is one of the most common regrets we hear from owners who built rather than bought: a beautiful pool that guests can't see from the sofa gets far fewer "we loved it here" reviews than a modest pool guests can see from the kitchen while cooking dinner.

## Workspace has become a real booking factor

A meaningful share of villa bookings, particularly for stays of a week or longer, now come from remote workers who need functional desk space, not just a coffee table. Villas with a proper desk and decent lighting in more than one bedroom — so two people staying together can both work without competing for the same spot — book noticeably better for longer stays than villas with a single shared workspace or none at all. Fast, reliable Wi-Fi throughout the property, not just near the router, matters just as much as the desk itself.

## A comparison of design choices and their rental impact

| Design element | Rents better | Rents worse |
|---|---|---|
| Bedroom sizing | Equal-sized, all en-suite | One obviously smaller/lesser room |
| Pool placement | Visible and open from living area | Separated, screened, or down a level |
| Workspace | Desk with good light in 2+ rooms | Single shared desk or none |
| Kitchen | Full, usable kitchen guests will cook in | Decorative kitchen, minimal equipment |
| Outdoor space | Covered alfresco dining, usable in rain | Uncovered dining only |
| Layout flexibility | Bedrooms can close off/open independently | Fixed, single-configuration layout |

## Design choices that matter less than owners expect

Some choices buyers assume drive bookings actually have a smaller effect than layout fundamentals: high-end designer furniture versus good-quality comfortable furniture rarely moves booking rates much on its own, and neither does pool size beyond a functional length for swimming. Guests notice and value a villa that photographs well and works practically far more than they value a specific furniture brand. This is worth knowing before overspending on finishes at the expense of layout fundamentals.

## Outdoor space and weather resilience

Bali's rainy season (roughly November to March) means a villa's outdoor dining and lounging areas need to be usable in rain, not just in sun, if you want consistent bookings year-round rather than a strongly seasonal pattern. A covered alfresco dining area, positioned so it doesn't flood or become unusable in heavy rain, extends the villa's usability across more of the year and is a design decision worth prioritising over purely aesthetic outdoor features. See our guide on [building materials for Bali's climate](/blog/buying/building-materials-bali-climate/) for how material and structural choices affect how well outdoor spaces hold up.

## Designing for the area you're building in

Design priorities shift somewhat by area. In Sanur, where families and longer stays are common, functional kitchens, multiple workspaces and a calm, enclosed garden matter more than proximity to nightlife. In Canggu, groups and shorter stays put more weight on social living spaces and proximity to the beach or cafés. Umalas sits between the two, suiting quieter, workspace-focused stays. Ungasan's clifftop sites often trade some flexibility for view, which can still rent very well if the view is genuinely used from the main living space rather than a single balcony.

## A design checklist before finalising layout

- Are bedrooms similarly sized and all en-suite, or is there an obviously lesser room?
- Can the living area see and access the pool directly, without walls or screens breaking the connection?
- Is there proper desk space with good light in at least two rooms?
- Is outdoor dining covered and usable in heavy rain?
- Is the kitchen a full, usable kitchen rather than a decorative one?
- Does the layout allow any flexibility (closing off a bedroom, separate entrance) for different guest group sizes?

## FAQ

### Do more bedrooms always mean better rental performance?

Not necessarily — bedroom flexibility and quality matter more than raw count. A well-designed 2-bedroom villa with equal-sized, en-suite rooms can outperform a poorly laid out 3-bedroom villa with an obviously lesser third room, because the smaller villa is easier to book confidently for a range of group types.

### Does pool size affect how well a villa rents?

Beyond a functional size for swimming and lounging, pool size matters less than pool placement and visibility from the living area. A modest pool that's visually connected to indoor living space tends to outperform a larger pool that's screened off or hard to see from where guests actually relax.

### How much does workspace design really affect bookings?

For stays of a week or longer, meaningfully — remote workers are a real and growing share of villa bookings, and functional desk space with good light in more than one room is now a genuine differentiator, not a nice-to-have. Reliable Wi-Fi throughout the property matters just as much as the desk itself.

### Should I design differently for Sanur versus Canggu?

Yes, guest expectations differ by area. Sanur favours families and longer, calmer stays where functional kitchens and workspace matter most; Canggu favours groups and social living space closer to the beach and cafés. Design to the guest profile most likely to book in your specific area.

### Is it worth spending more on designer furniture to boost bookings?

Generally not as much as buyers expect — comfortable, good-quality furniture that photographs well performs similarly to designer-branded pieces in booking terms. Layout fundamentals (bedroom flexibility, pool connection, workspace) have a far larger effect on booking performance than furniture brand.
