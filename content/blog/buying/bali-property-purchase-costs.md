The cost of buying property in Bali is the advertised price plus a set of additional costs that regularly catches first-time buyers off guard: transfer tax (BPHTB), notary and PPAT fees, due diligence, and sometimes a separate cost to formalise permits that were never completed. Budget an extra allowance on top of the purchase price for these, rather than assuming the sticker price is the whole number.

None of these additional costs are hidden exactly, but they are rarely front and centre in a listing, and the exact figures depend on the transaction structure (leasehold versus freehold-equivalent arrangements), the property's value, and which professionals you engage. Below is what typically makes up the total, and where the real variation between two similar-looking deals usually comes from.

## The main cost components

| Component | What it covers | Who typically pays |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | The property itself | Buyer |
| BPHTB (transfer tax) | Government tax on the transaction | Buyer, in most structures |
| Notary/PPAT fees | Drafting agreements, registering the deed | Buyer, sometimes split |
| Due diligence costs | Land certificate, permit and zoning checks | Buyer |
| Registration and administrative fees | Formal recording of the transaction | Buyer |
| Any outstanding permit costs | Closing gaps found in due diligence | Negotiable |

Each of these varies by transaction, and the exact percentages for tax and notary fees should be confirmed with your notary before you commit, since they depend on the transaction value and structure (as of 2026, verify current figures directly rather than relying on older published rates).

## Why leasehold and freehold-equivalent structures cost differently

Most foreign buyers in Bali purchase villas on long-term leasehold, since direct freehold ownership of land is generally restricted to Indonesian citizens. Leasehold transactions and other structures available to foreign buyers each carry different tax and fee treatments, and the notary fee itself may be calculated differently depending on which structure applies. This is one of the clearest reasons two villas at a similar price can end up costing meaningfully different totals to actually acquire; ask your notary to model the full cost for the specific structure on offer, not a generic figure.

## Due diligence costs are not optional

Skipping due diligence to save a relatively small upfront cost is the single most common mistake we see described by buyers after the fact. Verifying the land certificate, zoning (PKKPR), building permit (PBG) and function certificate (SLF) costs money and time, but finding a problem before you pay is vastly cheaper than finding it after. Our guide on buying a villa without permits covers what can go wrong when this step gets skipped.

## A realistic budgeting approach

1. Get the base purchase price confirmed in writing, including exactly what is included (furniture, fittings, pool equipment)
2. Ask your notary for a written estimate of BPHTB, their own fees, and registration costs for this specific transaction
3. Budget separately for due diligence, even if the villa looks clean on paper
4. Add a contingency for any permit gaps identified during due diligence
5. Confirm currency and payment method requirements, since Indonesian property transactions are settled in rupiah

## Ongoing costs after the purchase

The purchase transaction is a one-off cost, but owning a villa in Bali carries ongoing costs too: land and building tax (PBB), maintenance, staff if you are not using a management company, insurance, and utilities. Our guide on Bali villa holding costs covers what owning a villa actually costs year to year, separate from the one-time purchase costs covered here.

## Is buying property in Bali as a foreigner still a good investment?

That depends on your goals, the specific property, and how carefully the purchase itself is structured. A villa bought with full due diligence, clean permits and a properly costed transaction behaves very differently as an asset than one bought quickly on an attractive headline price with gaps discovered later. The cost of getting the purchase itself right is usually small relative to the value of avoiding a problem that surfaces years down the line.

| Stage | Typical timing |
|---|---|

| Due diligence costs incurred | During the process, before final payment |
| BPHTB and registration fees | At or shortly after signing |
| Notary/PPAT fees | Spread across the process, confirmed upfront |

## FAQ

### What percentage should I budget on top of the purchase price?
The exact figure depends on the transaction structure and value, and should be confirmed with your notary in writing before you commit; do not rely on a generic percentage quoted informally, since tax and fee treatment vary by structure (as of 2026, verify).

### Is BPHTB the only tax I need to pay when buying?
BPHTB (transfer tax) is the main transaction tax, but ongoing land and building tax (PBB) applies once you own the property, and separate tax treatment may apply depending on the transaction structure. Our guide on BPHTB covers the transfer tax specifically.

### Can I negotiate who pays notary fees, buyer or seller?
Yes, this is negotiable between the parties and should be agreed in writing before the transaction proceeds. Convention varies, so do not assume a default without confirming it explicitly.

### Are there extra costs specific to buying property in Bali for Airbnb-style rental?
Formalising a villa for short-term rental can involve additional licensing and compliance costs beyond the purchase itself, which are separate from the transaction costs covered here. Ask specifically about this if rental income is your goal.

### Do I need to pay in a specific currency?
Property transactions in Indonesia are settled in rupiah. If your funds are in another currency, factor in exchange rate timing and any transfer costs as part of your overall budget.
