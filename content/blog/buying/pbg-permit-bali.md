A PBG, Persetujuan Bangunan Gedung, is Indonesia's current building permit, the document confirming that a specific structure's construction is legally approved. It replaced the older IMB (Izin Mendirikan Bangunan) system, so older villas may still hold an IMB rather than a PBG, and confirming which applies, and whether it is valid, is a core part of due diligence before buying either a completed or off-plan villa.

This guide covers what a PBG actually confirms, how it relates to the PKKPR and SLF, and what to check as a buyer.

## Where PBG sits in the permit sequence

The general sequence is: PKKPR confirms zoning compatibility, PBG confirms the building's construction plan is approved, and SLF (Sertifikat Laik Fungsi) confirms the completed building is safe and fit for occupation. A villa without a valid PBG, or the older IMB it may have been built under, carries real risk regardless of how well-built it looks. Our guide on [PKKPR in Bali](/blog/buying/pkkpr-bali/) covers the step that comes before this one, and our guide on the [SLF certificate](/blog/buying/slf-certificate-bali/) covers the step that comes after.

## What to check on a completed villa

If you are buying a finished villa, ask specifically for either a valid PBG or, for an older building, the original IMB and confirmation of its current status under the newer system (as of 2026, confirm transition rules with a notary or licensed consultant, since the handling of older IMB permits under the PBG system has been clarified progressively). Do not accept a seller's assurance that "permits are fine" without seeing the actual documents.

## What to check on an off-plan or under-construction villa

For off-plan purchases, confirm the PBG has actually been approved for the specific building plan you are buying into, not just applied for. Our guide on [off-plan villa risks in Bali](/blog/buying/off-plan-villa-risks-bali/) covers this and other risks specific to buying before construction is complete, and our comparison of [off-plan versus completed villas](/blog/buying/off-plan-vs-ready-villa-bali/) is worth reading if you are still deciding between the two paths.

## Permit sequence at a glance

| Document | What it confirms | Comes before |
|---|---|---|
| PKKPR | Zoning compatibility for intended use | PBG |
| PBG (or older IMB) | Construction plan is approved | SLF |
| SLF | Completed building is safe and fit for occupation | Legal operation of the finished villa |

## Why this matters even if you plan to rent the villa out

A villa without valid permits carries risk beyond a resale or legal technicality: it can affect your ability to operate it as a rental business cleanly, and it complicates any future sale, since the next buyer's due diligence will surface the same gap. If you plan to have the villa professionally managed, our guide on [villa management fees in Bali](/blog/owners/villa-management-fees-bali/) assumes a villa with clean permits as the baseline for that relationship.

## What renovation or extension work means for an existing PBG

If you plan to renovate or extend a villa after purchase, check whether the planned work falls within the scope of the existing PBG or IMB, or whether it requires a new or amended permit application. Even relatively modest changes, adding a room, significantly altering the roofline, can trigger a new permit requirement, and doing the work without one carries the same risk as an unpermitted new build. Raise your renovation plans with a notary or licensed consultant before, not after, starting work, so you know exactly what additional approval, if any, is needed.

## How this affects financing and insurance

Some financing arrangements and insurance policies for villas in Bali expect valid permit documentation as a condition, so a missing or unclear PBG can complicate more than just a future resale. If you plan to insure the villa or use it as security for any financing, confirm with the relevant provider what permit documentation they require, since standards can vary between providers.

## A due diligence checklist for permits specifically

1. Ask for the specific PBG (or IMB for older builds) document, not a verbal assurance
2. Confirm the document matches the actual built structure, not an earlier or different plan
3. For off-plan purchases, confirm PBG approval status before paying beyond a small deposit
4. Check whether an SLF has been issued for a completed building
5. If any permit is missing, treat it as a real cost and risk to factor into your offer, not a minor formality

## What to do next

Never treat permit documentation as secondary to the villa's appearance or price. A missing PBG, or an unclear transition from an older IMB, is a real legal and financial risk that should shape your offer or your decision to walk away.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between a PBG and the older IMB?
The PBG (Persetujuan Bangunan Gedung) is the current building permit system, replacing the older IMB (Izin Mendirikan Bangunan). Older villas may still hold an IMB; confirm its current status and validity with a notary rather than assuming it is equivalent to a modern PBG.

### Do I need to see the actual PBG document before buying a villa?
Yes. Never rely on a seller's verbal assurance that permits are in order; ask for the specific document and have a notary confirm it matches the actual built structure and remains valid.

### Is a PBG required before building starts, or after?
Before. The PBG confirms the construction plan is approved, and construction should not begin, and certainly should not be relied upon by a buyer, before it is in place.

### What happens if a villa was built without a proper PBG or IMB?
It creates real legal and practical risk, potentially complicating a resale, formal rental operation, or future compliance action. Treat a missing permit as a material issue in due diligence, not a minor paperwork gap.

### How does PBG relate to the SLF certificate?
The PBG approves the construction plan before or during building; the SLF (Sertifikat Laik Fungsi) confirms the completed building is safe and fit for occupation. Both are separate documents a buyer should confirm.
