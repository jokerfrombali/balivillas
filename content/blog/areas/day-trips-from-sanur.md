Sanur sits on Bali's east coast, roughly central for reaching most of the island, which makes it a workable base for day trips without being the fastest option for any single direction. The most popular day trip from Sanur is Nusa Penida, reachable only by boat from Sanur's own harbour. Ubud and central Bali are a straightforward drive. West-coast destinations like Uluwatu take longer than most guests expect.

Here's what genuinely fits into a day, what doesn't, and how to plan around Bali's traffic, which is the biggest variable in any of this.

## Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan by boat

Sanur harbour is the main departure point for fast boats to the Nusa islands, and it's the day trip most guests staying with us actually take. Boats typically run from early morning, and a full day trip covering Nusa Penida's main viewpoints usually means an early start (boat around 7:30–8am) and a return by late afternoon or early evening.

Book a day or two ahead in high season, since popular morning departures fill up. We've written a dedicated guide to the Sanur–Nusa Penida ferry covering what's included, what costs extra, and how to compare operators.

## Ubud and central Bali

Ubud is roughly 45–60 minutes from Sanur by car outside peak traffic, making it one of the more comfortable day trips on this list. A day trip typically covers the rice terraces, a temple or two, and the town centre for lunch and shopping, though Ubud rewards more time than a single rushed day if you can spare it.

Leave by mid-morning at the latest to avoid the worst of the traffic in both directions, and expect the return leg in late afternoon to be the slower one.

## Uluwatu and the Bukit Peninsula

Uluwatu, at Bali's southern tip, is roughly 60–75 minutes from Sanur in reasonable traffic, longer during peak hours or on days with events. It's doable as a day trip for the temple and cliff views, but combining it with a beach club day or a sunset kecak performance usually means a longer day than guests plan for.

## Canggu and Seminyak

These are closer to a half-day-plus trip than a quick outing — about 45–75 minutes each way depending on traffic and time of day. Worth it if you want to compare Bali's west coast scene, or want a specific restaurant or beach club, but not a casual afternoon swap.

## Closer options that don't need a full day

Not every trip out of Sanur needs to eat your whole day. Denpasar city, with its markets and some cultural sites, is 20–30 minutes away. The temples and beaches along the Sanur coast itself, covered in our things-to-do-in-Sanur guide, don't require leaving the area at all.

## Booking a driver versus self-driving

For most day trips from Sanur, hiring a driver for the day is the more practical option, particularly for Ubud or Uluwatu where the return leg often coincides with heavy traffic. Drivers know the current traffic patterns and back-road alternatives better than a rental car GPS, and it removes the fatigue of navigating unfamiliar roads after a full day out.

Self-driving a scooter works for shorter, more local trips but isn't the best choice for longer day trips given the distances and traffic involved; a car with a driver is the more common choice among guests doing a full-day Ubud or Uluwatu trip from Sanur.

## Combining two destinations in one day

It's tempting to combine two day-trip destinations — say, Ubud in the morning and a stop near Uluwatu on the way back — but Bali's distances and traffic make this harder than it looks on a map. Two full destinations in one day generally means less time at each and a late return, so it's worth being realistic about how much ground you can cover comfortably rather than overpacking the itinerary.

## Planning around traffic

Bali's traffic is the single biggest variable in any day trip estimate. Roads toward the airport and south toward Uluwatu get particularly congested in the late afternoon. If your day trip has a fixed return time — a boat, a restaurant booking, a flight the next morning — build in buffer time rather than trusting a single drive-time estimate.

- Leave early (before 8am) for any day trip that involves a return drive south or west
- Check boat departure times for Nusa Penida a day or two ahead in high season
- Keep a loose afternoon plan rather than a tight schedule if you're driving both ways

## FAQ

### Can I visit Nusa Penida as a day trip from Sanur?

Yes, and it's the most common day trip from Sanur since the harbour is right there. Boats typically leave early morning, and a full day covering the main viewpoints usually returns by late afternoon or early evening. Book ahead in high season.

### How far is Sanur from the airport?

Sanur is a relatively short drive from Ngurah Rai International Airport, generally well under an hour outside peak traffic, though times vary with time of day and route.

### Is Ubud a good day trip from Sanur?

Yes, it's one of the more comfortable day trips from Sanur at roughly 45–60 minutes each way. Leave by mid-morning to avoid the worst traffic and allow extra time for the return leg in late afternoon.

### Is Uluwatu too far for a day trip from Sanur?

It's doable but longer than many guests expect — around 60–75 minutes each way in reasonable traffic. It works well as a day trip for the temple and cliffs, but pairing it with a sunset show or beach club day often runs later than planned.

### What's the easiest day trip from Sanur if I don't want to travel far?

Denpasar city is roughly 20–30 minutes away for markets and cultural sites, and Sanur's own coast — the beach promenade, local temples and market — offers a full day without leaving the area at all.
