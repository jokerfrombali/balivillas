Canggu is best known for surf and cafés, and that reputation is well earned, but it isn't the whole picture for a stay of a week or longer. The area also has rice terraces within a short ride, temples along the coast, a real local market, and enough day-trip range to fill out a longer visit without repeating the same beach day.

## Surf and beaches, the obvious starting point

Batu Bolong and Echo Beach cover the two main surf-focused beaches, with Batu Bolong more beginner-friendly and Echo Beach better suited to more experienced surfers. Even non-surfers find the beaches worth a visit for sunset, when both fill up with a relaxed crowd watching the sky over the water. Our dedicated guide to surfing in Canggu for beginners covers lessons and timing in more depth.

## Rice terraces a short ride away

Canggu sits close enough to inland rice terraces — including areas near Pererenan and further toward Tabanan — that a half-day trip to walk through them is realistic without a long drive. Early morning is the best time, both for light and to avoid the day's heat.

## Temples along the coast and inland

Tanah Lot, one of Bali's most recognisable coastal temples, is within a manageable drive from Canggu and a common half-day or sunset trip. Smaller temples sit closer within the Canggu area itself, worth a stop if you're already out and about rather than a dedicated trip.

## The local market and everyday Canggu

Away from the café strips, Canggu still has working local markets where day-to-day shopping happens, offering a different, more local side of the area than the tourist-focused streets. It's a good stop for produce, snacks and a sense of daily life if you want a break from café culture.

## Yoga and wellness

Canggu has a strong yoga and wellness scene, with studios offering drop-in classes across styles and levels, often alongside healthy-eating cafés. This is one of the more consistent daily activities for longer-stay guests, requiring no advance planning beyond checking a studio's schedule.

## Shopping beyond the beach strips

Beyond surf shops and boutiques on the main strips, Canggu has a growing concentration of independent design and homeware shops, particularly around Batu Bolong and Berawa, worth an afternoon if that interests you.

## Day trips beyond Canggu itself

Ubud, roughly an hour inland depending on traffic, is a common full-day trip for rice terraces, temples and the town's arts scene at a larger scale than what's immediately around Canggu. Uluwatu, at Bali's southern tip, is also a realistic day trip for cliffside temples and beach clubs, generally 45–75 minutes depending on traffic and route.

## Cultural experiences beyond temples

Beyond the well-known coastal and inland temples, Canggu and the surrounding area offer occasional access to local ceremonies and cultural events, particularly around Balinese holidays and temple anniversaries (odalan). These aren't scheduled for tourists specifically, so ask locally or check with your villa host about what's happening during your stay if this interests you, and always dress and behave respectfully if you do attend.

## Photography and viewpoints

Canggu's rice terraces, the coastline around Echo Beach and Batu Bolong, and Tanah Lot at sunset are the area's most photographed spots, though a short ride further inland toward the Pererenan back lanes often turns up quieter, less crowded rice-field views with a similar visual payoff and none of the crowds.

## Building a week that doesn't repeat itself

A common pattern among longer-stay guests: mornings for surf or yoga, midday for work or rest, afternoons split between café time and one bigger activity (a temple visit, market trip or rice terrace walk) every couple of days, evenings varying between quiet nights at the villa and occasional trips into Batu Bolong or Berawa.

## FAQ

### Is there more to do in Canggu than surfing and cafés?

Yes — rice terraces, coastal and inland temples, local markets, a strong yoga scene and easy day trips to Ubud or Uluwatu all sit within reach of a Canggu base, alongside the surf and café culture it's known for.

### How far is Tanah Lot from Canggu?

It's a manageable half-day trip from most parts of Canggu, commonly done around sunset for the temple's coastal setting, though exact drive time depends on your specific starting point and traffic.

### Is Canggu good for a week-long trip?

Yes, particularly if you mix beach and café time with at least a couple of inland or cultural day trips — rice terraces, temples, or a full day out to Ubud — rather than staying within the beach-facing strips the whole time.

### Does Canggu have good yoga studios?

Yes, it has one of Bali's more established yoga and wellness scenes, with drop-in classes across styles and levels, often paired with healthy-eating cafés nearby.

### Can I visit Ubud as a day trip from Canggu?

Yes, it's a common full-day trip, generally around an hour's drive depending on traffic, covering rice terraces, temples and Ubud's town centre and arts scene.
