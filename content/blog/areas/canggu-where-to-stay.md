Where to stay in Canggu comes down to picking one of four or five pockets, not the town as a whole. Canggu is not a single strip: Batu Bolong and Berawa sit close to the surf and the beach clubs, Pererenan is quieter and greener, and the area inland toward Tumbak Bayuh trades a short walk to the beach for lower traffic. Pick the pocket first, then the villa.

We manage two villas in Canggu ourselves, both in the Berawa area near the beach, so this guide leans on what we see guests ask about every week: noise, traffic, walking distance and price.

## The main sub-areas and who they suit

Canggu is really five or six neighbourhoods stitched together by one road system that gets congested at the edges of it. Knowing which pocket you are booking in matters more than knowing you are "in Canggu."

- **Batu Bolong** — the original strip, walkable, dense with cafés and shops, busiest traffic and least private.
- **Berawa** — beach clubs, larger villas, a five to ten minute drive or a 15–20 minute walk to the sand depending on the street.
- **Pererenan** — quieter, greener, a newer wave of cafés and villas, a short scooter ride to the surf breaks.
- **Echo Beach / Batu Mejan** — surf-focused, sunset crowds, more compact accommodation.
- **Tumbak Bayuh / inland Canggu** — cheaper, quieter, 10–15 minutes by scooter to the beach.

If your priority is walking to a beach club, Berawa or Echo Beach. If it is working from a villa with fewer distractions, Pererenan or inland Canggu.

## Traffic changes the calculus more than distance does

A villa "2 km from the beach" can mean a five-minute ride or a 20-minute crawl depending on the road and the time of day. Canggu's main arteries (Jalan Batu Bolong, Jalan Pantai Berawa, Jalan Raya Canggu) back up in the late afternoon and around sunset. When you compare listings, ask which road the villa sits off, not just the straight-line distance to the beach.

This is the single biggest thing that separates a relaxed week from a frustrating one: two villas that look identical on a map can have a 15-minute gap in real travel time.

## Getting around once you are there

Most guests rent a scooter for Canggu; a car with a driver is the alternative for families or anyone who prefers not to ride in traffic. Grab and Gojek work in most of Canggu but can be slow to arrive at peak times, and some streets restrict ride-hailing pickup, so confirm with your villa host how guests usually get around from that specific street.

## FAQ

### Is Canggu or Ubud better for a first Bali trip?
They serve different trips. Canggu is coastal, beach-club and surf-focused with reliable Wi-Fi for remote work; Ubud is inland, quieter, rice-terrace and wellness-focused with more traffic to get anywhere. Most first-time visitors who want beach time and nightlife pick Canggu; those prioritising yoga, art and jungle scenery pick Ubud, and some split a two-week trip between both.

### Is Canggu or Seminyak better to stay in?
Seminyak is more polished and closer to Kuta's nightlife strip, with higher-end shopping; Canggu is more surf and café-focused, with a younger, longer-stay crowd and generally more villa (versus hotel) accommodation. Both have similar traffic issues at peak times, so the choice is mostly about atmosphere rather than practicality.

### What is the best area to stay in Canggu for families?
Berawa and Pererenan work best for families: villas tend to have larger private pools, streets are calmer than central Batu Bolong, and both are still a short drive to cafés and shops. Confirm the pool is fenced or supervised if you are travelling with toddlers.

### How far is Canggu from the airport?
Canggu is roughly 45–75 minutes from Ngurah Rai International Airport depending on traffic and which part of Canggu you are headed to; allow extra time in the late afternoon.

### Do I need a scooter to get around Canggu?
Not strictly, but most guests find it the most practical option given the traffic and the spread-out pockets. Grab, Gojek and drivers are workable alternatives if you would rather not ride.
