Plenty of visitors do a full Bali trip without ever getting on a scooter, and it works fine as long as you plan transport rather than assume it. The three practical options are: a walkable villa base, ride-hailing apps for point-to-point trips, and a private driver by the day for anything further afield.

Scooters are everywhere in Bali because they're cheap and cut through traffic, but they're also the biggest source of injuries and travel-insurance headaches for visitors, and plenty of people simply don't want to ride one on unfamiliar roads. None of that has to stop you having a good trip.

## Why people skip the scooter

The most common reasons our guests give: no scooter licence or experience, nervousness about Bali's traffic and road surfaces, travelling with children, or a bad experience on a previous trip. Bali's roads mix scooters, cars, trucks and pedestrians with inconsistent lane discipline, and injury rates among tourist riders are a well-known concern locally — enough that many travel insurers exclude scooter accidents unless you hold a valid licence and wear a helmet.

## What actually works instead

- **A walkable base.** Staying somewhere you can cover daily errands on foot removes most of the need for transport in the first place. Sanur's beachfront path is the strongest example among the areas we manage villas in.
- **Ride-hailing apps.** Grab and Gojek both operate in Bali's tourist areas and let you book a car (or a scooter driven by someone else, if you want speed without riding yourself) for a fixed, visible price.
- **A private driver by the day.** For day trips — temples, waterfalls, Ubud, Nusa Penida ferry terminals — a driver hired for the day is often more comfortable and not dramatically more expensive than piecing together multiple taxi rides.
- **Airport transfers arranged in advance.** Avoids arriving tired and having to negotiate transport on the spot.

## Comparing your options

| Option | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Walking (Sanur beach path, parts of Umalas) | Daily coffee, beach, small errands | Only works in a genuinely walkable area |
| Ride-hailing app (car) | Point-to-point trips, restaurants | Waiting time varies, surge pricing at peak times |
| Ride-hailing app (scooter driver) | Fast, short trips without riding yourself | Less space for luggage or shopping |
| Private driver, full or half day | Day trips, multiple stops, families | Costs more than a single taxi ride, book in advance in high season |

## Choosing where to stay if you won't ride

Area choice matters more than any single transport decision. Sanur is the strongest fit — walkable for daily needs, with taxi apps working reliably for anything further. Umalas works reasonably well too, with a shorter walk to cafés and easy car access to Canggu and Seminyak. Ungasan's clifftop lanes are the hardest of the four to manage without your own transport, so budget for a driver if that's where you're staying; our [walkable areas in Bali](/blog/areas/walkable-areas-in-bali/) guide breaks this down area by area.

## Practical tips that make a difference

- Book ride-hailing apps a little earlier in peak traffic hours (roughly 7–9am and 4–7pm) since wait times rise.
- Agree a driver's day rate and route before setting off, especially outside app-based bookings.
- Keep your villa's address and a screenshot of the map pin handy — Bali addresses are often approximate and drivers rely on landmarks.
- If a day trip needs multiple stops (temple, waterfall, lunch, viewpoint), a single driver for the day is usually simpler and calmer than stringing together individual rides.

## What it costs to skip the scooter entirely

Relying on ride-hailing apps and drivers instead of a rented scooter does add to your daily budget compared with the cost of scooter rental and fuel, but the gap is smaller than many first-time visitors expect, especially split across a group or family. A short in-town trip by app car costs a modest, fixed amount shown before you book, and a full-day driver for a multi-stop excursion is usually cheaper per person once you're travelling with two or more people, since the cost is for the vehicle and driver, not per passenger.

## What travel insurers expect

Most standard travel insurance policies either exclude scooter accidents outright or require a valid motorcycle licence (an International Driving Permit with the correct category, recognised in Indonesia) and helmet use at the time of the accident for a claim to be valid. This is a genuine reason, beyond comfort or confidence, that many visitors choose to avoid riding altogether — an uninsured injury on unfamiliar roads is a real risk, not a theoretical one, and it's worth checking your own policy's wording before you travel rather than assuming standard cover applies (as of 2026, verify your specific policy).

## FAQ

### Do I need a scooter to get around Bali?
No. Ride-hailing apps (Grab, Gojek), private drivers and, in walkable areas like Sanur, simply walking cover most tourist needs without ever riding a scooter yourself.

### Is it safe to ride a scooter in Bali as a tourist?
Riding carries real risk for visitors unfamiliar with local traffic patterns and road surfaces, and many travel insurance policies won't cover scooter accidents without a valid motorcycle licence and helmet use. If you're not an experienced rider, it's worth skipping.

### How much does a private driver cost for a day in Bali?
Costs vary by distance, number of stops and season, and we'd rather point you to a current driver contact than quote a figure that goes stale — ask us on WhatsApp and we'll share what guests are currently paying for typical day-trip routes.

### Can you get everywhere in Bali by taxi app?
In and around the main tourist areas (Sanur, Canggu, Umalas, Ungasan, Seminyak, Kuta, Ubud), yes, reliably. In more remote or rural areas, coverage and wait times get less predictable, which is where a pre-booked driver helps.

### Which Bali area is easiest without a scooter?
Sanur, thanks to its walkable beachfront path for daily errands and reliable taxi-app coverage for everything else. Ungasan's spread-out clifftop lanes are the hardest of the areas we manage villas in to navigate without transport.

### Is it worth hiring a driver for the whole trip?
For most stays, a driver by the day for specific outings (temples, waterfalls, ferry terminals) works out more practical than a full-trip hire, combined with ride-hailing apps and walking for daily local trips.
