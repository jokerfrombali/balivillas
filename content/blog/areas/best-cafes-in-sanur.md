Sanur doesn't have a single café strip the way Canggu does. It has one long beach road (Jalan Danau Tamblingan and the beachfront path that runs behind it) with cafés scattered along a few kilometres, plus a handful inland near the market and the bypass. The best cafes in Sanur for you depend on what you're there to do.

If you need three hours of solid Wi-Fi and a power socket, that's a different search from wanting a table with a sea view for breakfast, or somewhere with high chairs and a shaded play corner for a toddler. We manage villas across Sanur and end up giving this same breakdown to guests most weeks, so here it is in one place.

## Cafes for working a full morning

Sanur has fewer dedicated co-working cafés than Canggu, but a real subset of places are set up for laptop time: strong Wi-Fi, plenty of plug points, and staff who don't hover once your cup is empty. Look for places set slightly back from the beach path, since the beachfront tables are built for turnover, not for a four-hour work session.

A practical filter: order a proper meal, not just a coffee, at a café you plan to camp in during busy hours (roughly 9am–1pm). It's not a house rule anywhere, but it's the unwritten etiquette that keeps you welcome at the same table next time.

## Cafes for breakfast with a sea view

The beachfront path between the harbour and the Inna Grand Bali Beach area has the highest concentration of places where you can eat with the water in view. These fill up between 8am and 10am, especially at weekends when local families join the tourist crowd for a walk along the promenade first.

Book or arrive early if you want an outdoor table at peak time; most don't take reservations for two or three people, so it's a case of turning up. If you're staying at one of our villas near Mertasari Beach, several of these beachfront cafés are a five-to-ten-minute walk, which is often quicker than driving and parking.

## Cafes that work well with kids

Sanur is the most family-oriented of the four areas we manage villas in, and its cafés reflect that. Look for places with a garden or open lawn area rather than pure street-side seating — kids get restless at a table faster than adults do, and a bit of running-around space changes the whole visit.

A short checklist we suggest to families booking with us:

- Confirm there's a high chair before you sit down with a toddler, rather than assuming one is free
- Ask if the menu has a genuinely separate kids' section, not just smaller portions of the adult menu
- Check the shade situation for midday visits — Sanur's beach road has long unshaded stretches
- If you need a change table, message ahead; not all cafés have one

## Cafes for a slow, quiet coffee

Not every visit needs Wi-Fi or a kids' menu. Sanur's appeal for a lot of long-stay guests is exactly that it's calmer than Canggu, and a handful of cafés lean into that — smaller, fewer tables, no music competing with conversation. These tend to be inland, a short walk or scooter ride from the beach path, in the quieter residential lanes.

## What to order, and what to skip

Sanur's cafés generally do Western breakfasts and Indonesian dishes equally well, which isn't true everywhere in Bali. Nasi goreng, mie goreng and a proper bubur ayam sit on most menus alongside smoothie bowls and avocado toast, so it's worth ordering local at least once rather than defaulting to the familiar options every visit.

Prices are broadly consistent between beachfront and inland cafés in Sanur, unlike Canggu where a trend-led café can charge noticeably more than one a street back. That makes Sanur a slightly easier place to café-hop on a budget without worrying about a big jump in price for a similar coffee.

## Wi-Fi speed and reliability

Wi-Fi quality varies more by individual café than by area in Sanur — a small inland spot with three tables can have faster, more consistent internet than a busy beachfront café juggling dozens of guests at once. If a work session depends on a stable connection, ask staff about typical speeds at busy times rather than assuming a beachfront location is automatically well equipped.

Power outages happen occasionally across Bali, including Sanur, more often in wet season storms. A café with a backup generator is a genuine advantage if you're mid-deadline; it's a reasonable question to ask if you're choosing a regular working spot for a longer stay.

## How café choice should shape where you stay

If cafés are a daily part of your routine — working remotely, or just wanting an easy walk to breakfast — it's worth choosing a villa within walking distance of the beach road rather than one further inland that needs a scooter or car for every coffee run. Several of our Sanur villas, including the townhouses near the beach path, are close enough that this becomes a walk rather than a trip.

For longer stays, some guests prefer being a five-minute drive inland instead, trading the walk for quieter surroundings at night. It's a genuine trade-off and worth thinking through before you book rather than after.

## What changes with the season

Sanur's café crowd swells noticeably in July–August and over the Christmas–New Year period, when both domestic and international visitors are in Bali in higher numbers. Wait times for a table go up, and Wi-Fi at the busier cafés can slow under the extra load. Outside these windows, most cafés are comfortably quiet on weekday mornings.

## FAQ

### Are there good cafes near Sanur beach for breakfast?

Yes — the beachfront path between the harbour and the Inna Grand Bali Beach area has the highest concentration of breakfast cafés with sea views. They get busy between 8am and 10am, especially at weekends, so arrive early or expect a short wait for an outdoor table.

### Is Sanur good for working from a cafe?

It's workable but has fewer dedicated co-working cafés than Canggu. A subset of inland cafés, set back from the beach path, offer reliable Wi-Fi and enough plug points for a full morning. Order a proper meal if you plan to stay several hours during busy times.

### Which Sanur cafes are best for families with young kids?

Look for cafés with a garden or lawn area rather than pure street-side seating, and confirm a high chair is available before you sit down. Sanur is generally more family-friendly than Canggu, with calmer streets and more space for kids to move around.

### How does the Sanur cafe scene compare to Canggu?

Sanur is quieter and more spread out, with cafés along one long beach road rather than a concentrated strip. Canggu has a denser cluster of trend-led cafés and dedicated co-working spaces. Sanur suits people who want calm mornings; Canggu suits people who want more options within a short walk.

### Do Sanur cafes get busy in high season?

Yes, noticeably so in July–August and around Christmas–New Year, when table waits increase and Wi-Fi can slow at the busiest spots. Weekday mornings outside these windows are usually easy.
