Ten days is the trip length where the "where to stay" question stops being simple and starts mattering a lot. It is too long for one hotel near the airport to make sense for everything, but too short to comfortably move base every two or three nights without losing whole days to transfers.

For a Bali 10 day itinerary, we usually recommend building the trip around two bases: one coastal area for the first stretch, and one contrasting area, often inland or on the Bukit, for the second half. Below is how to plan that split and which areas pair well.

## Why a two-base plan beats a moving itinerary

A common mistake with a 10-day itinerary is booking three or four different places to "see more of Bali": a few nights in Ubud, a few in Canggu, a few on the Bukit, each linked by a drive that can run well over an hour depending on traffic. On paper it covers more ground; in practice, a chunk of a short trip goes to loading and unloading a car rather than being anywhere.

Splitting the ten days into two longer stays, roughly five nights each, gives you enough time in each area to settle in, find a rhythm for meals and day trips, and still get real contrast between the two halves of the trip.

## A sample 10-day split that works for most first-time visitors

Start in a calm coastal base like Sanur, which suits arrival fatigue well: flat walkable beach path, calm sea, easy food options, and a shorter drive from the airport than most other areas. Spend the first four to five nights there, using it as a base for day trips rather than moving every day.

For the second half, choose based on what you have not yet seen: Ubud for rice terraces, temples and a slower cultural pace, or the Bukit around Ungasan and Uluwatu for cliffside beaches and beach clubs. Both pair well with a Sanur start because they offer a genuinely different feel rather than more of the same coastline.

| Nights | Base | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1-5 | Sanur | Arrival, beach, day trips, easing into the time zone |
| 6-10 | Ubud or Ungasan/Bukit | Contrast: culture and rice terraces, or cliffs and beach clubs |

## Planning day trips from your base

Once you have picked a base, day trips fill the gaps rather than the itinerary depending on them. From a Sanur base, Nusa Penida and the Nusa islands are reachable by boat for a full-day trip, and central attractions like Ubud's rice terraces are a manageable drive for a day rather than requiring a move. From a Bukit base, cliffside beaches and beach clubs are close by without much driving, while a trip into Ubud or central Bali is a longer half-day commitment.

Keep day trips to no more than two or three across a 10-day stay if you also want downtime at the villa; a common planning mistake is scheduling something every single day and ending up more tired at the end of the trip than the start.

## Building a 10-day itinerary with kids

A Bali 10-day itinerary with kids usually works best with fewer moves, not more, since packing and unpacking a young family costs more time and patience than it does for adults travelling alone. One base for the whole trip, chosen for a calm beach, a private pool and short drives to food, often works better than a two-base split for families with toddlers or very young children.

- Prioritise a private pool with a shallow area over a hotel pool shared with other guests.
- Keep day trips shorter and less frequent than you would for an adult-only trip.
- Choose a base within a short drive of a pharmacy and a clinic, in case you need one.
- Build in at least two or three full "no plans" days across the ten days.

## What a two-base itinerary means for your booking

Splitting the trip means booking two shorter stays rather than one ten-night stay, which is worth factoring into cost comparisons since some direct-booking discounts apply by length of stay. Ask each villa or manager what discount applies at five nights versus ten, and compare the total rather than assuming a longer single booking always wins. ## Getting between your two bases

The transfer between your two bases is worth planning as its own leg of the trip rather than an afterthought squeezed between checkout and check-in. Depending on the pairing, a Sanur-to-Ubud transfer or a Sanur-to-Ungasan transfer typically takes a meaningful chunk of a morning or afternoon once you allow for loading luggage, traffic and a stop along the way, so treat travel day as a lighter day rather than scheduling activities either side of it.

Most villas and managers, including us, can arrange a private driver for this leg, which tends to be more comfortable for a family or group with luggage than trying to coordinate ride-hailing pickups from two different locations. Booking this in advance, once your dates and both bases are confirmed, avoids the scramble of arranging transport on the morning you are meant to be leaving.

## Packing and logistics for a two-base 10-day trip

A two-base itinerary works best with luggage that is easy to move, since you will be packing it at least twice during the ten days. Consolidating into fewer, larger bags rather than several smaller ones usually makes the mid-trip transfer smoother, particularly if travelling with children or a group.

- Keep a small overnight bag separate from main luggage for the first night, in case bags are delayed or the transfer runs late.
- Confirm check-out and check-in times at both properties in advance so the transfer day has a realistic window either side.
- Ask each villa whether laundry is available partway through, so you are not carrying ten days of clothing from the start.
- If flying in and out of different areas is not an option, plan the transfer day around your actual arrival and departure flight times rather than a fixed default.

## Packing and logistics for a 10-day multi-activity trip

A 10-day itinerary that mixes beach days, an inland excursion or two and maybe a boat trip needs slightly more thought than a straightforward beach week. Pack layers you can add or remove — mornings at altitude near Ubud or Mount Batur run cooler than the coast, while afternoons back at a south Bali villa are warm and humid. Bring a dry bag or waterproof phone pouch if a boat trip or waterfall visit is on the list, and keep a change of clothes accessible in your day bag rather than buried in checked luggage for excursion days.

Ten days is also enough time that laundry becomes a practical consideration; most villas can arrange this, so check before you overpack. If your itinerary includes an early wake-up for something like a sunrise trek, plan the days immediately before and after as lighter ones, since the disrupted sleep affects more than just that one day.

## Balancing structure with downtime

The biggest planning mistake over 10 days is over-scheduling. A full itinerary with something booked every single day tends to leave travellers more tired than satisfied by the end. Building in at least two or three genuinely unstructured days — pool time, a slow breakfast, no fixed plan — usually makes the whole trip feel more like a holiday and less like a checklist, without meaningfully reducing what you get to see.

## FAQ

### Should I book one hotel or split a 10-day Bali trip between two places?

For most 10-day trips, splitting between two bases of around five nights each works better than one location or than moving every few nights. It gives genuine contrast, for example a beach base and a cultural or cliffside base, without spending too much of the trip in transit.

### What is the best base for a 10-day Bali itinerary with kids?

A single calm base for the full ten days usually suits families with young children better than splitting the stay, since packing and unpacking costs more time with kids involved. Sanur is a common choice for this because of its flat beach path, calm sea and short drive from the airport.

### How many day trips should I plan into a 10-day Bali itinerary?

Two or three day trips across ten days is usually enough to see highlights without exhausting the trip, leaving several days for rest or unplanned time at the villa. Scheduling something every single day tends to leave visitors more tired at the end of the trip than relaxed.

### Is Ubud or the Bukit a better second base after Sanur?

It depends on what you want from the second half of the trip: Ubud suits travellers who want rice terraces, temples and a slower cultural pace, while the Bukit around Ungasan and Uluwatu suits those who want cliffside beaches and beach clubs. Both pair well with a Sanur-based first half.

### Does splitting a 10-day trip into two bookings cost more than one long stay?

Not necessarily; it depends on each villa's discount structure for the length of stay, so it is worth asking directly rather than assuming. Compare the total cost of two five-night bookings against a single ten-night booking before deciding, since discounts vary by villa and season.
