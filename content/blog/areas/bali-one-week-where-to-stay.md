For a one-week trip to Bali, staying in a single area is almost always the better plan. Seven nights is short enough that changing villas partway through eats a real chunk of your holiday in packing, transfers and settling in twice, for a variety benefit most people don't actually use. Pick one base that matches your priorities — beaches, cafés, nightlife, family space — and take day trips from there instead.

The exception is a trip built specifically around seeing two different sides of the island, such as a few days on the coast plus a couple of nights in Ubud for temples and rice terraces. Even then, we'd suggest weighting it 5 nights to 2 rather than splitting evenly, so you're not spending your week mostly in transit.

## Picking one area for a week

Your choice should follow what you actually want to do most days, not which area photographs best.

- **Sanur** suits families, couples and anyone who wants a calm base with a flat beachfront path for walking or cycling, without heavy traffic or nightlife. It's also a practical hub for day trips to Nusa Lembongan and Nusa Penida by fast boat.
- **Canggu** suits surfers, remote workers and anyone who wants cafés, coworking spaces and a beach walkable from the villa, accepting more traffic and a livelier evening scene in return.
- **Umalas** suits people who want Canggu's café culture without being right in its busiest streets — quieter lanes, still a short drive to the beach and Seminyak.
- **Ungasan** suits couples and groups who want a private pool villa on the Bukit's cliffs, with beach clubs and dramatic scenery a short drive away.

## What a week actually costs to plan for

Villa rates vary by area, size and season, and we'd rather point you to check current pricing than quote a fixed number that goes stale. As a planning approach: decide your group size and bedroom count first, then compare a few villas in your chosen area at your actual dates, since the swing between low and high season is often larger than the swing between areas. Beyond the villa, budget for a scooter or car rental (or a driver on call), meals out most nights, and any day trips or activities. None of these costs are fixed enough to state as fact here — treat any number you see quoted elsewhere as a starting point, not a guarantee.

## A sample one-week structure

Rather than moving villas, structure your week around day trips from a single base:

| Day | Activity from a south Bali base |
|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive, settle in, short walk or swim near the villa |
| 2–3 | Local exploring: beach, cafés, area's main sights |
| 4 | Day trip further afield (Ubud, Nusa Penida, waterfalls) |
| 5 | Rest day, beach club or spa |
| 6 | Second day trip or activity (surf lesson, cooking class) |
| 7 | Slow morning, pack, departure |

This keeps one check-in and one check-out, and lets you use the middle of the week for the further-flung trips without hauling luggage.

## Common mistakes for a one-week trip

- **Splitting the week across two or three villas.** It looks appealing on paper but the actual time lost to transfers and re-settling is usually underestimated.
- **Booking the wrong area for your group.** A surf-focused Canggu base doesn't suit a family wanting calm beach days, and vice versa — match the area to your actual plans, not the area's general reputation.
- **Underestimating drive times to day trips.** Places like Ubud or the north coast take longer than they look on a map; build in buffer rather than a tight schedule.
- **Not asking about early check-in or late check-out.** Many villas can accommodate this outside peak season if you simply ask ahead.

## Villa size and layout for a week-long group

Bedroom count matters as much as area for a short, intense stay. A group of six splitting a 2-bedroom villa will feel cramped fast over a full week, while a couple in a 4-bedroom villa is paying for space they won't use. Our own villas range from 1-bedroom teak retreats in Ungasan up to a 9-bedroom resort-style villa in Canggu, so matching bedroom count and layout to your actual group is worth as much attention as picking the right area. Check whether bedrooms are all en-suite, whether there's a dedicated workspace if anyone needs to check in with work, and how the kitchen and living space work for a group cooking some meals in rather than eating out every night.

## Weather planning for a one-week trip

Bali's wet and dry seasons both bring workable weather for a week-long stay, but they suit different itineraries. Drier months generally mean more reliable outdoor plans — beach days, day trips, sunset beach clubs — without needing a backup plan. Wetter months bring short, often heavy downpours rather than constant rain, so a week during this period usually still has plenty of good weather, just with less certainty about which specific days. If your week is built around one outdoor priority — a boat trip to Nusa Penida, a sunrise trek — building in a spare day as a weather buffer is worth considering regardless of season.

## Adjusting the plan for a couple vs a family vs a group

A one-week trip looks different depending on who's travelling. A couple can move faster and more spontaneously, fitting in a beach club afternoon or a last-minute dinner booking without much coordination. A family with young kids generally needs a slower pace, shorter days out and a villa with enough downtime space that nobody is climbing the walls by day four. A larger group renting a bigger villa often does best building in a rotation of who plans what for the day, rather than trying to agree on every activity as one unit, which can eat into time better spent relaxing.

## Meals: cooking in vs eating out

A week-long stay is long enough that a mix of home-cooked and restaurant meals usually works better than eating out every night, both for budget and for pace. Most of our villas come with a fully equipped kitchen, so stocking up at a local supermarket on arrival for breakfasts and the odd home-cooked dinner is worth considering, especially with children or a larger group. Some villas can also arrange a private chef for a special night, which is worth asking about if you want a standout meal without leaving the villa.

## FAQ

### Should I split a one-week Bali trip between two areas?

Generally no. Seven nights is tight enough that two check-ins and check-outs, plus repacking and re-settling, cost more time than most travellers realise, for variety they often don't fully use. A single base with day trips covers most itineraries better; save the multi-base approach for trips of two weeks or longer.

### How much does it cost to stay in Bali for a week?

It depends heavily on villa size, area and season, so there's no single reliable figure — a 1-bedroom villa and a 4-bedroom family villa in the same area can differ substantially in nightly rate. The most useful approach is to fix your group size and dates first, then compare current quotes for a few specific villas rather than relying on a generic average.

### What is the best area to stay in Bali for a week with kids?

Sanur is a common choice for families because of its calm beachfront path, gentler pace and shorter distances between the villa, beach and restaurants. Umalas and Ungasan also work well depending on whether you want café culture or a clifftop setting; Canggu's busier traffic and nightlife make it a less relaxed fit for very young children.

### Is one week enough time to see Bali properly?

It's enough for one area plus a couple of day trips, not for covering the whole island. Trying to fit Sanur, Canggu, Ubud and the Bukit into seven days usually means more time in transit than anywhere worth stopping. Pick a base and a short list of day trips instead of an island-wide itinerary.

### What time should I plan for check-in and check-out on a one-week trip?

Standard check-in is from 3:00 pm and check-out by 11:00 am, but many villas can arrange earlier arrival or later departure outside high season if you ask when booking. This is worth confirming directly, especially if your flights land early morning or leave late at night, since it can save you a wasted half-day.
