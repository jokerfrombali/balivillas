Canggu has several established coworking spaces, ranging from large, well-known operators with hundreds of desks to smaller boutique spaces attached to cafés or guesthouses. The core trade-off against café-based work is straightforward: you pay a membership fee for reliability — guaranteed seating, consistent internet, meeting rooms and a working community — instead of relying on whichever café has space that morning.

Whether that's worth it depends on how long you're staying and how much your work depends on consistency.

## What coworking spaces in Canggu typically offer

Most operate on a mix of day passes, weekly and monthly memberships, with hot-desk and dedicated-desk options at different price tiers. Facilities generally include meeting rooms bookable by the hour, private call booths, printing, and a café or restaurant on site. Some run community events, workshops or networking sessions, which is part of the draw for solo remote workers wanting a social element to their work week.

Exact pricing changes fairly often across operators and isn't something to quote without checking current rates directly with each space, since membership tiers and included perks vary.

## When a coworking space is worth it over cafés

It's generally worth it if you're staying a month or longer and need consistent daily seating without arriving early to claim a spot, if your work involves regular video calls or client meetings that need a quiet booth or meeting room, or if you value the built-in community and events for a longer stay away from home. For a week-long trip with light work needs, cafés usually cover it without the added cost.

## What to check before signing up

- Ask for a day pass first before committing to a weekly or monthly membership
- Check internet speed at peak hours (10am–2pm), not just the advertised number
- Confirm what's included in your tier — meeting room hours, printing, drinks
- Ask about the cancellation or downgrade policy if your plans change mid-stay
- Visit at the time of day you'll actually be working, not just during a quiet tour

## How location affects your choice

Most of Canggu's coworking spaces sit in or near Batu Bolong and Berawa, the more developed neighbourhoods, since that's where the daily foot traffic and café culture already exist. If you're staying in Pererenan or Echo Beach for the quieter pace, factor the commute into your decision — a 15-minute scooter ride each way adds up over a month.

## Community and events

Many coworking spaces in Canggu build a genuine community around their membership base — networking events, workshops, and shared meals or drinks on certain evenings. For solo remote workers on a longer stay, this social element is often as valuable as the desk itself, particularly in the first few weeks of an extended trip before you've built your own local network.

## Meeting clients or running a small team

If you're meeting clients locally or coordinating a small remote team from Bali, a coworking space's meeting rooms and professional setting generally work better than a café table, both for practicality (reliable internet, quiet, screen-sharing capability) and for the impression it gives visiting clients. This is one of the clearer cases where the membership cost pays for itself quickly.

Pricing varies by operator and tier (day pass, weekly, monthly, hot-desk versus dedicated desk) and changes over time, so check current rates directly with each space rather than relying on older figures you might find elsewhere.
