Sanur is the area we recommend first when a family asks us where to stay in Bali. The beachfront path is flat and traffic-free, the sea close to shore is calm and shallow, and the streets around most villas are quiet enough for a stroller or a toddler on a bike, without needing a car for every outing.

That said, "best" still depends on your family's specifics: how old the kids are, whether you need to work during the trip, and how much you want to be near restaurants and shops versus tucked away. This guide walks through Sanur first, then the situations where Umalas or Ungasan are the better call.

## Why Sanur works for most families

Three things make Sanur easier than the alternatives with children. First, the beachfront promenade runs for several kilometres, flat and largely traffic-free, so you can walk to breakfast, a playground or the market without watching for scooters the whole way. Second, the water close to shore is calmer and shallower than Canggu's surf beaches, which matters with younger swimmers. Third, Sanur has an established base of cafés, small supermarkets and pharmacies, so daily logistics, nappies, snacks, a forgotten sun hat, are a short walk rather than a scooter trip. Our villa Sanur Garden Pool Villa, a 4-bedroom family villa near Mertasari Beach with a private 35 m² pool and four sunbeds, is a typical example of the villa type that suits a family of two adults and children well in this area.

## When Umalas is the better choice

Umalas suits a family better than Sanur in two situations: if one or both parents need to work during the trip, since Umalas has more coworking cafés and a quieter, more residential feel than Canggu while staying close to it; and if your children are older and you want easy access to Canggu's restaurants and activities without living inside the busiest part of it. Villa Papat, a 4-bedroom pool villa in Umalas with an open living room facing the pool and a large garden, works well for a two-family group or a larger single family in this setup. See our dedicated guide on [Umalas for families](/blog/areas/umalas-for-families/) for more detail on schools, paediatric care and daily logistics in that specific area.

## When Ungasan is the better choice

Ungasan suits a family that wants privacy and scenery over proximity to shops, typically families who plan to spend most of the trip at the villa and pool rather than out and about, or who are visiting with grandparents and want a calmer clifftop setting. The trade-off is that Ungasan is further from supermarkets and paediatric services than Sanur or Umalas, so it suits families comfortable driving 15 to 25 minutes for errands rather than walking to them.

## What to check before booking, regardless of area

- Confirm the pool has a shallow section or can be fenced if you have very young children
- Ask whether the villa has a cot, high chair or other baby gear, or whether you need to bring or hire your own
- Check the walk or drive time to the nearest pharmacy and supermarket, not just the beach
- Confirm bedroom configuration matches your family, some "family villas" split children across a floor away from parents

## FAQ

### Is Sanur or Canggu better for a family with young children?
Sanur is generally easier with young children: the beachfront path is flat and traffic-free, the water close to shore is calmer, and the streets around most villas are quieter than Canggu's busier main roads. Canggu can still work for families with older, more independent children who want more to do nearby.

### What is the best area in Bali for families in 2026?
The area mix has not changed meaningfully: Sanur remains the easiest base for families with young children, Umalas suits working parents who want quieter streets than Canggu, and Ungasan suits families wanting privacy over walkability. Choose based on your family's specific setup rather than a ranking.

### Do family villas in Bali usually have a fenced pool?
Not automatically. Many private pools are unfenced by default, so if you have young children, ask specifically before booking whether the pool has a shallow section, a fence or a cover option, and whether the villa can arrange one temporarily for your stay.

### Is Ungasan too remote for a family with kids?
Ungasan is quieter and further from shops than Sanur or Umalas, typically a 15 to 25-minute drive to a supermarket or pharmacy, so it suits families who plan to spend most of the trip at the villa rather than needing frequent nearby errands.

### How many bedrooms should a family villa in Bali have?
It depends on group size and whether you want children in a separate space from parents. A 3-bedroom villa comfortably fits two adults and two or three children with room to spare; our guide on [how many bedrooms a villa in Bali needs](/blog/renting/how-many-bedrooms-villa-bali/) walks through the trade-offs by group size.
