Sanur harbour is the main departure point on mainland Bali for fast boats to Nusa Penida, with the crossing typically taking around 30 to 45 minutes depending on the operator and sea conditions. Multiple boat companies run this route, and they differ more than the ticket price alone suggests — in departure reliability, what's included, and how crowded the boat gets.

If you're staying with us in Sanur, the harbour is usually a short drive or even a walk from your villa, which is one of the practical reasons guests choose this area as a base for island trips. Here's what to check before you book.

## How the crossing actually works

Fast boats leave from Sanur's harbour area at scheduled times through the morning, with the earliest departures typically around 7:30–8am and further sailings through the day. Nusa Penida has more than one landing point depending on the operator and which part of the island you're headed to first, so check where exactly your boat lands, since it affects how you'll get around once you arrive.

Sea conditions can affect the crossing, particularly in the windier months; delays or cancellations happen occasionally and operators generally rebook rather than refund outright, so check the specific operator's policy before paying.

## What's usually included, and what isn't

A basic ferry ticket covers the crossing only. Many day-trip packages bundle the boat with land transport on Nusa Penida (since the island's roads and distances between viewpoints need a car or scooter), entrance fees to specific sites, and sometimes lunch. Compare like for like — a cheaper "ticket only" price against a slightly higher "boat plus transport" price can end up costing about the same once you add a driver on the other side.

- Confirm whether land transport on Nusa Penida is included or needs booking separately
- Check whether entrance fees to specific viewpoints are covered
- Ask which landing point you'll arrive at and how far it is from the main sights
- Confirm the return departure time so you don't miss the last boat back

## Comparing two operators side by side

| What to check | Cheaper operator | Pricier operator |
|---|---|---|
| Crossing time | Similar, ~30–45 min | Similar |
| Boat size/crowding | Often more crowded | Sometimes smaller groups |
| Land transport included | Rarely | Often |
| Cancellation policy | Check carefully | Usually clearer |
| Departure reliability | Variable | Generally more consistent |

Price alone is a poor comparison point on this route. A meaningfully cheaper ticket often means a fuller boat, no land transport included, and a vaguer cancellation policy — fine if you're comfortable arranging your own transport on Nusa Penida, less fine if you want a straightforward day trip.

## Timing your day trip

A full day covering Nusa Penida's well-known viewpoints (spread across a large island with real distances between them) generally means an early boat out and a late-afternoon or early-evening boat back. Trying to cram the island's furthest-apart sights into a single day with a late morning departure usually means cutting the trip short somewhere.

If you'd rather not rush, staying a night or two on Nusa Penida itself is common, then returning to Sanur on a later crossing. Either way, book your return boat before you land on the island — same-day tickets back can sell out on busy days.

## Luggage and what to bring on the crossing

Fast boats generally have limited covered storage, so keep valuables, electronics and anything that shouldn't get wet in a dry bag or sealed plastic bag, since spray over the bow is common even in calm conditions. If you're staying overnight on Nusa Penida, pack light — the island's roads are steep and rough in places, and a large suitcase is genuinely awkward to move between the boat and your accommodation.

## Alternatives to Sanur harbour

While Sanur is the main departure point for most visitors, a small number of crossings to the Nusa islands also run from Padang Bai and Kusamba on Bali's east coast, generally used more by locals or for specific onward routes. For a standard day trip or short stay on Nusa Penida from a Sanur base, the Sanur harbour crossing remains the most convenient and frequent option, and it's the one we generally recommend to guests staying with us.

## Seasickness and sea conditions

The crossing is generally smooth in calm conditions but can be choppy, particularly during the windier months (roughly June–September brings stronger trade winds in parts of the season, though conditions vary year to year). If you're prone to seasickness, consider taking something before boarding rather than after symptoms start, and sit toward the middle of the boat where movement is felt less.

## What to do next

Book your crossing a day or two ahead in high season, confirm exactly what's included in your ticket, and decide whether you want to see Nusa Penida as a single long day or split it over two days with an overnight stay. ## FAQ

### How long does the ferry from Sanur to Nusa Penida take?

Typically around 30 to 45 minutes, depending on the operator, the specific landing point, and sea conditions. It can run a little longer in choppier weather.

### What time do boats from Sanur to Nusa Penida start running?

Early morning, with the first departures generally around 7:30–8am and further sailings through the day. Early boats are the most popular for full-day trips, so book ahead in high season.

### Is land transport included in the Nusa Penida ferry ticket?

Not usually with a basic ticket — that covers the crossing only. Many operators sell a combined package with a driver or scooter on Nusa Penida, since the island's sights are spread out and a boat ticket alone won't get you between them.

### Can I do Nusa Penida as a day trip from Sanur?

Yes, it's the most common way to see the island, with an early boat out and a late-afternoon or evening boat back. If you'd rather not rush between viewpoints, an overnight stay on Nusa Penida with a later return is a common alternative.

### Is the Sanur to Nusa Penida crossing rough?

It can be choppy in windier periods, roughly June to September in parts of the season, though this varies year to year. If you're prone to seasickness, take precautions before boarding and sit toward the middle of the boat.
