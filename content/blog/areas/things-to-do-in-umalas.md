Umalas isn't a destination in the way Canggu's beach clubs or Uluwatu's cliffs are. It's a residential area of quiet lanes, rice-field edges and villa compounds, which means things to do in Umalas tend to be low-key by design: yoga, spas, boutique shopping, a slow walk past the polo field, rather than a checklist of attractions.

That suits a specific kind of trip better than others. If you want constant activity on your doorstep, Umalas will feel quiet. If you want a calm base with enough nearby to fill your days without leaving the area every time, it works well. We manage villas here and this is the honest version of what fills a week.

## Wellness: yoga, spas and studios

Umalas has a genuine concentration of yoga studios and spas, partly spillover from Canggu's wellness scene and partly its own thing — quieter studios that don't need the beach-town energy to draw a crowd. Drop-in classes are common at most studios, so you don't need to commit to a package if you're only in the area for a short stay.

Spa pricing varies a lot by setting — a simple massage at a small local place costs noticeably less than the same treatment at a resort-style spa, and both exist within a short drive of most Umalas villas. Ask locally or check with us for current recommendations, since new studios open and close faster than any guide can keep up with.

## Shopping: boutiques and homeware

A number of small design studios, homeware shops and independent fashion boutiques are scattered through Umalas's lanes, often in converted villas rather than storefronts. This is Umalas's answer to Seminyak's more polished retail strip — smaller scale, fewer crowds, and worth a slow browse rather than a dedicated shopping trip.

It's the kind of thing to build into a rest day rather than plan around, since the shops are spread out enough that hopping between several means a scooter or short drives, not a single walkable strip.

## Watching polo or just walking past the field

The Umalas polo field is one of the area's genuine landmarks and worth knowing about even if you have no interest in the sport. Matches happen periodically through the year — check current schedules locally, since we won't guess at dates here — and the field itself is a pleasant green space to walk past even when nothing's on.

Restaurants and cafés cluster around the polo field precisely because of the footfall it draws, which ties into where you might eat during your stay; see our guide to [Umalas restaurants](/blog/areas/umalas-restaurants-cafes/) if that's the next thing on your list.

## Day trips from a Umalas base

Umalas's location — inland, between Seminyak and Canggu — makes it a workable base for day trips in either direction. Seminyak's beach clubs and shopping are a short drive south; Canggu's surf breaks and beach-club scene are a short drive north. Neither is truly walkable from most Umalas villas, so factor in a car or scooter.

| Direction | What's there | Typical trip length |
|---|---|---|
| Toward Seminyak | Beach clubs, shopping, beach access | Half day |
| Toward Canggu | Surf, beach clubs, nomad café scene | Half day to full day |
| Staying local | Wellness, boutiques, polo field, restaurants | Any length |

Traffic between Umalas and Canggu can be worse than the distance suggests, especially late afternoon; our guide to [Canggu traffic and getting around](/blog/areas/canggu-traffic-and-getting-around/) is worth a read before you plan a day trip that direction.

## Remote work and coworking

Umalas has become one of Bali's more established remote-work bases, sitting alongside Canggu as a destination for people combining a laptop job with an extended stay. Several coworking spaces and work-friendly cafés operate in the area, generally with a quieter atmosphere and less competition for desks than Canggu's busiest spots.

Wi-Fi reliability varies by venue, as it does everywhere in Bali, so it's worth testing a space for an hour before committing to a day pass or membership if a stable connection is critical to your work. Many of our villas also come with their own dedicated workspace, which is worth weighing against a daily coworking cost if you're staying for weeks rather than days.

## Markets and local culture

Beyond the more polished boutiques and cafés, Umalas still has pockets of everyday Balinese life worth seeking out — local markets, small temples and the rhythms of a residential area that hasn't been fully reshaped by tourism the way parts of Seminyak have. These aren't tourist attractions in the formal sense, but they're part of what makes a longer stay in Umalas feel different from a resort holiday.

Walking or scootering through the quieter lanes early in the morning, before the day's heat and traffic build up, is one of the simplest ways to get a sense of this side of the area.

## Getting around without your own transport

Not every visitor wants to rent a scooter, and ride-hailing apps generally cover Umalas well, though pickup can sometimes be slower in the smaller lanes than on main roads because drivers occasionally struggle to locate exact addresses. Sharing a precise map pin, rather than a written address, tends to solve this faster than explaining directions.

For longer stays, arranging a driver for specific days — a shopping trip, an airport run, a day out toward Uluwatu — is often more practical than renting a car outright, and several drivers work regularly with guests staying in the area.

## A simple checklist for planning your days

- Decide early how many days you want fully local versus how many involve a day trip out
- Book any wellness classes or spa slots a day ahead in high season, since popular studios fill up
- Check current polo match schedules locally if that's part of your plan
- Budget scooter or car time between activities — Umalas's lanes are pleasant but not densely walkable

## Rice fields and quiet outdoor walks

Pockets of rice field still exist on Umalas's edges, though development has reduced their footprint compared with a decade ago. Where they remain, they offer a genuinely quiet walk or an early-morning scooter ride, especially valuable if you want a break from the villa without driving all the way to Seminyak or Canggu.

These aren't landscaped attractions with paid entry the way some of Bali's more famous rice terraces are — they're working or semi-working fields on the edges of a residential area, so treat them as a pleasant backdrop rather than a formal sightseeing stop, and be respectful of any active farming you pass.

## Comparing cost against Canggu and Seminyak

Because Umalas sits between two more built-up, more expensive areas, a lot of what "things to do" costs here is lower — spa treatments, casual dining, even some wellness classes — simply because rents along its quieter lanes are cheaper than beachfront Canggu or central Seminyak. ## FAQ

### What is there to do in Umalas at night?

Umalas is quieter at night than Canggu or Seminyak — most evening activity centres on sit-down dinners rather than nightlife. For a livelier night out, guests typically drive to Canggu or Seminyak, both a short trip away, and return to Umalas's calmer lanes to sleep.

### Is Umalas good for a longer stay or just a short visit?

It works well for longer stays precisely because it's calm — the wellness studios, boutiques and restaurants are enough to fill weeks without repetition, and its central position makes day trips to Seminyak or Canggu easy when you want more energy. Short-stay visitors sometimes find it too quiet without a car.

### Are there beaches near Umalas?

Umalas itself is inland with no beach directly in the area. The nearest beach access is a short drive toward Seminyak or Canggu, both within easy reach. If beach access matters most to your trip, it's worth weighing that against Umalas's quieter, more residential setting.

### What is Umalas known for besides restaurants?

Wellness — yoga studios and spas — plus boutique shopping and the polo field are what locals and repeat visitors associate with Umalas beyond its dining scene. It has a reputation as a calmer, more residential alternative to Seminyak and Canggu, popular with longer-stay guests and remote workers.

### Do I need a scooter or car to enjoy things to do in Umalas?

Mostly yes. Umalas's lanes are pleasant to walk but spread out, and getting between wellness studios, shops and restaurants usually means a short scooter or car trip rather than a walk. Day trips to Seminyak or Canggu also require transport, since neither is within comfortable walking distance.
