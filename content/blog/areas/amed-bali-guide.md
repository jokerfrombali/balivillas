Amed is a stretch of small fishing villages along Bali's northeast coast, known for calm water, black volcanic sand and some of the island's most accessible diving and snorkelling, including a well-known wreck dive nearby. It's a very different experience from south Bali's villa areas — quieter, less developed, and built around the water itself rather than beach clubs, cafés or nightlife.

Because of its distance from Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan, Amed works best as its own dedicated stop on a longer Bali trip, not a day trip from a south coast villa.

## What Amed actually offers

The coastline here is calmer than Bali's surf beaches, with black sand and rocky stretches rather than the white or golden sand of the south. That calm water, combined with coral reefs close to shore in several spots, makes Amed a genuine draw for divers and snorkellers, including beginners taking a first certification course. Above the water, the pace is slow: small warungs, a handful of dive shops, and little in the way of organised nightlife or shopping.

## Villas and accommodation in Amed

If you're searching for a villa in Amed Bali, expect a smaller and more varied market than south Bali's — a mix of simple guesthouses, dive-focused lodges and some private villas, generally less standardised in quality and amenities than the purpose-built villas common in Sanur, Canggu or Umalas. ## Coworking and remote work in Amed

Coworking in Amed is limited compared with Canggu or Umalas, which have a developed ecosystem of dedicated spaces and laptop-friendly cafés. Amed's slower pace and smaller expat community mean fewer formal coworking options; remote workers here typically rely on a villa's own Wi-Fi rather than a coworking space, so it's worth checking connectivity specifically with any property before committing to work from there for an extended stay.

## Weather and the rainy season

Amed Bali rainy season affects the area much like the rest of the island, with wetter months bringing more rainfall and occasional rough sea conditions that can affect diving visibility and boat access. Diving is possible year-round, but conditions vary, so if diving is your main reason for visiting, it's worth checking typical seasonal patterns and current conditions with a local dive operator before booking rather than assuming any specific month is guaranteed calm.

## Buying property or leasehold in Amed

Leasehold in Amed Bali follows the same general framework as elsewhere on the island: foreign buyers normally purchase on long leasehold rather than freehold, with lease terms, extension options and land status varying property by property. Amed's land market is less developed and less standardised than south Bali's more established areas, so due diligence — checking zoning, land certificates and any existing permits — matters at least as much here, arguably more given fewer established local agents and less transaction history to reference. ## Planning a trip that includes Amed

Given the distance from south Bali, Amed suits travellers with a longer trip who specifically want to dive or slow down for a few days, often combined with nearby [Sidemen](/blog/areas/sidemen-bali-guide/) or a wider east and north Bali loop. It's not a realistic day trip from Sanur or Canggu; plan at least two or three nights to make the journey worthwhile.

## What to check before booking in Amed

- Confirm the property's actual distance to a dive shop or snorkel spot if that's your priority — Amed stretches along a fair length of coastline.
- Check Wi-Fi and connectivity specifically if you plan to work remotely, rather than assuming south Bali standards apply.
- Ask about seasonal conditions if diving is central to your trip, since visibility and sea state vary through the year.
- Read recent, specific reviews rather than relying on general area reputation, given the more varied accommodation standard.

## A typical day in Amed

Days here tend to revolve around the water: an early dive or snorkel session before conditions can shift later in the day, a slow lunch at a beachfront warung, and an afternoon spent reading, walking the black-sand shore or simply resting. Evenings are quiet, with dinner at a small local restaurant and an early night rather than any organised nightlife. It's a markedly different rhythm from a south Bali villa stay, and that contrast is exactly why some travellers build a few Amed nights into a longer trip.

## Combining Amed with the rest of your itinerary

Amed sits far enough from south Bali that most visitors treat it as a dedicated stop within a longer trip rather than a base for the whole holiday. A common pattern pairs a few nights in Amed with a stop in Sidemen or a loop through east Bali more broadly, before returning to a south Bali villa for the remainder of the trip where beach clubs, cafés and a shorter airport transfer are all close at hand again.

## Diving courses and equipment

Several dive shops along the Amed coastline offer beginner certification courses alongside guided dives for certified divers, and equipment rental is generally available locally rather than needing to bring your own gear. If diving is the main reason for your trip, it's worth booking with an established operator with proper certification credentials rather than the cheapest option, given the safety considerations involved in any diving activity.

## Getting to Amed from the airport or other areas

Amed sits a considerable distance from Ngurah Rai airport and from south Bali's villa areas, generally requiring several hours of driving along the east coast. Most visitors arrange a private driver for this transfer rather than self-driving, given the distance and the winding coastal road. If you're combining Amed with Sidemen or another east Bali stop, routing through the interior rather than backtracking to the south coast can save time.

## Snorkelling without diving certification

You don't need any certification to snorkel in Amed, and several of the best spots are accessible directly from the shore rather than requiring a boat trip, making it an easy option for anyone who wants to see the coral and marine life without committing to a full diving course.

## Currency and payments in Amed

As with much of rural Bali, card acceptance in Amed is patchy outside larger dive operators and hotels, so carrying sufficient cash is sensible for warungs, smaller guesthouses and day-to-day purchases. ATMs are available but less densely spread than in south Bali, so it's worth stocking up on cash before making the drive out.
