A day trip to Ubud from a south Bali villa is one of the most common excursions our guests plan, and it works well if you set realistic expectations about how much of the day the drive itself will take. Ubud sits inland, away from the coastal areas where most villas are, so the round trip alone is a meaningful part of your day — build your sightseeing list around that, not around an idealised itinerary you'd only manage with an overnight stay.

The good news is Ubud rewards a focused visit. Two or three well-chosen stops — a rice terrace, the monkey forest or a temple, and a proper lunch — make for a satisfying day without trying to see everything the town offers.

## How long the trip actually takes

Ubud is further inland than any of south Bali's coastal villa areas, and the drive time depends heavily on which area you're starting from and the traffic on the day. Rather than quoting a specific figure that may not hold on your travel dates, plan for a substantial portion of your day to be spent travelling each way, and build your itinerary around a realistic number of stops rather than a packed list.

## What to actually see in a single day

- **Tegallalang rice terraces** (or a similar terrace nearer Ubud) for the classic green-tiered view — go early to avoid both crowds and midday heat.
- **The Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary** for a shaded walk through forest temples, though keep an eye on belongings around the monkeys.
- **A temple visit** — Ubud and its surroundings have several worth a stop, each with a different atmosphere from the terraces.
- **Ubud's art market and central streets** for a browse and lunch, if time allows after the outdoor stops.
- **A nearby waterfall**, if you're not trying to fit in everything else — several sit within a short detour of Ubud's centre; see our [waterfalls guide](/blog/areas/bali-waterfalls-guide/) for specifics.

Trying to combine all of these in one day usually means rushing each stop. Pick two or three based on what genuinely interests your group.

## A sample day-trip schedule

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| Early morning | Depart south Bali, aim to beat both traffic and crowds |
| Mid-morning | Rice terrace visit, photos, short walk |
| Midday | Temple or monkey forest, then lunch in Ubud |
| Afternoon | Art market browse or a waterfall detour |
| Late afternoon | Head back before evening traffic builds |

Adjust based on your actual travel time from your villa — starting earlier gives more buffer on both ends.

## Day trip or overnight?

If Ubud genuinely interests you — the art scene, the yoga and wellness culture, the wider countryside — an overnight or two is a better use of the destination than a single rushed day. A day trip works well as a taster or for travellers whose main base and priorities are firmly coastal (beaches, surf, beach clubs) but who still want a look at Bali's inland side. Our guide to where to stay in Ubud covers the option if you decide the area deserves more than a day.

## Practical tips for the trip

- **Start early.** Traffic and crowds both build through the morning, so an early departure buys you a noticeably better experience at the popular sites.
- **Book a driver for the day** rather than self-driving if you're not confident on Bali's roads outside the flatter coastal areas — Ubud's surrounding roads climb and wind more than south Bali's.
- **Bring cash** for entrance fees at temples and rice terraces, and for the art market, where card acceptance is inconsistent.
- **Dress modestly for temple visits** — a sarong is often required and can usually be rented or borrowed at the entrance if you don't bring one.
- **Pace yourself** — trying to fit five stops into one day from a coastal villa usually means arriving home late and tired rather than satisfied.

## Combining Ubud with other inland stops

If you're making the drive inland anyway, some travellers pair Ubud with a waterfall detour or, for a more ambitious day, a stop en route to or from [Mount Batur](/blog/areas/mount-batur-sunrise-trek/) — though the latter is a very full day and usually better planned as its own trip given the early start it requires. Keep any combination realistic rather than optimistic; Bali's roads rarely move as fast as a map suggests.

## Food and where to stop for lunch

Ubud has a wider range of dining than most south Bali villa areas outside Canggu, from simple local warungs to well-regarded vegetarian and vegan restaurants that have built the town's reputation among wellness travellers. A day trip is a good chance to try something different from your usual coastal dinner spot, whether that's a rice-paddy-view café for lunch or a more considered sit-down meal before the drive back. Booking ahead is rarely necessary for lunch, though popular spots can have a wait around midday when day-trip traffic peaks.

## What if it rains on your Ubud day trip

Ubud's inland, slightly elevated setting means it can see rain even when the coast is dry, and the reverse is also true. A short downpour rarely derails a full day, since most stops — rice terraces, temples, the monkey forest — have some covered areas or are easily paused and resumed once it passes. Heavier, sustained rain is more disruptive, particularly for rice terrace photography and walking paths that can get slippery, so it's worth checking the forecast the morning of your trip and having a flexible backup plan, such as swapping an outdoor stop for the art market or a café.

## Shopping for souvenirs in Ubud

Ubud's art market and surrounding streets are a better spot for souvenir shopping than most of south Bali, with a wider range of textiles, woodcarving, jewellery and homeware than the more beach-focused shops closer to the coast. Bargaining is expected at the market itself, less so in fixed-price boutiques nearby. If souvenir shopping is a priority for your day, build in dedicated time for it rather than treating it as a quick five-minute stop between other sights.

## Traffic within Ubud itself

Beyond the drive in from the coast, Ubud's own central streets can get congested around midday, particularly near the monkey forest and main market, as day-trippers from across south Bali converge on the same handful of popular spots. Parking near the centre can also be limited; some drivers will drop you at a walkable distance rather than fighting for a spot directly outside a specific sight.

## Yoga and wellness as an add-on

If wellness is part of why you're interested in Ubud, several studios offer drop-in classes that can be added to a day trip without needing to commit to a longer stay. Booking a morning class before starting your sightseeing, or an evening session before the drive back, is a way to sample Ubud's wellness scene without dedicating your whole day to it.

## If you're travelling with older relatives

Ubud's rice terraces and temple sites often involve uneven ground, some steps and a fair amount of walking between stops, which is worth factoring in if you're travelling with older relatives or anyone with limited mobility. Choosing fewer, more accessible stops and allowing extra time between them usually makes for a more comfortable day than trying to match a faster-paced itinerary.

## FAQ

### How long does a day trip to Ubud take from south Bali?

It depends on which area you're starting from and the day's traffic, but plan for a substantial part of your day to go to travel time alone. Starting early and limiting yourself to two or three main stops generally produces a much better experience than trying to pack the day full.

### Is Ubud worth a day trip or should I stay overnight?

A day trip works well as an introduction, especially if your main interest is the coast and you just want a taste of Ubud's rice terraces and temples. If the art scene, wellness culture or wider countryside genuinely appeal, an overnight or two lets you see far more without the rushed pace of a single day.

### What should I see in Ubud if I only have one day?

A rice terrace (Tegallalang is well known), the Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary or a temple, and lunch in central Ubud make a solid, unhurried day. Trying to add a waterfall or the art market on top usually means rushing at least one stop, so prioritise based on what your group actually wants to see.

### Do I need a driver for a day trip to Ubud, or can I self-drive?

A driver for the day is generally the easier and safer option unless you're very comfortable on Bali's roads, since the route inland involves more winding and hilly sections than south Bali's flatter coastal roads. It also lets everyone in your group relax rather than navigating.

### Can I combine a day trip to Ubud with a waterfall visit?

Yes, and it's one of the more efficient combinations, since several waterfalls sit within a short detour of Ubud's centre. Plan to see one waterfall alongside your other Ubud stops rather than several, since travel between individual sites takes longer than the map distance suggests.
