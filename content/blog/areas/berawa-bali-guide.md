Berawa is the neighbourhood at Canggu's southern edge, bordering Seminyak, and it's the most developed of Canggu's pockets — a dense concentration of beach clubs, restaurants, gyms and villas that's grown quickly over the past several years. If you want the widest range of things within walking or short scooter distance, Berawa is usually the answer, at the cost of more traffic and a busier feel than Canggu's quieter neighbourhoods.

## What actually sits in Berawa

Berawa's main draw is density: several well-known beach clubs sit along or near Berawa Beach, alongside a wide spread of restaurants covering most cuisines, gyms and fitness studios, and a growing cluster of co-working spaces. It's the neighbourhood in Canggu where you're least likely to need a scooter for a typical day's errands and meals.

[Villa The Wave](/villa/canggu/the-wave-6br-beachside-villa-canggu/), one of our larger properties in Canggu, sits a 2–5 minute walk from Berawa Beach, which gives a sense of how central this part of the neighbourhood is to the beach itself.

## Traffic and getting around

Berawa's density is also its main downside: the main roads through the area, especially toward Batu Bolong and the beach access points, get genuinely congested at peak times — early evening and weekends particularly. Side lanes are quieter, and a villa a few streets back from the main road can mean a meaningfully calmer stay without losing much walking distance to amenities.

## Who Berawa suits

Berawa works well for groups and families wanting a wide choice of restaurants and beach clubs without much daily planning, for shorter stays where convenience matters more than absolute quiet, and for anyone who wants Seminyak's proximity without staying in Seminyak itself. It's less suited to guests prioritising quiet evenings or a slower pace — for that, Pererenan or the inland lanes further north are a better fit.

## Renting in Berawa

Villa rates in Berawa reflect its developed position — generally at the higher end of Canggu's range, particularly for properties close to the beach or the main dining strip. Rates vary by season, size and exact position, so treat any figure you see quoted elsewhere as indicative rather than current; ask for a same-day quote against your specific dates.

## Buying in Berawa

Property in Berawa, like the rest of Canggu, is sold to foreigners on a leasehold basis in most cases; freehold ownership by foreign individuals isn't generally available under Indonesian law. Land in Berawa's more developed core commands a premium over Canggu's newer, quieter pockets, reflecting both scarcity and proximity to amenities. If you're comparing villas for sale, check the remaining lease term carefully, since this varies significantly between listings and affects long-term value more than the headline price alone.

## How Berawa compares to its neighbours

Berawa's closest sibling neighbourhood is Pererenan, further north — where Berawa is dense and developed, Pererenan is calmer and greener, still growing into its amenities. If Berawa's pace doesn't suit you, it's worth comparing the two before settling on a booking or purchase; our Pererenan guide covers that neighbourhood in the same depth.

## Restaurants and dining in Berawa

Berawa has the widest spread of restaurant cuisines in Canggu, from Indonesian warungs to international fine dining, largely clustered along its main roads and around the beach club strip. This variety is one of its strongest daily-life advantages over quieter neighbourhoods, where a wider dinner choice usually means a short ride rather than a walk.

Prices at Berawa's more polished restaurants and beach club dining tend to sit higher than equivalent options in Batu Bolong or Pererenan, reflecting both the setting and the crowd it draws.

## Fitness and wellness in Berawa

Berawa has a strong concentration of gyms, fitness studios and wellness centres, more so than Canggu's quieter neighbourhoods, making it a popular base for guests wanting a structured fitness routine alongside their stay. Several offer day passes or short-term memberships suited to a holiday-length stay rather than requiring a long commitment.

## FAQ

### Is Berawa part of Canggu?

Yes, Berawa is the southernmost neighbourhood within the broader Canggu area, bordering Seminyak. It's the most developed of Canggu's pockets, with the highest concentration of beach clubs, restaurants and gyms.

### Is Berawa expensive compared to the rest of Canggu?

Generally yes — its density and proximity to Seminyak put villa rates and property prices at the higher end of Canggu's range, particularly for positions close to the beach or main dining strip. Rates vary by season and exact location, so get a current quote for specifics.

### Can foreigners buy property in Berawa?

Foreigners can generally hold property in Bali, including Berawa, on a leasehold basis rather than freehold, which isn't typically available to foreign individuals under Indonesian law. Check the remaining lease term on any specific listing, since it varies and affects long-term value.

### Is Berawa good for families?

It can work well for families who want a wide choice of restaurants and beach clubs nearby, though it's busier and more traffic-heavy than quieter Canggu neighbourhoods like Pererenan. A villa set back from the main roads gives a calmer base while keeping the convenience.

### How does Berawa compare to Pererenan?

Berawa is denser and more developed, with more amenities within walking distance but heavier traffic. Pererenan, further north, is calmer and greener with more rice-field-facing villas, generally suiting longer stays and quieter daily routines better than Berawa.
