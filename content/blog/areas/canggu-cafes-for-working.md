Canggu has more laptop-friendly cafés per square kilometre than anywhere else in Bali, and most have reasonable Wi-Fi as standard. The filter that actually separates a good working café from a frustrating one isn't the internet — it's seating comfort for hours at a stretch, ambient noise, plug access, and whether the place is genuinely set up for long stays or built for quick turnover.

We manage villas across Canggu and hear the same complaints from remote-working guests: not "the Wi-Fi was slow" but "I couldn't find a seat with a plug after 10am" or "the music was too loud to take a call." Here's what to actually check.

## Wi-Fi is rarely the real bottleneck

Most Canggu cafés advertise fast Wi-Fi, and most deliver something workable for browsing and email. The problems show up on video calls during peak hours (roughly 10am–2pm) when a popular café is full of people streaming or on calls simultaneously. If your work involves regular video calls, ask staff directly about typical speeds at busy times rather than trusting a posted number, or keep mobile data as backup.

## Seating and plug access matter more than people expect

A café can have excellent Wi-Fi and still be a poor place to work if the seating is designed for a quick coffee rather than four hours at a laptop — low tables, no back support, or plug points concentrated at two tables that fill by 9am. Arrive early (before 9am) at popular spots if you need a specific seat with power, since good working spots get claimed fast.

## Noise levels vary enormously by design

Some Canggu cafés play loud music deliberately, aiming for an all-day social atmosphere rather than a work one — fine for casual browsing, difficult for calls. Others are quieter by design, often set back from the busiest strips. If calls are a regular part of your day, scope out a café's noise level at the actual time you'll need it before committing to it as a daily base.

## Café etiquette for long work sessions

- Order a proper meal, not just one coffee, if you're staying more than an hour or two during busy periods
- Avoid the busiest lunch window (12–2pm) for calls if the café is popular for food, since it gets loud and full
- Ask before spreading out across more than one seat at busy times
- Consider rotating between two or three regular spots rather than expecting one café to always have space

## Power outages and backup plans

Power outages happen occasionally across Bali, including Canggu, more often during wet-season storms. A café with a backup generator is worth identifying in advance if a work session genuinely can't afford an interruption, and it's a reasonable question to ask staff directly rather than assuming.

Keeping a second, less-preferred café in mind as a backup is a sensible habit for anyone working daily in Canggu — busy cafés fill up, Wi-Fi occasionally drops, and having an alternative saves a wasted morning.

## Food quality and long-session value

Since working cafés expect you to order more than one coffee during a multi-hour session, food quality matters more here than at a café you're only visiting briefly. The better working cafés in Canggu tend to have genuinely good, varied menus rather than a token food offering built around coffee sales — worth factoring in alongside Wi-Fi and seating when picking a regular spot.

## How this affects where you should stay

If daily café work is central to your routine, staying within walking distance of two or three reliable spots matters more than staying near the busiest strip generally. Several of our villas in Canggu sit close to quieter, work-friendly café clusters rather than the loudest parts of Batu Bolong — worth mentioning when you enquire if this is a priority.

## FAQ

### Is Canggu good for remote work?

Yes, it's one of the more established remote-work bases in South-East Asia, with a wide spread of laptop-friendly cafés and several dedicated co-working spaces. The main planning point is picking the right café or space for your specific work pattern, since not all of them suit calls or long sessions equally.

### What time should I arrive at a Canggu café to get a good working seat?

Before 9am at popular spots, since seats with reliable power access fill quickly once the morning work crowd arrives, especially at well-known cafés on the main strips.

### Are Canggu cafés good for taking video calls?

Some are, but many are designed for a social, high-energy atmosphere that makes calls difficult. Quieter, inland cafés set back from the busiest strips are generally more reliable for calls than trend-led beachfront or main-strip spots.

### Should I use a café or a coworking space in Canggu?

Cafés work well for casual, flexible work and shorter sessions; a coworking space is generally the better choice if you need consistent desks, meeting rooms or a fixed monthly setup. See our coworking spaces in Canggu guide for the comparison.

### Do Canggu cafés mind if I work there for hours?

Most are fine with it as long as you order reasonably during your stay, particularly during their busy periods. Ordering a proper meal rather than just one coffee for a multi-hour session is the general etiquette.
