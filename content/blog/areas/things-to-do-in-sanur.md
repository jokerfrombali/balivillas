The honest answer is: not a huge amount inside Sanur itself, and that's the point. Sanur is the base you choose when you want a calm walking beach path, easy access to the harbour for island trips, and enough restaurants and cafés to fill your days, without the traffic and noise of Canggu or Seminyak.

What Sanur does have is a genuinely pleasant 5km beachfront promenade, a working local market, temples you can visit on foot or by bike, and a harbour that's the main jumping-off point for Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan. Here's how to fill a stay without padding your itinerary with things that aren't actually there.

## Walk or cycle the beach promenade

The paved path running along Sanur's beach is the area's best free activity. It stretches from the harbour past hotels, cafés, temples and stretches of sand, and is flat and shaded in parts, making it usable early morning, at sunrise, or in the late afternoon once the heat drops. Bicycles can be hired by the hour at several points along the path.

Sunrise is worth setting an alarm for — Sanur faces east, unlike Canggu and Seminyak's west-facing sunset beaches, so this is genuinely the better base if a beach sunrise matters to you.

## Visit the harbour and take an island day trip

Sanur harbour is the main departure point for fast boats to Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan. Even if you're not going for a full day trip, it's worth a look — the harbour area has its own small cluster of shops and warungs.

If you do want to see Nusa Penida, boats typically leave in the early morning; booking a day or two ahead in high season is sensible since popular departure times fill up. We cover this in more detail, including what to check before you buy a ticket, in our guide to the Sanur–Nusa Penida ferry.

## Browse the local market

Sanur's market (Pasar Sindhu, among others) runs mainly in the mornings and is a genuine local market rather than a tourist-focused one — produce, spices, snacks and some crafts. It's a good half-hour stop if you want a sense of daily life away from the beach road, and it's cheap.

## See the temples along the coast

Several small temples sit directly on or near the beach path, including Pura Blanjong and Pura Segara. None require a major detour — most are visible from the promenade itself. Dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered) and a sarong is sometimes provided or required at the entrance; check signage on arrival since rules vary by site.

## Eat your way along the beach road

Sanur's restaurant and café scene is one of its strongest draws for a multi-day stay. Options range from beachfront seafood warungs to more polished restaurants inland. We've written a separate guide to the best restaurants in Sanur if you want specific recommendations by cuisine and budget.

## Spend a day trip outside Sanur

Sanur's central east-coast position makes it a workable base for day trips to Ubud, Denpasar, or south to Uluwatu, all within roughly 45–75 minutes by car depending on traffic. It's not the fastest base for west-coast Bali (Canggu, Seminyak, Uluwatu are 1–1.5 hours away), so weigh that against how much time you plan to spend outside Sanur itself.

## Watersports and the reef

Sanur's calm, reef-protected sea makes it one of the better spots in Bali for stand-up paddleboarding, kayaking and gentle snorkelling, especially compared with the surf-heavy west coast. Conditions are generally mild year-round given the reef break offshore, though visibility for snorkelling varies with tide and season, so ask locally about current conditions rather than assuming every day is ideal.

Equipment hire is available at several points along the beach path, usually by the hour, and doesn't require advance booking outside peak periods.

## Cycling beyond the promenade

Beyond the beach path itself, Sanur's back lanes are relatively quiet and flat, making bicycle exploration a pleasant way to see the residential side of the area — small temples, local shops, and glimpses of daily life away from the tourist-facing beach road. A few hours with a rented bike covers a good stretch of the area without needing a scooter or car.

## Spa and wellness options

Sanur has a reasonable spread of spas and wellness venues, from simple beachfront massage stalls to more polished treatment centres, generally at lower prices than similar treatments in Seminyak or Canggu. A relaxed spa afternoon pairs well with a quieter Sanur stay, particularly after a day trip or an early Nusa Penida boat.

## A simple two-night itinerary

- Day one morning: promenade walk, market visit, lunch at a beachfront warung
- Day one evening: dinner along the beach road, early night if the Nusa Penida boat leaves early
- Day two: Nusa Penida day trip, or a half-day out to Ubud if you'd rather stay on the mainland

## FAQ

### Is there enough to do in Sanur for a week?

For most people, yes, when combined with day trips — the beach path, market, temples and restaurant scene fill several days on their own, and Sanur's central location makes Ubud, Uluwatu and the Nusa islands realistic day trips for the rest of the week.

### Is Sanur good for a family with kids?

Yes, it's generally considered the most family-friendly of Bali's main tourist areas: calm sea, a flat walkable promenade, and less traffic and nightlife than Canggu or Seminyak. Several of our villas in Sanur are set up for families with pool safety and space in mind.

### What's the best time of day to walk the Sanur beach path?

Sunrise or the hour before sunset, when the heat has dropped and the light is best. Sanur faces east, so it's the better base in Bali for a beach sunrise rather than a sunset.

### How far is Sanur from Ubud and Uluwatu?

Ubud is roughly 45–60 minutes by car depending on traffic; Uluwatu is closer to 60–75 minutes. Both are realistic day trips from a Sanur base, though traffic can extend either significantly during peak hours.

### Do I need a boat to see the Nusa islands from Sanur?

Yes — Sanur harbour is the main departure point for fast boats to Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan, with services typically running from early morning. See our guide to the Sanur–Nusa Penida ferry for what to check before booking.
