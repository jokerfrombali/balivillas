Relocating a family to Bali realistically takes three to six months from serious planning to feeling settled, longer if school enrolment or a specific visa category is involved. Families who rush the visa and school steps in particular tend to hit avoidable delays once they arrive, so a staged timeline, rather than trying to arrange everything at once, generally goes more smoothly.

We host a steady stream of relocating families on monthly and yearly villas while they sort out schools, visas and eventually a longer-term home, and this is the timeline we see work well.

## Three to four months out: research and visa

Start with your visa category, since it is usually the slowest-moving piece and affects your realistic move date. Our guide on [visas needed to rent a villa in Bali](/blog/long-term/visa-needed-to-rent-villa-bali/) covers what most families need to arrange before or shortly after arrival. In parallel, start researching schools if you have children of school age; good international schools often have limited mid-year spots, and our guide on [international school fees in Bali](/blog/long-term/international-schools-bali-fees/) is a useful starting point for budgeting this properly.

## Two months out: area and school shortlist

Narrow down to an area based on your priorities, proximity to a shortlisted school, walkability, and pace of life. Sanur and Umalas both have strong options for families; our guide on [schools near Sanur and Umalas](/blog/long-term/schools-near-sanur-and-umalas/) covers the local options in those two areas specifically. Book a monthly villa for your arrival period rather than committing to a yearly lease sight unseen; it gives you room to confirm the area actually suits you once you are living it day to day.

## Timeline at a glance

| Time before move | Focus |
|---|---|
| 3–4 months out | Visa category and application, school shortlist |
| 2 months out | Area shortlist, book arrival accommodation, school applications |
| 1 month out | Shipping or packing decisions, insurance, final school confirmation |
| Arrival week | Move into arrival villa, domicile registration, settle school start |
| First 4–8 weeks | Confirm area, sort a yearly lease if satisfied, set up local admin (bank, driving) |

## Arrival to eight weeks: settling in

Once in Bali, the first priority is usually a domicile letter and other local registration steps, which our guide on the [Bali domicile letter](/blog/long-term/bali-address-and-domicile-letter/) covers in detail. This period is also when most families decide whether their arrival area genuinely suits them, and either extend into a yearly lease at the same villa or move once the short-term booking ends. Our guide on [moving to Bali with kids](/blog/long-term/moving-to-bali-with-kids/) covers the practical side of this settling-in period beyond the timeline itself.

## What commonly goes wrong

- Underestimating how far in advance a good international school needs an application
- Signing a yearly lease before confirming the area suits daily family life
- Leaving visa paperwork until close to the planned move date
- Not budgeting for the arrival period separately from the eventual settled monthly cost

## A staged approach we recommend

1. Confirm visa category and start the application three to four months out
2. Shortlist two or three schools and start applications two months out
3. Book a monthly arrival villa rather than a yearly lease sight unseen
4. Use the first month in Bali to confirm the area before committing longer-term
5. Move to a yearly lease, or start considering a purchase, once settled

## FAQ

### How far in advance should I start planning a family move to Bali?
Three to four months is a realistic minimum, mainly to allow time for visa processing and school applications, both of which move more slowly than most families expect.

### Should I book a yearly villa lease before arriving in Bali?
No, we recommend a monthly booking for your arrival period instead. It gives you room to confirm the area genuinely suits your family before committing to a full year.

### How long does it take to enrol children in an international school in Bali?
It varies by school, but popular schools can have limited mid-year availability, so starting applications two to three months before your planned arrival is safer than leaving it until you land.

### What should I sort out in the first weeks after arriving in Bali?
Domicile registration, confirming your visa status is fully in order, setting up local banking if needed, and using the first few weeks to confirm your chosen area actually works for daily family life before locking in a longer lease.

### Is Sanur or Umalas better for a relocating family?
Both work well; Sanur suits families wanting a calmer, flatter, beach-focused daily routine, while Umalas suits families closer to Canggu's amenities and coworking options. Our [schools near Sanur and Umalas](/blog/long-term/schools-near-sanur-and-umalas/) guide covers the specifics for each.
