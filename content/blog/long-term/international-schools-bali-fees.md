International school fees in Bali are set by each school individually and change year to year, so there is no single number that applies island-wide. What we can say with confidence is what drives the range: curriculum (IB tends to sit at the top, national-plus or lower-cost international options sit well below it), campus reputation and facilities, grade level (secondary usually costs more than primary), and whether the school is in the Canggu/Umalas corridor, Sanur, or further out toward Ubud.

Budget for more than the headline tuition figure. Registration and enrolment fees, a refundable or non-refundable deposit, a capital or development levy some schools charge annually, uniforms, books, school trips, and bus transport if you are not within walking distance can add a meaningful amount on top of tuition. Ask each school for their full fee schedule in writing, not just the tuition line, before comparing two options.

## Why fees vary so much between schools

A school running full IB Diploma Programme accreditation carries higher costs than one offering a national-plus or Cambridge-lite curriculum, partly because of teacher qualifications and accreditation fees, and partly because IB families are often budgeting for university abroad and are willing to pay for that pathway. Newer campuses with significant facilities investment (pools, purpose-built labs, large sports fields) also tend to price higher to cover that capital cost.

Class size and teacher-to-student ratio matter too. A school marketing small class sizes as a selling point is usually pricing that into tuition. None of this means the most expensive school is automatically the best fit — it means the fee reflects a specific set of trade-offs you should ask about directly rather than assume.

## What is usually billed separately from tuition

| Fee type | Typical pattern | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Registration fee | One-off, non-refundable, paid at application | Is it credited against the first term if you enrol |
| Enrolment or capital levy | Often annual, sometimes one-off | What it funds, whether it is refundable if you leave |
| Deposit | Often refundable at withdrawal, subject to notice period | Notice period required and how refund is processed |
| Uniform and books | Per year, varies by grade | Whether second-hand options exist |
| Bus transport | Per term, by distance/zone | Whether your villa's area is covered |
| School trips and activities | Variable, sometimes optional | Whether they are compulsory for the curriculum |

Ask specifically about the withdrawal notice period. Many international schools require a full term's notice or forfeit a term's fees if you leave mid-year, which matters if your Bali stay is not guaranteed to run the full school year.

## Matching school choice to how long you are staying

A family committing to Bali for several years can weigh IB continuity and university pathway more heavily. A family testing a year or two might prioritise a school with flexible entry points, a shorter notice period, and less sunk cost in uniforms, deposits and levies if plans change. Be honest with yourself about which category you are in before you sign, because the fee structures genuinely reward different time horizons.

This is also where housing decisions and school decisions connect directly. Commuting on Bali's roads at school-run hours is slower than distance alone suggests, so choosing a villa within a realistic drive time of your shortlisted schools is worth doing before you fix on either.

## Comparing schools without just comparing tuition

Ask each school for a same-term-length, same-grade comparison, since a "per term" figure at one school and a "per year" figure at another are not directly comparable without doing the maths yourself. Request a tour and a conversation with current parents if possible — expat groups on the ground are usually candid about which schools deliver on what they charge for and which have had turnover or facilities issues worth asking about directly.

- Get the full written fee schedule, not just tuition, from every school on your shortlist
- Ask about the withdrawal notice period and what happens to fees already paid if plans change
- Confirm whether bus transport covers your intended villa area, since it changes your effective commute cost
- Ask current parents, not just admissions staff, about class size and turnover

## Where families tend to land in each area

Sanur and the areas around it host a smaller number of established schools and tends to appeal to families wanting a calmer, more residential base. The Canggu-Umalas corridor has the densest concentration of international school options and coworking-friendly infrastructure, which suits remote-working parents. ## FAQ

### Are international school fees in Bali higher than in Singapore or Hong Kong?
Generally lower, though top-tier IB schools in Bali can still represent a significant annual cost. The gap versus Singapore or Hong Kong tends to be largest at the mid-tier and national-plus end of the market, smaller at the premium IB end.

### Do international schools in Bali charge a deposit?
Most do, and it is usually refundable when you formally withdraw your child, subject to a notice period stated in the enrolment contract. Always get the deposit and refund terms in writing before paying, since policies differ meaningfully between schools.

### What is a capital or development levy?
It is an additional annual or one-off fee, separate from tuition, that some schools charge to fund facilities and campus development. Ask directly whether a school charges one, since it is easy to miss when comparing tuition figures alone between schools.

### Does school choice affect which area of Bali we should live in?
Yes, meaningfully. Commute times on Bali's roads during school hours can be long even over a short distance, so most families choose a villa within a realistic drive of their shortlisted schools before finalising either decision.

### Can we enrol partway through the school year?
Many schools accept mid-year enrolment, though availability depends on space in that grade and curriculum continuity, particularly for IB. Ask directly rather than assuming, and factor in a settling-in period for children joining outside the normal start of year.
