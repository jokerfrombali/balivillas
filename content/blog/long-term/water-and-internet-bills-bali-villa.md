Water and internet bills for a villa in Bali are generally the smallest of the recurring utility costs, well below electricity, but they're not always included in rent and the arrangement varies enough between villas that it's worth confirming before you move in. Water is often included up to a reasonable cap; internet is increasingly bundled into the rent, especially in villas aimed at remote workers.

Neither of these costs will make or break a long-term rental budget the way electricity can, but assuming they're included when they're not, or budgeting for a plan you don't actually get, is a common and avoidable source of friction after move-in.

## Water: how it typically works

Bali villas draw water either from municipal PDAM supply, a groundwater well, or both, and the source affects both taste and reliability. Many long-term rentals include water in the base rent, sometimes with a stated cap on usage before overage charges apply; a smaller number bill water separately based on a meter.

Water use in a villa is driven mainly by household size, garden irrigation and pool top-up needs (pools lose some water to evaporation and splash-out and need periodic refilling). None of this typically adds up to a large sum compared with electricity, but ask directly whether it's capped, included outright, or metered separately, so there's no surprise.

## Internet: what varies

Internet provision in Bali has improved substantially in recent years, with fibre now common in the main tourist and expat areas including Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan. What varies between villas is:

- Whether Wi-Fi is included in the rent at all
- The actual speed delivered versus what's advertised
- Whether it's a dedicated line for the villa or shared across a small compound
- Backup options (mobile hotspot, secondary provider) if the main connection drops

For remote workers, actual delivered speed and reliability matter more than the headline number on a router box. Ask for a recent speed test result from the villa itself rather than trusting a general claim.

## Why dry season and area matter for water

Some parts of Bali see reduced water pressure or supply interruptions during the dry season, particularly villas relying more heavily on well water in areas with higher demand. This isn't universal and varies by specific location, but it's worth asking the owner or manager directly whether the villa has experienced any supply issues, rather than assuming water access is uniformly reliable year-round.

## Questions worth asking before you sign

- Is water included in the rent, and if so, is there a usage cap?
- Is the water source PDAM, well, or a mix, and has supply ever been interrupted?
- Is Wi-Fi included, and can I see a recent speed test from the actual villa?
- Is the internet connection dedicated to this villa or shared with neighbours?
- Is there a backup option if the internet goes down, given how often that matters for remote work?

## Backup plans for remote workers

If your income depends on staying online, it's worth having a backup plan beyond whatever the villa provides as standard, regardless of how good the primary connection is. A local SIM with a generous data allowance, kept as a secondary option, covers most short outages cheaply. Some remote workers also keep a portable Wi-Fi hotspot device as a more robust backup for longer or more frequent interruptions, particularly useful if you have calls or deadlines that genuinely cannot wait for a connection to be restored. Ask your villa manager whether they've had issues with connectivity reliability specifically, rather than just asking about advertised speed, since reliability and raw speed are different questions with different answers.

## What tends to go wrong when it isn't confirmed in writing

The most common friction we see over water and internet isn't a large bill, it's a mismatch between what a tenant assumed at signing and what actually happens once they've moved in. A tenant who assumed unlimited water and then gets a message about an overage charge feels blindsided, even if the cap was technically mentioned somewhere in the original conversation. Getting the specific terms into the written lease, not just discussed verbally, removes this entire category of dispute, since there's a document both sides can point back to rather than relying on memory of a conversation from months earlier.

## How remote workers should treat internet reliability specifically

If your income depends on a reliable connection, treat internet not just as a convenience item on your checklist but as a genuine deal-breaker worth testing before you sign. Ask to do a video call from inside the actual villa during your viewing, at a normal time of day rather than a quiet early morning, so you get a realistic sense of how the connection performs when it matters. A villa that looks perfect on every other criterion isn't a good fit for remote work if the internet can't reliably support your calls, and it's much easier to discover this before signing a year-long lease than after.

## Checking supply history before a longer commitment

For a lease of a year or more, it's reasonable to ask the owner or manager whether the villa has had any notable water or internet outages in the recent past, and how they were resolved. A villa with an occasional, quickly-fixed internet blip is a different proposition from one with a known recurring issue that's never been properly addressed, and owners who've been managing the property for a while usually know the difference.

## FAQ

### Is water usually included in a long-term villa rental in Bali?
Often yes, sometimes with a usage cap before overage charges apply. It varies by owner, so confirm the specific terms rather than assuming it's fully included with no limit.

### How fast is internet typically in Bali villas?
Fibre internet is now common in the main expat and tourist areas, and many long-term villas advertise speeds in the hundreds of Mbps. Actual delivered speed can differ from the advertised figure, so ask for a real speed test from the villa.

### Does water supply ever get interrupted in Bali during the dry season?
It can, in some areas and for some villas more reliant on well water, though this isn't universal. Ask the owner or manager directly whether the specific villa has experienced supply issues before.

### Is internet usually included in the rent for long-term villas in Bali?
Increasingly yes, especially in villas marketed toward remote workers. Older or more budget-oriented villas may not include it, so confirm rather than assume, particularly if you need internet for work.

### What should I do if the villa's internet turns out to be slower than promised?
Raise it with the owner or manager promptly and ask for a fix or an alternative, such as a backup mobile hotspot. Getting the expected speed stated in writing before you sign gives you a clearer basis to raise the issue.
