Ending a long-term villa rental in Bali cleanly comes down to three things: giving the notice period your contract specifies, settling any outstanding utility bills before handover, and doing a proper walkthrough with the landlord or manager so your security deposit is returned promptly and without dispute. Most of the friction we see at the end of a tenancy comes from one of these three being handled informally or too late.

This guide covers what to expect and check as a long-stay renter approaching the end of a lease, whether you are leaving Bali entirely or moving to a different villa.

## Check your notice period first

Most yearly and monthly villa contracts specify a notice period, commonly 30 to 60 days for a yearly lease, though this varies by landlord and should be confirmed against your specific contract rather than assumed. Give notice in writing, by message or email, even if your relationship with the landlord is informal, so there is a clear record of the date. Our guide on [the villa rental contract checklist](/blog/long-term/bali-villa-rental-contract-checklist/) covers what a proper contract should specify on this point if you are still negotiating one.

## Settling utilities and outstanding costs

Before handover, settle any outstanding electricity, water or internet bills tied to the villa, and confirm with the landlord exactly what is included in your deposit versus billed separately. Our guide on [water and internet bills for a Bali villa](/blog/long-term/water-and-internet-bills-bali-villa/) explains how these are typically split between tenant and landlord, which is worth reviewing again before your final month so there are no surprises at handover.

## Getting your deposit back

A security deposit is typically returned after a walkthrough confirms no damage beyond normal wear and tear, and after any outstanding bills are settled. Do the walkthrough together with the landlord or their manager rather than relying on a one-sided assessment, and photograph the villa's condition on both move-in and move-out if you can. Our guide on [security deposits for Bali villa rentals](/blog/long-term/security-deposit-bali-villa-rental/) covers what counts as normal wear and tear versus chargeable damage.

## Timeline for a clean move-out

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| At notice deadline in contract | Give written notice of your move-out date |
| 4–6 weeks before leaving | Arrange final utility readings and settle any outstanding balances |
| 1–2 weeks before leaving | Schedule the move-out walkthrough with the landlord |
| Move-out day | Complete the walkthrough, return keys, confirm deposit timeline |
| After move-out | Follow up on deposit return if it has not arrived within the agreed window |

## If you need to leave before your lease ends

Ending a lease early is a different, more complex situation than a scheduled move-out. Our guide on [breaking a villa lease in Bali](/blog/long-term/breaking-a-villa-lease-bali/) covers what early termination typically involves, including any penalty clauses your contract may specify, and is worth reading well before you need to act on it if there is any chance your plans could change.

## What commonly causes disputes

- No written record of the notice date
- Utility bills not settled before the final walkthrough
- No shared documentation of the villa's condition at move-in, making move-out disputes harder to resolve fairly
- Assuming a verbal agreement on the deposit timeline is enough without it being in the contract

## If you are moving to another villa rather than leaving Bali

Many long-stay renters end one lease not because they are leaving Bali but because they are moving to a different villa or area, and the same three fundamentals still apply: notice, utilities and the walkthrough. The one extra consideration is timing the overlap, or lack of it, between your old and new lease so you are not paying for two villas longer than necessary, or scrambling with no accommodation gap in between. Discuss your intended move-out date with your new villa manager early so both timelines can be coordinated where possible.

## Keeping records throughout your tenancy, not just at the end

The clean move-outs we see are almost always ones where the renter kept basic records throughout the tenancy, not just at the final walkthrough: copies of the signed contract, records of rent and deposit payments, and any written communication about repairs or issues raised during the stay. Trying to reconstruct this history at move-out, from memory or scattered messages, makes any disagreement harder to resolve fairly for both sides.

## What to do next

A villa rental in Bali generally ends smoothly when notice is given in writing, utilities are settled before handover, and the move-out walkthrough is done jointly with clear documentation on both sides.

Next steps: check your contract's notice period now, even if you are not leaving soon, so you know what to expect, and keep your move-in documentation somewhere you can find it when the time comes. ## FAQ

### How much notice do I need to give before ending a villa lease in Bali?
It depends on your specific contract, but 30 to 60 days is common for a yearly lease. Always check the exact figure in your own agreement rather than assuming a standard period applies.

### Will I get my full security deposit back when I leave?
Generally yes, provided the villa is left in a condition consistent with normal wear and tear and all outstanding bills are settled. Photographing the villa's condition at move-in and move-out helps resolve any disagreement quickly.

### What happens if I need to leave before my lease term ends?
This is treated differently from a scheduled move-out and may involve a penalty depending on your contract terms. Our guide on [breaking a villa lease in Bali](/blog/long-term/breaking-a-villa-lease-bali/) covers what to expect and how to approach it with your landlord.

### Who settles the final electricity and water bill when I move out?
This depends on how your contract splits utilities between tenant and landlord. Confirm the split and arrange a final meter reading before your move-out walkthrough so there is no ambiguity.

### Should I do a walkthrough with my landlord before leaving?
Yes, always. A joint walkthrough at move-out, ideally compared against documentation from move-in, is the single most effective way to avoid a dispute over the security deposit.
