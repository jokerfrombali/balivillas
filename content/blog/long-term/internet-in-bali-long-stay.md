Internet in Bali for long stays is genuinely good in the main expat corridors — Canggu, Umalas, Sanur and central areas generally have fibre coverage capable of handling video calls, large file transfers and streaming without drama. The gap most remote workers underestimate is not average speed, it is reliability: brief outages happen, particularly during heavy rain, and a villa relying on a single connection with no backup can mean a missed meeting at an inconvenient moment.

Before booking anywhere for a long stay, ask specifically what internet speed the villa advertises, whether it is a dedicated line or shared, and whether there is a mobile data backup available on site. This matters more for a working stay than the headline speed number does.

## What speed actually means for remote work

A connection advertised at 100–300 Mbps is more than sufficient for video calls, cloud-based work and streaming for a single household, even with several people working simultaneously. What causes real problems is not the top speed but latency spikes and brief dropouts, which a speed number alone does not tell you. Ask a villa host directly whether they have had connectivity issues, rather than relying on the advertised figure alone.

| Setup | Good for | Backup needed for |
|---|---|---|
| Villa fibre only | Everyday work, streaming, calls | Critical live meetings, deadline-day reliability |
| Fibre + local SIM data | Most remote-work scenarios | Extended power or fibre outages |
| Fibre + dedicated mobile hotspot device | Serious daily reliance (client calls, livestreams) | Rare, but worth a generator too in storm season |

## Setting up a mobile data backup

A local SIM with a generous data plan, kept in a dedicated hotspot device or a spare phone, is the simplest backup and costs relatively little for the peace of mind. Indonesian mobile networks (Telkomsel, XL, Indosat and others) generally have solid 4G/5G coverage in south Bali's populated areas, making this a genuinely workable fallback rather than a token gesture.

- Buy a local SIM in your first days and test its data speed at your actual villa, not just in town
- Keep a dedicated hotspot device charged and ready rather than relying on a phone you also need for calls
- Ask your villa host what backup, if any, they already have on site
- For anything mission-critical (a live client call, a product launch), plan the backup in advance, not on the day

## Power cuts and how they affect connectivity

Fibre routers and modems need power, so a power outage takes your internet down with it even if the fibre line itself is fine. Some villas, including several of ours, have backup arrangements for exactly this scenario; ask directly rather than assuming. A charged mobile hotspot with its own battery is a simple way to stay connected through a short outage regardless of what backup the villa has.

## FAQ

### Is internet in Bali good enough for remote work and video calls?
Yes, in the main expat areas — Canggu, Umalas, Sanur and central locations — fibre connections are generally fast and stable enough for video calls, cloud work and streaming. The main risk is occasional brief outages rather than insufficient speed.

### Do I need a backup internet connection in Bali?
For anyone relying on internet for work, yes, it is worth having. A local SIM with a data plan in a spare phone or hotspot device is a low-cost, effective backup for the occasional outage or power cut that affects the main fibre connection.

### What causes internet outages in Bali?
Heavy rain and power cuts are the most common causes of brief disruptions, since routers and modems need power and weather can affect infrastructure. These are usually short, but worth planning around if your work depends on staying connected at specific times.

### Which areas of Bali have the best internet for long stays?
Canggu, Umalas and Sanur generally have the most consistent fibre coverage given the concentration of remote workers and coworking infrastructure in those areas. More rural parts of the island can have less consistent coverage, worth checking directly before committing to a long stay there.
