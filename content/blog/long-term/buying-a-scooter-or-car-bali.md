Buying a scooter in Bali is common practice for anyone staying more than a few months, since it is usually cheaper over a long stay than continuous daily rental, and it gives you far more flexibility than relying on ride-hailing apps for everything. The complication most first-timers do not expect is registration: foreigners generally cannot register a vehicle (STNK) directly in their own name in Indonesia, so most long-term scooter purchases are registered in the name of an Indonesian friend, partner, or through a nominee arrangement with a local dealer, and each of those routes carries different levels of practical risk (as of 2026, verify current registration rules directly with a dealer or notary, since this is an area where the practical workaround matters more than the formal law).

Before you buy, get clear answers on three things: who will hold the registration, whether you are legally able to drive it under your visa and licence status, and what insurance is actually available to you as the buyer rather than the registered owner.

## Renting versus buying: the real comparison

| Factor | Renting a scooter | Buying a scooter |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Low, pay as you go | Higher, one-off purchase |
| Total cost over 6+ months | Generally higher than buying | Generally lower over a longer stay |
| Registration hassle | None, handled by rental company | Requires an Indonesian name on the STNK |
| Flexibility | Easy to change bike, no resale hassle | Yours to modify, but resale needed when you leave |
| Maintenance responsibility | Usually the rental company's problem | Yours to manage |

For stays under a couple of months, renting is usually simpler and not meaningfully more expensive. For longer stays, buying tends to work out cheaper, provided the registration and insurance questions are handled properly upfront.

## The registration question, honestly

Because vehicle registration in Indonesia is tied to Indonesian citizenship or specific residency documentation, most foreign buyers use one of a few common routes: registering in the name of a trusted Indonesian partner or friend, buying through a dealer who offers a documented nominee or leasing-style arrangement, or in some cases leasing rather than owning outright. Each route has different legal and practical risk if the relationship or arrangement breaks down, so this is worth a direct, honest conversation with a local dealer or a notary before you commit money, not something to figure out informally after the fact.

## Licensing: what you actually need to drive it legally

You need a valid licence to ride a scooter legally in Indonesia, and for most foreigners on a longer stay this means either converting an existing licence or obtaining an Indonesian licence (SIM), sometimes alongside an International Driving Permit depending on your circumstances. Our companion guide to the Indonesian driving licence process for foreigners covers this step in detail, since it applies whether you rent or buy, and driving without a valid local licence carries real legal and insurance risk if something goes wrong.

## Insurance: do not skip this

Basic third-party liability cover is worth having at minimum, and ideally cover for the scooter itself against theft and damage given how common minor scooter accidents are on Bali's roads. Ask specifically whether a policy is available to you as the actual rider and buyer, even if the registration sits in someone else's name, since some policies are tied to the registered owner rather than the rider.

- Confirm who will hold the STNK registration before agreeing a purchase price
- Sort your licence situation (conversion, IDP, or Indonesian SIM) before you start riding regularly
- Ask directly whether insurance covers you as rider even if registration is in another name
- Inspect the scooter's service history and get a mechanic's opinion before buying second-hand
- Agree in writing who is responsible for reselling or handling the registration when you eventually leave

## Buying new versus second-hand

New scooters cost more upfront but come with a warranty and no unknown history. Second-hand scooters are cheaper and widely available through dealers and expat resale groups, but service history and accident history are not always transparent, so an independent mechanic's check before buying is worth the small cost. Long-stayers leaving Bali often sell directly to incoming long-stayers through local expat groups, which is where many second-hand purchases happen.

## Practical riding considerations

Bali's roads carry a real accident risk, particularly for newer riders unused to the traffic patterns, road surface conditions, and the mix of vehicles sharing the same narrow lanes. A properly certified helmet, conservative riding especially in your first weeks, and avoiding riding at night on unfamiliar roads until you know them are worth taking seriously rather than as generic safety advice.

## FAQ

### Can foreigners buy a scooter in Bali?
Yes, buying is common, but registration (STNK) generally cannot be in a foreigner's own name under standard rules, so most buyers use an Indonesian partner, friend or a dealer's documented arrangement (as of 2026, verify current requirements directly with a dealer or notary).

### Do I need an Indonesian driving licence to ride a scooter I bought?
Yes, a valid licence is required to ride legally, whether through licence conversion, an International Driving Permit where accepted, or obtaining an Indonesian SIM. Our guide to the Indonesian driving licence process for foreigners covers the current options.

### Is it cheaper to rent or buy a scooter for a long stay in Bali?
For stays of a few months or longer, buying generally works out cheaper than continuous rental, provided registration and insurance are handled properly. For shorter stays, renting is usually simpler without a meaningful cost disadvantage.

### Is insurance available for a scooter registered in someone else's name?
It depends on the insurer and policy, so ask directly whether cover applies to you as the actual rider even if the STNK registration sits with another person. Do not assume standard cover automatically extends to you without confirming this first.

### What should I check before buying a second-hand scooter in Bali?
Ask for the service history, get an independent mechanic's opinion on its condition, and confirm the registration situation before paying. Second-hand purchases through expat resale groups are common but vary widely in transparency, so a proper inspection is worth the time.
