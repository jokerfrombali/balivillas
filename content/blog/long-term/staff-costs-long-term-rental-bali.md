Staff costs for a long-term villa rental in Bali depend mainly on what's already included by the owner versus what you arrange yourself. Gardening and pool maintenance are commonly bundled into the rent because the owner needs the grounds kept up regardless of who's renting; daily housekeeping, cooking and a dedicated nanny are almost always separate arrangements you set up and pay for directly if you want them.

Many long-term tenants in Bali do hire some household help, since it's a normal and widely available part of life here, but the decision to take it on, and the cost, sits outside the base villa rent in most cases.

## What's typically bundled versus separate

| Role | Usually included in rent | Usually a separate arrangement |
|---|---|---|
| Gardener | Often | — |
| Pool maintenance | Often | — |
| Daily housekeeping | Rarely | Common, hired by the tenant |
| Cook | Rarely | Less common, hired if wanted |
| Nanny/childcare | Never | Always a separate arrangement |
| Driver | Never | Always a separate arrangement |

If a villa listing says staff are included, always confirm exactly which roles and how many hours or days per week, since "staff included" can mean anything from a twice-weekly gardener to daily housekeeping.

## How tenants typically arrange their own staff

If you want housekeeping, cooking or childcare beyond what's included, the common routes are: asking the villa owner or manager if they can recommend someone (often the easiest and most reliable route, since they already know local staff), asking other long-term expats for a referral, or using a local agency. Word of mouth through the expat community is widely used and tends to produce more reliable results than an unknown hire, since you get a track record from someone else's experience.

## What to agree before hiring directly

If you hire staff yourself rather than through the villa, treat it as a real employment relationship, even if informal:

- Agree hours, days and specific duties clearly upfront
- Agree pay and how often it's paid (commonly monthly or weekly)
- Discuss holidays, particularly around Indonesian public holidays and Nyepi, and how these are handled
- Clarify who supplies cleaning products and equipment
- Be clear about trial periods and how either side ends the arrangement

Indonesian labour norms and expectations around staff can differ from what tenants are used to at home; if you're unsure how to structure a fair arrangement, ask your villa manager or other long-term residents in your area what's typical.

## Why "included staff" hours still matter

Even when gardening or pool maintenance is included, ask how often staff actually come and what they cover. A pool serviced once a week is different from one serviced three times, particularly in the wet season when leaves and debris accumulate faster. Similarly, "gardener included" might mean basic upkeep only, not full landscaping or plant replacement, which are worth clarifying if the garden matters to you.

## Staff costs as part of your total budget

Staff you hire yourself sits alongside electricity, water and internet as one of the variable costs outside the base rent on most long-term villas. ## What to ask before you commit to a villa

- Which staff roles, if any, are included in the rent, and how many hours or visits per week?
- Can you recommend reliable housekeeping, cooking or childcare help if I want to arrange it myself?
- Is there a manager on-site or reachable who handles staff issues if something goes wrong?
- How are staff holidays around Nyepi and other public holidays handled for included roles?

## How staff arrangements differ from what many tenants expect at home

For tenants coming from countries where hiring household help is uncommon or expensive relative to income, it can take a bit of recalibration to know what's reasonable to ask for and expect in Bali. Household help is a normal, widely available part of life here, not a luxury reserved for a small segment of residents, and many long-term tenants who never considered hiring a cook or housekeeper at home find it a genuinely useful and common arrangement once settled. That said, treating any staff arrangement fairly, with clear terms, agreed hours and reasonable pay, matters regardless of how affordable it may seem relative to your home country's costs.

## When a villa manager can help with staff issues

One advantage of renting through a villa with active management, rather than directly and informally from an individual owner, is having someone to turn to if a staff arrangement runs into problems, whether that's a scheduling conflict, a dispute over hours, or simply needing a replacement while someone is on leave. A manager who already has relationships with local staff can often resolve these issues faster and more smoothly than a tenant working entirely independently.

## Paying staff fairly and reliably

If you take on staff directly, paying on time and at a rate that reflects genuine local experience matters more than most first-time employers realise, both ethically and practically: reliable, well-treated staff are considerably easier to retain than to replace, and turnover disrupts the smooth running of a household. If you're unsure what a fair rate looks like for a given role, ask your villa manager or other long-term residents rather than guessing from home-country expectations, which rarely translate directly.

## What happens to staff arrangements if you move or renew

If you renew your lease or move to a different villa, think through what happens to any staff relationship you've built directly. Staff hired to work specifically in your villa (a cook, a dedicated housekeeper) generally aren't tied to the property in the way included gardening or pool staff are, so if you move, you'd typically need to either bring the arrangement with you (checking whether that's practical given the new villa's location) or end it properly with fair notice, similar to any other employment relationship ending. Handling this transition thoughtfully matters both for fairness to the staff member and because word travels within the local household-staff community about how previous employers have behaved.

## How staff arrangements differ between an informal listing and a managed villa

A villa rented informally, direct from an individual owner with no professional management, often leaves staff arrangements entirely to be figured out between tenant and owner as they go, with less structure than tenants coming from more formal rental markets might expect. A professionally managed villa, by contrast, usually has clearer answers ready on day one: which roles are included, how often they visit, and who to contact if a staffing issue comes up. Neither approach is wrong, but knowing which kind of arrangement you're entering helps you set expectations correctly and know what to ask before you sign, rather than discovering the gaps after you've moved in.

## A final word on treating staff well

Beyond the practical mechanics of agreeing hours and pay, how you treat staff over a long tenancy genuinely shapes your day-to-day experience living in Bali. Clear communication, reasonable expectations and basic courtesy go a long way, and tenants who build a good working relationship with included or hired staff often find it pays off in small, meaningful ways, from a gardener flagging a maintenance issue early to a housekeeper going the extra mile when it matters.

## FAQ

### Do long-term villa rentals in Bali usually include housekeeping?
Not usually as a default; gardening and pool maintenance are more commonly included, while daily housekeeping is typically arranged and paid for separately by the tenant if wanted.

### How do I find reliable household staff in Bali if I want to hire directly?
The most common and reliable routes are asking your villa owner or manager for a recommendation, or getting a referral from other long-term expats in your area. Both give you some track record before hiring, unlike an unknown local listing.

### Is a gardener normally included in villa rent in Bali?
Often yes, since the owner typically needs the grounds maintained regardless of tenancy. Confirm the frequency of visits, since "included" can still mean anything from weekly to occasional upkeep.

### What should I agree with staff I hire myself in Bali?
Clear hours, duties, pay and payment frequency upfront, plus how public holidays like Nyepi are handled. Treating it as a real, if informal, employment relationship avoids misunderstandings later.

### Does pool maintenance come with the rent on a long-term villa?
Frequently yes, since a neglected pool affects the property regardless of tenant. Ask how often the pool is actually serviced, since frequency varies and matters more in the wet season.
