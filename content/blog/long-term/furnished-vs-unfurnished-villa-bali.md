Furnished or unfurnished villa in Bali is really a question about your timeline: furnished suits anyone staying under roughly two years, moving between countries, or who doesn't want to deal with buying and later disposing of furniture. Unfurnished only makes sense if you're settling in for years, already own furniture worth shipping, or want full control over the interior.

Most villas advertised for long-term rental in Bali come furnished by default, because that's what the majority of tenants — remote workers, expat families on a few years' posting, seasonal residents — actually want. Unfurnished options exist but are a smaller share of the market and usually need to be specifically requested.

## The real cost comparison

Furnished rent typically runs somewhat higher than the equivalent unfurnished rate, since the owner has capital tied up in furniture and is factoring in wear. But furnishing a villa from scratch in Bali — beds, sofas, dining set, kitchen equipment, appliances, linens — is a real upfront cost that only pays off if you'll be there long enough to amortise it, and you still need to deal with it (sell, store or ship) when you eventually leave.

| Scenario | Furnished | Unfurnished |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Low (deposit only) | High (furniture purchase) |
| Monthly rent | Typically higher | Typically lower |
| Flexibility to move | High | Low, furniture is a sunk cost |
| Control over style/quality | Limited to what's provided | Full |
| Exit cost | None beyond normal deposit checks | Sell, store or ship furniture |

For a stay of a year or two, furnished nearly always wins on total cost and hassle. For a multi-year settled stay where you want a specific setup, unfurnished can work out better, but budget realistically for the furniture spend and the eventual exit.

## What furnished actually includes

As covered in our guide to what's included in a long-term villa rental, "furnished" is not standardised. At minimum expect beds, wardrobes, a sofa and dining table. A well-furnished villa also includes a complete kitchen (pots, pans, plates, cutlery), linens and towels, and basic appliances like a kettle, toaster or rice cooker. Always ask for a written inventory rather than assuming from the word "furnished" alone.

## When unfurnished makes sense

Unfurnished is worth considering if:

- You're committing to Bali for several years and want furniture that matches your taste, not the owner's
- You already have furniture in storage or are shipping belongings from elsewhere, see our guide on shipping belongings to Bali
- You've found a villa you love that isn't available furnished and the rent difference makes buying furniture worthwhile
- You plan to buy a villa eventually and want furniture you can move into your own property later

## The practical side of going unfurnished

If you go unfurnished, budget for furniture shopping (Bali has a large local furniture-making industry, so custom pieces are realistic on a normal timeline, though lead times can run several weeks), appliance purchases, and setup logistics. Factor in where you'll store or sell everything when you eventually move on, since shipping furniture out of Bali is rarely worth the cost for standard pieces.

## Mixing the two: partly furnished

Some owners offer a middle ground: core furniture included, kitchen equipment and soft furnishings left to the tenant, or vice versa. This is worth asking about directly if a villa you like is only listed one way, since owners are often more flexible than the listing suggests, especially on a longer lease where you're a more attractive tenant.

## What to check regardless of your choice

Whichever way you go, confirm in writing: what stays in the villa when you move in, what condition it's in (photograph everything at move-in), what happens to furniture you bring in if you rent unfurnished and later leave, and who's responsible for damage to owner-supplied furniture versus your own.

## The hidden cost of going unfurnished mid-lease

A scenario worth planning for, even if you start out intending to stay furnished, is what happens if your plans change and you end up staying much longer than expected. Some tenants who begin on a furnished lease find, two or three years in, that they'd now prefer their own furniture and a more personal setup, having originally expected a shorter stay. If this happens to you, it's entirely possible to gradually replace furnished items with your own over time, room by room, rather than treating furnished-versus-unfurnished as a single irreversible decision made on day one. Just clarify with the owner what happens to their furniture while you're doing this (usually stored, sometimes an agreed reduction in rent if you're effectively supplying your own furniture for part of the villa).

The reverse also happens: tenants who start unfurnished and later realise buying everything was more time-consuming and expensive than expected sometimes ask the owner about adding furnished elements back, which owners are often willing to discuss since it can mean less furniture for the tenant to eventually dispose of at the end of the lease, simplifying the exit for both sides.

## Comparing furnished quality across villas

Not all furnished villas are furnished to the same standard, and this is worth factoring into your rent comparison as much as the presence or absence of furniture itself. A villa furnished with basic, older pieces at a slightly lower rent may end up feeling like a worse deal than a villa furnished to a higher standard at a modestly higher rent, particularly if you're planning to spend a year or more actually living with the furniture day to day. When comparing furnished options, ask about the age and condition of key items, not just whether they exist, since "furnished" says nothing about quality on its own.

## What renters moving from abroad often get wrong

International tenants relocating to Bali sometimes carry assumptions from home markets that don't map cleanly onto how furnished and unfurnished rentals work locally. In many Western rental markets, "unfurnished" is the default and furnished commands a clear premium reserved mostly for short lets; in Bali's long-term villa market, furnished is the default and the more commonly available option across nearly all price points and areas, including Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan. Coming in expecting to negotiate hard for a furnished discount, as you might elsewhere, usually isn't the right frame; it's more productive to focus your negotiating energy on the completeness and quality of what's furnished, since that's where genuine variation exists.

## What owners think about when deciding to furnish

It helps to understand the decision from the owner's side too. An owner offering a villa furnished has effectively made an investment in furniture that they need to recoup through rent over time, and they're taking on the risk of wear and eventual replacement. An owner offering unfurnished is passing that investment and that risk to the tenant. Neither approach is inherently better for the owner; it usually reflects what kind of tenant they're trying to attract, longer-settled residents who'd rather furnish their own space, or shorter-to-medium-stay tenants who want to move in without any setup at all.

Knowing this helps explain why furnished villas dominate listings aimed at the international, shorter-to-medium-stay market that makes up most of Bali's long-term rental demand, while unfurnished options are more often found when dealing directly with an owner open to a longer, more settled arrangement.

## A note on quality, not just presence

Whichever way you go, the quality of what's provided matters as much as whether it's provided at all. A furnished villa with old, uncomfortable furniture and a poorly equipped kitchen isn't necessarily a better deal than an unfurnished villa where you can set up exactly what you want. When viewing a furnished villa, look past the presence of "included" items to their actual condition: does the mattress look recent, is the kitchen equipment functional and complete, are appliances in good working order. These details affect your day-to-day comfort far more than the simple furnished-or-not label.

## What to do next

## FAQ

### Is it cheaper to rent a furnished or unfurnished villa in Bali?
Unfurnished rent is typically lower per month, but once you add the cost of buying furniture, furnished usually works out cheaper overall for stays under about two years. Longer stays can tip the balance toward unfurnished.

### What does a furnished villa in Bali typically include?
At minimum, beds, wardrobes, a sofa and dining furniture. Better-furnished villas add a complete kitchen, linens, towels and small appliances. Always get a written inventory since the word "furnished" alone doesn't specify what's included.

### Can I negotiate a partly furnished villa in Bali?
Yes, some owners will adjust what's included if you ask, especially on a longer lease. It's worth requesting even if the listing only shows one option.

### What happens to my own furniture if I rent an unfurnished villa and then leave Bali?
You'd need to sell, store or ship it, and shipping standard furniture out of Bali is rarely cost-effective. Plan your exit before committing to buy furniture, particularly if your stay length is uncertain.

### Do furnished villas in Bali include kitchen equipment?
Often, but not always completely. Some furnished villas have a full set of cookware and appliances, others have only basics. Ask for specifics on kitchen equipment separately from general furniture before you sign.
