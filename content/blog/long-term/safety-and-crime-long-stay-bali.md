Is Bali safe to live in? For the vast majority of long-stay residents, yes, with the caveat that the real everyday risks are road traffic accidents, petty theft and various scams targeting tourists and newer arrivals, rather than violent crime, which remains relatively uncommon in the areas most long-stayers and villa renters live in. Like anywhere, safety is not uniform across the island and depends on the area, your habits and basic precautions.

We are not a security or law enforcement authority and this article reflects the practical patterns long-stay renters and our own team commonly encounter, not statistics or official crime data, which we have deliberately avoided quoting since we cannot verify current figures.

## The biggest actual risk: road traffic

Ask any long-stay expat in Bali what the real safety concern is, and traffic comes up before crime almost every time. Scooters are the dominant transport, roads can be narrow and poorly lit at night, and driving standards vary considerably. If you plan to ride a scooter, a proper helmet, an appropriate international or local licence, and caution around wet-season road conditions matter far more to your actual safety than most crime-related precautions.

Our guide to Bali traffic and commutes covers the practical side of getting around safely and efficiently in more detail, including how this varies by area.

## Petty theft and what actually gets targeted

Petty theft in Bali, where it happens, tends to target unattended valuables at beaches, unlocked scooters, and occasionally opportunistic bag-snatching in busier tourist areas. Villas with secure gates, lockable storage and attentive staff, which describes most properly managed long-stay accommodation, substantially reduce this risk compared with more open, less secured stays.

Basic precautions cover most of the actual risk: do not leave valuables visible in an unlocked scooter, use your villa's safe or lockable storage for passports and cash you are not carrying, and be more cautious with unattended belongings in the busiest tourist strips than in quieter residential long-stay areas.

## Scams: what to watch for

The more common safety-adjacent issue long-stay renters encounter is not crime in the traditional sense but scams: inflated pricing for tourists who do not know local rates, dishonest intermediaries in visa or property transactions, and occasionally deposit or rental scams targeting people searching for long-term accommodation online before they arrive.

| Risk type | How it typically shows up | Practical precaution |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic accidents | Scooter riding, wet roads, night driving | Proper licence, helmet, caution in rain |
| Petty theft | Unattended valuables, unlocked scooters | Secure storage, locked vehicles |
| Rental and deposit scams | Too-good-to-be-true listings found online, pressure to pay before viewing | Verify the manager, avoid paying large deposits sight unseen |
| Overpricing scams | Inflated prices for unfamiliar tourists | Ask locals or your villa host for typical rates |
| Informal visa "fixers" | Cash payment, no receipt, vague promises | Use licensed, checkable agents only |

Booking a long-term villa rental directly through a company with a real address and a track record, rather than sending a deposit to an unverified listing found through an informal channel, avoids the most common version of this risk. ## How area choice affects the picture

Safety experience varies somewhat by area, though not dramatically for anyone taking basic precautions. Sanur's quieter, more residential character and slower pace is often cited by long-stay families specifically as feeling calmer day to day, something covered in our guide to living in Sanur. Busier, more nightlife-oriented areas like parts of Canggu carry the ordinary safety considerations that come with any busier social scene, mainly around late-night road safety and the usual precautions in crowded venues.

None of this means one area is unsafe and another is safe; it means the specific texture of daily precautions shifts slightly depending on whether you are in a quiet residential lane or a busy beach-club strip.

## Natural and environmental considerations

Bali sits in a seismically active region, and being broadly aware of basic earthquake safety practices is sensible for any long-stay resident, the same way it would be for someone moving to any similar region. Villas and buildings in the areas we manage are standard modern construction, but knowing basic guidance, such as where to go during a tremor and keeping an emergency contact list accessible, is reasonable preparation rather than anxiety.

Rainy season brings its own practical considerations, from occasional localised flooding on low-lying roads to slicker driving conditions, which we cover in our separate guide to living in Bali during rainy season.

## Building sensible habits, not anxiety

The honest answer to is Bali safe to live in is that ordinary precautions, the same ones a sensible person would take in most mid-sized cities or resort regions globally, cover the great majority of the real risk. Traffic caution, basic property security, scepticism toward too-good-to-be-true deals, and awareness of your surroundings in busier tourist areas are the practical foundation, not elaborate security measures.

## What to do next

Bali is broadly safe to live in for long-stay residents who take ordinary precautions around traffic, property security and common scams, with violent crime being relatively uncommon in the areas most renters and expats live in. ## FAQ

### Is Bali safe to live in as a solo traveller or single expat?
Generally yes, with the same ordinary precautions that apply broadly: traffic caution, securing valuables, and scepticism toward deals that seem too good to be true. Many long-stay expats live solo in Bali without significant safety issues beyond these everyday considerations.

### What is the biggest safety risk for long-stay residents in Bali?
Road traffic, specifically scooter accidents, is the risk that comes up most often among long-stay expats, ahead of crime. Wearing a proper helmet, holding an appropriate licence and being cautious in wet or low-visibility conditions matters more to overall safety than most other precautions combined.

### Are there scams to watch out for when renting a long-stay villa in Bali?
Yes, particularly rental and deposit scams tied to unverified online listings and inflated pricing for unfamiliar arrivals. Book through a verifiable manager with a real address and track record, and be cautious of pressure to pay a large deposit before any proper verification or viewing.

### Is Bali safe for families living there long term?
Yes, and Sanur specifically is often chosen by long-stay families for its quieter, more residential character. The same general precautions apply, with families typically also considering proximity to schools and paediatric healthcare as part of the area decision.

### Do I need to worry about earthquakes or natural hazards living in Bali?
Bali sits in a seismically active region, so basic awareness of earthquake safety practices is sensible, similar to living in any comparable region. This is a reasonable, low-anxiety precaution rather than a significant deterrent to living there.
