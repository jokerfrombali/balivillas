Indonesia's retirement visa is a long-stay permit aimed at foreigners generally aged 55 and over who want to live in Bali without working locally, subject to meeting income or pension proof and other conditions set by immigration (as of 2026, verify exact figures with a licensed visa agent, since requirements are periodically adjusted). It sits inside the broader KITAS system of limited-stay permits, and once granted it lets you rent a villa on a monthly or yearly basis as a genuine resident rather than a repeat tourist.

This guide covers what the retirement visa actually is, how it interacts with renting long-term, and the practical steps to take before you sign a lease.

## What the retirement visa actually is

The retirement visa is one type of KITAS, Indonesia's limited-stay permit, distinct from the standard tourist visa or visa-on-arrival that most short-stay guests use. Where a tourist visa is designed for visits of weeks, the retirement visa is designed for a resident who intends to stay for an extended period and typically renews annually. It generally requires proof of a minimum monthly income or pension, health insurance, and either a local guarantor or a registered visa agent handling the sponsorship (as of 2026, verify current figures and process with a licensed agent, since these details change). Our broader guide to the [KITAS system in Bali](/blog/long-term/kitas-bali-guide/) explains how the different KITAS categories relate to each other if the retirement visa turns out not to be the right fit.

## Why this matters before you rent a villa

A landlord or villa manager renting monthly or yearly will usually ask what visa status you plan to hold for the stay, partly because it affects how long you can realistically commit to a lease and partly because Indonesian long-term rental contracts are typically tied to a resident's documented status rather than a rolling tourist stay. If your retirement visa application is still in progress when you want to move in, it is worth being upfront with the villa manager about the timeline; most flexible operators, us included, can work around a visa that is pending rather than fully issued, but a hard requirement to have papers finalised before signing varies by landlord.

## What to check before applying

- Confirm current age, income and health insurance requirements with a licensed visa agent, not a forum post
- Ask whether the visa allows single or multiple entries and what that means if you plan to leave Bali during your stay
- Check the exact renewal timeline so you are not caught mid-renewal without valid status
- Understand the local guarantor or agent role, since most applicants use a registered agent rather than applying solo

## FAQ

### What is the minimum age for Indonesia's retirement visa?
The retirement visa generally targets applicants aged 55 and over, though the exact threshold and any exceptions should be confirmed with a licensed visa agent, since immigration rules are reviewed periodically (as of 2026, verify).

### Can I work in Bali on a retirement visa?
No, the retirement visa does not permit local employment. It is intended for residents living on pension income, savings or income earned outside Indonesia, not for taking up local work.

### How is the retirement visa different from a KITAS in general?
The retirement visa is one specific category within the broader KITAS system of limited-stay permits; other KITAS categories cover investors, spouses of Indonesian citizens, and other circumstances. Our [KITAS guide](/blog/long-term/kitas-bali-guide/) explains how the categories differ.

### Do I need a visa agent to apply for the retirement visa?
Most applicants use a registered visa agent or local guarantor to handle the application and ongoing renewals, since the paperwork and requirements are detailed and change periodically. We can put you in touch with agents we have worked with if you ask.

### Can I rent a villa in Bali while my retirement visa is still being processed?
Often yes, though it depends on the individual landlord's requirements. Some, including us, can work with a pending application if you can show it is genuinely in progress; others prefer the visa finalised before signing a yearly contract. Ask us directly about your specific timeline.
