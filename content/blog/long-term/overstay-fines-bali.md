If you overstay your visa in Bali by a short margin, typically a matter of days, the usual outcome is a per-day administrative fine paid at the airport when you leave, with no further consequence beyond the payment itself (as of 2026, verify the current fine amount, since it changes and is set in rupiah). Beyond a certain number of days, overstay stops being a simple fine-on-exit matter and becomes a more serious issue involving immigration detention, deportation, and potential re-entry bans.

The exact day threshold where this shift happens, and the current fine rate, are the kind of specific figures that change and should be confirmed directly with a licensed agent or imigrasi.go.id rather than assumed from older information you may have seen online.

## The short-overstay scenario: a fine and nothing more

For a brief overstay, commonly a handful of days past your visa's expiry, the standard process is paying a fixed per-day fine at the immigration counter when you depart Indonesia (as of 2026, verify the current daily rate with imigrasi.go.id, since it is periodically revised). This is generally treated as an administrative matter, handled at the point of departure, and does not typically prevent you leaving or involve a separate court process at this scale.

This does not mean a short overstay should be treated casually. The fine is real money, the process can add friction and stress to what should be a routine departure, and repeated short overstays on subsequent visits can affect how immigration treats future applications from you.

## When overstay becomes serious

Beyond the short-overstay threshold, the consequences escalate meaningfully. Longer overstays can result in detention, a formal deportation process, and being placed on a list that prevents re-entry to Indonesia for a period, or in more serious cases, indefinitely (as of 2026, verify the current day threshold and consequence tiers with a licensed immigration agent, since this is an area subject to change and enforcement discretion). This is a genuinely serious outcome, not a bureaucratic inconvenience, and should be treated accordingly.

## What to do the moment you realise you have overstayed

If you notice your visa has already expired, the single most useful thing to do is act immediately rather than wait, hope it resolves itself, or delay because you are unsure of the process. Contact a licensed visa agent or the immigration office directly and explain the situation honestly; how you handle it from the point of discovery matters for the outcome.

- Do not wait for a scheduled flight to deal with it; contact an agent or immigration as soon as you realise
- Gather your passport, visa documentation and any evidence of why the extension was missed
- Ask directly what the current fine or process is for your specific number of overstay days, since this depends on the exact day count and current rules

## Why overstays happen even to careful people

Overstay is often less about carelessness and more about the extension process itself running later than expected, a flight change disrupting plans, or genuine confusion about which visa category was held and when it actually expired. ## Prevention is simpler than the cure

The practical prevention steps are not complicated: know your exact visa expiry date, start any extension with real margin before that date, and if your travel plans or extension timeline shift, check in with your visa agent rather than assuming everything is still on track. Most overstay situations we hear about from long-stay renters trace back to one of these steps being skipped under time pressure, not to a deliberate decision to ignore visa rules.
