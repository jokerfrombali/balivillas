Villa or apartment for a month in Bali comes down to what you're prioritising: space, privacy and a private pool point toward a villa, while lower cost, building security and shared amenities like a gym point toward an apartment. Bali's apartment market is smaller and more concentrated than in cities with large residential towers, so villas remain the more common choice for a monthly stay, but serviced apartments do exist, particularly in and around Seminyak and parts of Denpasar.

## The practical differences

| | Apartment | Villa |
|---|---|---|
| Typical cost | Lower | Higher |
| Space | Limited, unit-sized | Full house, often with garden |
| Pool | Sometimes a shared building pool | Usually private |
| Privacy | Neighbours close by | Fully self-contained |
| Building amenities | Gym, security, lift, sometimes included | None, but you get the whole property |
| Availability in Bali | Limited, concentrated in a few areas | Widely available across most areas |

## Why villas dominate the monthly market in Bali

Bali's residential development has historically favoured standalone villas and townhouses over apartment towers, particularly in Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan, the areas where most long-term rental demand sits. This means if you want a monthly stay outside a handful of specific apartment developments, a villa or villa-style townhouse is often simply the more available option, not just the preferred one.

## When an apartment makes sense anyway

If your priority is minimising cost and you don't need much space, or you specifically want building security and amenities like a shared gym or 24-hour reception, a serviced apartment can be a reasonable choice where available. This is more relevant if you're based in a more urban part of South Bali rather than the quieter villa-dominated areas.

## When a villa makes more sense

If you're travelling with family or a group and want to split the cost across several people, if a private pool and outdoor space matter to your month, or if you want a proper kitchen and workspace without sharing walls with neighbours, a villa is the stronger option. Per person, a shared villa often compares favourably to individual apartment units once you factor in the extra space and amenities.

## What a villa gives you that most apartments don't

A private pool is the standout difference: even a modest 1–2 bedroom villa in Bali typically includes its own pool, something rare in apartment living anywhere. Garden or outdoor space, a full kitchen rather than a kitchenette, and genuine quiet without shared walls are the other consistent advantages villas have over apartments for a monthly stay.

## What an apartment gives you that a villa doesn't

Building security, sometimes a front desk or concierge, shared gym facilities, and typically a lower price point are the practical advantages of apartment living where it's available. If those matter more to you than space and privacy, and you can find a suitable apartment in your target area, it's a reasonable option.

## Questions to ask before booking either

- What's the actual monthly all-in cost, including utilities, not just the headline rent?
- For an apartment: what building amenities are genuinely included versus available at extra cost?
- For a villa: is the pool private or shared with neighbouring villas?
- How far is the location from where you'll actually spend your time?

## What tends to surprise first-time comparers

People weighing a villa against an apartment for the first time are often surprised by two things once they start actually comparing options in Bali. First, how much genuine choice there is on the villa side, ranging from a compact 1-bedroom townhouse to large multi-bedroom properties, compared to the relatively narrow band of apartment options available. Second, how close the per-person cost of a shared villa can land to an individual apartment unit once you're travelling with even one other person, since the villa's fixed costs (rent, utilities base) spread across more people while an apartment's don't.

It's also worth checking, if you do find apartment options in your target area, what condition and age the building is, since apartment stock in Bali varies more in build quality and upkeep than the more consistently maintained villa market, where properties are more often professionally managed for exactly this kind of stay.

## What to check specifically if you do find an apartment option

If you do locate an apartment for your target area and dates, verify a few things a villa comparison wouldn't require: whether the building has 24-hour water and power reliability, what the actual monthly service or maintenance charge is beyond the headline rent, and whether the unit's listed amenities (pool, gym) are currently operational, since older buildings sometimes have facilities that are technically present but not well maintained. These checks matter less for villas, where you're typically dealing with a single owner managing a single property rather than a building's shared infrastructure.

## Thinking beyond the first month

If there's a reasonable chance you'll extend beyond your initial month, factor that into your choice now rather than treating it purely as a one-month decision. A villa lease is generally easier to extend into a longer-term rental with better rates the further out you commit, since owners value a longer, more predictable tenancy; an apartment, particularly a shorter-term serviced one, may not offer the same kind of long-term rate improvement, or may have limited availability beyond your initial booking window. If you suspect a month might turn into several, ask about longer-term rates for both options before deciding.

## A note on noise and privacy specifically

Apartment living, even in a well-built development, typically means shared walls, floors and ceilings with neighbours, which some people adjust to easily and others find genuinely disruptive over a month-long stay, particularly if you're used to detached housing at home or need quiet for concentrated work. A villa's standalone structure removes this variable entirely. If noise sensitivity or the need for uninterrupted quiet is a real factor for you, weight it explicitly in your decision rather than assuming it won't matter until you're actually living with it.

## The short version, restated

If you value space, privacy and a private pool, and don't mind Bali's villa-dominated residential landscape, a villa is very likely the more available and more satisfying choice for a monthly stay. If minimising cost and having building amenities matters more, check what genuinely exists in your target area before assuming an apartment is an easy fallback option.

## FAQ

### Is it cheaper to rent an apartment than a villa for a month in Bali?
Generally yes on a per-unit basis, though villas can compare favourably per person if you're splitting the cost among a family or group. Apartments also come with a smaller, more limited market in Bali than villas.

### Are there many apartments available for monthly rent in Bali?
Fewer than villas; Bali's residential market leans heavily toward standalone villas and townhouses, particularly in the main expat areas. Apartment options exist but are more concentrated in specific developments, mainly in more urban parts of South Bali.

### Do villas in Bali usually have private pools even for a monthly stay?
Yes, most villas offered for monthly rental in Bali include a private or semi-private pool as standard, which is one of the main reasons people choose a villa over an apartment for their stay.

### What building amenities do serviced apartments in Bali typically offer?
Where available, security, sometimes a front desk, and shared facilities like a gym or communal pool are common. These vary by specific building, so confirm what's actually included rather than assuming a standard package.

### Which is better for a family staying a month in Bali, a villa or an apartment?
A villa is usually the better fit for a family, offering more space, a private pool and a full kitchen, often at a comparable or lower per-person cost than booking multiple apartment units.
