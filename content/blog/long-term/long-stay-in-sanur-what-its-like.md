Living in Sanur suits people who want Bali's practicalities, decent infrastructure, a walkable beach path, reasonable access to shops and healthcare, without the busier nightlife and traffic density of areas like Canggu or Seminyak. It is consistently the area long-stay families gravitate toward among the four we manage villas in, largely because of its calm, flat, beachside character and slower daily pace.

We manage 11 villas in Sanur, more than in any of our other three areas, which reflects the area's popularity for exactly this kind of longer, calmer stay.

## What daily life in Sanur actually looks like

Sanur's defining feature for a long-stay resident is its long, flat beachfront promenade, used daily by locals and long-stayers alike for walking, running and cycling, calm swimming (the reef offshore keeps the water notably calmer than Bali's surf beaches), and a scattering of cafés and small restaurants along the path. The town itself has a settled, slightly old-fashioned character compared with the newer, faster-growing tourist strips elsewhere on the island.

Daily errands, groceries, pharmacies, clinics, are generally straightforward within the area without needing to travel far, which matters more over a long stay than a short one. Our guide to Bali traffic and commutes notes Sanur as one of the comparatively lighter-traffic areas, which adds up over months of daily errands.

## Sanur bali cost of living: what drives it

We are not going to quote specific cost-of-living figures, since these shift and vary by lifestyle and household size, but the general drivers in Sanur are similar to elsewhere in Bali: accommodation is usually the largest single cost, followed by transport, food and utilities, with the exact mix depending heavily on whether you eat out frequently or cook, and whether you run air conditioning extensively. Sanur's dining scene skews toward a mix of local warungs and a reasonable range of international options, generally with somewhat less premium pricing than the most tourist-dense parts of Canggu or Seminyak.

## What Sanur is less well suited for

If your priority is being within easy walking distance of a dense coworking and café scene, or you specifically want beach-club nightlife and a younger, faster-paced social scene, Sanur will likely feel quieter than you want, and Canggu or parts of Umalas would suit better. Surfers specifically will find Sanur's calm reef-protected water unsuitable for the surf conditions found on Bali's west and south coasts.
