Bringing a dog to Bali is possible and plenty of long-stayers do it, but it is not a same-week decision. You need an import permit from the Indonesian quarantine authority, up-to-date vaccination and health documents from an accredited vet, and in most cases a period of quarantine once the animal lands. Start the paperwork at least two to three months before your planned travel date.

The other half of the problem is not the government, it is the villa. Many rental properties in Bali do not allow pets at all, and of those that do, fewer still are genuinely set up for a dog — a garden with secure fencing, no koi ponds a puppy can fall into, and neighbours who will not mind barking. Sort the villa question early, because it constrains your travel dates as much as the permit process does.

## The import process, step by step

Indonesia treats live animal imports as a quarantine matter, not just a customs one, and the rules are specific about documentation, timing and the port of entry. Because requirements shift and vary by country of origin, we are not going to state exact form numbers or fees here — confirm the current process directly with Indonesia's quarantine agency (Badan Karantina) and, ideally, a pet relocation agent who does this for a living (as of 2026, verify).

What stays fairly constant across most import regimes:

- A rabies titer test done a set number of months before travel, not just a vaccination record
- A health certificate issued close to the travel date by an accredited vet in the country of departure
- An import permit applied for in advance, often tied to a specific arrival airport
- A quarantine period on arrival, the length of which depends on documentation and the animal's origin

Missing any one of these can mean your dog is held at the border or sent back, so most people who have done this once say the relocation agent's fee was worth avoiding that risk alone.

A specialist pet relocation agent handles the vet paperwork chain, books the cargo or excess-baggage slot with the airline, and knows the current quarantine requirements at Ngurah Rai airport. Doing it yourself is possible if you have time and patience for Indonesian bureaucracy in a language you may not read, but the margin for a paperwork error that costs you weeks is real.

## Finding a villa that actually allows dogs

Long-stay listings often say "pet friendly" loosely, meaning a small cat is tolerated but a Labrador is a different conversation. Before you commit to travel dates, ask specifically: is the garden fully fenced, is there a pond or unfenced pool edge, are there other pets or livestock nearby that could be a problem, and what happens to a damage deposit if a dog scratches a door.

## Vet care and ongoing costs once you are settled

Bali has vet clinics used to expat pet owners, particularly around Denpasar, Sanur, Canggu and Ubud, and most can handle routine care, vaccinations and minor emergencies. Costs are typically lower than in the UK, US or Australia for routine visits, though imported medication and specialist procedures can cost more because of shipping and availability. Ask other long-term pet owners in local expat groups which clinic they use for anything beyond routine care.

Rabies remains present in parts of Bali, so keeping vaccinations current and avoiding contact with stray dogs is a genuine safety matter, not just a bureaucratic box to tick.

## What changes if you are only staying a few months

If your stay is short — a few months rather than years — think hard about whether bringing a pet makes sense at all. The import process, quarantine, and eventual return export process (which has its own paperwork) can add weeks of logistics and real cost on both ends of a stay that may not be long enough to justify it. Some short-stay visitors instead choose to leave pets at home with family or a sitter and revisit the decision once they know their Bali stay will be a year or more.

## Settling a dog into a new villa and neighbourhood

The first weeks matter for a dog that has just been through a long flight and quarantine. Keep the villa's outdoor space secure while the animal adjusts, introduce it to the household staff who may be on site (many of our long-stay villas include gardening or housekeeping visits, so tell us in advance if a dog will be present), and be mindful of Bali's heat and mosquito-borne illnesses that affect animals as well as people.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to bring a dog to Bali?
Plan for two to three months minimum from starting the paperwork to your dog clearing quarantine in Bali, mostly driven by the lead time on a rabies titer test and permit approval (as of 2026, verify current timelines with a relocation agent, since requirements can change).

### Do all villas in Bali allow dogs?
No. Many long-stay listings do not allow pets, or only tolerate small, quiet animals. Ask specifically about fencing, other animals on site, and the deposit policy for pet damage before booking, rather than assuming a "pet friendly" label covers a dog your size.

### Is quarantine required for dogs entering Bali?
In most cases yes, though the exact length depends on your documentation and country of origin (as of 2026, verify current requirements with Indonesia's quarantine authority). Complete paperwork generally shortens the quarantine period compared with incomplete documentation.

### Can I use a regular vet or do I need a specialist for the import paperwork?
Most countries require an accredited or government-approved vet to issue the export health certificate and titer test results, not just any local vet. Check with the relocation agent or Indonesian quarantine agency which accreditations they will accept before booking an appointment.

### Is bringing a dog to Bali worth it for a short stay?
For stays under roughly six months, weigh the import cost, quarantine time and eventual export process against how much of the stay your dog would actually spend settled in. Many people bringing pets for shorter periods choose to wait until they have a confirmed year-long stay instead.
