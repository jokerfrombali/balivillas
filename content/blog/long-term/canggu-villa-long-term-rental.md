Canggu is Bali's default base for long-stay remote workers and surfers, and it earns that reputation honestly — surf breaks, a dense café and coworking scene, and beach clubs at sunset, all within a compact area. The trade-off for a long-term tenant is traffic that's grown alongside the area's popularity, and a busier, more built-up feel than Sanur or Umalas.

We manage two villas in Canggu, both larger properties suited to groups or families wanting space and a beachside setting rather than the smaller townhouse-style units more common in Umalas.

## What villa styles are available in Canggu

Our Canggu villas run larger than our typical Sanur or Umalas stock: Villa The Wave is a 6-bedroom beachside villa a short walk from Berawa Beach, and Leonardo is a 9-bedroom resort-style property in Pererenan with a home cinema, piano and garden on a mountain river. Both suit groups, multi-family stays or anyone wanting genuine space rather than a compact pool villa — Canggu's long-term rental market at this end tends toward bigger properties more than Umalas or Ungasan's smaller teak or townhouse villas.

## Daily life in Canggu for a long-stay tenant

Long-term life in Canggu revolves around its café and coworking culture — reliable Wi-Fi, a dense choice of workspaces, and a social scene built around fellow long-stayers doing the same thing. Surf access is a genuine daily-life factor here, not just a holiday activity, with several breaks within a short ride. The trade-off is traffic on the main roads (Canggu's beach road especially) at most times of day, and an area that's grown fast enough that quiet corners are harder to find than they were a few years ago.

## What a typical week looks like for a long-stay tenant

Long-term life in Canggu tends to revolve around a mix of work and surf — an early session in the water before the wind picks up, a working day from a café or coworking space, and evenings split between beach clubs and quieter dinners depending on the week. It's a more socially active daily rhythm than Sanur or Ungasan, with a constant flow of other long-stayers doing the same thing, which suits people who want that community energy as part of their stay rather than a purely quiet retreat.
