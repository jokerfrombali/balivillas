Living in Bali in rainy season means intermittent heavy downpours, often in the afternoon or evening, rather than continuous rain all day, interspersed with periods of sun and humidity. The general pattern in Bali, as in much of tropical Indonesia, runs wetter through the northern hemisphere winter months and drier through the middle of the year, though exact timing and intensity shift year to year, so treat any specific month-by-month claim as a general tendency rather than a guarantee (we have avoided quoting a specific graph or exact percentages here since current, verified figures should come from a meteorological source rather than general knowledge).

For a long-stay renter, the practical question is less "how much does it rain" and more "what changes about daily life," which is the focus of this guide.

## What actually changes day to day

The most noticeable daily impact is on outdoor plans and driving. Afternoon downpours can be intense and arrive with relatively short notice, so scooter riders in particular need to build in flexibility rather than assuming clear roads throughout a planned trip. Roads can develop localised standing water on low-lying stretches, and visibility drops sharply during the heaviest bursts.

Humidity tends to feel higher during wetter months, laundry takes longer to dry (a genuine practical consideration if your villa relies on line-drying rather than a dryer), and outdoor furniture, pool areas and gardens need slightly more maintenance attention, which a well-managed villa handles as part of routine upkeep rather than something a tenant needs to worry about directly.

## Temperature versus rainfall: two different things

A common misconception is that rainy season in Bali means noticeably cooler weather; in practice, temperatures stay warm and tropical year-round, with humidity often being the more noticeable shift than temperature itself. Do not pack for cooler weather expecting rainy season to bring a significant temperature drop; a light rain layer over your normal tropical wardrobe is more useful than warmer clothing.

## Living in Canggu specifically during rainy season

Canggu's beach-facing, lower-lying areas can be more prone to localised flooding on certain roads during the heaviest rain events compared with some other parts of the island, something worth factoring into daily commute planning if you are staying there during wetter months. Our guide to living in Canggu covers the area's character more broadly, and our guide to Bali traffic and commutes covers how weather affects getting around specifically.

This is not a reason to avoid Canggu during rainy season, simply a practical planning point: build a little more flexibility into travel timing around expected downpour periods, typically later in the day, rather than assuming a fixed commute time year-round.

## Practical adjustments worth making

- Keep a light, packable rain layer accessible daily rather than relying on always checking forecasts in advance
- Choose quick-dry fabrics and expect laundry to take longer to air-dry during wetter stretches
- Build buffer time into travel plans, particularly for early evening trips when downpours are more common
- Check your villa has adequate drainage and covered areas if you plan to spend significant time outdoors
- Keep an umbrella and appropriate footwear accessible rather than packed away

## FAQ

### When exactly is rainy season in Bali?
The general tendency runs wetter through the northern hemisphere winter months and drier around the middle of the year, but exact timing shifts year to year, so check a current meteorological source rather than relying on a fixed calendar. We have deliberately avoided quoting specific months here since accuracy depends on the current year's pattern.

### Does it rain all day in Bali during rainy season?
No, typically not. Rainy season in Bali usually brings intermittent, sometimes intense downpours, often concentrated in the afternoon or evening, interspersed with dry, sunny periods rather than continuous rain throughout the day.

### Is Bali much cooler during rainy season?
Not significantly. Temperatures stay warm and tropical year-round; the more noticeable shift during rainy season is higher humidity rather than a meaningful temperature drop, so pack for humidity and rain rather than cold.

### Is Canggu more affected by rainy season than other areas like Sanur?
Canggu's lower-lying, beach-facing areas can see more localised flooding on certain roads during the heaviest downpours compared with some other parts of the island. ### What should I do differently in a Bali villa during rainy season?
Keep a rain layer accessible daily, expect laundry to take longer to dry, build travel buffers around likely downpour periods, and lean into indoor and covered outdoor activities during the wettest stretches. A well maintained villa with good drainage and covered living space handles most of the rest.
