Yearly villa rental prices in Bali vary by area for reasons that have little to do with tourist reputation and a lot to do with land cost, plot size norms and how much local competition exists for a given bedroom count. Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan each sit at different points on that scale, and none of them is uniformly the "cheap" or "expensive" option once you compare like-for-like villas.

We manage villas across all four areas, so rather than quote a single figure, this guide explains what tends to differ between them and points you to a direct quote for the specific area and size you need.

## Why area affects the price at all

Land in Bali isn't priced uniformly — beachfront and near-beachfront plots in established tourist areas command a premium over inland lanes, and each of the four areas we manage villas in has a different mix of plot sizes, garden space and proximity to the coast. Sanur's larger, more established residential plots differ structurally from Umalas's denser townhouse-style developments, which changes what a yearly lease actually buys in each place.

## How the four areas compare

| Area | Typical villa style | What tends to affect price most |
|---|---|---|
| Sanur | Standalone villas, larger gardens, family-sized | Distance from the beach path, bedroom count |
| Canggu | Larger beachside houses, higher-spec finishes | Proximity to the surf breaks and beach clubs |
| Umalas | Townhouse-style pool villas, denser layout | Distance from the café cluster, workspace quality |
| Ungasan | Teak-built villas, clifftop plots | View, proximity to Melasti/Green Bowl beaches |

This is a structural comparison, not a price list — get a live quote for the specific bedroom count and villa style you want in each area, since the spread within an area (a compact 1-bedroom versus a 4-bedroom family villa) is often larger than the spread between areas.

## Sanur: family-sized villas, calmer demand curve

Sanur's yearly rental market leans toward larger, standalone villas with real garden space, reflecting the area's family and long-stay-oriented reputation. Demand is steadier than in Canggu, which can mean slightly more room to negotiate a yearly rate, particularly outside peak season.

## Canggu: premium for beach and surf proximity

Canggu commands a premium the closer a villa sits to the surf breaks and the main café strip, and that premium has grown as the area has become the default base for Bali's remote-work crowd. A villa a short walk from Batu Bolong or Berawa will typically cost more than a similar one a 10-minute scooter ride inland.

## Umalas: mid-range, workspace-driven demand

Umalas sits between Canggu's premium and calmer areas on price, and demand here is increasingly shaped by remote workers wanting a quiet lane close to Canggu's cafés without Canggu's traffic. Workspace quality (a proper desk, reliable Wi-Fi) noticeably affects what tenants are willing to pay for a yearly lease here.

## Ungasan: view and beach access set the range

Ungasan's clifftop villas price largely on view and proximity to specific beach access points like Melasti and Green Bowl. Two villas with the same bedroom count can differ meaningfully in yearly rate depending on whether one has an ocean view and the other doesn't.

## How seasonality interacts with area pricing

Area pricing patterns hold reasonably steady year-round for yearly leases, since a twelve-month commitment averages out across high and low season anyway. Where seasonality matters more is in the timing of when you sign — an owner negotiating a yearly lease during a quieter booking month may have more flexibility than one fielding strong short-stay demand for the same villa. This applies across all four areas, though it's most noticeable in Canggu, where short-stay demand fluctuates the most between peak and quiet periods.

## A worked comparison: matching bedroom count before area

To see why matching bedroom count matters more than area, it helps to walk through an example. Imagine you're offered a yearly quote for a 2-bedroom Umalas townhouse and a 2-bedroom Sanur villa. On paper, that looks like a clean area-to-area comparison. But if the Sanur villa has a larger garden, a private pool and sits a few streets back from the beach path, while the Umalas townhouse has a smaller footprint with a shared pool, the gap in price between them may have almost nothing to do with "Sanur versus Umalas" as areas, and almost everything to do with plot size, pool type and finish level.

Now compare a 4-bedroom family villa in Sanur against a 1-bedroom teak villa in Ungasan. Even if the Ungasan villa has the better view and a premium clifftop plot, the sheer difference in space and bedroom count in Sanur's favour will usually dominate the price gap. Neither comparison tells you which area is "cheaper Bali" in general, both only tell you about those two specific villas.

The practical takeaway: before requesting quotes, write down the bedroom count, pool type (private or shared) and rough finish level you want, then ask for quotes matching those three criteria across the areas you're considering. That's the only way an area-to-area comparison means anything.

## What goes wrong when guests compare prices across areas

The most common mistake is treating a single quote from each area as representative of that area's whole market, when in fact the spread within an area, between a basic townhouse and a high-spec family villa, is often larger than the spread between areas for a similar villa. One quote from Canggu and one from Sanur tells you about two specific villas, not about Canggu versus Sanur generally.

A second mistake is assuming a headline "average" price for an area exists and is meaningful. Given how much plot size, view, pool type and finish vary within Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan individually, an averaged figure smooths over exactly the details that determine what you'll actually pay for a specific villa.

A third is not accounting for how a villa's position within an area changes the number as much as the area label itself. A villa a short walk from Canggu's main strip and one a 15-minute ride inland can differ meaningfully even though both are "in Canggu"; the same applies to distance from Sanur's beach path or Umalas's café cluster.

## FAQ

### Which area in Bali has the cheapest yearly villa rentals?
There's no consistent answer — it depends on the specific villa's size, style and proximity to the coast within each area. Sanur's larger family villas and Ungasan's compact teak villas sit at different price points that don't map neatly onto "cheap area versus expensive area."

### Is Canggu more expensive than Sanur for a yearly villa rental?
Villas closest to Canggu's beach and surf breaks tend to command a premium, but a comparable Sanur family villa away from the beachfront can cost similarly. Comparing matched bedroom counts and styles gives a fairer picture than comparing areas in the abstract.

### Does Umalas cost more or less than Canggu for long-term rental?
Umalas generally sits a notch below Canggu's premium beachside pricing, reflecting its position slightly inland, while still offering easy access to Canggu's café and coworking scene.

### What affects yearly villa prices in Ungasan specifically?
Ocean view and proximity to specific beach access points like Melasti or Green Bowl are the biggest factors in Ungasan, more so than in the flatter areas, because the clifftop terrain creates real variation in outlook between otherwise similar villas.

### Should I compare prices between areas before choosing?
Only once you've matched bedroom count and villa style — otherwise you're comparing unlike properties. Decide your priorities (family space, surf access, workspace, views) first, then request quotes.
