An Indonesian driving licence for foreigners is usually obtained one of two ways: converting an existing valid licence from your home country into a local SIM (Surat Izin Mengemudi), or, in some cases, using an International Driving Permit alongside your home licence for a limited period. Which route applies depends on your nationality, your residency status (tourist visa versus KITAS), and the specific vehicle category you want to drive, and the exact rules are administered by local traffic police and can change, so confirm current requirements directly with the SATPAS office handling licensing in your area before assuming either route applies to you (as of 2026, verify).

Riding or driving in Indonesia without a valid licence for the vehicle category you are using carries real legal risk if you are stopped or, more seriously, if you are involved in an accident, since it can affect both liability and insurance cover. This is not a step worth skipping even for a short stay if you plan to ride regularly.

## Conversion versus starting from scratch

For residents holding a KITAS, converting an existing home country licence is generally the more common route, and typically requires your original licence, translation where required, your residency documentation, and a medical check, though the specific document list varies (as of 2026, verify with your local SATPAS office). Starting from scratch with no existing licence generally means sitting both a written and practical test, similar to what an Indonesian citizen would do, which is a longer and more involved process.

| Situation | Typical route | What it generally needs |
|---|---|---|
| Valid home licence, hold a KITAS | Licence conversion | Original licence, translation if required, residency docs, medical check |
| Valid home licence, short-stay visa only | International Driving Permit, where accepted | IDP alongside your home licence, subject to current rules |
| No existing licence | Full local test | Written and practical test, similar process to a first-time local applicant |

## International Driving Permits: what to check

An International Driving Permit (IDP) can, in some circumstances, be used alongside a valid home licence for driving in Indonesia, but acceptance and duration limits vary and are worth confirming before you rely on one, particularly since IDP rules and enforcement can differ from what is stated in general international guides (as of 2026, verify current acceptance with local authorities). Get your IDP issued in your home country before travelling, since it generally cannot be obtained once you are already in Indonesia.

## What the process typically involves

- Gathering your original home licence and any required translation or notarisation
- Providing residency documentation (typically KITAS) if converting rather than testing from scratch
- A basic medical or eyesight check at the licensing office
- Photos and standard application paperwork
- For a full test route: written test and practical riding or driving test

Using a local agent or fixer to help navigate the process is common practice and can save real time, particularly with language and paperwork, though confirm any agent is reputable and that the licence issued is genuinely valid rather than an informal document that will not hold up if checked.

## Motorbike versus car licence categories

Indonesia licenses motorbikes and cars under different categories, and having one does not automatically cover the other. If you plan to ride a scooter, confirm you are pursuing the correct category (commonly referred to informally by engine size bands) rather than assuming a car licence conversion covers a motorbike, or vice versa.

## What happens if you are stopped without a valid licence

Being stopped without a valid Indonesian licence or acceptable IDP can result in a fine and, in more serious cases involving an accident, can complicate insurance claims and liability. This is a genuine practical reason to sort licensing early rather than treating it as optional admin, particularly if you are also buying a scooter, which our companion guide to buying a scooter in Bali covers in more detail regarding registration and insurance.

## Timing: when to start the process

Start the conversion or testing process as soon as your residency status (typically KITAS) is confirmed, since it can take time to schedule appointments and gather documentation, and riding without a valid licence in the interim is a risk not worth carrying for convenience.

## FAQ

### Can I drive in Bali on my home country's licence alone?
Generally not for an extended period without either an International Driving Permit or a converted Indonesian licence, and the exact rules depend on your visa status and nationality. Confirm current requirements before relying on your home licence alone for anything beyond a short visit.

### How do I convert my home driving licence to an Indonesian SIM?
Typically by providing your original licence, a translation if required, residency documentation such as a KITAS, and passing a basic medical check, though exact requirements vary and change, so confirm with your local SATPAS office (as of 2026, verify).

### Do I need a separate licence for a scooter versus a car in Indonesia?
Yes, motorbikes and cars are generally licensed under different categories, so confirm you are applying for the correct category for the vehicle you actually plan to drive rather than assuming one licence covers both.

### Is an International Driving Permit accepted in Bali?
It can be accepted in some circumstances alongside a valid home licence, but acceptance and duration limits vary and are worth confirming with local authorities before relying on one (as of 2026, verify current rules, since IDP acceptance can differ from general international guides).

### What happens if I am caught driving without a valid licence in Bali?
It can result in a fine, and in the event of an accident it can seriously complicate insurance claims and liability. Sorting a valid licence before you start driving regularly is a practical safeguard, not just a bureaucratic formality.
