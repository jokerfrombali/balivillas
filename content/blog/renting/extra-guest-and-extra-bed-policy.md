Villas set a base occupancy — typically two guests per bedroom — and most charge an extra fee per guest above that, whether it's a friend joining for a few nights, a baby who needs a cot, or a group that's slightly larger than the bedroom count technically allows for. The fee and the threshold vary by property, so it's worth confirming the exact numbers before your group's headcount is locked in, not after arrival.

## How base occupancy typically works

A 2-bedroom villa with a base occupancy of four guests (two per room) doesn't automatically mean it can't host five or six — many villas can accommodate extra guests on a sofa bed, rollaway or with an extra mattress, but usually for an additional per-night or per-stay charge. Ask specifically what the base occupancy is for the villa you're considering, and what the maximum guest count is with extras factored in.

## What typically triggers an extra charge

| Situation | Common approach |
|---|---|
| One extra adult above base occupancy | Extra-guest fee, per night or per stay |
| Infant or young child needing a cot | Sometimes free, sometimes a small fee — ask directly |
| Extra bed/mattress required | May carry a setup fee separate from the guest fee |
| Guest attending but not staying overnight (e.g. a day visitor) | Policy varies — confirm if this applies to your situation |

None of these are standardised across Bali villas generally, which is exactly why it's worth asking rather than assuming a policy from one villa applies to another.

## Cots and infants

Travelling with a baby often raises a different question from an extra adult guest — many villas will provide a cot for infants without counting them toward the guest total at all, but this isn't universal. Confirm specifically whether a cot is available, whether there's a charge, and whether the villa's pool and layout are otherwise suitable for a baby (fencing, shallow areas, stairs) while you're asking.

## Why the base occupancy number matters for your search

If you're searching by bedroom count alone, it's easy to undercount what a villa can actually hold once extra-guest options are factored in. A [3-bedroom villa](/blog/renting/three-bedroom-villa-bali/) with a base occupancy of six might comfortably extend to seven or eight with an extra bed arrangement — useful if your group is just over the "clean" bedroom-to-guest ratio and doesn't want to book a larger, pricier villa for one or two extra people.

## Questions to ask before finalising your booking

- What is the base occupancy for this specific villa?
- What is the maximum guest count including any extra-bed arrangements?
- What is the per-guest, per-night extra charge above base occupancy?
- Is there a separate one-off fee for setting up an extra bed or cot?
- Does the extra-guest charge apply to infants, or only from a certain age?

## Avoiding a surprise on arrival

The most common source of friction here isn't the fee itself, it's finding out about it on check-in day rather than when booking. Confirm your exact group size — including anyone joining partway through the stay — when you first enquire, and get the extra-guest terms in writing as part of your booking confirmation.

## Splitting a larger group across bedroom types

When a group's headcount doesn't divide neatly into a villa's bedroom mix, it's often worth comparing two routes side by side: paying extra-guest fees on a smaller villa versus stepping up to a villa with one more bedroom. A 3-bedroom villa with an extra-guest arrangement for two additional people can sometimes cost close to what a 4-bedroom villa charges outright, in which case the larger villa may be the better value once you factor in the extra space, privacy and an additional bathroom, rather than a shared extra-guest setup on a sofa bed. This is worth working through with actual numbers for your dates and group size rather than assuming either route is automatically cheaper — the answer depends on the specific villa, the season and how many extra guests are involved.

## FAQ

### How many guests can stay in a villa before extra charges apply?

It depends on the villa's base occupancy, typically calculated as two guests per bedroom. Guests beyond that number usually incur a per-night extra-guest fee, which varies by property.

### Do babies count toward the guest total at a Bali villa?

Often not, particularly if using a provided cot rather than a full bed, but this isn't universal. Confirm directly whether infants are excluded from the guest count and whether a cot is available.

### Can a villa provide an extra bed if our group is slightly over the bedroom capacity?

Many can, via a sofa bed, rollaway or extra mattress, usually for an additional fee. Confirm the maximum guest count with extras factored in before assuming a villa can't fit your group.

### Is there a separate charge for setting up an extra bed or cot?

Sometimes, separate from the per-guest nightly fee. Ask specifically whether a one-off setup charge applies in addition to any extra-guest cost.

### How do I avoid a surprise extra-guest charge on arrival?

Confirm your exact group size, including anyone joining partway through the stay, when you first enquire, and get the extra-guest terms and any fees confirmed in writing before you travel.
