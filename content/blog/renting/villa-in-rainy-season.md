Bali's rainy season runs roughly from November through March, with the heaviest rainfall typically in December and January. Rain usually comes as short, intense downpours rather than a full day of grey skies, so a typical day still has long dry stretches for the beach, sightseeing, or the pool. A villa with covered outdoor living space handles this pattern comfortably, since you are not stuck indoors between showers the way you might be in a hotel room.

Temperatures stay warm year-round, generally in the high twenties to low thirties Celsius, with humidity noticeably higher during rainy season than the dry months of April to October. Most guests find the trade-off worthwhile: fewer crowds, lower rates on many bookings, and lush green scenery, against the chance of an afternoon downpour most days.

## How the season actually behaves, month by month

Rainfall is not evenly spread across the season. Early months (November, December) tend to build up gradually, January is usually the wettest, and by March the rain tapers off toward the dry season. September, technically outside the wet season, occasionally sees an early shower but is generally reliable and dry.

| Month | What to expect |
|---|---|
| November | Rain increasing, still plenty of dry hours |
| December | Wet, warm, busy with holiday travellers despite the rain |
| January | Usually the wettest month, heaviest downpours |
| February | Similar to January, gradually easing |
| March | Rain tapering off, transitioning toward dry season |
| September | Outside the wet season proper; occasional early shower, mostly dry |

## What this means for a villa stay day to day

A downpour in Bali typically lasts anywhere from twenty minutes to a couple of hours, then clears. The practical effect on a villa stay is manageable if the property is set up for it:

- **Covered outdoor space matters.** A villa with a roofed terrace or living area lets you sit outside, watch the rain, and keep working or relaxing without getting wet — most of our villas have this built in.
- **Pool use is barely affected.** Many guests still swim during or right after a shower, since the water and air stay warm.
- **Roads can flood briefly** in low-lying areas after heavy rain, usually clearing within an hour or two; this is worth asking about for specific villa locations, particularly in Canggu where some lanes sit lower than the main roads.
- **Power can flicker** during heavy storms; see our guide on [power cuts in Bali](/blog/renting/power-cuts-in-bali/) for what most villas, including ours, have in place to manage this.

## Choosing where to stay for rainy season

Some areas handle heavy rain better than others because of drainage, road elevation and how built-up the immediate surroundings are. Sanur, on the calmer east coast, tends to have fewer flooding issues than the low-lying parts of Canggu near the rice fields. Umalas, set back from the coast, is generally well drained. Ungasan, on the elevated Bukit, rarely floods but can see stronger wind during storms given its clifftop position. None of this should be a reason to avoid a particular area outright, but it is worth asking us about a specific villa's drainage and road access if you are booking for December or January.

## What to pack and plan for

A short checklist that covers most of what rainy season adds to normal Bali packing:

1. A light rain jacket or poncho, since umbrellas are less useful in strong wind.
2. Waterproof or quick-dry footwear for wet pavements and occasional puddles.
3. A dry bag or waterproof phone pouch if you are out on a scooter.
4. Backup entertainment for an afternoon indoors — a book, downloaded films, a deck of cards.
5. Flexible day plans, since outdoor tours are the most likely activity to shift with weather.

## Things to do when it rains

Rainy season does not mean a wasted day. Most guests simply shift plans around the weather: an indoor cooking class, a spa treatment (see our guide on [in-villa massage](/blog/renting/massage-and-spa-at-your-villa/)), a museum or gallery visit, or simply a slow morning at the villa followed by an evening out once the rain clears, which it usually does by late afternoon. Because downpours are short, many guests find they barely notice the season once they adjust their expectations away from constant sunshine.

## Villa versus hotel in the wet season

The main advantage of a villa over a hotel room during rainy season is space and outdoor shelter. A hotel room during a two-hour downpour means staying in a single room or heading to a shared lobby; a villa with a covered terrace, living area and private pool gives a family or group room to spread out, cook, and wait out the rain comfortably without leaving the property.

## Traffic and getting around

One underappreciated benefit of the wet season is lighter traffic on Bali's often congested main roads, since fewer visitors are travelling during these months compared with the July–August and December peaks. A drive between Canggu and Ungasan that might take considerably longer during peak season can move more smoothly outside the Christmas and New Year window, which partly offsets the inconvenience of an occasional downpour when planning day trips or transfers.

## Photography and the upside of the wet season

Guests interested in photography often find rainy season genuinely rewarding: dramatic cloud formations, greener rice terraces, and the particular quality of light right after a downpour clears, when the air is washed clean and colours look more saturated. Sunsets during rainy season can be especially striking, with cloud cover creating more colour variation than the clearer skies of the dry months. This is a minor point compared with the practical planning above, but it is one reason some photographers and content creators deliberately choose to visit during the wetter months rather than avoiding them.

## Booking rates and availability in the wet season

Because fewer travellers plan trips for December through March compared with the July–August peak, rates on some villas ease during parts of the rainy season, and last-minute availability is often better. This is not universal — the Christmas and New Year weeks inside rainy season are among the busiest of the whole year despite the weather, so book those specific dates early regardless of season. Outside that holiday window, January and February tend to be quieter, and worth asking about if flexibility on timing matters more to you than guaranteed sunshine.

## What long-stay guests notice

Guests staying a month or longer through the wet season describe an adjustment period in the first week or two, followed by a routine that works around the weather naturally: mornings and early afternoons for outdoor plans, since rain is more likely later in the day, and evenings spent in the villa's covered living space. Laundry takes a little longer to dry and needs cover or a dryer on the wettest days, and scooters need more careful riding on wet roads, but neither is a significant obstacle once anticipated. Villas with a fully separate, covered kitchen and living area, which most of ours have, make this adjustment considerably easier than an open-plan layout with limited shelter.

## FAQ

### When is Bali's rainy season?

Roughly November through March, with December and January typically the wettest months. Rain usually falls as short, heavy downpours rather than continuous rain, leaving long dry stretches most days.

### Is Canggu worse for rain than other areas?

Some low-lying lanes in Canggu, particularly near the rice fields, can flood briefly after heavy downpours before draining within an hour or two. Sanur, Umalas and Ungasan generally have fewer drainage issues; ask us about a specific villa's location if this matters to your plans.

### What is the temperature like during rainy season in Bali?

Warm year-round, typically high twenties to low thirties Celsius, with humidity noticeably higher than the dry season. Evenings stay mild enough to sit outside comfortably once a downpour has passed.

### Is it worth visiting Bali during rainy season?

Many guests find it a good trade: fewer crowds, often lower rates, and lush scenery, against the chance of an afternoon shower most days. A villa with covered outdoor space and a private pool handles the pattern comfortably.

### Do power cuts happen more often during rainy season?

Storms can cause brief flickers or outages more often than in the dry months. See our guide on power cuts in Bali villas for what most properties, including ours, have in place to manage this.

### What should I pack for Bali's rainy season?

A light rain jacket rather than an umbrella, quick-dry footwear, a waterproof phone pouch if you plan to use a scooter, and some backup indoor entertainment for the occasional slow afternoon.
