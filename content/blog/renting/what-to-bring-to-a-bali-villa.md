## Start with what the villa already has

Before packing, check what is included, because most of the "what to pack for Bali" advice online is written for hotels, not self-contained villas. Every villa we manage comes with bed linen, towels, a fully equipped kitchen, air conditioning or fans, and Wi-Fi, and most have a washing machine on site or nearby. That changes the packing list considerably compared with a generic tropical holiday guide, so it is worth reading the specific villa's amenity list before you pack rather than assuming.

Bath towels, pool towels, bedding and basic kitchen equipment are covered. What is not always covered: a universal adapter, specific toiletries beyond a starter set, beach gear, and anything you would use daily and prefer in your own brand.

## Electrical and connectivity essentials

Indonesia runs on 230V with European-style round two-pin sockets, so a universal or EU-style adapter is worth packing if you are coming from the UK, US, Australia or most of Asia. Villa Wi-Fi speeds are generally fast — several of our villas run 200–300 Mbps — but if you are working remotely, bring your own backup, such as a local SIM or an e-SIM, in case of an outage, since even a well-managed connection can go down during heavy rain.

## Clothing and what the climate actually calls for

Bali is hot and humid year-round with a wet season roughly November to March, so lightweight, breathable clothing matters more than volume. A light rain jacket or a small umbrella is useful even in the dry season, since afternoon downpours happen without much warning. Pack for the specific things you plan to do: smart-casual for dinners out, something modest for temple visits (many require a sarong, though most temples rent or lend one at the entrance), and swimwear you are comfortable wearing at a private pool with staff sometimes present.

## Health, sun and insect protection

- Reef-safe sunscreen, since standard sunscreen is easy to buy locally but reef-safe formulas are less common in convenience stores
- Insect repellent, particularly for evenings near gardens or water features
- A basic first-aid kit or at least the essentials: rehydration salts, anti-diarrhoeal medication, and any personal prescriptions in their original packaging
- A physical or digital copy of your travel insurance details

Tap water is not drinkable in most of Bali; villas typically supply drinking water in refillable dispensers or bottles, and topping this up is straightforward through grocery delivery once you are settled in, which we cover in a [separate guide to villa grocery delivery](/blog/renting/grocery-delivery-to-your-villa/).

## A short pre-departure checklist

1. Confirm what the specific villa includes before packing anything duplicate
2. Pack a universal adapter and any prescription medication in original packaging
3. Bring modest clothing for one or two temple visits and a rain layer for the wet season
4. Pack reef-safe sunscreen and insect repellent, both easy to forget and not always easy to find locally
5. Leave towels, linens and full kitchenware at home

## Working remotely from a villa

If part of your trip is work, a few extras make a real difference beyond the Wi-Fi backup already mentioned: a laptop stand or a separate keyboard if you'll be at a desk for hours, noise-cancelling headphones for video calls if the villa is near a road or a lively neighbouring property, and a surge-protected power strip if you're travelling with multiple devices, since not every villa desk has enough sockets within reach. Several of our villas have a dedicated desk or workspace built in, but bringing your own small comfort items still helps for a multi-week stay.

## Money, documents and connectivity

Carry a mix of payment methods. Cards are widely accepted at larger shops and restaurants in Sanur, Canggu and Umalas, less so at smaller warungs and markets, so keep some rupiah in cash for day-to-day spending, tipping and smaller purchases. Bring physical copies or clear photos of your passport, visa approval, and travel insurance, stored somewhere accessible but not all in one bag. An e-SIM or local SIM card bought on arrival is worth arranging early if you plan to rely on mobile data outside the villa's Wi-Fi coverage, particularly if you're renting a scooter and need maps on the move.

## Kitchen and grocery basics worth packing

Villa kitchens come fully equipped with cookware and appliances, but a few small personal items are worth packing if you cook a lot: your own coffee or tea if you have strong preferences, a reusable water bottle for trips out, and any specific spices or condiments that can be hard to source locally. Everything else — rice, oil, fresh produce, snacks — is easy to order in once you arrive, which our [grocery delivery guide](/blog/renting/grocery-delivery-to-your-villa/) covers in detail, including which apps cover which areas and what typically isn't stocked by the villa itself.

## Packing for families with children

If you're travelling with young children, pack any specific formula, medication or comfort items you can't easily substitute locally, since brand availability varies. A travel cot, high chair or child safety gate is not standard in most villas unless specifically requested in advance, so check with the property before assuming one will be there. Life jackets for pool use are worth packing or requesting ahead of time rather than assuming the villa stocks children's sizes.

## Fitness, hobbies and downtime items

If your routine includes exercise, check whether the villa has a gym or gym access nearby, since not all do, and pack resistance bands or a yoga mat if you'd rather not rely on finding one locally. Snorkelling gear is worth packing if you plan beach days and prefer your own fit over rental equipment, though most beach clubs and dive shops rent good-quality gear if you'd rather travel lighter. A dry bag is a small, easy addition that protects electronics on any day trip involving water, boats or unpredictable weather.

## Practical extras that solve small annoyances

A few low-effort items consistently make villa stays smoother: a small torch or your phone's flashlight function for evening walks around a garden with uneven paths, a spare phone charging cable since sockets in a villa are sometimes spread across a room rather than clustered near the bed, and a laundry bag to keep worn clothes separate if you're doing your own washing rather than using a laundry service. None of these are essential, but travellers who've done a longer villa stay before tend to mention them.

## Packing differently by season

The dry season, roughly April to October, calls for less rain protection and more sun-focused packing — stronger sunscreen, a hat, and light layers for cooler evenings in areas like Ungasan where sea breeze off the Bukit cliffs can be noticeably cooler after dark. The wet season, November to March, means humidity stays high even as temperatures dip slightly, so quick-drying fabrics matter more than warmth, and a compact umbrella earns its space in the bag more than a heavy raincoat would. Neither season requires dramatically different packing, but small adjustments make the difference between comfortable and constantly improvising.

## FAQ

### Do Bali villas provide bed linen and towels?

Yes, essentially all managed villas, including all of ours, supply bed linen and both bath and pool towels as standard. You do not need to pack these. If a specific listing is unclear, ask the property directly before your trip rather than assuming and over-packing.

### What plug adapter do I need for Bali?

Indonesia uses 230V with the European-style round two-pin socket (Type C/F). Travellers from the UK, US, Australia and most of Asia will need an adapter; a universal travel adapter covers this along with most other destinations, so it is worth having one regardless of where else you are travelling.

### Should I bring my own toiletries?

Villas typically provide a starter set of toiletries, but if you have specific brand preferences or sensitive skin, bring your own, since restocking mid-stay depends on the villa and isn't guaranteed daily. Pharmacies and supermarkets in Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan stock most common brands.

### Is tap water safe to drink in a Bali villa?

No, tap water is not safe to drink anywhere in Bali, villas included. Villas typically provide refillable drinking water dispensers or bottled water, and topping up through grocery delivery once you arrive is straightforward and normal practice for longer stays.

### Do I need to pack a sarong for temple visits?

Not necessarily. Most temples in Bali rent or lend a sarong at the entrance for a small fee or as part of the entry ticket. Packing a lightweight one of your own is convenient if you plan several temple visits, but it is not essential for a short trip.
