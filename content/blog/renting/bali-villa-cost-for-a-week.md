The cost of a Bali villa for a week depends on three things: how many bedrooms you need, which area you book, and which month you travel. A one-bedroom pool villa on the Bukit can cost a fraction of a six-bedroom villa near Berawa beach in Canggu during August, so there is no single "average price" worth quoting. What you can do is understand the levers and work out a realistic range for your own trip.

Most guests booking direct end up somewhere between a modest studio-style villa and a larger family home, and the weekly rate usually works out cheaper per night than booking the same villa night by night. Below we go through what changes the number, what else to budget for beyond the villa itself, and how to get an accurate quote instead of guessing from a search result.

## What actually sets the weekly rate

Villa pricing in Bali is not standardised the way hotel pricing is. Owners and managers set rates based on:

- **Bedroom count and layout** — a 1-bedroom villa and a 4-bedroom villa are different products, not the same villa at a different size.
- **Area** — Sanur, Umalas, Canggu and Ungasan each have their own price bands, driven by proximity to the beach, nightlife and demand from long-stay guests versus short-trip tourists.
- **Season** — July–August and the December–January holidays push rates up everywhere; shoulder months (April–June, September–November) are calmer and cheaper.
- **Amenities** — a private pool versus a shared one, fast Wi-Fi, air conditioning in every room, and a dedicated workspace all affect what an owner can reasonably charge.
- **Length of stay discount** — many villas, including ours, knock a percentage off for a full week versus paying nightly.

None of this means the market is opaque. It means a week's cost is really "bedrooms × area × season", and once you fix those three, the range narrows fast.

## Costs beyond the villa rate

The quoted weekly rate rarely covers everything you'll spend on the ground. Typical extras to plan for:

1. **Airport transfers** — not always included; ask when you book.
2. **Extra housekeeping or linen changes** beyond what's standard for a week.
3. **A villa tax or service charge**, which some properties add on top of the rate rather than folding it in — see our guide on [villa tax and service charge for guests](/blog/renting/bali-villa-tax-and-service-charge-guests/) for how that usually works.
4. **Groceries and eating out**, which vary hugely by how much you cook versus dine.
5. **A security deposit**, refundable, held against damage.

We cover the full list of things guests often miss in [hidden costs of renting a villa in Bali](/blog/renting/hidden-costs-bali-villa/), which is worth reading alongside this page if you're budgeting a full trip rather than just the accommodation line.

## How area changes the weekly number

Sanur tends to sit at the calmer, more value-oriented end of the market — it's a quiet beachside area popular with families and longer stays, and villa rates there often reflect that. Canggu and Umalas, closer to the surf, cafés and nightlife, typically carry a premium, especially in peak months. Ungasan, on the clifftop south side near Melasti and Green Bowl beaches, has its own pricing logic tied to sea views and beach access.

## How season moves the price

The same villa can carry noticeably different rates in August versus May. Peak season (roughly July, August, and the Christmas–New Year fortnight) sees the highest demand and the highest prices; shoulder and low season bring rates down and give you more room to negotiate, especially for a full week or longer. If your travel dates are flexible, shifting by even two or three weeks outside a peak period can make a real difference to the weekly total. To make this concrete, imagine a family of four looking for a 2-bedroom pool villa for a week in July. The same family looking in Canggu, closer to Berawa beach and the beach club scene, would likely see a higher weekly rate for a comparable 2-bedroom villa, purely because that area's July demand is higher. This is the pattern worth internalising: the same bedroom count and season can still produce noticeably different weekly totals depending on which of the four areas you choose. If area flexibility isn't a constraint for your trip, comparing quotes across two or three areas before booking is one of the simplest ways to manage the weekly cost without downgrading villa size or amenities.

## Why nightly listings can mislead on the weekly total

A villa's nightly rate, especially as shown on a third-party platform, doesn't always reflect what a full week actually costs. Some listings show a lower nightly figure that excludes taxes, service charges or a cleaning fee applied per booking, which only becomes visible once you reach checkout. Others show a higher nightly rate that already includes everything. When you're comparing a nightly figure across two listings, multiply it out to a full week and then ask directly whether that total is final, rather than assuming the nightly number alone tells you the real weekly cost.

This is one reason booking direct tends to give a clearer picture: instead of piecing together a total from a platform's fee structure, you get a single quoted figure for your exact dates, and can ask immediately what it does and doesn't include.

## FAQ

### Is it cheaper to book a villa for a week than night by night?
Usually yes. Most villas, including ours, offer a discount for longer stays — we advertise 10% off a full week and 15% off a month, on top of a standard 5% direct-booking discount. Booking night by night at a nightly rate, especially through a third-party platform, tends to work out more expensive overall.

### Does the weekly rate include cleaning and utilities?
This varies by villa, so always ask. Many villas include standard housekeeping and utilities in the quoted rate but charge separately for extra linen changes, high air-conditioning use, or additional cleaning requests. Confirm what's included before booking so there are no surprises on arrival.

### How far in advance should I book to get the best weekly rate?
There's no fixed rule, but booking a few months ahead of peak season (July–August, December–January) generally gives you more choice and better rates, since the most in-demand villas fill up first. For shoulder and low season, you often have more flexibility to book closer to your travel dates.

### Is a security deposit normal for a week-long villa stay?
Yes, a refundable security deposit is standard practice for villa rentals, including ours, and is held against any damage during your stay. It's separate from the rental rate and is typically returned shortly after checkout once the villa has been checked.

### Can I negotiate the weekly rate directly with the villa manager?
Booking direct, rather than through an OTA, is usually where you have the most room to discuss rate, especially for a full week or longer, or during low season. We quote the same day and are upfront about what the rate includes, so it's worth asking directly rather than assuming the first number is fixed.
