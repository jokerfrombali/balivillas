Booking a Bali villa with baby cot and high chair on hand isn't something you should assume from a listing calling itself "family-friendly." Baby equipment varies villa to villa and isn't guaranteed by default, so it needs a direct question before you book, not after you arrive with a six-month-old and no cot.

We manage villas across four areas of Bali and this guide covers exactly what to confirm and what else matters when travelling with an infant.

## What to confirm before booking

Ask specifically: is a cot included as standard, or arranged on request; is it a travel cot or a fixed crib; is a high chair available and what condition is it in; and is there a cost for either item. None of this should be assumed from "baby-friendly" language in a listing. We're upfront that not every villa on our site has baby equipment ready by default, and we'll tell you honestly which of our villas can accommodate this and how far in advance we need notice to arrange it.

- Confirm cot type (travel cot vs. fixed crib) and whether it's included or on request
- Ask about high chair availability and give notice if needed
- Check pool fencing or gating if your baby is mobile or close to being so
- Ask whether the villa has a shaded outdoor area for daytime naps or play
- Confirm kitchen access for preparing bottles or baby food

## Pool safety with an infant

Beyond cot and high chair, pool safety is the detail parents of young children raise most. Ask whether the pool has any fencing or gate separate from the rest of the villa, and what the depth is at the shallowest point. Villa Kesari 4 in Sanur has a shallow area built into its pool, which some families with young children specifically ask for — worth mentioning if you have a similar need for another villa too.

## Which area suits travelling with a baby

Sanur tends to suit families with infants best: calmer streets, less traffic to navigate with a stroller, and a calm swimmable beach rather than surf. Umalas and Ungasan can work too, particularly if you're staying somewhere quiet rather than needing to be near a lot of activity. Canggu is more workable than it might seem, but its busier streets and less pram-friendly footpaths in places are worth factoring in if you'll be out and about a lot with a stroller.

| Consideration | Sanur | Canggu |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic/stroller friendliness | Generally easier | Busier, more variable footpaths |
| Beach type | Calm, swimmable | Surf beach |
| Pace | Quieter | More activity nearby |

## Booking timeline for baby equipment

If a cot or high chair isn't standard at your chosen villa, we typically need a few days' notice to arrange it, sometimes longer during busy periods. Mention it at the time of booking rather than closer to your stay, so we have time to confirm availability rather than scrambling last minute.

## Other equipment worth asking about

Beyond a cot and high chair, families travelling with an infant sometimes need other equipment: a baby bath, a stroller suited to the villa's surroundings (gravel paths and steps are common in Bali gardens, which not all strollers handle well), baby monitors, or a blackout option for daytime naps if the bedroom has large windows. None of this is standard across our villas, so build a full list of what you need and ask us to confirm each item rather than assuming a "family-friendly" villa covers everything.

If you're travelling with formula or specific baby food brands, it's worth knowing that imported baby products are available in Bali's larger supermarkets, particularly in Canggu and Seminyak, but selection is more limited in Sanur, Umalas and Ungasan, so consider bringing enough for the first few days if you have specific brand requirements.

## Health and safety considerations beyond the villa

Traveling to Bali with an infant involves the usual precautions any tropical destination requires: mosquito protection (nets or repellent suitable for infants), being careful with tap water for formula preparation (bottled or boiled water is the standard approach), and knowing where the nearest clinic is in case of a minor illness. Sanur has reasonable medical facilities within a short drive of most villas; other areas are a bit further from equivalent care, worth factoring in in your choice.

- Ask about mosquito nets or screens if travelling with a very young infant
- Confirm nearest clinic or hospital distance from your chosen villa
- Bring backup formula or specific baby food brands for the first few days if not confident of local availability

## Choosing an area with an infant in mind

Beyond equipment, area choice matters more with an infant than it might for older children. Sanur's calmer streets and shorter distances to a pharmacy or clinic tend to suit families with a very young baby better than areas requiring more driving for basic errands. If your trip involves multiple areas, consider front-loading the more remote or activity-focused parts of the trip for when the baby is a bit older during a longer stay, and keeping the immediate newborn or very young infant period somewhere calmer and more convenient.

## What other parents wish they'd known

Feedback from parents who've stayed with us suggests two recurring lessons: pack more spare clothing and supplies than feels necessary, since laundry turnaround and shopping trips take longer to arrange than at home, and build slack into the daily schedule, since travel with an infant rarely goes exactly to plan. Neither of these is specific to villa choice, but both affect how smoothly a trip with a baby actually goes, regardless of how well-equipped the villa itself is.

## Booking further ahead for baby equipment

Because not every villa keeps baby equipment on hand as standard, and arranging it can take a few days' lead time, it's worth booking further ahead than you might for a trip without an infant, particularly during busier periods when our team has less flexibility to source extra equipment quickly. If your travel dates are close and you haven't yet confirmed equipment availability, message us as soon as possible rather than waiting, so we have the best chance of arranging everything before you arrive.

## A realistic summary for parents planning this trip

Travelling to Bali with a baby is very manageable with the right preparation: confirm cot and high chair arrangements directly, check pool safety features, choose a calmer area like Sanur if that suits your family, and build in extra time for the unpredictability that comes with travelling with an infant. None of this should discourage the trip; it simply means planning a little more specifically than you would for a couple's or older-children's holiday.

## Packing versus renting locally

For some items, it's simpler to bring your own rather than rely on villa-provided equipment — a favourite travel cot sheet, a specific bottle brand your baby is used to, or a compact travel high chair that attaches to a normal chair. For bulkier items like a full-size cot, relying on the villa or a local rental service usually makes more sense than trying to bring one from home. Weigh each item on a case-by-case basis rather than assuming either approach is always right.

## FAQ

### Do all Bali villas provide a baby cot and high chair?
No, it varies by villa, and shouldn't be assumed from general "family-friendly" language in a listing. Always confirm directly whether a cot and high chair are included as standard or need to be arranged in advance.

### How much notice do I need to give for baby equipment at a villa in Bali?
A few days is usually enough for most requests, though it's best to mention it at the time of booking rather than closer to your stay, especially during busier periods when arranging extra equipment takes longer to confirm.

### Are Bali villa pools safe for babies and toddlers?
It depends on the specific villa. Some, like Villa Kesari 4 in Sanur, have a shallow area built in, but not every pool does, and fencing varies. Always ask directly about depth and any separation from open water before booking with a young child.

### Which Bali area is easiest for travelling with a baby?
Sanur is generally the easiest, with calmer streets, less traffic, and a calm swimmable beach rather than a surf beach. It's a common choice for families with infants for exactly these reasons.

### Can I get baby food or formula preparation facilities at a Bali villa?
Yes, all our villas include a fully equipped kitchen, which covers preparing bottles or baby food. If you need anything more specific, mention it when booking and we'll confirm what's realistic for the villa you're considering.
