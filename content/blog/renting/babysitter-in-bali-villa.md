Booking a babysitter for a villa stay in Bali means going through one of three routes: the villa's host or management team, a registered nanny agency, or a personal recommendation from another guest or expat network. There is no single official babysitting platform covering the whole island, so who you choose to trust matters more than the search itself.

Most families booking a villa in Sanur, Canggu, Umalas or Ungasan want the same thing: someone reliable for a few hours in the evening, or occasionally a full day, so the adults can have dinner out, go diving, or simply sleep in. The logistics are straightforward once you know which channel to use and what to check before you leave.

## The three ways to actually find one

**Through the villa host or management.** This is usually the fastest and, in our experience, the safest starting point, because a host who manages a villa long-term has an ongoing relationship with the babysitter and a reason to only recommend someone reliable. Ask us on WhatsApp when you book and we will tell you who is available for your dates and area.

**Through a nanny or babysitting agency.** Several agencies operate across south Bali, vetting and training their sitters, and can usually confirm a booking within a day or two. Agencies tend to cost more than an informal arrangement but come with a paper trail: a booking confirmation, an agency to complain to if something goes wrong, and often a replacement sitter if the first cancels.

**Through recommendations.** Expat parent groups on Facebook and community WhatsApp groups in Canggu and Sanur regularly share babysitter contacts. This route can work well but carries more risk, since you are relying entirely on someone else's judgement of a person you have never met.

| Route | Typical reliability | Typical cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Villa host or management | High — vetted through ongoing use | Moderate | Most families, especially first visit |
| Nanny agency | High — formally vetted, replaceable | Higher | Longer stays, multiple bookings, peace of mind |
| Community recommendation | Variable | Lower to moderate | Guests already embedded in local expat networks |

## What a babysitter actually costs

Babysitting rates in Bali vary by hours booked, number of children, whether the sitter has formal childcare training, and whether the booking is daytime or overnight. As an indicative range, expect an hourly rate that is modest by international standards but higher for overnight stays, multiple children, or a sitter with nursing or early-years qualifications. Agencies typically charge more than informal arrangements because the rate includes vetting and a booking guarantee. Always confirm the total for the hours you need, not just an hourly figure, since some sitters set a minimum booking length.

## What to check before you book

A short checklist before confirming any babysitter, regardless of which route you used to find them:

- Ask for a reference or how long they have worked with the villa host or agency.
- Confirm whether they have first-aid training, particularly if your children are very young or have any medical needs.
- Agree the exact hours, and what happens if you run late.
- Confirm whether they will stay inside the villa the whole time or expect to take children out (to a beach, playground, or shop) — and whether you are comfortable with that.
- Leave a written note with allergies, sleep routine, and an emergency contact number, even if you have discussed it verbally.
- Save the sitter's phone number and, if you can, a second contact (host or agency) in case you cannot reach them directly.

## Setting the villa up for a babysitting evening

Most of our villas have features that make an evening booking straightforward: a lockable pool gate or pool fence area, a baby monitor or clear sightlines to bedrooms, and Wi-Fi so the sitter can reach you or the host if needed. Before you go out, walk the sitter through the villa: where the pool safety features are, how the air conditioning and lights work, where the first-aid kit is, and how to reach building security or the villa host if there is an issue. If you are unsure whether a specific villa suits an evening with children left with a sitter, ask us when you book; we know each property's layout, including pool fencing and stair access.

## Daytime versus overnight bookings

A few hours in the evening is the most common booking, but families staying longer sometimes want a full day of childcare, for example to go diving, hike a volcano, or take a day trip without young children. Daytime and overnight bookings usually carry different rates and different expectations: confirm whether meals, naps, and outings are included, and whether the sitter is comfortable with an activity you are planning, such as a beach visit that involves supervising swimming.

## Working around Nyepi and public holidays

If part of your stay falls near Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, availability for babysitters, drivers and most services drops sharply because the whole island effectively pauses for the day; see our guide on [staying in a villa during Nyepi](/blog/renting/villa-during-nyepi/) for what that means practically. Book any childcare around that period well ahead, and expect reduced or no availability on the day itself.

## What can go wrong

The most common issues families report are last-minute cancellations, unclear pricing agreed only verbally, and mismatched expectations about whether the sitter can leave the villa with the children. All three are avoidable by confirming details in writing beforehand, even if it is just a WhatsApp message summarising hours, rate and what is and is not expected.

## Trusting your instincts on the first meeting

Even after checking references and confirming training, the first in-person meeting matters. A good sitter, agency or otherwise, is happy to answer questions, engage naturally with your children before you leave, and give you a clear sense they understand the routine you have described. If something feels off in that first meeting, it is entirely reasonable to postpone or ask the agency or host for an alternative rather than proceeding out of politeness. Trusting that instinct is a normal and sensible part of the process, not an overreaction.

## Longer stays and repeat bookings

Families staying a month or longer often want a more regular arrangement than a one-off evening booking, for example a sitter who comes a few afternoons a week so parents can work or explore alone. This is usually easiest to arrange through an agency or a host who can commit a specific person's regular availability, rather than hoping the same informal contact is free every time. If this is what you are planning, mention it early, since a regular booking often needs to be arranged a week or more ahead, especially in peak season when good sitters are already committed to other families.

## Language and communication

Most babysitters working with tourist families in south Bali speak enough English for basic care and instructions, but fluency varies, and for anything detailed, such as a specific medical condition or allergy, it is worth writing it down rather than relying on a verbal explanation alone. If your children speak a language other than English at home, mention this too; it rarely changes whether a sitter can care for them well, but it helps the sitter understand the child better in the first hour or two.

## FAQ

### How much does a babysitter cost in a Bali villa?

Rates vary with hours, number of children, and whether the sitter has formal training, and are generally higher for overnight bookings or agency-sourced sitters than informal arrangements. Ask for the total cost for your specific hours rather than assuming a flat hourly rate applies, since many sitters set a minimum booking.

### Is it safe to book a babysitter through my villa host?

In our experience it is usually the most reliable route, because a host managing a villa long-term has an ongoing relationship with the sitter and a reason to only recommend someone trustworthy. Still ask about their experience and, for young children, whether they have first-aid training.

### Can a babysitter take my children out of the villa?

Some will, to a nearby beach, playground or shop, but this should be agreed explicitly beforehand, not assumed. If you are not comfortable with your children leaving the villa, say so clearly when you book, and confirm the sitter agrees.

### What should I prepare before leaving my kids with a sitter?

A written note covering allergies, sleep routine and an emergency contact, plus a walkthrough of the villa's pool safety features, first-aid kit location, and how to reach the host or building security. Save the sitter's number and a backup contact before you leave.

### Can I book a babysitter for a full day, not just an evening?

Yes, many sitters and agencies offer daytime or overnight bookings, typically at a different rate than a short evening slot. Confirm what is included — meals, naps, outings — and check the sitter is comfortable with any specific activity you are planning, such as swimming supervision.

### Will a babysitter be available during Nyepi?

Availability for almost all services, including babysitting, drops sharply around Nyepi because the island effectively pauses for the day. If your stay overlaps with it, book childcare well in advance and check our guide on staying in a villa during Nyepi for what to expect.
