Power cuts happen occasionally in Bali, most often during heavy rainy-season storms, and typically last from a few minutes to an hour or two before the grid is restored. They are not a daily occurrence in most areas, but they are common enough that a well-run villa should have a plan for one, rather than leaving guests in the dark with no working Wi-Fi.

The practical impact on a stay depends far more on how the villa is set up than on how often outages happen. A villa with backup lighting, a generator or inverter for essentials, and staff who know how to respond calmly turns a power cut into a minor inconvenience. One without any of that turns it into a genuinely disruptive evening.

## Why power cuts happen

Indonesia's national grid, run by PLN, generally supplies reliable power across populated parts of Bali, but outages still occur for a few recurring reasons:

- **Storm damage.** Heavy rain, lightning and wind, most common between November and March, can trip breakers or damage lines; see our guide to a [Bali villa in rainy season](/blog/renting/villa-in-rainy-season/) for how the wet months affect a stay more broadly.
- **Scheduled maintenance.** PLN occasionally schedules maintenance work in specific areas, usually with some advance notice to residents, though tourists rarely see that notice directly.
- **Overloaded local circuits.** Rare in villa areas compared with the busiest tourist strips, but a spike in demand during peak season can occasionally cause brief local outages.
- **Construction nearby.** A neighbouring building project accidentally striking a cable is an occasional, very localised cause.

## What a well-run villa should already have

Before booking, it is fair to ask any villa host what happens if the power goes out. A property that has genuinely thought this through will typically have:

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| Backup lighting (torches, rechargeable lamps) | Keeps the villa usable and safe in the dark |
| Generator or inverter for key circuits | Keeps Wi-Fi router, some lighting and fans running |
| Staff on call or nearby | Someone who can respond quickly and explain what's happening |
| Battery power banks provided | Keeps phones charged during a longer outage |
| Clear communication with guests | A message explaining the cause and expected timing, not silence |

We keep this kind of setup across our own 20 villas precisely because an unexpected outage is one of the few things genuinely outside anyone's control, and the response matters more than the event itself.

## What to do if the power goes out during your stay

A short, practical sequence covers almost every situation:

1. Check whether it is just your villa or the wider area — a quick look at neighbouring buildings' lights usually tells you.
2. Message your host or the villa's WhatsApp contact immediately; most outages are already known and being tracked.
3. Use torches or your phone light rather than candles near curtains or bedding.
4. If you have medication needing refrigeration, ask the host whether a generator can prioritise the fridge, or whether a neighbouring property can help temporarily.
5. Avoid opening the fridge and freezer unnecessarily; they hold temperature for a few hours if left closed.
6. If the outage runs long, ask about backup accommodation or a temporary move, though this is rarely needed given how quickly most cuts resolve.

## How this differs by area

Power reliability is broadly similar across the main tourist areas of south Bali, since they share the same regional grid infrastructure. Sanur and Ungasan, being less densely built up than central Canggu, occasionally see marginally quicker restoration simply because there is less competing demand on local lines during a fault. This is a minor factor rather than a reason to choose one area over another; villa-level preparedness matters far more than area-level differences.

## Power cuts versus a lack of hot water

Guests sometimes conflate a power cut with a hot water issue, but they are usually unrelated. Most villas in Bali use electric water heaters, so a power cut does briefly affect hot water too, but a lack of hot water on its own, with power otherwise working, usually points to a different cause entirely. See our guide on [hot water in Bali villas](/blog/renting/water-pressure-and-hot-water-villa/) for that distinction.

## Nyepi is a different case entirely

Lights being kept deliberately low or off during Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, is a planned observance rather than a power cut, and it is worth not confusing the two if your stay overlaps with it. See our guide on [staying in a villa during Nyepi](/blog/renting/villa-during-nyepi/) for what that day actually involves.

## What to ask before you book

If reliable power matters to your plans, for example if you are working remotely or travelling with medication needing refrigeration, ask us directly what backup is in place at the specific villa you are considering. We can tell you honestly what each of our 20 villas has set up, rather than leaving it as an assumption.

Questions worth asking any host, not just us, before you commit to dates:

- Is there a generator or inverter, and which circuits does it cover — lighting only, or Wi-Fi and fans too?
- How quickly has power historically been restored after an outage at this specific property?
- Is there a member of staff on-site or on call who can respond immediately, day or night?
- What happens to hot water during an outage, given most villas heat water electrically?
- Is there a backup plan for guests with medical equipment or refrigerated medication?

A host who answers these clearly and specifically, rather than with a vague reassurance, is usually one who has actually thought it through rather than hoping it never comes up.

## Remote work and continuous power

Digital nomads and remote workers staying in Umalas and Canggu in particular sometimes ask about power reliability before anything else, since a dropped video call or lost work session is a real cost, not just an inconvenience. If continuous connectivity is central to your stay, it is worth asking not only about backup power but about the villa's internet setup more broadly — whether the router sits on a UPS or inverter circuit separately from general lighting, and whether the property has a secondary connection such as a mobile hotspot as a fallback. ## The bigger picture: how Bali's grid compares

Compared with many popular tropical destinations, Bali's power grid is relatively developed and outages in the main tourist belt of south Bali are the exception rather than the routine. Visitors coming from parts of the world with frequent scheduled load-shedding often find Bali's reliability better than expected once they arrive, even accounting for the occasional storm-related cut. That said, "occasional" still means guests should have a basic plan, the same way you would pack a torch for any trip involving an older or more remote property, rather than assuming nothing will ever happen during a two-week stay.

## FAQ

### How often do power cuts happen in Bali villas?

Not daily in most areas, but they do happen occasionally, most often during heavy rainy-season storms between November and March. A well-managed villa with backup lighting and generator or inverter support turns a brief cut into a minor inconvenience rather than a disruption.

### Does a power cut affect Wi-Fi?

Yes, unless the villa has a backup inverter or generator routed to the router. Ask specifically about this if you need continuous internet access, for instance for remote work, since not every property keeps Wi-Fi running through an outage.

### What should I do if the power goes out in my villa?

Message your host immediately, since most outages are already known about, use a torch or phone light rather than candles, and keep the fridge closed to hold its temperature. Ask about backup power for anything time-sensitive, such as refrigerated medication.

### Are power cuts more common in one area of Bali than another?

Reliability is broadly similar across Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan, since they share regional grid infrastructure. Villa-level preparedness — backup lighting, an inverter, responsive staff — matters far more to your experience than which area you choose.

### Is a power cut the same as the lights being off during Nyepi?

No. Lights kept low or off during Nyepi are a planned religious observance, not an outage. See our guide on staying in a villa during Nyepi for what that day involves if your stay overlaps with it.
