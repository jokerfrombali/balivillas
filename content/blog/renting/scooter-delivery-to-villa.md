Scooter delivery to a villa is one of the easiest things to arrange in Bali — a local rental operator or your villa manager can usually have one dropped off within an hour or two of booking, sometimes the same day you arrive. The scooter itself is rarely the hard part. Getting the legal and insurance side right before you ride is where guests get caught out, sometimes expensively.

This matters more than it might seem. Riding without the correct documentation can affect your travel insurance in the event of an accident, and traffic stops targeting tourists on scooters are common in some areas.

## How delivery actually works

You contact a rental operator, either directly or through your villa, agree the scooter model, rental length and price, and someone rides it to your villa, usually asking for a copy of your passport and, depending on the operator, a deposit either in cash or by holding your passport (a practice worth avoiding where possible — a cash or card deposit is safer for you). Pickup at the end works the same way in reverse.

## Documents you actually need

This is the part worth getting right before you ride, not after a checkpoint stops you. As of 2026, riding a motorbike or scooter in Indonesia as a foreign visitor generally requires an International Driving Permit (IDP) that covers motorcycles, obtained in your home country before travel — this specific requirement should be verified against current rules before you rely on it, since enforcement and requirements can shift. A regular home-country driving licence alone is typically not sufficient on its own.

## What a rental agreement should specify

A clear agreement should state the daily or weekly rate, what happens with existing damage (photograph the scooter together before you ride off), what the deposit is and how it is returned, and whether basic insurance is included or a separate add-on. Verbal-only agreements with no paperwork are common with informal operators but leave you with no recourse if a dispute comes up over damage or the deposit at drop-off.

## Helmets, insurance and everyday risk

A helmet should be provided and is non-negotiable to wear, both legally and practically, given how much scooter traffic Bali carries. Basic rental insurance, where offered, usually covers limited damage to the bike itself but rarely covers medical costs, so check your own travel insurance covers scooter accidents specifically — many standard policies exclude motorbike riding unless you hold the right licence and were wearing a helmet at the time.

## Where a scooter makes more sense than a driver

A scooter is well suited to short, flexible, local trips around your own area — a café, the beach, a nearby supermarket run — where a private car and driver would be overkill. For longer excursions, groups, or guests less confident on two wheels, arranging a [car and driver from your villa](/blog/renting/car-and-driver-from-your-villa/) is the better fit; that guide covers day rates and what to check before booking one. The two aren't really competing options — most longer-stay guests end up using both depending on the trip.

## A short checklist before you ride off

- Confirm you have an IDP covering motorcycles, or check current requirements if unsure
- Photograph the scooter's existing condition with the delivery rider present
- Get the rate, deposit terms and insurance coverage in writing
- Check your travel insurance covers scooter accidents
- Wear the helmet provided, every time, even for short trips

## Rental length and what changes with it

A single-day rental is the most flexible but the most expensive per day, and suits guests who only want a scooter for a specific short trip or two. Weekly rentals bring the daily rate down noticeably and suit most standard villa stays, while monthly rentals suit longer stays and often come with a lower deposit relative to the rental value, since the operator has more certainty over the booking. If your plans might change mid-stay, ask whether the rental can be extended day by day rather than committing to the longest term upfront, since switching operators partway through a stay is more hassle than it sounds.

## Choosing the right scooter for the terrain

Bali's terrain varies significantly by area, and the right scooter depends partly on where you're staying. Sanur and Umalas are relatively flat with wide, manageable roads, so a standard automatic scooter suits most riders. Ungasan and the wider Bukit peninsula involve steeper hills and winding roads, where a slightly higher-powered scooter is more comfortable, particularly with a passenger. Canggu sits somewhere in between, with heavier traffic in places like Berawa and Batu Bolong that calls for confidence manoeuvring in slow-moving queues rather than raw engine power.

## What to do if something goes wrong mid-rental

Keep the rental operator's contact details saved and accessible, not just in an email you'd need Wi-Fi to find. If the scooter develops a mechanical fault, most operators will swap it out, sometimes with delivery to your villa, so reporting an issue early is better than trying to ride it back yourself if something feels wrong. In the event of an accident, prioritise medical attention first, then contact the rental operator and your travel insurance provider, and take photos of the scene and any other vehicle involved if you're able to safely do so.

## Riding conditions specific to Bali

Traffic in Bali moves differently from many riders' home countries: lanes are loosely observed, overtaking happens on both sides, and scooters, cars and occasional livestock share the same narrow roads, particularly outside the main tourist strips. Rainy season riding adds standing water and reduced visibility to the mix, and it's common practice to simply wait out a heavy downpour under cover rather than push through it, since visibility and road surface both suffer quickly. New riders in Bali specifically, rather than new riders generally, benefit from a short test loop around quiet villa lanes before heading into busier roads like Jalan Raya Sanur or the main Canggu strip.

## Returning the scooter at the end of your stay

Arrange the return time and location before your last day, ideally matching it to your checkout time so there's no gap where you're paying for a scooter you're no longer using or, worse, without transport before your ride to the airport. Confirm how the deposit is returned — cash on the spot after a condition check is most common — and don't hand back the scooter without getting confirmation the deposit has been settled, particularly if it was held as a cash amount rather than a card authorisation.

## Costs to expect

Daily and weekly rental rates vary by scooter model, rental length and season, and any number you see online should be treated as an indicative range rather than fixed pricing. Weekly and monthly rentals are typically cheaper per day than a single day's hire, and rates in quieter areas can differ from busier tourist zones. Ask your villa manager or the operator directly for a current quote before committing.

## FAQ

### Do I need an International Driving Permit to ride a scooter in Bali?

As of 2026, riding legally generally requires an International Driving Permit covering motorcycles, arranged before you travel — this should be verified against current requirements since rules and enforcement can change. Riding without one can also affect your travel insurance if you're involved in an accident.

### Can a scooter be delivered directly to my villa?

Yes, this is standard practice. Rental operators or your villa manager can typically arrange delivery within a few hours of booking, sometimes the same day, and pick it up the same way at the end of the rental.

### Is a helmet included with a rented scooter?

Yes, a helmet should always be provided as part of the rental and must be worn, both for safety and because riding without one can void insurance coverage and lead to a traffic stop. If a helmet isn't offered, ask for one before accepting the scooter.

### What happens if the scooter is damaged during my rental?

This depends entirely on the rental agreement, which is why getting terms in writing and photographing the scooter's condition before you ride matters. Clear agreements state how damage is assessed and what it costs; informal, undocumented arrangements leave more room for dispute at drop-off.

### Is it safer to hire a driver instead of riding a scooter myself?

For guests unfamiliar with Bali traffic or without the right documentation, a driver is the safer and less stressful option. A scooter suits short, local, flexible trips for confident riders with proper paperwork; longer or group excursions are usually better handled with a private car and driver.
