A villa for remote work Bali booking lives or dies on Wi-Fi speed and desk setup, not the pool photo. It's easy to book on aesthetics and discover on day two that the Wi-Fi can't hold a video call, or that the only workspace is a low table by the pool with glare on your screen all afternoon.

Several of our villas were specifically set up with remote work in mind, and this guide covers what to check before booking one, whether it's ours or anyone else's.

## Wi-Fi speed: ask for the number, not the word "fast"

Almost every villa listing says "fast Wi-Fi." Ask for the actual speed instead. Several of our villas run at 200–300 Mbps, which comfortably handles video calls and large file uploads; a villa advertising "fast Wi-Fi" without a number attached could mean anything. If your work depends on stable video calls, ask this question directly and, if possible, ask whether the connection has ever dropped during a call — a manager who actually knows the answer is a good sign.

## Desk setup: real desk versus dining table

A dedicated desk, ideally with a proper chair and good light, makes a longer working stay noticeably more comfortable than working from a dining table or a low pool-side table. ## Quiet for calls

Beyond desk and Wi-Fi, think about where you'll actually take calls. An open living room facing the pool looks great but can be noisy if others are around, or picks up pool and garden sound on camera. If calls are a regular part of your work, ask whether the workspace is in a more enclosed room versus fully open-plan.

- Ask for the actual Wi-Fi speed in Mbps, not just "fast Wi-Fi"
- Confirm a dedicated desk exists, not just a dining or coffee table
- Ask whether the workspace is enclosed or open-plan if calls are frequent
- Check proximity to cafés or coworking spaces for a change of scenery

## Why Umalas and Sanur suit working stays

Where to stay in bali for remote work often comes down to Umalas or Sanur over Canggu, mainly because both offer quieter streets with less daily traffic to navigate, while still being close enough to cafés and coworking spaces (particularly from Umalas) for a change of scenery when needed. Canggu has plenty of coworking options too, but its busier streets can make a longer working stay feel more chaotic day to day. Sanur, meanwhile, suits guests wanting a calmer, more residential base for a working stay, with several villas offering a dedicated desk and strong Wi-Fi.

## Power reliability and backup

Bali's power supply is generally reliable but not immune to occasional outages, more common in the wet season than the dry. If your work depends on uninterrupted connectivity, ask whether the villa has any backup power arrangement, and consider a mobile data plan as backup to villa Wi-Fi for critical calls. This is a reasonable precaution rather than a common problem, but worth planning for if a dropped connection during an important meeting would be genuinely costly.

## Time zone considerations

Bali runs on WITA (UTC+8), which puts it roughly in line with Singapore and the Philippines, a few hours ahead of most of Europe and a full day or more offset from the Americas depending on the specific city. If you're coordinating regularly with a team in a very different time zone, think about which hours of the day you'll actually be working before committing to a long stay, and consider whether a villa with a quiet, enclosed workspace (rather than one built around communal, open-plan living) suits early or late calls better.

## Balancing work and the reason you came to Bali

The honest reality of a working stay is that it's easy to end up working the same hours you would at home, in a nicer setting, without actually experiencing much of Bali. Villas in Umalas and Sanur that are a short trip from a beach or a change of scenery help counter this, since a walk or swim at the end of the working day is easier to fit in than it would be after a long commute at home. Building this into your routine from day one tends to work better than planning to "catch up on exploring later," which often doesn't happen once you're settled into a working rhythm.

## Setting up your workspace on arrival

Once you've arrived, a few small adjustments make a working villa stay more comfortable: check the desk height and chair against your usual setup and ask about an extra cushion or a laptop stand if needed, confirm the strongest Wi-Fi point in the villa (sometimes it varies by room), and figure out the best-lit spot for video calls, since natural light in a tropical villa can be intense and inconsistent through the day. None of this requires anything from us beyond a quick message if something isn't working as expected.

## Balancing solo work with a partner or group

If you're travelling with a partner or friends who aren't working the same hours, or at all, it's worth agreeing on quiet hours or a workspace boundary so your work doesn't dominate the shared living space for everyone else. Villas with more than one distinct area — like Premier Umalas 17's two separate workspaces — handle this more gracefully than a single open-plan villa where work and leisure are competing for the same space all day.

## Remote work villa sanur versus Umalas for a working base

If you're weighing a remote work villa sanur stay against Umalas specifically, the distinction usually comes down to pace and proximity: Sanur offers a calmer, more residential setting with decent Wi-Fi in several villas but fewer coworking spaces nearby, while Umalas sits closer to Canggu's denser café and coworking scene. Neither is a wrong choice; Sanur suits guests who want to work from the villa itself most days, while Umalas suits guests who want the option of a change of scenery at a coworking space or café without a long commute.

## A final word on choosing where to stay in bali for remote work

There's no single best answer to where to stay in bali for remote work, since it depends on your specific balance of budget, desired pace, and how often you want to leave the villa during work hours. What we'd suggest is being honest with yourself about that balance before booking, then matching it to Umalas or Sanur's quieter settings, or Canggu's busier, more social working environment, rather than picking based on general reputation alone.
