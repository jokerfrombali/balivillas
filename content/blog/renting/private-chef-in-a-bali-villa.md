A private chef in a Bali villa cooks meals on-site, usually working from a menu you agree in advance, and is almost always a separate booking from the villa itself rather than something bundled into the nightly rate. The chef shops for ingredients, cooks in the villa's kitchen, and typically cleans up afterwards.

Pricing is normally per meal or per day rather than a flat inclusion, and it varies with the number of guests, the menu (local Indonesian food is generally cheaper to source than imported ingredients for Western dishes) and how many days you want the service running. Here's how to think about booking one well.

## What a private chef service typically includes

- Menu planning, usually with some back-and-forth so you can request dietary preferences, allergies or cuisine style.
- Grocery shopping, either included in the quoted price or billed separately as a pass-through cost.
- Cooking in the villa kitchen, generally for breakfast, lunch and/or dinner depending on what you book.
- Basic clean-up of cooking equipment, though not always full kitchen deep-cleaning.

What's usually **not** included: alcohol and specialty imported ingredients (often billed at cost), service beyond the agreed meal times, and grocery costs if the quote was for labour only. Always ask which pricing model applies — "all-in per meal" versus "chef fee plus groceries at cost" — since the two can look very different on paper for the same actual cost.

## Indicative cost structure

Prices vary by villa, group size and menu complexity, so treat this as a guide to the shape of typical arrangements, not a quote.

| Arrangement | Typical structure | What drives the price |
|---|---|---|
| Single meal (e.g. dinner) | Chef fee + ingredients, per person or per group | Menu complexity, group size |
| Full day (breakfast, lunch, dinner) | Day rate, sometimes with ingredients included | Number of meals, guest count |
| Multi-day stay | Discounted day rate for consecutive days | Length of booking, repeat menu efficiency |
| Special occasion menu | Premium pricing for specific requests (seafood, imported cuts) | Ingredient sourcing, prep time |

Always ask for a written quote before the chef starts, covering the full stay, not just the first meal — this avoids ending up mid-negotiation on day three of your trip.

## How to compare two chef offers properly

Don't just compare a headline number. Ask each offer the same set of questions so you're comparing like for like:

- [ ] Does the quote include groceries, or are those billed separately at cost?
- [ ] How many meals per day, and for how many guests?
- [ ] Can the menu be changed day to day, or is it fixed in advance?
- [ ] Are dietary restrictions and allergies handled at no extra cost?
- [ ] Is there a minimum booking (e.g. two days minimum) or a cancellation policy?
- [ ] Who cleans the kitchen afterwards, and is that included?

A quote that looks cheaper per meal but excludes groceries can end up costing more than an all-in quote once you add up ingredient costs across a multi-day stay.

## Ordering versus arranging in advance

Some villas can arrange a chef with a day or two of notice; a specific request for a special-occasion menu (a birthday dinner, a particular cuisine) generally needs more lead time so the right ingredients can be sourced. If a chef is important to your trip, ask about it when you first enquire about the villa rather than once you've arrived — this is one of the most common last-minute requests we get, and it's far easier to arrange well in advance.

## How this differs from breakfast being included

A private chef and an included breakfast are two different things, even though guests sometimes conflate them. An included breakfast is typically a simpler, standardised offering bundled into the room rate; a private chef is a bookable, flexible, separately priced service that can cover any meal and adapts to your menu preferences. See our [breakfast-included guide](/blog/renting/villa-breakfast-included-bali/) for how that works, since some guests are actually looking for one and asking about the other.

## Why villas differ on this

Villas with an established local team, like ours, usually have a network of chefs they've worked with directly and can quote quickly with a clear price breakdown. Villas relying on ad-hoc third-party bookings may take longer to confirm, and pricing transparency can be inconsistent between requests. If a chef matters to your stay, ask the villa directly whether they can arrange it in-house or need to outsource it — in-house arrangements are usually faster to confirm and easier to adjust once you've arrived.

## How location affects chef arrangements

In Ungasan's clifftop villas, further from restaurants, a private chef is often a more practical choice than driving out for dinner, and many guests there book one for at least part of their stay. In Sanur and Canggu, where walkable dining is closer, a chef is more of a convenience or occasion choice than a necessity. ## FAQ

### How much does a private chef cost in a Bali villa?

It varies by meal count, group size and menu — there's no single fixed rate, and quotes differ between villas and chefs. Ask for a written breakdown covering chef fee and groceries separately so you can compare offers accurately. Multi-day bookings are often priced at a lower day rate than single, one-off meal requests.

### Does the chef shop for groceries themselves?

Usually yes, either as part of the quoted price or billed separately at cost. Ask which model applies before booking, since "groceries included" and "groceries at cost" can result in very different final bills, especially if you request imported ingredients, seafood, or a specialty cuisine that needs sourcing from further afield.

### Can a private chef handle dietary restrictions?

In most cases, yes — mention allergies, intolerances or preferences (vegetarian, halal, gluten-free) when you first enquire so the chef can plan the menu accordingly. This is usually handled without extra cost, though a highly specialised diet requiring imported substitute ingredients may add to the grocery bill.

### Is a private chef worth it for a short stay?

For a one- or two-night stay, many guests find it more practical to book a single special dinner rather than full chef service for every meal. For longer stays, especially in areas further from restaurants like Ungasan, a chef for most meals often works out more convenient and comparable in cost to eating out repeatedly.

### How far in advance should I book a private chef?

A few days' notice is usually enough for a standard menu; a special-occasion or specific cuisine request benefits from more lead time so ingredients can be sourced properly. If a chef matters to your trip, raise it when you first ask about the villa rather than waiting until you've checked in.
