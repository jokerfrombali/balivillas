Ask ten villa listings what's included and you'll get ten different answers, some deliberately vague. The short version: the nightly rate almost always covers the villa itself, the private pool, daily housekeeping, wifi, air conditioning and basic kitchen equipment. It usually does not cover food, airport transfers, a private chef, extra guests beyond the stated capacity, or a security deposit.

That gap between "usually included" and "usually extra" is where most guest disappointment comes from, not because the villa lied, but because nobody read the listing closely before booking. Below is what to actually check, area by area, before you send a deposit.

## The core inclusions almost every villa offers

Across Sanur, Canggu, Umalas and Ungasan, the baseline is fairly consistent. A private pool is standard on nearly every villa listing in these areas — it's the main reason people choose a villa over a hotel room. Daily or near-daily housekeeping is also standard, though the exact frequency varies (see our guide to [daily housekeeping in a Bali villa](/blog/renting/daily-housekeeping-bali-villa/) for how that actually works).

Wifi is included everywhere that markets itself to remote workers; speeds vary more than the listings suggest. Air conditioning in bedrooms is standard; air conditioning in living areas is not always included, and running it constantly can affect an electricity bill if that's billed separately (rare, but worth confirming for longer stays).

A fully equipped kitchen — fridge, stovetop, basic cookware, kettle — is typical, even though most guests don't cook much. It matters for anyone on a longer stay who wants to self-cater some meals.

## What is usually NOT included

This is the list that catches people out:

- **Meals and groceries** — breakfast is sometimes included on villa rentals (see our [breakfast-included guide](/blog/renting/villa-breakfast-included-bali/)), but lunch and dinner almost never are unless you book a private chef separately.
- **Airport transfer** — often offered as a paid add-on, occasionally complimentary on longer bookings; always confirm rather than assume.
- **A private chef or extra staff beyond standard housekeeping** — see our guide on [private chef service in a Bali villa](/blog/renting/private-chef-in-a-bali-villa/).
- **Extra guests** — most listings state a maximum occupancy and charge extra per additional guest beyond that.
- **A security deposit** — common on villa bookings, refunded after checkout minus any damage or missing-item deduction.
- **Government tax and service charge** — sometimes built into the quoted rate, sometimes added at checkout. See our guide on [Bali villa tax and service charge for guests](/blog/renting/bali-villa-tax-and-service-charge-guests/) for how that's usually structured.

## Why "included" varies so much between listings

Villas that are professionally self-managed, like ours, tend to state inclusions clearly up front because the same team handles bookings, cleaning and guest questions, so there's no incentive to hide extras. Villas managed through a chain of agents or resold across multiple booking platforms often carry inconsistent listings — one platform lists breakfast, another doesn't, because nobody updated all of them.

When you're comparing options, ask directly rather than assuming the listing is complete: "does the quoted rate include tax, service charge and daily cleaning, and is anything charged separately at checkout." A direct WhatsApp conversation clears this up in minutes and avoids surprises at checkout.

## How staff and service differ from hotel service

A villa with daily housekeeping is not the same as a villa with live-in staff. Most rental villas in Bali are staffed during the day for cleaning, pool maintenance and light garden upkeep, not overnight. If you're picturing a butler on call 24 hours, that's a different (and rarer, more expensive) category of property. Our guide on [whether Bali villas have staff](/blog/renting/do-bali-villas-have-staff/) breaks down exactly what "staffed" means in practice.

## What changes by area

Sanur villas, often booked for longer family stays, are more likely to include a fuller kitchen setup and sometimes a welcome grocery pack. Canggu and Umalas villas geared toward digital nomads tend to foreground wifi speed and workspace over meal inclusions. Ungasan's clifftop villas, being further from restaurants, more often include or strongly recommend a private chef arrangement simply because dining out means a drive. ## FAQ

### Is breakfast included in a Bali villa rental?

Not by default. Some villas include breakfast as part of the package, especially on longer or family stays, but many do not and expect guests to self-cater or order in. Always ask directly; if breakfast matters to you, request it as part of your quote rather than assuming it's standard across every listing.

### Does the villa rate include tax and service charge?

It depends on the villa. Some quote an all-inclusive rate; others add tax and service charge at checkout. Ask for a rate breakdown before booking so there's no surprise at the end of the stay. Our guide to [tax and service charge for guests](/blog/renting/bali-villa-tax-and-service-charge-guests/) explains the usual structure.

### Is a security deposit normal for villa rentals in Bali?

Yes, it's common, particularly for private-pool villas with higher-value furnishings. It's typically refunded within a few days after checkout, minus any damage or missing items. Ask how the deposit is collected (cash, card hold or bank transfer) and how refunds are processed before you arrive.

### Does the rental include airport pickup?

Sometimes, particularly on longer stays, but it's not universal. Many villas offer it as a paid add-on instead. Confirm at the time of booking whether pickup is included, optional, or not offered at all, and get the price if it's an add-on so you can compare it against a taxi or ride-hailing app.

### What's the difference between a villa with staff and one without?

Nearly all rental villas have some daytime staff for cleaning and pool maintenance; "staffed" in the fuller sense — a dedicated chef, driver or live-in helper — is a separate, usually paid, arrangement. See our guide on [whether Bali villas have staff](/blog/renting/do-bali-villas-have-staff/) for the distinction.
