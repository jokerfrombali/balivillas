Bali villa Wi-Fi speed usually sits between 100 and 300 Mbps on fibre connections in Sanur, Canggu and Umalas, which is enough for video calls, screen sharing and streaming from most rooms. The number that matters more than the plan speed is signal strength at your desk, not at the router, and that is what you should ask about before you book.

Internet in Bali has improved a great deal in the past few years. Fibre (IndiHome, Biznet, MyRepublic and similar providers) now reaches most residential streets in the south, and villa owners who host guests for a living tend to pay for a business-grade plan rather than a basic home package. Still, "Wi-Fi included" on a listing tells you nothing about coverage, backup, or what happens when the exchange goes down for an afternoon, which happens a few times a year everywhere on the island.

## What speed you actually need

Video calls, remote work and streaming do not need the headline number on the router; they need a stable, low-latency connection at the spot where you sit.

| Task | Realistic minimum | Comfortable |
|---|---|---|
| Voice call, browsing, messaging | 5 Mbps | 15 Mbps |
| One video call (Zoom, Meet) | 10 Mbps up/down | 25 Mbps |
| Video call plus screen share, two people online | 20 Mbps | 50 Mbps |
| 4K streaming, large file uploads | 25 Mbps | 100 Mbps+ |
| Live streaming or large video export | 30 Mbps up | 100 Mbps up |

A villa advertising a "300 Mbps fibre line" easily covers all of this in theory. What it does not guarantee is that the signal reaches the garden bale, the upstairs bedroom, or a villa with thick concrete walls between the router and where you want to sit.

## Why the advertised number is not the whole story

Three things determine what you actually experience, and none of them show up on a listing page:

- **Router placement.** A single router in the living room often cannot reach an outdoor pool deck or a second-floor bedroom without a mesh extender or access point.
- **Shared load.** In a multi-bedroom villa with several guests on calls at once, an unmanaged connection can bottleneck even with a decent base plan.
- **Local outages.** Fibre lines in Bali do go down, usually from construction work cutting a cable rather than capacity issues. How fast a villa can restore service (backup SIM, secondary line, on-call technician) matters more than the plan's top speed.

We fit our own villas with mesh Wi-Fi covering indoor and outdoor areas and keep a mobile hotspot as backup for guests who need to stay online for calls during an outage. It is a detail worth asking about wherever you book.

## Starlink as a backup or primary option

Some villas, particularly newer builds or ones in areas with patchier fibre coverage, now run Starlink alongside or instead of a fixed line. Starlink in Indonesia typically delivers 50–150 Mbps with low latency, which is more than enough for calls and remote work, and it is largely unaffected by local cable cuts since it does not depend on the same ground infrastructure.

The trade-off is that Starlink can be more exposed to weather (heavy rain sometimes causes brief drops) and dish placement needs a clear view of the sky, which is not always simple under a villa's tree canopy. As a backup connection sitting behind fibre, it is close to ideal; as the sole connection it is very good but not flawless.

## Questions to ask before you book

If a stable connection is important to your trip, ask the host these questions directly rather than trusting the listing copy:

1. What is the actual plan speed, and is it fibre, cable or Starlink?
2. Is there Wi-Fi coverage at the desk, pool area and bedroom you will use, or just near the router?
3. What happens if the internet goes down: is there a backup line, hotspot, or nearby co-working space?
4. How many other guests or staff share the same connection?
5. Can they send a speed test result from the property before you commit?

A host who manages villas professionally will usually have this answered within minutes on WhatsApp, since it is one of the most common questions from remote workers booking a longer stay.

## Working around a slow connection on arrival

If you check in and the Wi-Fi is slower than expected, a few things usually fix it faster than waiting for a technician:

1. Move to the room or spot closest to the router first; walls and distance are the most common cause of a weak signal, not the plan itself.
2. Restart the router once, since a router that has been running for weeks without a reboot is a common, boring cause of slowdowns.
3. Ask whether a mesh extender exists and can be moved closer to where you are working.
4. Check whether other guests or staff devices are streaming or downloading heavily at the same time.
5. If none of that helps, ask about a mobile hotspot or the nearest co-working space as a same-day fallback while the connection is sorted.

A host who manages the villa directly, rather than through a distant agency, can usually action most of this within the hour. That responsiveness is worth asking about before you book if your work depends on it.

## Co-working spaces as a backup plan

Even with a strong villa connection, it is sensible to know your nearest backup option, especially for an important call you cannot afford to lose. Canggu and Umalas have the highest density of co-working spaces and laptop-friendly cafés on the island, which is part of why those areas attract long-stay remote workers in the first place. Sanur and Ungasan have fewer dedicated co-working venues but still have reliable café options with Wi-Fi. If your stay is work-heavy, factor proximity to a backup workspace into your area choice alongside the villa's own connection.

## How villa Wi-Fi compares across our four areas

Fibre coverage in Bali is not identical everywhere, and while individual villa setup matters more than area, general infrastructure differs:

- **Sanur:** well-established fibre coverage, generally stable, quieter demand on shared local infrastructure than the busier west-coast areas.
- **Canggu:** dense fibre coverage given the concentration of remote workers, though local infrastructure can be more heavily loaded at peak times.
- **Umalas:** similar to Canggu in coverage quality, with slightly less congestion due to lower density.
- **Ungasan:** coverage has improved significantly in recent years but can still be patchier on the Bukit peninsula than in the south's more built-up areas, which is one reason some Ungasan villas lean on Starlink.

## What separates a good villa setup from an average one

The difference rarely shows up in the advertised Mbps figure. It shows up in mesh coverage reaching every room and the pool area, a UPS or backup line keeping calls alive through a brief outage, and a desk positioned somewhere with both signal and decent light for video calls. Villas built or renovated in the last two or three years tend to plan for this from the start; older properties sometimes still route Wi-Fi as an afterthought.

## FAQ

### Do Bali villas have reliable internet for remote work?

Most villas in Sanur, Canggu and Umalas now run fibre plans of 100 Mbps or higher, which comfortably supports video calls and remote work. Reliability varies more by property age and router setup than by area; ask the host about coverage at your desk and what backup exists if the line drops, rather than relying on the advertised speed alone.

### Is Starlink common in Bali villas now?

It is becoming more common, especially as a backup to fibre or in villas slightly outside the main fibre-covered streets. Speeds typically run 50–150 Mbps with low latency, which works well for calls and browsing. It is not universal yet, so confirm with the specific villa rather than assuming it is included.

### What internet speed do I need for video calls in a villa?

A single stable video call needs around 10 Mbps up and down; screen sharing or two people on calls at once pushes that closer to 20–25 Mbps. Most villa fibre plans exceed this easily on paper. The real risk is Wi-Fi coverage at your specific spot in the villa, not the plan speed itself.

### What happens if the villa's internet goes down mid-stay?

It depends entirely on the host. Some villas keep a mobile hotspot or a secondary line as backup and can restore connectivity within minutes; others have no fallback and you would need to find a nearby café or co-working space. Ask this before booking if uninterrupted connectivity matters to your trip.

### Can I test the Wi-Fi speed before booking a villa?

Yes, ask the host to send a speed test screenshot taken from inside the unit, ideally from the room or outdoor area where you plan to work. A host who manages the property directly, rather than through a third party, can usually provide this the same day.
