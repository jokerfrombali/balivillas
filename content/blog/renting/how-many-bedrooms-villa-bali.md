The bedroom number people search for is usually a proxy for a different question: will everyone in our group be comfortable, or will we be tripping over each other by day three. Bedroom count alone doesn't answer that — bathroom ratio, whether rooms are en-suite, and how much shared living space the villa has all matter as much or more.

As a working rule: count couples and solo travellers who each need their own room, add any families needing an extra room for kids, then check the bathroom count against that number before you look at anything else.

## Start with bathrooms, not bedrooms

A villa with four bedrooms and two bathrooms works fine for a family sharing, but is a poor fit for four separate couples who each expect privacy getting ready in the morning. Most of our villas run close to a 1:1 bedroom-to-bathroom ratio with en-suite rooms — for example the [Sanur Black Pool villa](/villa/sanur/villa-black-pool-4br-sanur/) has four bedrooms and four bathrooms — which matters more for mixed groups of friends than raw bedroom count does.

## Kids change the calculation

For families, a spare bedroom often matters less than pool safety, a ground-floor room to avoid stairs with a toddler, and whether the kitchen can handle simple meals for children on their own schedule. Some of our villas, like [Villa Kesari 4](/villa/sanur/villa-kesari-4-3br-pool-villa-sanur/) in Sanur, have a shallow section built into the pool specifically for this — worth asking about directly if you're travelling with young children, rather than assuming any private pool is child-safe.

## Groups of friends: privacy versus shared space

Friend groups splitting a villa often care more about not overhearing each other than about raw bedroom count. Rooms with separate entrances or set apart from the main social area — some of our Sanur properties are structured this way — suit groups who want to keep late nights and early risers from disturbing each other. Ask specifically about room layout relative to the living area and pool, not just "how many bedrooms."

## When one big villa beats two smaller ones

Groups above 10–12 people sometimes assume they need to split across two villas. One larger property, like a [9-bedroom resort-style villa in Canggu](/villa/canggu/leonardo-9br-resort-pererenan/), can be more practical: everyone shares one pool, one kitchen, one booking and one bill, rather than coordinating logistics across two addresses. Whether that's better depends on whether the group actually wants to be together most of the time, or values having separate spaces to retreat to.

## Remote workers: bedroom count isn't the real question

If part of the group is working during the stay, the real question is how many people need a quiet space with a desk at the same time, not how many bedrooms exist. Several of our Umalas and Sanur villas have a dedicated desk in every bedroom for exactly this reason — worth confirming directly if overlapping work calls are part of the trip.

## A simple sizing checklist

- Count adults needing separate sleeping privacy, then add rooms for children.
- Check the bathroom count against that number, not just bedroom count.
- If travelling with young kids, ask specifically about pool depth and ground-floor rooms.
- If it's a friend group, ask about room layout relative to shared spaces.
- If anyone is working remotely, confirm desk availability per bedroom.

## What to do next

Work out your real number using bathrooms and shared-space needs, not just headcount divided by two. That's the number worth sending to a villa manager, along with any specifics — young kids, remote work, groups wanting privacy from each other.

## FAQ

### Is bedroom count the best way to choose a villa size?

Not on its own. Bathroom ratio, whether rooms are en-suite, and shared living space comfort matter as much as bedroom count, especially for groups of friends or families with children.

### How many bedrooms do we need for two couples travelling together?

Two bedrooms is the minimum, but check the bathroom count and room layout — two en-suite bedrooms with some separation from each other suit two couples better than two bedrooms sharing one bathroom.

### Is it better to book one large villa or two smaller ones for a big group?

It depends on whether the group wants to share one pool, kitchen and living space, or prefers separate retreat spaces. One larger villa, including 9-bedroom resort-style options, simplifies logistics and billing for groups of 10 or more.

### What should families with young children check beyond bedroom count?

Ask specifically about pool depth or a shallow kids' area, ground-floor room availability to avoid stairs, and whether the kitchen suits preparing simple meals on a child's schedule.

### Do villa bedrooms usually have their own bathroom?

Many do, but not all — some villas have a lower bathroom-to-bedroom ratio. Always confirm the exact ratio and whether bathrooms are en-suite or shared before booking for a group.
