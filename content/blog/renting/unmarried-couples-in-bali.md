Unmarried couples book private villas in Bali constantly, and marital status is not something a villa manager asks about or checks. Passport details go on the booking form for standard identification purposes — the same as any international rental — and that's the extent of it.

The confusion usually comes from a 2022 change to Indonesia's criminal code that technically restricts unmarried cohabitation. In practice, this provision is aimed at complaints from family members against Indonesian citizens, not at foreign tourists booking a private villa, and we're not aware of it being applied to short-term holiday rentals. Still, because this is a legal question and not a company policy, treat the specifics as something to verify rather than take as settled (as of 2026, verify).

## What villa booking actually requires

Booking a private villa works the same way regardless of relationship status: names, passport numbers, arrival and departure dates, and payment once dates are confirmed. Nobody asks for a marriage certificate, and nothing on a booking form distinguishes a married couple from an unmarried one, friends travelling together, or a family group. This is different from some budget hotels, particularly smaller local establishments, which have historically been more cautious with unmarried Indonesian couples specifically — that dynamic doesn't extend to how international villa rentals operate.

## Why private villas are simpler than hotels on this

A private villa is a rented residence for the length of your stay, not a hotel room with front-desk staff monitoring who comes and goes. There's no check-in desk asking questions about your relationship, and privacy is part of what a villa is for. This is one of the practical reasons couples — married or not — often prefer a villa over a hotel for exactly this kind of trip.

## What long-term expat couples do

Unmarried couples who relocate to Bali for months or years, rather than a short holiday, rent villas and apartments the same way as short-stay tourists — no different documentation, no different process. Long-term visa categories are handled separately through Indonesian immigration and have their own requirements unrelated to relationship status; anyone planning a long-term move should look into that process directly rather than assuming it interacts with villa booking at all. For a short holiday stay, none of this is relevant in the first place.

## What guests actually experience at check-in

In practice, check-in for an unmarried couple looks identical to check-in for anyone else: the villa manager or caretaker greets you, confirms your names against the booking, runs through the villa's features and house rules, and hands over keys or access details. No question is asked about the nature of your relationship, and none of the paperwork involved distinguishes between guest types in this way. Guests who arrive expecting some kind of scrutiny are usually surprised by how routine the process is.

## Family and honeymoon trips booked as one group

A related question we sometimes get is whether an unmarried couple travelling as part of a larger family or friend group, for example alongside parents or siblings, needs to book separate villas or rooms. This is entirely a personal or family preference rather than anything a villa requires — a group booking a larger multi-bedroom villa together, married or not, books it the same way regardless of how the group chooses to split bedrooms internally. Nothing about villa booking dictates room-sharing arrangements within a group.

## Where the confusion actually comes from

The 2022 revision to Indonesia's criminal code drew significant international media coverage at the time, much of it framed in a way that suggested a blanket ban affecting all visitors. The reality, as most legal commentary on the change has clarified since, is narrower: the provision generally requires a formal complaint from a spouse, parent or child to trigger enforcement, which makes it structurally aimed at family disputes involving Indonesian citizens rather than routine enforcement against tourists. That said, because this sits in criminal law rather than villa policy, we are not in a position to give legal advice, and anyone with a specific concern beyond ordinary holiday booking should check current guidance from a qualified source rather than relying on general reassurance, including ours (as of 2026, verify).

## FAQ

### Can unmarried couples share a room in a Bali villa?

Yes. Villa booking doesn't involve any check on marital status, and there's no separate room arrangement expected for unmarried couples. You book the villa as a couple the same way you'd book it as a family or group of friends — names and passport details, nothing more.

### Is it illegal for unmarried couples to stay together in Bali?

Indonesia's criminal code does technically restrict unmarried cohabitation following a 2022 revision, but in practice this has been applied to complaints involving Indonesian citizens rather than to foreign tourists on holiday. This is a legal question, not a villa policy one, so treat specifics as something to verify with current guidance rather than settled fact (as of 2026, verify).

### Do hotels in Bali ask for a marriage certificate?

Some smaller, local budget hotels have historically been more cautious about unmarried Indonesian couples specifically, though this isn't universal and isn't typically applied to international tourists. International-standard hotels and private villas generally don't ask. If you're booking a hotel rather than a villa elsewhere on your trip, it's worth checking that specific property's policy.

### Why do couples prefer a private villa over a hotel in Bali?

Privacy is the main reason — a villa is a self-contained space for the length of your stay, without a front desk or shared corridors. It also tends to suit couples wanting a quieter setting; areas like Sanur and Umalas are popular for exactly this. None of this relates to marital status specifically, it's simply how villas differ from hotels generally.

### Does booking a villa in Bali require proof of relationship?

No. Booking requires passport details and payment after dates are confirmed, the same as any international rental. There's no field or question anywhere in the process that asks about the nature of the relationship between guests booking together.
